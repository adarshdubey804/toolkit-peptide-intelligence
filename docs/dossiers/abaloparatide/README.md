# 047 — Abaloparatide (TYMLOS)

**Curation status:** in_progress  
**Workbook sequence:** 047  
**Entity class:** synthetic modified peptide; parathyroid hormone-related protein (PTHrP) analogue; PTH1R agonist  
**Evidence cut-off:** 4 November 2026

> **Scope.** Abaloparatide is a synthetic 34-amino-acid peptide analogue of the N-terminal region of human PTH-related peptide (PTHrP). Intermittent subcutaneous dosing stimulates bone formation and is used to treat osteoporosis in selected patients at high fracture risk. It is not a bisphosphonate or a PTH replacement product.

## 2. Molecular identity and sequence

- **Length:** 34 amino acids.
- **Sequence:** `Ala-Val-Ser-Glu-His-Gln-Leu-Met-His-Asn-Leu-Gly-Lys-His-Leu-Asn-Ser-Met-Glu-Arg-Val-Glu-Trp-Leu-Arg-Lys-Lys-Leu-Gln-Asp-Val-His-Asn-Phe`.
- **One-letter sequence:** `AVSEHQLMHNLGKHLNSMERVEWLRKKLQDVHNF`.
- **Molecular formula:** C174H300N56O49.
- **Molecular weight:** approximately 3,960 Da.
- **Chemical nature:** synthetic peptide analogue with multiple residue substitutions relative to human PTHrP(1–34).

Abaloparatide is not identical to teriparatide. It is derived from PTHrP sequence architecture and was designed to favour a receptor-conformation/signalling profile associated with anabolic bone effects. Verify exact formula and molecular mass against the current label and reference standard before final analytical database use.

## 3. Physiological background

PTHrP is expressed in many tissues and has local paracrine/autocrine roles. PTH and PTHrP share the type 1 PTH receptor (PTH1R), which is important in skeletal development and adult calcium/bone homeostasis. Abaloparatide is a pharmacological PTH1R agonist engineered for intermittent exposure.

## 4. Mechanism of action

**PTH1R agonism with ligand- and exposure-dependent signalling:**

1. Abaloparatide binds PTH1R on osteoblast-lineage cells and other receptor-expressing tissues.
2. PTH1R activates G-protein signalling, including Gs/adenylyl cyclase/cAMP/PKA and Gq/11-related pathways.
3. Intermittent once-daily exposure promotes osteoblast activity, differentiation and survival and increases bone formation.
4. PTH1R ligand conformation and receptor trafficking/signalling kinetics influence the balance of downstream responses. Abaloparatide has been characterized as favouring a transient signalling profile relative to some PTH ligands in experimental systems; this mechanistic distinction should not be simplified into a claim of absolute osteoblast selectivity.
5. Bone remodelling remains coupled: osteoblast-lineage signalling can influence osteoclastogenesis through mediators such as RANKL and OPG.

The clinically relevant effect is an increase in bone formation and bone mineral density with reduction in fracture risk in studied high-risk populations. Abaloparatide does not directly inhibit osteoclasts.

## 5. Approved clinical use

In the United States, TYMLOS is indicated for:
- treatment of postmenopausal women with osteoporosis at high risk for fracture; and
- increasing bone density in men with osteoporosis at high risk for fracture.

The current label defines high risk using factors such as prior osteoporotic fracture, multiple risk factors for fracture, or failure/intolerance of other available osteoporosis therapy. Consult current label wording for exact population and limitation-of-use statements.

Some jurisdictions may have narrower indications or differing duration recommendations. Do not assume the US indication applies globally.

## 6. Administration and dosing

- **US labelled dose:** 80 micrograms once daily.
- **Route:** subcutaneous injection into the periumbilical abdominal region.
- **Presentation:** prefilled single-patient-use pen containing multiple doses (verify current pack/device presentation).
- **Duration:** current US label advises use for more than 2 years during a patient's lifetime only if the patient remains or has returned to being at high risk for fracture. Follow current label and specialist guidance.
- **Calcium:** supplemental calcium and vitamin D may be recommended when dietary intake is inadequate, based on individual assessment.

This is a summary of prescription labelling, not self-injection training. Follow the current product instructions for storage, priming, injection, needle disposal and pen handling.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics

- **Absorption:** rapidly absorbed after subcutaneous administration.
- **Time to peak concentration:** approximately 0.5 hour in label-described studies.
- **Half-life:** approximately 1 hour after subcutaneous dosing.
- **Metabolism:** peptide is degraded by proteolytic pathways.
- **Elimination:** renal elimination of metabolites is expected; assess current label for renal impairment guidance.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** transient post-dose increases in serum calcium may occur; bone-formation markers and BMD increase with continued intermittent therapy.

Exact PK values and population-specific effects should be transcribed from the current label before clinical decision-support use.

## 8. Clinical evidence

The pivotal ACTIVE study compared abaloparatide with placebo and open-label teriparatide in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis at high fracture risk. The trial evaluated new vertebral fractures, nonvertebral fractures, clinical fractures, BMD and safety over 18 months.

The trial demonstrated reduced new vertebral fracture incidence versus placebo and increases in BMD at lumbar spine, total hip and femoral neck. Some nonvertebral fracture comparisons were statistically significant versus placebo. Avoid unqualified head-to-head superiority claims over teriparatide; the trial was not designed to establish superiority for every fracture endpoint.

The ATOM study evaluated abaloparatide in men with osteoporosis, primarily using BMD endpoints. Fracture-outcome evidence in men is less extensive than the postmenopausal women dataset.

For numerical efficacy, extract endpoint counts, denominators, risk ratios, confidence intervals and multiplicity handling from the primary publications and FDA review.

## 9. Safety and precautions

Important labelled risks and precautions include:
- hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria;
- orthostatic hypotension, especially after initial doses;
- dizziness, nausea, headache, palpitations and injection-site reactions;
- caution in patients with active or recent urolithiasis;
- potential for transient increases in serum calcium;
- avoid use in patients with increased baseline risk of osteosarcoma, as specified in the current label, including relevant skeletal malignancy/radiation and hereditary risk contexts.

The FDA label includes a warning regarding potential risk of osteosarcoma based on findings in rats. The clinical significance in humans is uncertain; use should follow the current label's risk-factor precautions. Do not conflate this warning with teriparatide's historical boxed warning, which FDA removed in 2020.

Review current product information for contraindications, drug interactions, pregnancy/lactation and full adverse-event data.
