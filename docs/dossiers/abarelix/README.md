## 4. Mechanism of action

Abarelix is a **competitive GnRH receptor antagonist**.

1. It binds pituitary GnRH receptors and blocks endogenous GnRH signalling.
2. This suppresses pituitary LH and FSH secretion.
3. Lower LH reduces testicular testosterone synthesis; reduced FSH suppresses gonadal signalling.
4. Testosterone falls to castrate levels without the initial agonist-driven testosterone surge associated with GnRH agonists.

This distinguishes abarelix from leuprolide, goserelin, triptorelin, histrelin, and nafarelin. Abarelix is an antagonist and does not rely on a flare-then-downregulation mechanism.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- Rapid suppression of LH, FSH, and testosterone occurs after administration.
- The clinical pharmacodynamic objective is maintenance of serum testosterone in the castrate range in men with advanced prostate cancer.
- Because it blocks GnRH receptors directly, an initial testosterone surge is not expected as with GnRH agonists.
- Testosterone suppression may reverse after treatment cessation; recovery depends on treatment exposure and patient factors.

The product label and clinical programme include testosterone and PSA measurements. PSA response is a disease-monitoring marker, not a direct measure of receptor occupancy.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

Abarelix is administered as an intramuscular depot suspension. Its observed exposure profile is influenced by dissolution and release from the injection site.

The historical label describes:
- Slow absorption from the depot following intramuscular administration.
- Extensive peptide metabolism to smaller fragments.
- Predominantly peptide catabolism rather than CYP450-dependent oxidative metabolism.
- No validated CYP-mediated interaction profile comparable to small-molecule oncology drugs.

Exact numeric PK values are not reproduced here because they require reconciliation with the archived FDA label tables and assay conditions. Do not transfer values from abarelix acetate, free peptide, or other GnRH antagonists.

## 7. Clinical evidence and historical labelled indication

**Historical US indication:** palliative treatment of men with advanced symptomatic prostate cancer, in whom GnRH agonist therapy was not appropriate and who refused orchiectomy.

The indication was deliberately restricted because of serious hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis risk and the requirement for administration in a setting capable of managing acute reactions.

Clinical studies compared abarelix with leuprolide-based therapy and evaluated testosterone suppression, PSA, and clinical disease-related outcomes. These studies should be reported in their historical context and not treated as evidence that abarelix is a currently preferred standard of care.

## 8. Administration and formulation

- Route: intramuscular injection only.
- Product: PLENAXIS lyophilised powder for injectable suspension, reconstituted with supplied diluent.
- Historical labelled regimen: 100 mg intramuscularly on days 1, 15, and 29, then every 28 days.
- Administration was restricted to healthcare settings with appropriate monitoring and emergency treatment capability because of acute systemic hypersensitivity risk.
- Do not substitute the historical depot schedule for current prostate-cancer treatment guidance.

This dossier is not a prescribing guide. Use current local regulatory information and specialist clinical judgement.

## 9. Safety and warnings

The major safety issue in the PLENAXIS label is **acute systemic hypersensitivity**, including anaphylaxis.

Other important risks and precautions include:
- Severe allergic reactions, including reactions after prior uneventful injections.
- Hypotension, syncope, urticaria, bronchospasm, and other acute hypersensitivity manifestations.
- Injection-site reactions.
- Hot flashes and other androgen-deprivation effects.
- Potential QT/QTc effects and cardiovascular considerations associated with profound androgen suppression.
- Effects on bone mineral density, sexual function, metabolic health, and fertility associated with androgen deprivation.
- Monitoring and immediate access to emergency treatment were required under the historical restricted-use programme.

Do not imply that the product's safety profile is interchangeable with newer GnRH antagonists such as degarelix or relugolix.

## 10. Drug interactions

Formal interaction data are limited. Abarelix is a peptide that undergoes proteolytic metabolism; classic CYP450 interaction assumptions are not directly applicable. Consider additive effects from androgen deprivation and medicines affecting cardiac repolarisation where clinically relevant. The historical label's hypersensitivity precautions are central to its safe-use profile.

## 13. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- Primary target: **GNRHR**, pituitary GnRH receptor.
- Endocrine axis: hypothalamic GnRH → pituitary LH/FSH → testicular steroidogenesis.
- Pharmacological action: direct competitive receptor antagonism.
- Downstream effect: reduced testosterone and androgen-receptor signalling in prostate tissue.
- Relevant disease context: androgen-dependent prostate cancer.
- No validated abarelix-specific pharmacogenomic biomarker is established in the historical label.
