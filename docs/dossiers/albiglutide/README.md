# Albiglutide (Tanzeum / Eperzan) — dossier 014

**Curation status:** `in_progress`

**Pharmacology:** long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist.

## 2. Molecular architecture and sequence

Albiglutide is a recombinant fusion protein comprising:
1. two tandem copies of modified human GLP-1 fragment residues 7–36; and
2. a human serum albumin (HSA) moiety comprising 585 amino-acid residues.

The GLP-1 fragment is modified by substitution of glycine for the naturally occurring alanine at position 8 of the GLP-1 sequence. This substitution confers resistance to dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4)-mediated proteolysis.

The two GLP-1-derived fragments are genetically fused in tandem to the N-terminal end of human albumin. The albumin domain extends systemic exposure and supports once-weekly administration.

**Sequence policy:** do not treat albiglutide as a 30- or 36-residue peptide. The active molecule is the complete recombinant fusion protein, approximately 73 kDa. The full expressed amino-acid sequence, signal peptide processing, mature chain boundaries, linker/junction residues and post-translational modifications must be reconstructed from the authoritative product sequence/CMC record before canonical sequence import. The FDA label's structural description is authoritative for domain architecture but is not a substitute for a validated full-length sequence file.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Albiglutide is an agonist of GLP-1R. It augments glucose-dependent insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells and slows gastric emptying.

The GLP-1 receptor is a class B G-protein-coupled receptor. Receptor activation stimulates intracellular cAMP signalling and enhances insulin secretion when glucose concentrations are elevated. Albiglutide also reduces fasting glucose and postprandial glucose excursions in people with type 2 diabetes.

The insulinotropic response is glucose-dependent; albiglutide did not impair the glucagon response to low glucose concentrations in a single-dose study described in the US label. Hypoglycaemia can nevertheless occur, particularly with concomitant insulin or sulfonylureas.

## 5. Molecular basis of prolonged action

Albiglutide's long duration of action is achieved through two principal design features:
- DPP-4 resistance from the Ala8-to-Gly substitution in each GLP-1-derived fragment; and
- fusion of the two GLP-1 fragments to human albumin, increasing molecular size and prolonging systemic exposure.

The albumin fusion is part of the covalent active-substance architecture. It is not a co-formulated excipient and should not be described as non-covalent albumin binding of the type used by lipidated GLP-1 analogues.

## 6. Pharmacodynamics

FDA label information includes:
- increased glucose-dependent insulin secretion;
- reduction in fasting plasma glucose;
- reduction in postprandial glucose excursions;
- slowing of gastric emptying;
- preservation of the glucagon response to low glucose in the cited study.

In a Phase II study in Japanese participants with type 2 diabetes, albiglutide 30 mg reduced postprandial glucose AUC over 0–3 hours by approximately 22% at week 16 versus placebo after a mixed meal. A single 50 mg subcutaneous dose did not impair glucagon response to low glucose concentrations in the label-described study. A single 100 mg dose (twice the maximum approved dose) slowed gastric emptying for solids and liquids in healthy participants. These study-specific results must retain their dose and population context.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics

The FDA label describes a prolonged terminal half-life supporting once-weekly dosing. The archived US prescribing information reports:
- median time to maximum concentration: approximately 3–5 days;
- mean terminal half-life: approximately 5 days;
- accumulation with repeated once-weekly administration;
- slow absorption and prolonged systemic exposure consistent with the albumin-fusion architecture.

## 9. Formulation and presentations

### Tanzeum (United States)
The archived FDA label describes a sterile lyophilised powder for subcutaneous injection supplied in prefilled pens:
- 30 mg dose pen;
- 50 mg dose pen.

The 30 mg pen contains 40.3 mg lyophilised albiglutide and 0.65 mL diluent, designed to deliver 30 mg in 0.5 mL after reconstitution. The 50 mg pen contains 67 mg lyophilised albiglutide and 0.65 mL diluent, designed to deliver 50 mg in 0.5 mL after reconstitution.

Inactive ingredients include mannitol, polysorbate 80, sodium phosphate and trehalose dihydrate. The product does not contain a preservative. Reconstituted solution is yellow. Product-specific instructions for reconstitution, storage and injection must be taken from the applicable archived/current label.

### Eperzan (European Union)
The EU product was supplied as 30 mg and 50 mg powder and solvent for solution for injection in pre-filled pens. The authorisation was withdrawn in 2018 for commercial reasons.

## 10. Regulatory history and market status

### United States
- FDA BLA 125431.
- Original approval date: 15 April 2014.
- Tanzeum was indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- FDA Purple Book currently lists the 30 mg and 50 mg presentations as discontinued.
- The US product was discontinued commercially. Discontinued marketing status is not equivalent to an FDA safety withdrawal; do not infer a safety-based withdrawal without a specific FDA action.

- EMA product information and assessment reports remain useful for historical scientific/regulatory research, but the authorisation is no longer valid.

## 11. Administration and handling

Tanzeum was administered subcutaneously once weekly. The archived US label specified 30 mg once weekly, with increase to 50 mg once weekly if additional glycaemic control was needed and the lower dose was tolerated. Product-specific injection sites, reconstitution steps, missed-dose instructions and storage conditions must be taken from the exact label revision.

This is historical product information, not a current treatment recommendation. Do not use discontinued commercial presentation instructions as advice for obtaining or administering the medicine.

## 12. Safety profile

Archived US prescribing information includes warnings and precautions for:
- pancreatitis;
- hypoglycaemia, particularly with concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea;
- hypersensitivity reactions;
- immunogenicity and anti-drug antibodies;
- renal impairment and volume depletion in the setting of gastrointestinal adverse reactions;
- gastrointestinal adverse reactions;
- heart-rate increase;
- pulmonary aspiration considerations around general anaesthesia/deep sedation in updated label language, where applicable.

Common adverse reactions reported in clinical trials included diarrhoea, nausea and injection-site reactions. Exact incidence must be extracted from the archived label with dose, comparator, population and study duration. ## 13. Drug interactions and precautions

- Delayed gastric emptying may affect absorption of concomitant oral medicines.
- Concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea can increase hypoglycaemia risk.
- Renal function and hydration may be relevant in people experiencing severe gastrointestinal adverse reactions.

## 14. Clinical evidence programme

Key albiglutide clinical evidence includes:
- **HARMONY programme:** glycaemic control across monotherapy and add-on settings in type 2 diabetes.
- **HARMONY Outcomes:** cardiovascular outcomes trial in people with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease; demonstrated superiority for the primary major adverse cardiovascular event composite in the published trial.
- **HARMONY 3:** comparison with standard glucose-lowering therapies in type 2 diabetes.
- **HARMONY 6:** comparison with prandial insulin lispro in people using basal insulin.
