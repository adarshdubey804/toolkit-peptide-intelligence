## 1. Executive overview

Angiotensin II is an endogenous octapeptide hormone in the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS). It is a major regulator of vascular tone, renal sodium handling, aldosterone release, and blood pressure.

Giapreza is the FDA-approved intravenous angiotensin II product indicated to increase blood pressure in adults with septic or other distributive shock. It is used in a critical-care context when additional blood-pressure support is required. The ATHOS-3 trial demonstrated a higher proportion of patients achieving the prespecified mean arterial pressure response at three hours compared with placebo, but this short-term haemodynamic endpoint should not be confused with proof of improved survival.

Angiotensin II is a peptide hormone but has broad systemic effects. AT1-receptor activation causes vasoconstriction and can promote aldosterone release, sodium retention, sympathetic activity, and other downstream effects. Important safety concerns include thromboembolic events, ischaemia, and excessive hypertension.

Angiotensin II is a linear peptide and does not contain the intramolecular disulfide ring found in vasopressin. It is generated physiologically from angiotensin I by angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), although other enzymatic pathways can also contribute to angiotensin peptide metabolism.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

Angiotensin II exerts its major pressor effect through AT1 receptors (AGTR1), which are G-protein-coupled receptors expressed in vascular smooth muscle, adrenal tissue, kidney, heart, and other organs.

### AT1 receptor signalling
1. Angiotensin II binds AT1 receptors.
2. AT1 activates Gq/11-linked signalling.
3. Gq stimulates phospholipase C-β (PLCβ).
4. PLCβ generates IP₃ and DAG from PIP₂.
5. IP₃ increases intracellular calcium; calcium–calmodulin signalling promotes vascular smooth-muscle contraction.
6. Systemic vascular resistance and blood pressure rise.

AT1 signalling also contributes to aldosterone release from the adrenal zona glomerulosa, renal sodium retention, sympathetic facilitation, and other cardiovascular and renal effects.

### AT2 receptor signalling
Angiotensin II can also bind AT2 receptors (AGTR2). AT2 signalling is often associated with counter-regulatory effects such as vasodilation, natriuresis, and tissue-growth modulation, depending on tissue and physiological context. The clinical pressor action of infused angiotensin II is principally associated with AT1 activation.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- **Vasoconstriction:** AT1 activation increases vascular smooth-muscle tone.
- **Blood pressure:** Increased systemic vascular resistance raises arterial pressure.
- **Aldosterone:** Adrenal AT1 signalling stimulates aldosterone secretion, influencing sodium and potassium handling.
- **Renal effects:** Angiotensin II influences renal vascular tone and tubular sodium reabsorption.
- **Sympathetic effects:** Angiotensin II can facilitate sympathetic activity and neurotransmission.
- **Endocrine and tissue effects:** Chronic RAAS activation has broader cardiovascular and renal consequences; acute ICU infusion should be interpreted in its specific clinical context.

The haemodynamic response to infused angiotensin II depends on the underlying shock phenotype, volume status, concomitant vasopressors, receptor responsiveness, and organ perfusion.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

Angiotensin II is rapidly metabolized by peptidases in plasma and tissues. The current Giapreza prescribing information should be used for exact product-specific PK parameters and administration details.

Important considerations:
- Short circulating persistence relative to many conventional small-molecule vasopressors.
- Rapid metabolism to shorter angiotensin peptides and amino-acid products.
- Exposure is controlled by continuous IV administration in the labelled clinical setting.
- Clearance and haemodynamic response may be influenced by critical illness and organ perfusion.

## 7. Clinical evidence

### ATHOS-3

The pivotal ATHOS-3 study evaluated intravenous angiotensin II in adults with vasodilatory shock who remained hypotensive despite conventional vasopressor therapy.

- Study design: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial.
- Population: adults with vasodilatory shock receiving background vasopressor support.
- Primary endpoint: proportion of patients achieving the prespecified mean arterial pressure response at three hours.
- Main result: 69.9% of patients receiving angiotensin II met the primary endpoint versus 23.4% receiving placebo.
- Interpretation: the trial demonstrated a short-term haemodynamic response. The primary endpoint was not a mortality endpoint and should not be presented as proof of survival benefit.

The FDA indication is to increase blood pressure in adults with septic or other distributive shock. Clinical use requires individualized critical-care judgement and concurrent management of the underlying cause of shock.

### Evidence limitations
- Haemodynamic response is not equivalent to improved survival or organ recovery.
- The trial population and background vasopressor context matter.
- Subgroup findings should not be generalized without appropriate statistical and clinical qualification.
- Long-term outcomes and comparative effectiveness depend on the broader critical-care strategy.

## 8. Safety and warnings

Giapreza carries important warnings and precautions. Always use the current FDA prescribing information for the full label.

Key safety issues include:
- **Thromboembolic events:** angiotensin II treatment has been associated with thromboembolic events; the label includes a warning and recommends appropriate thromboprophylaxis unless contraindicated.
- **Ischaemia:** excessive vasoconstriction can compromise tissue perfusion.
- **Peripheral, mesenteric, and cardiac ischaemia:** monitor clinical perfusion and organ function.
- **Hypertension:** excessive pressor response can cause harm.
- **Cardiovascular stress:** afterload and vascular resistance changes may affect cardiac function.
- **Electrolyte and renal effects:** RAAS-mediated effects can influence potassium, sodium, and renal haemodynamics.

Adverse-event rates must be tied to the specific trial or label population; do not present one study's event frequencies as universal.

## 9. Drug interactions and clinical context

Angiotensin II is administered in critically ill patients, often alongside other vasopressors and supportive therapies. Relevant considerations include:
- Concomitant vasopressors and inotropes may produce additive haemodynamic effects.
- RAAS-modifying medicines can affect endogenous pathway activity and clinical response.
- Drugs affecting potassium balance may complicate electrolyte management.
- Anticoagulant and thromboprophylaxis decisions must account for bleeding risk and the product warning on thromboembolic events.
- Fluid status and cardiac function materially influence haemodynamic response.

Consult the current product label and critical-care pharmacy guidance for a complete interaction assessment.
