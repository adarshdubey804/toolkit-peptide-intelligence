# Dossier 094 — Anidulafungin (Eraxis)

**Workbook entry:** 094 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **First US approval:** 2006 | **Status:** FDA-approved  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and chemistry
Anidulafungin is a semisynthetic echinocandin antifungal derived from a fermentation product of *Aspergillus nidulans* var. *echinulatus*. It is a cyclic hexapeptide-like lipopeptide with a lipophilic side chain and non-proteinogenic residues. The marketed drug is anidulafungin; it should be represented as a complex stereochemically defined macrocycle, not as a linear peptide.

## Mechanism
Anidulafungin noncompetitively inhibits β-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase, disrupting fungal cell-wall synthesis. It is fungicidal against many *Candida* species and fungistatic against *Aspergillus* hyphae. FKS mutations are a major mechanism of acquired echinocandin resistance. Activity is limited against organisms lacking the target glucan architecture, including many non-*Candida* yeasts and moulds.

## Pharmacology and PK
Anidulafungin is administered intravenously. It undergoes slow chemical degradation at physiological temperature and pH rather than extensive hepatic metabolism; metabolites are eliminated through biliary/fecal routes. It is highly protein bound and has a terminal half-life around 40–50 hours. Renal impairment generally does not require dose adjustment. It has minimal CYP-mediated interaction potential, but the current label should be consulted for exact interaction and special-population information.

## Clinical evidence and indications
Eraxis is indicated for adults with esophageal candidiasis, candidemia and other forms of Candida infection including intra-abdominal abscess and peritonitis. It is also used for selected invasive candidiasis in paediatric patients under label-specific conditions. Randomized trials demonstrated noninferiority to fluconazole for invasive candidiasis in selected populations. Do not extrapolate to cryptococcosis or mould infections as first-line therapy.

## Safety
Common adverse events include diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, fever, hypokalaemia and infusion-related reactions. Histamine-mediated reactions, including rash, urticaria, flushing, dyspnea and hypotension, can occur, particularly with rapid infusion. Hepatic laboratory abnormalities and anaphylaxis are important safety considerations.

## Formulation and quality
Eraxis is supplied as lyophilized powder for IV infusion after reconstitution and dilution. Critical quality attributes include macrocycle identity, stereochemistry, potency, related substances, degradation products, residual solvents, water content, reconstitution, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter and container closure.

## Regulatory status
FDA first approved in 2006. The label's adult and paediatric indications and age restrictions should be checked against the current product information.
