# Dossier 106 — Aviptadil (Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide; VIP)

**Workbook entry:** 106 | **Category:** Immunology, inflammation & transplant | **FDA approval:** No | **Status:** Investigational in the US; not FDA-approved  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and sequence
Aviptadil is synthetic human vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP), a 28-amino-acid neuropeptide. Sequence:

`HSDAVFTDNYTRLRKQMAVKKYLNSILN-NH2`

VIP is amidated at the C-terminus. It is a member of the secretin/glucagon peptide superfamily and is widely distributed in the nervous, gastrointestinal, respiratory and immune systems. The active drug should be distinguished from combination products and regional formulations containing VIP or related analogues.

## Mechanism
VIP activates VPAC1 and VPAC2 G-protein-coupled receptors, increasing intracellular cAMP and influencing smooth-muscle relaxation, glandular secretion, vascular tone and immune signalling. In pulmonary models, proposed effects include bronchodilation, epithelial protection and modulation of inflammatory cytokines. These mechanisms are biologically plausible but do not prove clinical benefit in ARDS or COVID-19. VIP also has vasoactive effects that may influence blood pressure.

## Pharmacology and PK
VIP is rapidly degraded by peptidases and has a short circulating half-life. Aviptadil has been investigated by intravenous and inhaled routes; route-specific exposure and tolerability differ substantially. Do not transfer PK values between inhaled and intravenous preparations or from endogenous VIP physiology without source qualification.

## Clinical evidence
Aviptadil has been investigated for acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), including COVID-19-associated respiratory failure, and in other indications. Clinical trial results have been mixed and do not establish an FDA-approved ARDS treatment. The workbook also notes erectile dysfunction combination products outside the US; any such product must be assessed by its own formulation, active ingredients and national authorization. Do not describe aviptadil alone as an FDA-approved erectile dysfunction treatment.

## Safety
Potential risks include hypotension, flushing, tachycardia, gastrointestinal effects and other consequences of VIP-mediated vasodilation and secretion. Safety depends on route and clinical context. Investigational use should be limited to authorized trials or applicable regulatory pathways; no approved US prescribing information establishes a routine dose or safety profile.

## Formulation and quality
Aviptadil is a synthetic amidated peptide. Critical quality attributes include sequence identity, C-terminal amidation, purity, related substances, oxidation/degradation, aggregation, peptide content, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter and route-specific aerosol or infusion performance. Inhaled and parenteral formulations require distinct quality and delivery specifications.

## Regulatory status
Not FDA-approved as a standalone medicinal product. Investigational status should be checked in ClinicalTrials.gov and FDA records; trial registration or emergency-use requests do not equal approval. Regional combination products require separate product-level verification.
