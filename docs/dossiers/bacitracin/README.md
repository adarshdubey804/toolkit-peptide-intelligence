# Dossier 091 — Bacitracin

**Workbook entry:** 091 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **US approval:** historical topical products; injectable approval withdrawn in 2020 | **Status:** FDA-approved topical products remain; injectable product withdrawn  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and chemistry
Bacitracin is a mixture of closely related cyclic polypeptide antibiotics produced by *Bacillus* species, with bacitracin A as a major active component. It contains unusual amino acids and a thiazoline-containing ring system; it is not a simple linear peptide. Commercial bacitracin zinc topical products and historical injectable bacitracin are distinct dosage forms and regulatory records. Do not conflate topical availability with injectable product status.

## Mechanism
Bacitracin binds the C55-isoprenyl pyrophosphate (undecaprenyl pyrophosphate) carrier involved in transporting peptidoglycan precursors across the bacterial membrane. By preventing dephosphorylation and recycling of the lipid carrier, it blocks cell-wall synthesis. Activity is mainly against Gram-positive organisms; the outer membrane limits access to many Gram-negative bacteria.

## Pharmacology and clinical use
Topical bacitracin products are used for prevention/treatment of minor superficial skin infection in labelled contexts and are often included in combination topical preparations. Systemic exposure from limited topical application is generally low, but damaged skin or extensive application can alter absorption. Injectable bacitracin historically had serious toxicity concerns and is no longer an available FDA-approved injection product. Do not describe the topical product as an approved systemic treatment.

## Safety
Topical bacitracin can cause allergic contact dermatitis and, rarely, severe hypersensitivity. Avoid prolonged or extensive unsupervised application. Systemic bacitracin exposure is associated with nephrotoxicity; this is an important historical reason to avoid treating the old injectable formulation as a routine contemporary therapy. Product-specific warnings must be checked.

## Formulation and quality
Topical products include bacitracin zinc ointments and combination formulations. Critical quality attributes include component profile, identity, potency, zinc content, related substances, microbial quality, preservative/excipient compatibility and stability. For any historical injectable reference, keep its sterile-product quality controls separate from topical ointment specifications.

## Regulatory status
The workbook's status field should be interpreted at dosage-form level. Topical bacitracin products remain marketed/approved in relevant product categories. FDA requested withdrawal of the approved bacitracin for injection application; the injectable product is not a currently marketed approved systemic therapy. Do not report a single approval date for all bacitracin products.
