## 1. Executive overview

Bivalirudin is a synthetic 20-residue hirudin-derived peptide anticoagulant. It is a direct thrombin inhibitor that binds both the catalytic active site and anion-binding exosite I of thrombin. By inhibiting circulating and clot-associated thrombin, it suppresses fibrin formation and thrombin-mediated activation of coagulation factors and platelets.

Angiomax was first approved in the United States in 2000. The product has been used as an anticoagulant in percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), including selected patients with or at risk of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT/HIT with thrombosis syndrome, HITTS). Exact indications and use restrictions vary by label revision and product; the current label must be checked before describing present authorization.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

Bivalirudin is a bivalent, reversible direct thrombin inhibitor.

1. The N-terminal portion interacts with thrombin's catalytic active site.
2. The C-terminal region engages thrombin's anion-binding exosite I.
3. This interaction inhibits thrombin-mediated cleavage of fibrinogen to fibrin.
4. It also inhibits thrombin-mediated activation of factor XIII, factors V and VIII, and platelets.
5. Inhibition affects both soluble thrombin and thrombin associated with a developing clot.
6. Thrombin slowly cleaves the Arg-Pro bond within the inhibitor, contributing to recovery of enzyme activity and the drug's relatively short duration of action.

**Key distinction:** Unlike unfractionated heparin, bivalirudin does not depend on antithrombin to inhibit thrombin. It also does not bind platelet factor 4 in the way that creates the classic heparin–PF4 antigenic complex, which is relevant to its use in patients with HIT history or risk.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- Direct inhibition of thrombin prolongs activated clotting time (ACT), activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT), thrombin time (TT), and prothrombin time (PT) in a concentration-dependent manner.
- It reduces fibrin generation and thrombin-driven platelet activation.
- Anticoagulant effect begins rapidly after IV administration.
- The effect declines relatively quickly after discontinuation because of proteolytic cleavage and renal elimination.
- ACT is used in procedural settings according to the applicable product label and institutional protocol.

Laboratory anticoagulant measures are not interchangeable; assay response depends on reagent, baseline coagulation state, and clinical context.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

Bivalirudin exhibits approximately linear pharmacokinetics after IV administration in PCI patients.

| Parameter | Label/review value |
|---|---|
| Plasma protein binding | No meaningful binding to plasma proteins other than thrombin; does not bind red blood cells |
| Elimination half-life, normal renal function | Approximately 25 minutes |
| Clearance | Renal mechanisms plus proteolytic cleavage |
| Renal impairment | Clearance falls and half-life increases as renal function worsens |
| Dialysis | Hemodialyzable; older FDA label reported approximately 25% removal during hemodialysis |

The archived FDA label reports half-life values of approximately 22 minutes in mild renal impairment, 34 minutes in moderate impairment, 57 minutes in severe impairment, and 3.5 hours in dialysis-dependent patients (off dialysis). These historical label estimates should not replace current product-specific information.

Bivalirudin undergoes proteolytic cleavage, including cleavage of the thrombin-recognition bond, and a portion is eliminated renally. Renal function is therefore a major determinant of exposure and anticoagulant duration.

## 7. Clinical evidence

### REPLACE-2

REPLACE-2 compared bivalirudin with provisional glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor use versus heparin plus planned glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibition in patients undergoing PCI. The 30-day composite endpoint (death, myocardial infarction, urgent revascularization, or in-hospital major bleeding) was 9.2% versus 10.0%, meeting the study's noninferiority criterion. Major bleeding was lower with bivalirudin. The comparison reflects the antithrombotic practice of that era; contemporary radial access and potent P2Y12 inhibitor use affect applicability.

### ACUITY

ACUITY evaluated anticoagulant strategies in moderate- to high-risk acute coronary syndrome patients undergoing an invasive strategy. Bivalirudin monotherapy reduced major bleeding compared with heparin/enoxaparin plus glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor strategy, while ischemic outcomes and the net clinical endpoint depended on the comparator and treatment context. Avoid describing one arm's result as universal superiority.

### HORIZONS-AMI

HORIZONS-AMI studied patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction undergoing primary PCI. Compared with heparin plus glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor strategy, bivalirudin was associated with less major bleeding and lower rates of some clinical endpoints in the trial's setting, but early acute stent thrombosis was an important safety concern. Results should be interpreted in light of the trial's comparator regimen and procedural era.

### Evidence synthesis caution
Bivalirudin's comparative performance depends on access route, procedural setting, concomitant antiplatelet treatment, GPI use, and the specific comparator. Do not extrapolate historic trial results into a universal claim that bivalirudin is always safer or more effective than heparin.

## 8. Safety and warnings

The principal hazard is bleeding, which may be serious or fatal. Other clinically relevant issues include:
- Excess anticoagulation and haemorrhage, including access-site and gastrointestinal bleeding.
- Accumulation/prolonged effect in significant renal impairment.
- Acute stent thrombosis in particular PCI settings and regimens.
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including rare serious reactions.
- Medication errors related to reconstitution, concentration, infusion, or switching between anticoagulants.

Monitor coagulation according to the current label and clinical setting. The complete contraindications, warnings, and adverse-event frequencies must be taken from the current product label rather than inferred from the mechanism.

## 9. HIT and immunologic distinction

HIT is an immune-mediated prothrombotic reaction commonly associated with antibodies to PF4–heparin complexes. Bivalirudin is not heparin and does not form the same PF4–heparin antigenic complex. It has been used as an alternative anticoagulant in patients with or at risk of HIT undergoing PCI. The precise authorized indication and evidence base should be checked against the current jurisdiction-specific label and guidelines. It is not a treatment for the underlying immune process itself; it provides anticoagulation.

## 10. Interactions and special populations

- **Renal impairment:** clinically important because clearance decreases and half-life increases.
- **Other anticoagulants / antiplatelet agents:** additive haemorrhage risk is expected.
- **Thrombolytics:** combined haemostatic effects require specialist assessment.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** consult current product information; do not infer safety from peptide composition.
- **Hepatic impairment:** proteolytic cleavage and renal clearance contribute to disposition; product-specific evidence should be checked.
- **HIT:** bivalirudin's direct thrombin inhibition makes it a potential alternative procedural anticoagulant in defined settings.

## 11. Formulation, manufacturing, and quality

Angiomax is supplied as a sterile lyophilized powder for reconstitution for IV use. FDA product descriptions identify bivalirudin trifluoroacetate, mannitol, and sodium hydroxide for pH adjustment in the vial. Exact formulation and reconstitution instructions are product-specific and should be taken from the current label.

Critical quality attributes include:
- Correct 20-residue sequence and N-terminal D-phenylalanine stereochemistry.
- Peptide content, identity, purity, and potency.
- Related peptide impurities and degradation products.
- Control of oxidation and other chemical modifications.
- Residual solvents/reagents from peptide synthesis and purification.
- Counterion (trifluoroacetate) content and water content where applicable.
- Sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter, and container-closure integrity.

Bivalirudin is produced by synthetic peptide chemistry; do not describe it as a recombinant protein.

## 12. Bioinformatics and translational mapping

- Target: thrombin / coagulation factor IIa, encoded by **F2**.
- Pathways: coagulation cascade, fibrin formation, platelet activation, and thrombus stabilization.
- Compare direct thrombin inhibition with antithrombin-dependent heparin activity and factor Xa inhibition.
- Model renal function as a determinant of exposure and anticoagulant duration.
- Keep ACT/aPTT pharmacodynamic data separate from clinical endpoints such as death, MI, revascularization, and major bleeding.
- Capture trial era, comparator regimen, access route, and concomitant GPI/antiplatelet treatment as structured covariates.
