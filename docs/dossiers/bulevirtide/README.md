# Dossier 083 — Bulevirtide (Hepcludex; bulevirtide-gmod)

**Workbook entry:** 083 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **US approval:** 22 May 2026 | **Status:** FDA accelerated approval  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and molecular class
Bulevirtide is a synthetic lipopeptide derived from the preS1 domain of hepatitis B virus (HBV) large surface protein. It contains an N-terminal fatty-acyl modification (myristoylation) that supports interaction with the hepatocyte entry receptor NTCP. It is not a conventional endogenous human hormone or a generic antimicrobial peptide. Hepcludex is supplied as 8.5 mg lyophilized powder for subcutaneous injection; the US product is bulevirtide acetate/bulevirtide-gmod.

The exact marketed sequence, stereochemistry, terminal chemistry and molecular formula must be transcribed from the FDA chemistry/label record before publishing a definitive structure file. Do not substitute the entire HBV preS1 sequence for the drug substance.

## Mechanism
Bulevirtide binds sodium taurocholate cotransporting polypeptide (NTCP/SLC10A1) on hepatocytes and blocks HBV and hepatitis delta virus (HDV) entry. HDV uses HBV surface proteins to assemble infectious particles and therefore depends on HBV for its envelope. NTCP blockade inhibits new hepatocyte infection; it does not directly eradicate intracellular viral genomes or instantly eliminate established infected hepatocytes.

## Pharmacology
The approved US label describes bulevirtide as an NTCP-directed HDV attachment inhibitor. It is administered subcutaneously once daily. NTCP inhibition can alter bile-acid transport, so bile-acid elevations are a mechanism-related pharmacodynamic consideration. Record exposure and dose-response values from the US label and pivotal publications, not from European dosing assumptions. Pediatric efficacy and safety are not established in the US label.

## Clinical evidence
The FDA indication is treatment of chronic HDV infection in adults without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis. Accelerated approval on 22 May 2026 was based on reductions in HDV RNA and ALT normalization; improvement in disease-related clinical outcomes has not yet been established. The Phase 3 MYR301 programme provides the central clinical evidence, including virological/biochemical response and longer-term follow-up. Distinguish surrogate endpoints from clinical outcomes such as decompensation, transplantation, hepatocellular carcinoma and mortality.

## Safety
The US label has a boxed warning for severe acute exacerbation of hepatitis D and B after treatment discontinuation. Patients stopping therapy require close clinical and laboratory monitoring for at least six months, including HBV DNA and HDV RNA. Hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis has been reported. Common adverse reactions include injection-site reactions, headache, abdominal pain, fatigue and pruritus. Bile-acid increases are expected from NTCP inhibition and should be described with label context.

## Formulation and quality
Hepcludex is an 8.5 mg single-dose lyophilized vial for subcutaneous use. Quality attributes include peptide sequence and acylation identity, peptide content, purity/related substances, oxidation and degradation, reconstitution properties, residual solvents, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter, and container closure integrity. The N-terminal lipid is a critical identity and potency attribute.

## Regulatory status
FDA BLA 761468; original US approval 22 May 2026, accelerated approval. The US indication is adults with chronic HDV without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis. The approval is conditional on confirmatory verification/description of clinical benefit. Do not conflate this US status with earlier European authorization or its different product name/dosing.
