# 048 — Calcitonin (salmon)

**Curation status:** in_progress  
**Workbook sequence:** 048  
**Entity class:** synthetic 32-amino-acid peptide hormone; calcitonin receptor agonist  
**Evidence cut-off:** 4 November 2026

> **Scope.** Salmon calcitonin is a synthetic analogue of the 32-residue hormone originally isolated from salmon ultimobranchial tissue. It is a potent inhibitor of osteoclast-mediated bone resorption and has been used in selected metabolic bone conditions and for analgesia in acute vertebral fracture. Its contemporary clinical role is limited by availability of more effective alternatives and safety concerns, including a malignancy signal in long-term clinical-trial data.

Salmon calcitonin differs in sequence from human calcitonin and has greater potency and longer biological activity in several experimental and clinical settings. It must not be confused with human calcitonin or with the 84-residue parathyroid hormone.

## 3. Physiological background

Endogenous calcitonin is produced by parafollicular C cells of the thyroid gland. Its secretion increases in response to elevated serum calcium. Calcitonin acts principally on osteoclasts to inhibit bone resorption and can influence renal handling of calcium and phosphate. In humans, calcitonin is not the dominant long-term regulator of calcium homeostasis; PTH and vitamin D have larger roles.

## 4. Mechanism of action

**Calcitonin receptor agonism and osteoclast inhibition:**

1. Salmon calcitonin binds the calcitonin receptor (CTR/CALCR), a class B GPCR expressed on osteoclasts and other tissues.
2. Receptor activation can couple to Gs/adenylyl cyclase/cAMP and, depending on cell context, additional G-protein pathways.
3. In osteoclasts, receptor signalling rapidly suppresses osteoclast motility, ruffled-border activity and resorptive function.
4. Reduced osteoclast-mediated degradation of bone matrix decreases release of calcium and phosphate from bone.
5. The net acute pharmacological effect is reduced bone resorption and a decrease in serum calcium in appropriate clinical settings.

Calcitonin is not a direct osteoblast anabolic agent. Its antiresorptive action can be accompanied by compensatory changes and tachyphylaxis with prolonged exposure.

## 5. Clinical uses and formulation distinctions

Historically and in selected jurisdictions, salmon calcitonin products have been used for:
- treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis in selected patients when alternatives are unsuitable;
- treatment of hypercalcemia, including malignancy-associated hypercalcemia, with product-specific labelling;
- Paget disease of bone;
- short-term analgesic use in acute vertebral compression fracture in some guidelines and jurisdictions.

These are not universal indications across all formulations or countries. Intranasal and injectable products have different labels. The US regulatory history of intranasal Miacalcin/Fortical must be distinguished from injectable calcitonin products and from non-US labels.

## 6. Administration and dosing

Product-specific doses differ by indication, formulation and jurisdiction. Historical US labelling has included:
- intranasal osteoporosis product: 200 international units once daily, alternating nostrils;
- injectable product for hypercalcemia or Paget disease: indication-specific subcutaneous or intramuscular regimens.

These are historical label examples, not a current prescribing recommendation. Verify the exact current product label, availability, dose, duration and storage before adding an executable regimen to the application. Intranasal administration is not interchangeable with injectable dosing.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics

- **Pharmacodynamics:** rapid inhibition of osteoclast-mediated bone resorption; can reduce serum calcium.
- **Absorption:** route-dependent; intranasal bioavailability is low compared with parenteral administration.
- **Half-life:** short plasma half-life after parenteral administration; exact value varies by source and assay and should be transcribed from the relevant product label.
- **Metabolism:** proteolytic degradation, with renal and other tissue contributions.
- **Tachyphylaxis:** diminished response can occur during prolonged treatment, in part through receptor regulation and cellular adaptation.

Do not merge PK values from nasal and injectable formulations.

## 8. Clinical evidence and limitations

Salmon calcitonin has demonstrated antiresorptive effects and modest increases or preservation of bone mineral density in some studies. Older trials reported vertebral fracture reduction in selected postmenopausal osteoporosis populations, but evidence for reduction in nonvertebral or hip fractures is limited.

A malignancy imbalance was observed in pooled long-term randomized controlled trials of calcitonin-salmon products. The FDA label and regulatory communications have consequently restricted or discouraged long-term use for osteoporosis, and the European Medicines Agency recommended withdrawal of intranasal calcitonin products for osteoporosis and restriction of injectable use to short-term indications. The exact risk estimate and current regulatory language must be quoted from the relevant agency document, with formulation and duration specified.

Do not describe salmon calcitonin as a first-line contemporary osteoporosis medicine.

## 9. Safety and precautions

Important safety concerns include:
- hypersensitivity, including serious allergic reactions; salmon-derived peptide may be immunogenic;
- nausea, flushing and injection-site reactions with injectable products;
- rhinitis, epistaxis and nasal irritation with intranasal formulations;
- hypocalcemia, particularly in patients with low vitamin D or inadequate calcium intake;
- antibody formation and reduced response in some patients;
- malignancy signal observed in long-term clinical-trial data.

For prolonged treatment, evaluate whether benefit outweighs risk and use the shortest duration consistent with the indication and current local label. Review formulation-specific contraindications, calcium/vitamin D status, renal considerations and full adverse-event tables.

## 11. Formulation and quality

Salmon calcitonin is a synthetic peptide with a disulfide bridge and C-terminal amide. Injectable products require control of sequence identity, disulfide connectivity, peptide-related impurities, potency, sterility, endotoxin and stability. Intranasal products add requirements for spray delivery, dose uniformity, preservative compatibility, microbial quality and in-use stability. The exact formulation and excipients are product-specific.
