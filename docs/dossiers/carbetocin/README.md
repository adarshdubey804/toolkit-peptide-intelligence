> Educational dossier only. Carbetocin is a prescription uterotonic used in obstetric care. This is not a postpartum haemorrhage prevention or treatment protocol.

## 1. Executive overview
Carbetocin is a long-acting synthetic oxytocin analogue used in several countries for prevention of uterine atony and postpartum haemorrhage after childbirth. It activates the oxytocin receptor (OXTR) on uterine myometrium, increasing intracellular calcium and promoting sustained uterine contraction. Compared with oxytocin, carbetocin has a longer duration of action and greater resistance to enzymatic degradation.

Carbetocin is included in WHO recommendations as an option for prevention of postpartum haemorrhage in settings where quality-assured product is available. It is not FDA-approved in the United States; this dossier must not imply US authorization from approvals in Canada, Europe, or other markets. Brand names include Duratocin and Pabal, depending on jurisdiction.

## 3. Chemical identity and structure
Carbetocin is described chemically as **1-deamino-1-monocarba-(2-O-methyltyrosine)-oxytocin**. It is a nonapeptide analogue with:
- Deamination/removal of the N-terminal amino group at position 1.
- Replacement of the sulfur-containing cysteine linkage with a carbon-containing (monocarba) bridge, producing a more metabolically stable cyclic framework.
- O-methylation of the tyrosine phenolic hydroxyl group at position 2.
- A C-terminal glycinamide, as in oxytocin.

These substitutions make carbetocin more resistant to degradation than native oxytocin and prolong its uterotonic action. Do not represent it as having the native oxytocin Cys1–Cys6 disulfide bond: the bridge chemistry is deliberately altered.

## 4. Mechanism of action
Carbetocin is an oxytocin receptor agonist:
1. It binds OXTR on uterine smooth muscle.
2. OXTR couples predominantly to Gq/11.
3. PLCβ activation generates IP3 and DAG.
4. IP3-mediated calcium release and calcium influx increase intracellular Ca2+.
5. Calcium-calmodulin activates myosin light-chain kinase and uterine contraction.
6. Sustained uterine tone compresses uterine blood vessels at the placental bed, reducing bleeding due to uterine atony.

Carbetocin is a uterotonic, not a coagulation factor, antifibrinolytic, or uterine relaxant. Its role is prevention of atony-related bleeding; treatment of established haemorrhage requires a complete emergency obstetric protocol.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics and comparative duration
Carbetocin has a longer duration of action than oxytocin because of structural modifications that reduce enzymatic degradation. It produces sustained uterine contractions and increased uterine tone after administration. Quantitative onset and duration vary with route, dose, population, and formulation and should be taken from the specific national product information.

The longer duration does not automatically mean superior outcomes in every delivery setting. Comparative effectiveness must be based on randomized evidence and the clinical context (vaginal birth versus caesarean section, baseline PPH risk, and availability/cost of heat-stable formulations).

## 6. Pharmacokinetics
Carbetocin is administered parenterally. It is a peptide and is subject to proteolytic degradation. Published product information describes a longer terminal half-life than oxytocin (often reported in the range of roughly 40 minutes, depending on route and study), but exact PK values must be extracted from the specific label and study. Do not transfer the half-life of oxytocin or another analogue.

## 7. Clinical evidence and comparative effectiveness
### WHO evidence context
WHO recommends a uterotonic for prevention of postpartum haemorrhage at all births. Carbetocin is among the options; WHO's 2025 consolidated guideline recommends heat-stable carbetocin (HSC) for prevention of PPH in all births where oxytocin is unavailable or its quality cannot be guaranteed. Oxytocin remains recommended where quality-assured product is available and the cold chain can be maintained.

### CHAMPION trial
The CHAMPION trial compared heat-stable carbetocin with oxytocin for prevention of postpartum haemorrhage after vaginal birth in more than 29,000 women. Heat-stable carbetocin was noninferior to oxytocin for the primary endpoint of blood loss of at least 500 mL or use of additional uterotonics, and for severe PPH of at least 1,000 mL. This large trial supports use where maintaining oxytocin quality through cold-chain storage is challenging. The trial tested a heat-stable formulation; do not assume every carbetocin product has the same heat-stability profile.

### Caesarean section
Randomized trials and meta-analyses have compared carbetocin with oxytocin after caesarean delivery. Results vary by study and outcome definition; some analyses report reduced need for additional uterotonics, while effects on severe PPH and transfusion are less consistent. Extract exact meta-analysis methods and certainty before making a superiority claim.

## 8. Safety
Adverse effects reflect uterotonic and cardiovascular activity and may include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, flushing, headache, hypotension, tachycardia, and other haemodynamic changes. The full list and incidence must be taken from the relevant national label. Contraindications, precautions, and route-specific restrictions differ by product. As with other uterotonics, use in pregnancy before delivery and in settings outside the authorized postpartum context must follow the product label.

## 9. Interactions and special populations
- Other uterotonics may produce additive uterine and cardiovascular effects.
- Vasoconstrictors and medicines affecting blood pressure may alter haemodynamic tolerability.
- Obstetric comorbidities and cardiovascular disease require product-label review.
- Breastfeeding and neonatal exposure statements should be sourced from local product information.
- Do not extrapolate safety from heat-stable formulations to all excipient or packaging presentations.

## 10. Formulation, manufacturing, and quality
Carbetocin is supplied as a sterile parenteral solution in many markets. Heat-stable carbetocin (HSC) is a specific formulation designed to retain quality at elevated temperatures; it is not a generic property of every carbetocin product. Critical quality attributes include peptide identity, monocarba bridge integrity, O-methyltyrosine identity, peptide purity, potency, related substances, pH, particulate matter, sterility, endotoxin, container closure, and stability under the labelled storage conditions.

## 11. Bioinformatics and translational mapping

- Signalling: Gq/11–PLCβ–IP3/DAG–Ca2+–calmodulin–MLCK.

## 12. Regulatory status
Carbetocin is authorized in multiple countries but is not FDA-approved in the United States. Authorization, brand, indication, and route are jurisdiction-specific. WHO recommendation is a public-health guideline, not a marketing authorization. The workbook status should therefore remain **not FDA-approved / approved outside the US**, with each country recorded separately when verified.
