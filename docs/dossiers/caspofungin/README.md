# Dossier 092 — Caspofungin (Cancidas)

**Workbook entry:** 092 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **First US approval:** 2001 | **Status:** FDA-approved  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and chemistry
Caspofungin is a semisynthetic echinocandin antifungal derived from a fermentation product of *Glarea lozoyensis*. It is a cyclic hexapeptide-like lipopeptide with a long acyl side chain and multiple non-proteinogenic residues. It is not a conventional linear peptide. The marketed drug is caspofungin acetate for IV infusion; preserve salt form and stereochemistry in chemical records.

## Mechanism
Caspofungin noncompetitively inhibits the fungal cell-wall enzyme complex β-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase, reducing synthesis of an essential structural polysaccharide. This weakens the fungal cell wall and can produce fungicidal activity against many *Candida* species and fungistatic activity against *Aspergillus* hyphae. Mammalian cells lack a cell wall and this target, providing selective toxicity. Echinocandin resistance can arise through FKS gene hotspot mutations.

## Pharmacology and PK
Caspofungin is administered intravenously because oral bioavailability is inadequate. It is highly protein bound and undergoes slow chemical degradation and hepatic metabolism; renal excretion of unchanged drug is limited. Exposure can be affected by hepatic impairment and interacting enzyme-inducing medicines. The current label includes specific dose considerations for hepatic dysfunction and certain concomitant drugs; exact recommendations must be transcribed from label, not generalized across the class.

## Clinical evidence and indications
Cancidas is indicated for treatment of invasive candidiasis, including candidemia and selected deep Candida infections; treatment of esophageal candidiasis; and as salvage therapy for invasive aspergillosis in patients refractory to or intolerant of other therapies, with age-specific label details. It is also used for empirical therapy in febrile neutropenic patients under labelled conditions. Pivotal trials support efficacy in candidiasis and empirical therapy; aspergillosis evidence is more limited and supports salvage positioning rather than first-line equivalence.

## Safety
Common adverse reactions include fever, rash, nausea, diarrhea, infusion-related events and elevated liver enzymes. Serious hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis and hepatic adverse events are possible. Histamine-mediated infusion reactions may occur. Monitor liver tests and review interactions with cyclosporine, tacrolimus and enzyme-inducing drugs according to the current label.

## Formulation and quality
Caspofungin acetate is supplied as sterile lyophilized powder for IV infusion after reconstitution/dilution. Critical attributes include identity, peptide/lipopeptide integrity, potency, related substances, residual solvents, water content, reconstitution characteristics, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter and container closure integrity.

## Regulatory status
FDA first approved in 2001. Product-specific labels define invasive candidiasis, esophageal candidiasis, empirical therapy in febrile neutropenia and salvage treatment of invasive aspergillosis. Do not treat all these indications as having equivalent evidence strength.
