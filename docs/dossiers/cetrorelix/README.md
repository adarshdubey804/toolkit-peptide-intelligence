> Educational dossier only. Cetrorelix is a prescription GnRH antagonist used in specialist-controlled assisted reproduction. This document is not a fertility treatment protocol.

## 1. Executive overview
Cetrorelix is a synthetic decapeptide GnRH receptor antagonist supplied as cetrorelix acetate. It competitively blocks pituitary GnRH receptors and suppresses LH secretion, thereby reducing the likelihood of a premature LH surge during controlled ovarian stimulation. Its onset is rapid and it does not produce the initial gonadotropin flare associated with GnRH agonists.

Cetrotide is supplied as a lyophilized powder for subcutaneous injection after reconstitution. The FDA-approved use is in women undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation to prevent premature LH surges and premature ovulation.

## 3. Sequence and chemical identity
Cetrorelix is a modified decapeptide commonly represented as:

`Ac-D-2Nal-D-4Cpa-D-3Pal-Ser-Tyr-D-Cit-Leu-Arg-Pro-D-Ala-NH2`

Key modifications include N-terminal acetylation, D-3-(2-naphthyl)alanine, D-4-chlorophenylalanine, D-3-(3-pyridyl)alanine, D-citrulline, and C-terminal D-alaninamide. These substitutions distinguish it from native GnRH and from other antagonists such as ganirelix.

The commercial drug substance is cetrorelix acetate. The official product label's molecular formula and molecular weight must be transcribed with the correct salt/hydrate basis; avoid mixing free peptide and acetate salt masses.

## 4. Mechanism of action
Cetrorelix competitively binds pituitary GnRH receptors and prevents endogenous GnRH from activating the receptor:
1. Receptor occupancy blocks native GnRH signalling.
2. Gq/11–PLCβ signalling is suppressed.
3. Pituitary LH release is rapidly reduced, with accompanying FSH suppression.
4. The premature LH surge and ovulation are prevented during the ovarian stimulation window.

Unlike GnRH agonists, cetrorelix does not cause a preceding gonadotropin flare. The suppression is reversible after drug withdrawal.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics
- Rapid suppression of serum LH is the principal PD effect.
- FSH and estradiol patterns depend on concomitant gonadotropin stimulation and ovarian response.
- Cetrorelix prevents a premature LH surge but does not itself stimulate follicular growth.
- Follicular monitoring and trigger timing are integral to clinical protocols.
- Endocrine suppression is a surrogate; pregnancy and live birth are separate outcomes.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics
After subcutaneous administration, cetrorelix is absorbed systemically. The product information describes a terminal elimination half-life of approximately 30 hours after repeated administration, with high plasma protein binding. It is metabolized by peptide degradation and eliminated through renal and faecal routes. Exact bioavailability, Cmax, AUC, and excretion fractions should be taken from the current product information; do not substitute ganirelix PK values.

## 7. Clinical evidence
Clinical studies established cetrorelix as an alternative to GnRH agonist down-regulation for preventing premature LH surges in controlled ovarian stimulation. Label trials assessed prevention of LH surge, ovulation prevention, follicular response, and pregnancy outcomes. The clinical pregnancy rate depends on the stimulation regimen and patient factors. ## 8. Safety
Common adverse effects include injection-site reactions, headache, nausea, and ovarian or abdominal symptoms. Hypersensitivity reactions, including rare serious systemic reactions, have been reported. Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome is a complication of controlled ovarian stimulation and must be monitored as a treatment-cycle risk. Pregnancy outcomes and congenital anomaly observations require interpretation in the context of IVF and concomitant gonadotropins.

## 9. Interactions and special populations
- Administered in combination with gonadotropins under specialist supervision.
- No established broad CYP interaction profile should be inferred from peptide chemistry.
- Use in renal/hepatic impairment requires product-label review; data may be limited.
- Paediatric use is not an established indication.
- Pregnancy is not an indication for cetrorelix administration; cycle timing and discontinuation follow the clinical protocol.
