## 1. Executive summary

CJC-1295 is a name used for modified growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analogues. The term is frequently used imprecisely in commercial and online sources. A critical distinction is between the long-acting **DAC** construct, which contains a drug-affinity-complex modification designed to bind circulating albumin, and the shorter modified GHRH(1–29) construct commonly marketed as **“CJC-1295 without DAC”** or **Mod GRF(1–29)**. They should not be treated as interchangeable substances.

Published human pharmacology for the long-acting CJC-1295 construct showed sustained increases in growth hormone (GH) and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) in healthy volunteers. These were early-phase pharmacology studies, not demonstrations of clinical benefit for growth hormone deficiency, muscle gain, fat loss, anti-aging, or other patient outcomes. Human evidence for the “without DAC” commercial construct is substantially less established.

CJC-1295 has not become an FDA-approved drug. FDA has identified potential significant safety risks for CJC-1295-related bulk drug substances proposed for compounding, including immunogenicity and peptide-impurity/API-characterization concerns; FDA materials also discuss serious adverse events including increased heart rate and systemic vasodilatory reaction. The precise status of individual nominations and current compounding policy must be checked against the live FDA records.

### Nomenclature warning

“CJC-1295” is not a sufficiently precise material specification on its own. In research papers, the long-acting construct was described as a long-acting GHRH analogue. In the marketplace, “CJC-1295 with DAC” and “CJC-1295 without DAC” are commonly distinguished. A dossier, analytical certificate, or research protocol must specify the exact molecular construct, linker/modification pattern, salt form, and reference standard. Do not transfer the pharmacokinetic values of the DAC construct to the non-DAC construct.

## 3. Structure and chemistry

The parent pharmacophore is based on the N-terminal 1–29 amino-acid segment of human GHRH, with substitutions intended to improve resistance to enzymatic degradation. The long-acting DAC construct adds a linker/reactive group that enables covalent association with albumin. Albumin association is the design feature responsible for prolonged systemic exposure.

**Important identity limitation:** vendor descriptions of exact sequences, linker chemistry, molecular formula, and molecular weight are inconsistent. The phrase “CJC-1295” is used for different constructs, and the DAC chemistry is not accurately represented by a simple 29-residue peptide sequence. Therefore, this dossier does not publish one unqualified amino-acid sequence, formula, molecular weight, or CAS number as though it applied to every material sold under this name.

For a validated chemical record, obtain the original development compound structure or an authoritative regulatory/primary-paper structural description, then independently confirm:
- Full residue sequence and stereochemistry.
- Each amino-acid substitution relative to native GHRH(1–29).
- Exact DAC linker identity and attachment site.
- Whether the construct is covalently albumin-binding or simply a non-DAC peptide.
- Salt/counter-ion and hydration state.
- Intact mass, peptide mapping, and orthogonal identity evidence.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

CJC-1295 constructs are designed to activate the **GHRH receptor (GHRHR)** on anterior-pituitary somatotroph cells.

1. The GHRH analogue binds GHRHR.
2. GHRHR activates predominantly Gs-coupled signalling.
3. Adenylyl cyclase activity increases intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP).
4. cAMP-dependent signalling promotes GH synthesis and secretion from pituitary somatotrophs.
5. Circulating GH acts directly on tissues and stimulates hepatic and peripheral production of IGF-1.

**DAC-specific pharmacology:** albumin association prolongs exposure to the receptor-active peptide construct. The pharmacodynamic effect can therefore persist well beyond the brief exposure associated with native GHRH fragments.

This mechanism is distinct from ipamorelin and other ghrelin mimetics, which activate GHSR1a. Combining the two mechanisms in online protocols does not establish clinical benefit or safety.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

The principal demonstrated human pharmacodynamic effects for the long-acting construct are increases in circulating GH and IGF-1.

In the randomized, placebo-controlled ascending-dose studies reported by Teichman and colleagues:
- Mean GH concentrations increased in a dose-dependent manner.
- Mean IGF-1 concentrations increased for several days after a single administration.
- IGF-1 remained above baseline for longer after repeated administrations.
- The authors reported evidence of a cumulative effect with repeated exposure.

These results establish biological activity in healthy volunteers. They do not establish improvement in disease outcomes, physical performance, body composition, longevity, or quality of life.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

For the long-acting CJC-1295 construct, Teichman et al. reported an estimated half-life of approximately **5.8–8.1 days** in the studied healthy-adult population. This is a study-specific estimate for the investigated long-acting construct, not a universal value for all products labelled CJC-1295.

Albumin binding is central to the prolonged exposure profile. Repeated administration was associated with sustained IGF-1 elevation and evidence of accumulation in the study period.

For “CJC-1295 without DAC” / modified GRF(1–29), a comparable peer-reviewed human pharmacokinetic profile has not been established in the same way. Do not use the DAC half-life, exposure, or dosing intervals to describe the non-DAC form.

Further data needed:
- Exact construct-specific bioanalytical assay and analyte definition.
- Distribution, metabolism, and elimination pathways.
- Renal/hepatic impairment effects.
- Long-term accumulation and washout.
- Exposure-response relationships across age and endocrine status.
- Product-specific immunogenicity and anti-drug antibody assessment.

## 7. Clinical evidence

### Early-phase human pharmacology

**Teichman SL et al. (2006), Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism.**  
“Prolonged stimulation of growth hormone (GH) and insulin-like growth factor I secretion by CJC-1295, a long-acting analog of GH-releasing hormone, in healthy adults.” DOI: 10.1210/jc.2005-1536; PMID: 16352683.

- Two randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind ascending-dose trials.
- Participants were healthy adults aged 21–61 years.
- Study periods were 28 and 49 days.
- The long-acting CJC-1295 construct produced dose-dependent increases in mean GH and IGF-1.
- Estimated half-life was 5.8–8.1 days.
- No serious adverse reactions were reported in these relatively small, short-duration studies.

**Interpretation:** this was early-phase pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic research. It was not an efficacy trial in patients with diagnosed GH deficiency and does not support non-medical enhancement claims.

### Additional mechanistic/biomarker research

Sackmann-Sala et al. (2009) reported serum protein-profile changes following activation of the GH/IGF-1 axis by CJC-1295 in normal adult subjects. These findings are exploratory biomarker research, not validated clinical endpoints.

### Evidence gap: non-DAC form

The commercial “without DAC” / modified GRF(1–29) form must be evaluated separately. Direct controlled human clinical evidence for this exact construct is limited or not established in the peer-reviewed literature reviewed for this dossier. Pharmacological claims for the non-DAC product are often extrapolated from native GHRH fragments or the DAC construct; such extrapolation must be labelled as indirect.

### Clinical development status

No approved indication or established phase 3 efficacy programme is identified in the sources curated here. Trial-registry status and any unpublished/terminated studies should be rechecked before future dossier closure.

## 8. Safety and tolerability

### FDA-identified risks

FDA's Category 2 compounding-risk materials identify CJC-1295-related bulk drug substances as potentially presenting significant safety risks. The agency describes:
- Potential immunogenicity associated with peptide aggregation and peptide-related impurities.
- Complexities in API characterization.
- Serious adverse events associated with CJC-1295, including increased heart rate and systemic vasodilatory reaction.
- Limited available clinical safety data.

These statements should be attributed to FDA. They do not justify claiming that every event was definitively caused by CJC-1295 or that every construct and route has an identical risk profile.

### GH/IGF-1 axis considerations

Sustained elevation of GH and IGF-1 is a pharmacological effect, not inherently a clinical benefit. Relevant research and clinical safety considerations include:
- Glucose metabolism and insulin sensitivity.
- Fluid retention and soft-tissue effects associated with GH-axis stimulation.
- Changes in IGF-1 relative to age-adjusted reference intervals.
- Potential consequences of prolonged or excessive GH/IGF-1 signalling.
- Immunogenicity, hypersensitivity, and formulation-related risks.

There is no approved product label with validated contraindications, adverse-event frequencies, or interaction instructions. Do not invent a label-equivalent safety profile.

## 9. Regulatory status and compounding

- **FDA approval:** no FDA-approved CJC-1295 product or indication identified.
- **503A nominations:** FDA's December 2024 PCAC materials describe CJC-1295-related bulk drug substance nominations for treatment of growth hormone deficiency. The nominations were withdrawn, and FDA stated that it was evaluating the substances at its discretion.
- **503B Category 2:** FDA's interim compounding-risk page lists CJC-1295 among bulk substances that may present significant safety risks, citing immunogenicity/API-characterization concerns and serious adverse events including tachycardia and systemic vasodilatory reaction.
- **Compounded products:** compounding does not confer FDA approval and does not establish efficacy, safety, or equivalence to an approved drug.
- **Non-DAC naming:** the regulatory identity of a specific “without DAC” preparation must not be inferred from the name alone.

Regulatory lists and policies can change. Check the live FDA page and relevant PCAC documents before making a current legal determination.
