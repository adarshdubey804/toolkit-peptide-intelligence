# Dossier 089 — Colistin / Colistimethate Sodium (Coly-Mycin M)

**Workbook entry:** 089 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **First US approval:** historical date not established in workbook | **Status:** FDA-approved product  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and nomenclature
Colistin (polymyxin E) is a cyclic cationic lipopeptide antibiotic mixture, principally colistin A and colistin B. Colistimethate sodium (CMS, also called colistin methanesulfonate) is a chemically modified prodrug administered parenterally or by inhalation in some jurisdictions; it is converted in vivo to active colistin. These terms are not interchangeable. The US Coly-Mycin M injection contains colistimethate sodium, not a vial of preformed active colistin. Preserve the product's potency convention and units; international units, colistin-base activity and CMS mass are not directly interchangeable without the correct conversion.

## Mechanism
Colistin binds lipid A in the lipopolysaccharide (LPS) of Gram-negative bacterial outer membranes, displacing stabilizing divalent cations and disrupting membrane integrity. It can also affect the cytoplasmic membrane. This causes leakage and bacterial death. Intrinsic or acquired resistance can involve LPS/lipid A modification, including addition of phosphoethanolamine or loss/modification of LPS pathways.

## Pharmacology and PK
CMS is a prodrug that undergoes hydrolysis to active colistin. Conversion is variable and delayed, while active colistin is predominantly renally cleared. Renal function, critical illness and extracorporeal support can substantially alter exposure. The narrow therapeutic window makes nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity central concerns. Do not transfer PK values from active colistin to the CMS prodrug without specifying analyte and assay.

## Clinical evidence and use
Colistimethate has been used for serious infections due to susceptible multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacteria, including selected *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Acinetobacter baumannii* and *Klebsiella pneumoniae*. Contemporary treatment guidance increasingly favours newer active agents when available because of colistin's toxicity and PK limitations. Susceptibility testing is technically challenging and should follow validated standards. Do not imply that colistin is appropriate for all MDR Gram-negative infections or that in-vitro susceptibility guarantees clinical success.

## Safety
Nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity (including paresthesia, weakness and neuromuscular blockade) are major risks. Bronchospasm can occur with inhaled formulations. Hypersensitivity and electrolyte/renal complications require clinical assessment. Avoid confusing CMS dosing units with active colistin-base activity.

## Product quality
Colistin is a mixture of related cyclic lipopeptides; CMS is a mixture of sulfomethylated derivatives with conversion-dependent activity. Critical quality attributes include component profile, degree of sulfomethylation, potency/unit definition, residual reagents, degradation, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter and stability. Product and route-specific specifications must be maintained.

## Regulatory status
Coly-Mycin M is an FDA-approved colistimethate sodium injection. The workbook has no first-approval date; do not invent one. Approval and availability differ by product and route internationally.
