# Dossier 039 — Copper Cu 64 dotatate (DETECTNET)

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Evidence cut-off:** 4 November 2026  
**US NDA:** 213227  
**US initial approval:** 3 September 2020  
**Class:** DOTA-chelated cyclic somatostatin-analogue peptide radioconjugate  
**Use:** PET localisation of somatostatin-receptor-positive neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) in adults.

> DETECTNET is a diagnostic radiopharmaceutical, not a therapeutic peptide. It is distinct from gallium-68 DOTATATE, gallium-68 DOTATOC and therapeutic lutetium-177 DOTATATE.

## 1. Identity and classification

Copper Cu 64 dotatate is a radiolabelled somatostatin analogue. The peptide targets somatostatin receptors; DOTA chelates copper-64; copper-64 supplies the positron-emitting signal for PET.

- Brand: DETECTNET.
- Active substance: copper oxodotreotide Cu-64.
- Synonyms: 64Cu-DOTATATE; copper-64 DOTA-TATE.
- FDA application: NDA 213227.
- Labeler: Curium US LLC (DailyMed SPL effective 31 January 2025).
- Route: intravenous.
- Dosage form: sterile radioactive solution.
- Approved population: adults. Do not extrapolate the US indication to paediatric patients.

## 2. Peptide sequence and structure

The DOTATATE targeting moiety is a cyclic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin:

**H-D-Phe-Cys-Tyr-D-Trp-Lys-Thr-Cys-Thr-OH**

- Chelator: DOTA attached at the N-terminal D-phenylalanine.
- Cyclisation: disulfide bond between Cys2 and Cys7.
- Targeting pharmacophore: Tyr3-octreotate / TATE.
- Radiometal: copper-64 coordinated by DOTA.

Do not substitute the unlabelled peptide molecular weight for the radiolabelled copper complex.

Copper-64 physical half-life is approximately 12.7 hours, longer than gallium-68 (approximately 68 minutes). This influences production, distribution and imaging logistics.

## 4. Mechanism of action

1. DOTATATE binds somatostatin receptors, with highest affinity for SSTR2.
2. Many NET cells overexpress SSTR2, localising the tracer to receptor-positive lesions.
3. DOTA coordinates copper-64.
4. Copper-64 emits positrons; annihilation produces pairs of 511-keV photons.
5. PET detects coincident photons and reconstructs radiotracer distribution.

This is a receptor-targeted diagnostic imaging mechanism, not tumour-cell killing or therapeutic radionuclide delivery.

## 5. Receptor pharmacology and specificity

- Primary target: SSTR2; target family: SSTR1–SSTR5.
- SSTR2 is frequently expressed in NETs and metastases.
- Uptake is not unique to NETs; other tumours, pathologies and normal tissues may show activity.
- Physiologic uptake, including in the pancreatic uncinate process, can complicate interpretation.
- Uptake is a functional receptor signal, not a stand-alone histological diagnosis.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics and biodistribution

The DETECTNET label reports:
- Maximum radioactivity at 1–3 hours in adrenal glands, kidneys, pituitary glands, spleen and liver.
- In a small single-dose assessment (n=6), 16%–40% of injected radioactivity was recovered in urine over 6 hours.
- Metabolism is not known from the product label.
- Copper-64 physical half-life is approximately 12.7 hours.

Do not confuse physical radionuclide decay with peptide metabolism or biological clearance.

## 7. Approved indication and clinical use

The US FDA indication is use with PET to localise somatostatin-receptor-positive NETs in **adult patients**. Potential clinical contexts include localisation, staging, assessment of disease distribution and detection of primary or metastatic receptor-positive lesions. DETECTNET is a diagnostic agent, not a NET treatment.

## 8. Dose and imaging procedure

US label:
- Adult dose: 148 MBq (4 mCi) by intravenous bolus.
- Begin PET acquisition 45–90 minutes after administration.
- Product presentation: 148 MBq (4 mCi) in 4 mL at calibration (37 MBq/mL).

Activity is time-dependent and must be measured using appropriate calibrated equipment. This dossier is not a radiopharmacy preparation or administration protocol.

## 9. Formulation and presentation

DailyMed lists:
- Copper Cu 64 dotatate: 1 mCi/mL at calibration.
- Ascorbic acid: 40 mg/mL.
- Dehydrated alcohol (ethanol): 0.05 mL/mL.
- Sterile water for injection.
- Sodium hydroxide and/or hydrochloric acid for pH adjustment.
- Single-use 4 mL vial containing 148 MBq (4 mCi) at calibration.
- NDC: 69945-064-01.
- Storage: upright at 20–25°C in a shielded container, per label.

These details refer to the listed Curium product presentation.

## 10. Safety and precautions

- Radiation risk: safe handling, preparation and administration are required.
- Hydration and frequent voiding after administration are advised in the label.
- Hypersensitivity reactions include rash and pruritus; most reported reactions were reversible spontaneously or with routine symptomatic management.
- Uptake in non-NET tumours, other pathologies and physiologic variants can cause image misinterpretation.
- Reported adverse reactions include nausea, vomiting and flushing.
- Pregnancy and lactation require assessment under current radiopharmaceutical precautions and official labelling.

Do not transfer adverse-effect assumptions from therapeutic copper compounds or therapeutic radioligands.

## 11. Interactions and imaging confounders

The label does not describe a conventional drug-interaction profile. Somatostatin analogue therapy can affect receptor availability and tracer uptake. Timing of short-acting or long-acting analogues should follow current product information and specialist imaging protocols; do not invent a withholding interval. Other confounders include physiologic SSTR expression, inflammatory/pathological uptake, lesion size and receptor heterogeneity.
