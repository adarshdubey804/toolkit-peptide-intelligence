# Dossier 097 — Cyclosporine (Cyclosporin A; Sandimmune, Neoral, Gengraf, Restasis)

**Workbook entry:** 097 | **Category:** Immunology, inflammation & transplant | **First US approval:** 1983 | **Status:** FDA-approved products  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and chemistry
Cyclosporine (cyclosporin A) is a cyclic undecapeptide of fungal origin, originally isolated from *Tolypocladium inflatum*. It contains 11 amino-acid residues, including several N-methylated and non-proteinogenic residues, and one unusual amino acid (MeBmt). It is a neutral, highly lipophilic macrocycle with no free termini. Its cyclic structure is not accurately represented by a simple linear sequence. Different approved formulations (Sandimmune, Neoral, Gengraf, Restasis and others) have distinct routes, absorption characteristics and labels.

## Mechanism
Cyclosporine binds intracellular cyclophilin to form a complex that inhibits calcineurin phosphatase. This prevents dephosphorylation and nuclear translocation of NFAT transcription factors, reducing transcription of IL-2 and other cytokines required for T-cell activation. The effect is immunosuppressive rather than broadly cytotoxic. Cyclosporine also interacts with other cyclophilins and pathways; the calcineurin mechanism is the established principal pharmacology for systemic immunosuppression.

## Pharmacology and formulation distinctions
Systemic oral cyclosporine has variable absorption and extensive CYP3A4/P-glycoprotein interaction potential. Neoral and Gengraf are modified/microemulsion formulations with different bioavailability from Sandimmune; they are not automatically interchangeable milligram-for-milligram. Therapeutic drug monitoring and formulation-specific prescribing are important in transplant care. Topical ophthalmic cyclosporine (Restasis and other products) is a separate low-systemic-exposure formulation for dry-eye disease and must not be merged with systemic PK or indications.

## Clinical evidence and indications
Systemic formulations are used for prevention of organ transplant rejection and selected autoimmune/inflammatory conditions, depending on product label. Restasis is indicated to increase tear production in patients whose tear production is presumed to be suppressed due to ocular inflammation associated with keratoconjunctivitis sicca. Evidence and endpoints differ substantially across transplant, dermatology/rheumatology and ophthalmic products. Do not treat all cyclosporine formulations as one interchangeable therapy.

## Safety
Systemic cyclosporine carries major risks including nephrotoxicity, hypertension, serious infections, malignancy/lymphoproliferative disorders, neurotoxicity, hyperkalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hepatotoxicity and drug interactions. Therapeutic drug monitoring, renal function, blood pressure and interaction review are central. Ophthalmic products have local adverse effects such as ocular burning and stinging and have distinct systemic exposure. Product-specific warnings must be used.

## Formulation and quality
Systemic products include oral solutions, capsules and IV formulations; ophthalmic products are sterile emulsions or solutions depending on product. Critical quality attributes include macrocycle identity, stereochemistry, N-methylation pattern, purity, related substances, solvent residues, dissolution/emulsification, particle/globule size for emulsions, sterility for parenteral/ophthalmic products, endotoxin where applicable and container integrity.

## Regulatory status
First US approval in 1983, with multiple subsequent formulations and indications. Sandimmune, Neoral, Gengraf and Restasis have distinct application histories and product labels. The workbook's approval date should be attached to the original systemic product history, not automatically copied to every later formulation.
