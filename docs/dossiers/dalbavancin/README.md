# Dossier 086 — Dalbavancin (Dalvance)

## Identity and molecular class
Dalbavancin is a semisynthetic lipoglycopeptide derived from a teicoplanin-like glycopeptide natural-product scaffold. It is a large, highly glycosylated and lipophilic antibacterial molecule, not a conventional linear peptide or protein. Its extended terminal lipophilic side chain contributes to prolonged exposure and tissue distribution. Sequence-based representations are inappropriate; curate the complete chemical structure, stereochemistry and salt form from authoritative chemical records.

## Mechanism
Dalbavancin binds the D-alanyl-D-alanine terminus of peptidoglycan precursors and inhibits bacterial cell-wall synthesis, interfering with transglycosylation and transpeptidation. It is bactericidal against susceptible Gram-positive pathogens, including *S. aureus* and streptococci. Its pharmacodynamic activity is associated with AUC/MIC. Glycopeptide resistance mechanisms can reduce susceptibility.

## Pharmacokinetics
Dalbavancin is administered intravenously and has a long terminal half-life. The US label describes a three-compartment model; terminal half-life in healthy-subject data is approximately 346 hours, while population PK analysis in patients estimates an effective half-life of approximately 8.5 days. It is highly protein bound (about 93%) and eliminated through renal and nonrenal pathways. These long exposures enable one- or two-infusion regimens in labelled ABSSSI settings; they also mean that adverse reactions or inappropriate treatment cannot be rapidly reversed by stopping a daily dose.

## Clinical evidence and indication
Dalvance is indicated for adult and paediatric patients with acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections (ABSSSI) caused by designated susceptible Gram-positive microorganisms. Pivotal DISCOVER studies compared dalbavancin regimens with vancomycin followed by linezolid and supported noninferiority on early clinical response. Later studies evaluated single-dose and simplified regimens. Do not extend the FDA indication to bacteremia, endocarditis or osteomyelitis solely because of off-label observational use.

## Safety
Important adverse reactions include nausea, headache and diarrhea. Serious hypersensitivity, infusion-related reactions, ALT elevations and *C. difficile*-associated diarrhea have been reported. Renal impairment affects exposure and may require regimen adjustment under the current label. Consider long half-life when evaluating delayed adverse events and antimicrobial stewardship.

## Formulation and quality
Dalvance is supplied as sterile lyophilized powder for IV reconstitution. Critical quality attributes include identity, potency, glycopeptide component/impurity profile, related substances, residual solvents, water content, reconstitution properties, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter and container closure integrity.

## Regulatory status
FDA initial US approval in 2014. Current US indication includes adult and paediatric ABSSSI due to designated susceptible Gram-positive organisms. Product-specific label revision and paediatric age/weight restrictions should be verified before use.
