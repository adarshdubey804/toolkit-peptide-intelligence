# Dossier 088 — Daptomycin (Cubicin; Cubicin RF)

**Workbook entry:** 088 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **First US approval:** 2003 | **Status:** FDA-approved  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and chemistry
Daptomycin is a fermentation-derived cyclic lipopeptide antibiotic produced by *Streptomyces roseosporus*. It contains a 13-amino-acid cyclic peptide core and a decanoyl lipid tail. The macrocycle includes a characteristic ester linkage and non-proteinogenic residues; represent its stereochemistry and lipid attachment from an authoritative structure record rather than as an ordinary linear sequence. Cubicin and Cubicin RF are product presentations; check their product-specific labels.

## Mechanism
In the presence of calcium, daptomycin binds bacterial membranes and oligomerizes, causing rapid membrane depolarization and disruption of essential membrane functions. This leads to inhibition of macromolecular synthesis and bactericidal activity against susceptible Gram-positive bacteria. It is not active against Gram-negative organisms because of the outer membrane barrier. The exact sequence of membrane events is still an area of mechanistic study; avoid reducing its action to a simple nonspecific detergent effect.

## Pharmacology and PK
Daptomycin is administered intravenously and is primarily renally eliminated. Exposure is related to calcium-dependent membrane activity; renal impairment requires label-specific adjustment. It is highly protein bound and has a terminal half-life on the order of 8–9 hours in adults with normal renal function. Creatine phosphokinase (CPK) elevation is a key pharmacodynamic/safety monitoring issue. It is inactivated by pulmonary surfactant and is not indicated for pneumonia.

## Clinical evidence and indications
US-labelled uses include complicated skin and skin-structure infections and *Staphylococcus aureus* bloodstream infection, including right-sided infective endocarditis, with age/population details defined in the current label. The pivotal skin infection and bacteremia/endocarditis programmes established efficacy in selected Gram-positive infections. Do not infer efficacy for left-sided endocarditis or pneumonia from these indications. Off-label use in osteomyelitis and other deep infections should be clearly separated from labelled evidence.

## Safety
Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis are major risks; monitor CPK as directed by the current label and assess muscle symptoms. Eosinophilic pneumonia, peripheral neuropathy, hypersensitivity and *C. difficile*-associated diarrhea are additional concerns. Concomitant HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors may increase muscle toxicity risk; manage according to current label and clinical guidance. Renal function affects exposure.

## Formulation and quality
Sterile IV lyophilized powder. Critical quality attributes include identity, cyclic/lipid structural integrity, potency, related substances, residual solvents, water content, reconstitution, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter and container closure integrity. Calcium content in the administration environment is mechanistically relevant; do not improvise compatibility claims.

## Regulatory status
FDA first approved in 2003. Current label distinguishes skin infection from *S. aureus* bacteremia/right-sided endocarditis indications and includes age and renal considerations. Verify exact current product wording.
