- **Brand:** FIRMAGON.
- **Developer/origin:** FE 200486; Ferring.
- **Route/formulation:** subcutaneous injection after reconstitution of lyophilised powder.
- **FDA approval:** initial US approval 2008; NDA 022201.
- **FDA active-moiety UNII:** SX0XJI3A11.
- **CAS:** 214766-78-6 (free degarelix, as mapped in FDA GSRS).
- **ATC:** L02BX02.
- **Classification note:** This is a peptide-based GnRH antagonist, not a small-molecule non-peptide antagonist. Its extensive noncanonical amino-acid substitutions make it structurally distinct from native GnRH.

## 2. Sequence and primary structure

The FDA product description identifies degarelix as a **synthetic linear decapeptide amide containing seven unnatural amino acids, five of which are D-amino acids**.

The condensed structural description reported in PubChem is:

**Ac-D-2Nal-D-Phe(4-Cl)-D-3Pal-Ser-Phe(4-S-dihydroorotamido)-D-Phe(4-ureido)-Leu-Lys(iPr)-Pro-D-Ala-NH₂**

This shorthand is useful for annotation but should not be treated as a conventional one-letter amino-acid sequence: several residues are chemically modified. The complete chemical name in the current product label is the primary product identity reference.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Degarelix is a **competitive/reversible GnRH receptor antagonist** at pituitary gonadotrophs.

1. It binds pituitary GnRH receptors and blocks endogenous GnRH signalling.
2. This rapidly suppresses LH and FSH release.
3. Reduced LH lowers Leydig-cell testosterone synthesis; reduced FSH also suppresses gonadal signalling.
4. Testosterone falls to the castration range without the initial agonist-driven testosterone surge associated with GnRH agonists.

This distinguishes degarelix from leuprolide, goserelin, triptorelin, histrelin, and nafarelin, which are GnRH agonists and have an initial stimulation/flare phase before downregulation with sustained exposure. Degarelix does not require that agonist flare mechanism to suppress testosterone.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- A 240 mg starting dose produces rapid reductions in LH, FSH, and testosterone.
- The labelled pharmacodynamic endpoint is maintenance of testosterone below the conventional castration threshold of 50 ng/dL.
- Suppression is achieved rapidly compared with depot GnRH agonist regimens and without an initial testosterone surge.
- PSA generally declines as androgen-dependent prostate cancer activity is suppressed; PSA response is a monitoring marker, not a direct receptor-occupancy measurement.
- Testosterone recovery after stopping treatment depends on duration of therapy and patient factors; do not infer a universal recovery interval.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

Degarelix is administered subcutaneously and forms a depot at the injection site. Absorption from this depot is prolonged; serum concentration and testosterone suppression profiles are therefore not comparable to a simple immediate-release peptide injection.

The product label reports:
- Following subcutaneous administration, median time to maximum plasma concentration is approximately 2 days after the starting dose and approximately 2 days after the maintenance dose.
- Apparent terminal half-life is approximately 53 days after the 240 mg starting dose and approximately 27 days after the 80 mg maintenance dose; these values reflect slow depot release.
- Distribution volume is approximately 1 L/kg.
- Plasma protein binding is approximately 90%.
- Peptide degradation through the hepatobiliary system is the principal elimination route; unchanged degarelix is not expected to be a major urinary elimination route.
- Mild to moderate renal impairment has limited effect on exposure in the studied range; severe renal impairment is insufficiently characterised.
- Severe hepatic dysfunction has not been adequately studied; use caution as specified in product information.

The long apparent half-life is formulation/depot-dependent and should not be interpreted as the intrinsic plasma half-life of free peptide in circulation.

## 7. Clinical evidence and labelled indication

**United States labelled indication:** treatment of patients with advanced prostate cancer.

The pivotal phase III comparison with leuprolide evaluated testosterone suppression and PSA-related outcomes. Degarelix rapidly achieved and maintained castration-range testosterone without the agonist flare observed with leuprolide. The precise clinical interpretation depends on the trial population and endpoint; the dossier does not generalise this result to all prostate-cancer settings.

The European product information includes additional high-risk localised/locally advanced settings in combination with radiotherapy. These European indications must not be silently imported into the US label.

## 8. Administration and formulation

- Route: subcutaneous only.
- Dosage form: sterile lyophilised powder containing degarelix acetate and mannitol; supplied with solvent for reconstitution.
- US product configurations: 120 mg degarelix-equivalent vials for the starting regimen and an 80 mg degarelix-equivalent vial for maintenance.
- Labelled regimen structure: 240 mg starting dose as two 120 mg subcutaneous injections, followed by 80 mg subcutaneously every 28 days.
- The two starting injections are administered at separate abdominal sites; injection-site technique and preparation must follow the current product instructions.
- Do not administer intravenously or intramuscularly.

This dossier is not a patient-specific dosing instruction. Use current local product information and clinician direction.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

Important labelled risks and precautions include:
- Injection-site reactions, including pain, erythema, swelling, induration, and nodules; local reactions are common.
- Hot flashes, weight gain, fatigue, and other consequences of androgen deprivation.
- QT/QTc prolongation; consider cardiovascular risk factors, electrolyte abnormalities, and concomitant QT-prolonging medicines.
- Hepatic enzyme elevations; monitor according to the local label and clinical context.
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including rare serious reactions.
- Androgen-deprivation class risks, including metabolic changes, loss of bone mineral density, cardiovascular considerations, and effects on sexual function.
- Potential effects on fertility and reproductive function.

Contraindications and warnings must be taken from the current jurisdiction-specific label. No individual risk estimate is inferred here.

## 10. Drug interactions

Formal clinical drug-interaction studies are limited. The clinically relevant interaction concern includes additive QT/QTc prolongation with other QT-prolonging drugs and medicines or conditions that increase arrhythmia risk. Because degarelix is highly protein-bound and has a long depot-associated exposure profile, interaction assessment should use the current prescribing information rather than extrapolating from short-lived peptide drugs.

## 13. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- Primary target: **GNRHR**, the pituitary GnRH receptor.
- Signalling axis: hypothalamic GnRH → pituitary gonadotroph GnRHR → LH/FSH → gonadal steroidogenesis.
- Degarelix acts at the receptor level as an antagonist, suppressing the upstream gonadotropin signal.
- Downstream pathway consequence: reduced testicular testosterone synthesis and reduced androgen-receptor stimulation in androgen-dependent tissues.
- No validated nafarelin/degarelix-specific pharmacogenomic biomarker or individual-response genomic test is established in this dossier.
