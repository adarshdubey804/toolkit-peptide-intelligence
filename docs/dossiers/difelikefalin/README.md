## 3. Mechanism of action
A selective kappa-opioid receptor agonist designed for limited central nervous system penetration. Peripheral KOR activation modulates itch signalling. The IV product is used for moderate-to-severe CKD-associated pruritus in adults undergoing haemodialysis. Adverse effects include diarrhoea, dizziness, nausea, and gait-related events.

## 15. Verified chemical identity and product formulation

The current US prescribing information identifies difelikefalin as a synthetic peptide with a single stereoisomer, supplied as an acetate salt. Its chemical name is **4-amino-1-(D-phenylalanyl-D-phenylalanyl-D-leucyl-D-lysyl)piperidine-4-carboxylic acid, acetate salt**. The molecule contains the D-Phe-D-Phe-D-Leu-D-Lys peptide sequence attached to a 4-aminopiperidine-4-carboxylic-acid moiety; it is therefore not adequately represented as a simple free tetrapeptide.

## 16. Mechanism and pharmacology — evidence qualification

KORSUVA is a selective kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) agonist with limited CNS penetration relative to centrally acting opioid agonists. The FDA label explicitly states that the relevance of KOR activation to therapeutic effectiveness is not known. Accordingly, KOR agonism is an established pharmacological property, while the precise causal pathway by which peripheral receptor activation reduces CKD-associated itch remains incompletely resolved.

Do not present a detailed peripheral neuronal or immune-cell itch pathway as proven clinical mechanism without direct human evidence. Avoid implying that the drug's clinical efficacy establishes the contribution of any single KOR-expressing cell population.

## 17. Pharmacokinetics and dialysis

Difelikefalin is administered intravenously, has low plasma protein binding (approximately 23–28% in haemodialysis patients), and is primarily eliminated by the kidneys. The label reports that high-flux haemodialysis for four hours clears approximately 70–80% of difelikefalin from plasma; it was not detectable at the end of the second of two dialysis cycles. This dialysis clearance is relevant to the product's timing around haemodialysis and must not be generalized to other dialyzer types or dialysis schedules.

The label also documents increased exposure in renal impairment and specific clinical pharmacology considerations in patients undergoing haemodialysis. Capture the full numerical Cmax/AUC and half-life tables from the current label before finalizing the PK section; do not extrapolate a terminal half-life from dialysis extraction data alone.

## 18. Phase 3 clinical evidence

### KALM-1
KALM-1 (NCT03422653) was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in adults receiving haemodialysis with moderate-to-severe CKD-associated pruritus. At week 12, 51.9% of observed difelikefalin recipients versus 30.9% of placebo recipients achieved at least a 3-point reduction in Worst Itching Intensity Numerical Rating Scale (WI-NRS). The imputed analysis was 49.1% versus 27.9% (P<0.001). Itch-related quality-of-life measures also improved. Diarrhoea, dizziness, and vomiting were more common with difelikefalin.

### KALM-2
KALM-2 (NCT03636269) was a second pivotal phase 3 placebo-controlled study. The primary endpoint was the proportion achieving a ≥3-point improvement in WI-NRS at week 12. The study met its primary endpoint; reported rates were 54.0% for difelikefalin versus 42.2% for placebo (P=0.02). The magnitude of placebo response and between-study differences should be retained when synthesizing both trials.

### Pooled phase 3 efficacy
A pooled analysis of KALM-1 and KALM-2 included 851 randomized participants (426 difelikefalin; 425 placebo). At week 12, ≥3-point WI-NRS improvement occurred in 51.1% versus 35.2% (P<0.001), and ≥4-point improvement in 38.7% versus 23.4% (P<0.001). Quality-of-life improvements were also reported. Pooled analyses should be labelled as such and not represented as a single independent randomized trial.

### Pooled safety
A pooled analysis across four phase 3 studies included 848 participants in the placebo-controlled safety cohort (424 per group) and 1,306 participants in the all-difelikefalin-exposure cohort. Common treatment-emergent events with a ≥1% higher incidence than placebo included diarrhoea (9.0% vs 5.7%), dizziness (6.8% vs 3.8%), nausea (6.6% vs 4.5%), gait disturbances including falls (6.6% vs 5.4%), hyperkalaemia (4.7% vs 3.5%), headache (4.5% vs 2.6%), somnolence (4.2% vs 2.4%), and mental status changes (3.3% vs 1.4%). Most events were mild or moderate in the pooled analysis. These pooled figures are not a substitute for the current label's adverse-reaction table.

## 19. Safety and interaction considerations

The US label warns about dizziness, somnolence, mental status changes, and gait disturbances, including falls. Concomitant centrally acting sedating medicines and opioid analgesics may increase CNS-depressant effects; review the full current label for interaction wording and monitoring recommendations. The drug is not a controlled substance under the US label's DEA schedule entry. Adverse events should be assessed in the dialysis population's context, including comorbidity and polypharmacy.
