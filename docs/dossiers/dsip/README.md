# DSIP (delta sleep-inducing peptide; emideltide)

> Workbook entry: **117/128** · Curation status: **in_progress**

## Overview

Synthetic nonapeptide historically investigated for sleep and withdrawal-related outcomes. It is not an approved medicine; historical human studies are small, old and inconsistent.

## Identity and chemistry

Nonapeptide sequence: Trp-Ala-Gly-Gly-Asp-Ala-Ser-Gly-Glu (WAGGDASGE), commonly written H-Trp-Ala-Gly-Gly-Asp-Ala-Ser-Gly-Glu-OH. Emideltide is a recognized name for DSIP.

## Mechanism of action

The physiological receptor and definitive mechanism remain unresolved. Proposed neuroendocrine and sleep-regulatory actions have not yielded a validated molecular target or reproducible mechanistic model.

## Pharmacodynamics

Historical studies measured sleep architecture and related outcomes after intravenous administration. Findings were inconsistent, and some controlled studies found little or no clinically meaningful benefit.

## Pharmacokinetics

No approved formulation or modern validated PK label. Historical human experiments used intravenous administration; those findings cannot be extrapolated to unapproved subcutaneous or intranasal products.

## Clinical evidence

Small human studies from the 1980s and 1990s investigated insomnia and withdrawal-related outcomes, with conflicting conclusions and limited sample sizes. There is no modern confirmatory clinical programme establishing efficacy.

## Safety and tolerability

The small, dated exposure base is inadequate to characterize safety. Lack of contemporary adverse-event reporting or modern trials must not be interpreted as evidence of safety.

## Regulatory status

Not FDA-approved and no verified major-regulator marketing authorization identified. Historical nominations or advisory review concerning compounding do not constitute drug approval.

## Formulation and manufacturing

No standardized authorized medicinal formulation. Research materials require independent identity, purity, aggregation, endotoxin and sterility characterization.

## Bioinformatics and translational research

Potential research: sleep-stage EEG endpoints, circadian/neuroendocrine networks and peptide-receptor deorphanization. Mechanistic target discovery remains a major gap.
