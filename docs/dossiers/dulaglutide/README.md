## 2. Molecular architecture and sequence

Dulaglutide consists of two identical, disulfide-linked chains. Each chain contains:
1. an N-terminal modified human GLP-1 analogue;
2. a short synthetic peptide linker; and
3. a modified human IgG4 heavy-chain Fc fragment.

The GLP-1 analogue region has approximately 90% amino-acid sequence homology to native human GLP-1(7–37). Structural modifications reduce DPP-4-mediated degradation. Further modifications to the IgG4 Fc region reduce high-affinity Fc-receptor binding and half-antibody formation; changes were also made in a potential T-cell epitope region.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Dulaglutide is a long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist. GLP-1R is a cell-surface receptor coupled to adenylyl cyclase in pancreatic beta cells. Receptor activation increases intracellular cAMP and promotes glucose-dependent insulin release.

Additional pharmacological actions:
- decreases inappropriate glucagon secretion;
- slows gastric emptying; and
- lowers fasting and postprandial glucose concentrations.

The glucose dependence of insulin secretion contributes to a lower intrinsic hypoglycaemia risk than insulin secretagogues when dulaglutide is used alone. Hypoglycaemia risk increases when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.

## 5. Molecular basis of prolonged action

Dulaglutide's extended activity is engineered through:
- resistance to DPP-4 degradation in the GLP-1 analogue region;
- fusion to a large IgG4 Fc fragment, increasing molecular size and slowing absorption;
- reduced renal clearance relative to a small peptide; and
- Fc engineering intended to limit Fc-receptor-mediated immune activity and half-antibody formation.

The intact molecule remains a GLP-1R agonist; the Fc portion primarily provides pharmacokinetic and molecular-engineering functions.

## 6. Pharmacodynamics

The US label reports:
- reductions in fasting and postprandial glucose;
- increased first- and second-phase insulin secretion;
- glucose-dependent insulin release;
- reduced glucagon secretion; and
- delayed gastric emptying.

In one clinical pharmacology study, once-weekly treatment with 1.5 mg reduced fasting glucose, 2-hour postprandial glucose, and postprandial incremental glucose AUC versus placebo. These are study-specific results and should not be generalized beyond the studied population and regimen.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics

The current US label reports:
- steady-state Tmax range: 24–72 hours, median 48 hours;
- steady state reached after approximately 2–4 weeks of once-weekly administration;
- accumulation ratio approximately 1.56;
- absolute bioavailability: 65% after a single 0.75 mg dose and 47% after a single 1.5 mg dose;
- apparent central volume of distribution: 3.09 L;
- apparent peripheral volume of distribution: 5.98 L;
- apparent clearance: 0.142 L/hour;
- elimination half-life: approximately 5 days;
- presumed degradation to component amino acids through general protein catabolism.

The label reports no clinically relevant PK effect from age, sex, race, ethnicity, body weight, or renal/hepatic impairment in the populations studied. Apply the exact label caveats for severe renal disease and limited hepatic experience.

## 8. Manufacturing and formulation

- **Production system:** recombinant DNA technology in CHO cells.
- **Drug product:** clear, colourless, sterile, preservative-free aqueous solution for subcutaneous use.
- **Reference US presentations:** single-dose pens containing 0.75 mg, 1.5 mg, 3 mg, or 4.5 mg in 0.5 mL.
- **Label excipients:** anhydrous citric acid, mannitol, polysorbate 80, trisodium citrate dihydrate, and water for injection.
- Product-specific excipient quantities vary by strength. Record the label version and strength with each formulation record.

## 9. Regulatory history and indications

### United States
- Initial approval: 2014.
- Current US label (2026) includes glycaemic control in adults and paediatric patients aged 10 years and older with type 2 diabetes, as specified in the current prescribing information.
- The US label contains a boxed warning regarding thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rodents and contraindicates use in patients with personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2.

### European Union
- EU marketing authorisation issued: 21 November 2014.
- EMA indicates use in adults and children from 10 years of age with insufficiently controlled type 2 diabetes as an adjunct to diet and exercise, under the conditions in the current product information.

Approval status and current marketed availability are separate attributes; verify national availability and presentation status independently.

## 11. Safety and precautions

The current US label includes:
- boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents; human relevance unknown;
- contraindication with personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2;
- pancreatitis warning;
- hypoglycaemia risk, especially with insulin or sulfonylurea;
- acute kidney injury risk in the setting of severe gastrointestinal reactions/dehydration;
- severe gastrointestinal adverse reactions and use limitations in severe gastroparesis;
- hypersensitivity reactions;
- gallbladder disease;
- diabetic retinopathy complications reported in some settings and requiring label-specific interpretation.

Extract adverse-event incidence, contraindications, pregnancy/lactation data, paediatric safety, post-marketing reports, and full warnings from the exact current US and EU labels. Do not treat all class warnings as interchangeable between GLP-1 products.
