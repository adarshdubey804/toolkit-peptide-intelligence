# 049 — Ecallantide (KALBITOR)

**Curation status:** in_progress

**Entity class:** recombinant protein; 60-amino-acid Kunitz-domain kallikrein inhibitor  
**Evidence cut-off:** 4 November 2026

> **Scope and classification.** Ecallantide is a recombinant protein therapeutic that inhibits plasma kallikrein. It is used for treatment of acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE) in patients aged 12 years and older in the United States. Although it is a small protein with a defined amino-acid sequence, it should be classified as a protein biologic/protease inhibitor rather than forced into a conventional synthetic-peptide category.

## 3. Disease biology and physiological context

Hereditary angioedema is commonly associated with deficient or dysfunctional C1 esterase inhibitor (C1-INH). Uncontrolled activation of the contact system increases plasma kallikrein activity, which cleaves high-molecular-weight kininogen to release bradykinin. Bradykinin acting at the B2 receptor increases vascular permeability and causes episodic swelling of skin, gastrointestinal tract and upper airway.

Ecallantide acts downstream of the C1-INH defect by inhibiting plasma kallikrein and reducing bradykinin generation.

## 4. Mechanism of action

**Selective plasma kallikrein inhibition:**

1. Ecallantide binds plasma kallikrein with high affinity.
2. It inhibits kallikrein-mediated cleavage of high-molecular-weight kininogen.
3. Reduced cleavage decreases generation of bradykinin.
4. Lower bradykinin production reduces B2-receptor-mediated vascular permeability and helps limit HAE swelling during an acute attack.

Ecallantide does not replace C1-INH and does not directly antagonize the bradykinin B2 receptor. Its pharmacological target is plasma kallikrein.

## 5. Approved clinical use

In the United States, KALBITOR is indicated for treatment of acute attacks of HAE in patients 12 years of age and older.

It is an on-demand treatment for an established acute attack, not routine long-term prophylaxis. Because anaphylaxis can occur, administration must be performed by a healthcare professional with appropriate capability to manage severe hypersensitivity.

## 6. Administration and dosing

The US label describes:
- **Dose:** 30 mg total per attack.
- **Administration:** three separate 10 mg (1 mL) subcutaneous injections.
- **Sites:** each injection at a different site in the abdomen, thigh or upper arm.
- **Repeat dose:** an additional 30 mg may be administered within a 24-hour period if the attack persists.
- **Presentation:** 10 mg/mL solution in single-use vials.

This is a label summary for clinical professionals, not self-administration guidance. Follow the current label for preparation, injection technique, monitoring and repeat-dose criteria.

## 7. Pharmacology and disposition

- **Target engagement:** inhibition of circulating plasma kallikrein.
- **Pharmacodynamic effect:** reduced kallikrein activity and bradykinin generation.
- **Half-life:** approximately 2 hours in label-described pharmacokinetic analyses; verify the precise estimate and population from the current label.
- **Metabolism:** as a small recombinant protein, expected to undergo proteolytic catabolism to peptides and amino acids.
- **Immunogenicity:** anti-ecallantide antibodies, including neutralizing antibodies, have been observed; assess current label data for incidence and clinical relevance.

Do not infer that rapid plasma clearance means the clinical attack response is necessarily short-lived; clinical effect and PK are not interchangeable endpoints.

## 8. Clinical evidence

The FDA approval programme included randomized, placebo-controlled trials of acute HAE attacks. The EDEMA programme evaluated symptom improvement using patient-reported symptom-complexity scores and treatment response over the early post-dose period.

## 9. Safety and precautions

**Boxed warning:** anaphylaxis has occurred after KALBITOR administration. Because anaphylaxis can be difficult to distinguish from an HAE attack, administration should be performed by a healthcare professional with appropriate medical support available.

Other reported adverse reactions include headache, nausea, diarrhoea, pyrexia, injection-site reactions and upper respiratory symptoms; consult current label tables for exact frequencies.

Additional considerations:
- contraindicated in patients with known clinical hypersensitivity to ecallantide;
- monitor for hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis after administration;
- anti-drug antibodies may occur;
- use only for acute HAE attacks in the labelled age group and setting;
- review pregnancy/lactation and paediatric information in the current label.

## 11. Formulation, manufacturing and quality

KALBITOR is a sterile, preservative-free solution for subcutaneous injection, supplied at 10 mg/mL in single-use vials. Ecallantide is produced recombinantly in *Pichia pastoris*. Critical quality attributes include amino-acid sequence and disulfide mapping, higher-order structure, potency in kallikrein inhibition assays, host-cell proteins and DNA, process-related impurities, aggregates, sterility, endotoxin and container-closure integrity.

Because it is a recombinant protein, it should be assessed using biologic product quality methods, not only small synthetic peptide purity testing.
