## 2. Molecular architecture and sequence

Efpeglenatide is an exendin-4-derived GLP-1 receptor agonist engineered for prolonged exposure by linking the peptide component to a human IgG4 Fc fragment through a linker. The Fc domain increases molecular size and is intended to extend systemic residence time.

This differs from albiglutide, which is a tandem GLP-1–human albumin fusion. Do not substitute the sequence of exenatide, dulaglutide or another Fc-fusion biologic.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Efpeglenatide acts as a GLP-1 receptor agonist. GLP-1R is a class B G-protein-coupled receptor involved in glucose homeostasis. GLP-1R activation is associated with glucose-dependent enhancement of pancreatic beta-cell insulin secretion, reduced inappropriate glucagon secretion when glucose is elevated, delayed gastric emptying and effects on appetite/energy intake.

For an evidence-grade mechanism record, add efpeglenatide-specific GLP-1R binding affinity, functional potency, cAMP assay, pathway-bias assays, species cross-reactivity and assay provenance. Do not present general GLP-1 class biology as if every effect had been independently quantified for efpeglenatide.

## 5. Prolonged-action design

The Fc-fusion domain increases molecular size and is intended to extend exposure through FcRn-mediated recycling and reduced renal filtration relative to a small peptide.
