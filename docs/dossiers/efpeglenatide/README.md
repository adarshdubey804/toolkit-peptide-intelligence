## 2. Molecular architecture and sequence

Efpeglenatide is an exendin-4-derived GLP-1 receptor agonist engineered for prolonged exposure through an Fc-fusion approach. The peptide component is linked to a human IgG4 Fc fragment using a linker. The Fc portion increases molecular size and extends systemic residence time through Fc-related pharmacokinetic recycling mechanisms.

The precise mature peptide sequence, linker sequence, Fc sequence, disulfide connectivity, glycosylation pattern and exact molecular mass must be taken from a validated primary patent, regulatory/CMC record or sponsor characterization document. Do not substitute the sequence of exenatide, dulaglutide or another Fc-fusion biologic. The architecture is not equivalent to albiglutide: albiglutide is a tandem GLP-1–albumin fusion, whereas efpeglenatide is an exendin-4-derived peptide linked to an IgG4 Fc domain.

**Sequence status:** not yet canonicalised. No unverified full sequence is reproduced in this dossier. A sequence import must identify the mature chain boundaries and any linker cleavage/processing explicitly.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Efpeglenatide acts as a GLP-1 receptor agonist. GLP-1R is a class B G-protein-coupled receptor involved in glucose homeostasis.

Expected label/trial-described GLP-1 pharmacology includes:
- glucose-dependent enhancement of pancreatic beta-cell insulin secretion;
- reduction of inappropriate glucagon secretion when glucose is elevated;
- delayed gastric emptying;
- reduction in fasting and postprandial glucose;
- appetite/energy-intake effects associated with GLP-1 receptor agonism.

For a final evidence-grade mechanism record, add efpeglenatide-specific GLP-1R binding affinity, functional potency, cAMP assay, beta-arrestin or other pathway-bias assays, species cross-reactivity and assay provenance. Do not present general GLP-1 class biology as if each effect had been independently quantified for efpeglenatide.

## 5. Molecular basis of prolonged action

The Fc-fusion domain increases molecular size and is intended to extend exposure through FcRn-mediated recycling and reduced renal filtration relative to a small peptide. The linker and fusion architecture maintain the GLP-1-derived agonist domain while extending its circulating lifetime.

The exact contribution of FcRn binding, Fc engineering mutations, proteolytic stability and linker design should be sourced from primary development papers or patent/CMC records. Do not infer a precise half-life solely from the Fc-fusion design.
