## 4. Mechanism of action

Eloralintide is a selective, long-acting amylin receptor agonist. Amylin receptors are receptor complexes formed by the calcitonin receptor (CTR) with receptor activity-modifying proteins (RAMPs). The recognised amylin receptor subtypes include AMY1R, AMY2R and AMY3R.

The 2025 discovery/translational paper used cells expressing human AMY1R, AMY3R or CTR and reported:
- approximately 12-fold greater potency at human AMY1R than human CTR in cAMP activation assays;
- approximately 11-fold greater potency at human AMY1R than human AMY3R;
- full agonism (Emax greater than 80%) at the tested human receptor systems;
- approximately 8-fold greater binding affinity for human AMY1R than for human CTR or AMY3R in the reported ligand-binding assays.

Reported human cAMP EC50 values:
- AMY1R: 23.9 pM;
- CTR: 291.0 pM;
- AMY3R: 253.8 pM.

These values are specific to the paper's in-vitro assay system and must be stored with species, cell system, receptor composition, endpoint and assay conditions.

Proposed physiological effects of amylin receptor activation include:
1. central satiation and appetite signalling;
2. reduced food intake and meal size;
3. modulation of gastric emptying;
4. downstream reduction in body weight, primarily through reduced fat mass in the reported preclinical models.

Do not describe eloralintide as a GLP-1, GIP or glucagon receptor agonist. Its clinical programme also studies combination with incretin medicines, but combination pharmacology must be represented separately.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

Evidence domains under investigation:
- appetite and food intake;
- body weight and body composition;
- fat-mass change;
- gastric emptying;
- glycaemic and insulin-sensitivity measures;
- tolerability and conditioned taste avoidance in preclinical models.

In diet-induced obese rats, eloralintide dose-dependently reduced food intake and body weight, with weight reduction primarily attributable to fat-mass loss. In lean rats, it induced less conditioned taste avoidance than cagrilintide in the reported comparison. These are preclinical observations and should not be translated into claims of superior human tolerability.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

The discovery paper reports favourable PK in rats and monkeys and a profile supporting once-weekly dosing in humans.

The 12-week Phase 1 multiple-ascending-dose study (NCT06143956 is a separate chronic-weight-management protocol; Phase 1 MAD evidence is published under the Phase 1 programme) reported dose-proportional exposure at week 12:
- dose-normalised geometric mean ratio for AUCτ,ss: 1.1;
- dose-normalised geometric mean ratio for Cmax: 1.0.

Complete human PK extraction remains required:

## 10. Clinical development — standalone eloralintide

### Phase 1 single-ascending-dose study — NCT05295940

A randomised, placebo-controlled, participant/investigator-blinded study evaluated single ascending doses (0.04–12 mg) in healthy participants. The discovery paper reports 48 participants in eloralintide cohorts. Nine participants reported 16 adverse events, most mild; two participants reported four gastrointestinal events, including one moderate vomiting event.

At four weeks after single doses of 4 mg and 12 mg, mean body-weight change was −2.5% and −4.4%, respectively, compared with +0.6% for placebo. These are exploratory single-dose findings and not a chronic treatment estimate.

### Phase 1 multiple-ascending-dose study

The published 12-week randomised, placebo-controlled study enrolled 100 participants with overweight or obesity, mean BMI 32.6 kg/m². Participants received once-weekly eloralintide or placebo in five multiple-ascending-dose cohorts without dose escalation within each cohort.

The most common treatment-emergent adverse events with eloralintide were decreased appetite (19%), headache (12%), fatigue (11%) and COVID-19 (11%). See the complete paper for cohort-level PK, PD, weight and safety details.

### Phase 2 48-week obesity study — NCT06230523

A 48-week multicentre, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled study enrolled 263 adults with overweight/obesity without type 2 diabetes. The primary endpoint was percentage change in body weight from baseline at week 48.

Published efficacy-estimand mean body-weight changes:
- 1 mg: −9% (95% CI −12.6 to −6.3);
- 3 mg: −12% (95% CI −14.9 to −9.8);
- 6 mg: −18% (95% CI −20.7 to −14.5);
- 9 mg: −20% (95% CI −22.7 to −17.5);
- 6-to-9 mg: −20% (95% CI −22.7 to −17.0);
- 3-to-9 mg: −16% (95% CI −18.6 to −14.1);
- placebo: −0.4% (95% CI −2.2 to 1.4).

The paper reports nausea and fatigue as common adverse events, with frequencies varying by dose/cohort. For the exact cohort percentages and full adverse-event profile, use the primary publication tables. Preserve the efficacy estimand and confidence intervals; do not report these figures as a universal expected individual response.

### Phase 2 chronic weight-management master protocol — NCT06143956

A separate Phase 2 chronic weight-management master protocol is listed in the trial registry. It must not be conflated with the completed 48-week NCT06230523 study. Capture its individual cohorts, protocol amendments, status and endpoints separately.

### Phase 3 — ENLIGHTEN programme

## 11. Combination development — EloraTZP (separate entity/evidence)

EloraTZP is a combination of eloralintide and tirzepatide. It is not a single hybrid peptide and must be stored as a combination product/programme with two distinct active ingredients.

A Phase 2b study in 367 adults with obesity/overweight and type 2 diabetes (NCT06603571) evaluated eloralintide and tirzepatide alone and in combination. Lilly announced positive topline results on 30 September 2026, reporting up to 23.3% mean weight reduction and up to 2.9 percentage-point HbA1c reduction in combination arms versus tirzepatide 15 mg. These are sponsor-reported combination results; do not attribute them to eloralintide monotherapy.

Lilly announced plans to initiate Phase 3 EloraTZP trials in Q4 2026. The AMPLIFY-1 and AMPLIFY-2 trial records should be tracked as combination trials and not included in the standalone eloralintide dossier efficacy summary.
