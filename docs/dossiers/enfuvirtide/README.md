# Dossier 082 — Enfuvirtide (Fuzeon)

## Identity and chemistry
Enfuvirtide is a synthetic 36-amino-acid peptide derived from the HR2 region of HIV-1 gp41. It is a fusion inhibitor used in combination antiretroviral therapy for HIV-1 infection in treatment-experienced patients with evidence of HIV-1 replication and resistance to multiple antiretroviral agents. Brand: Fuzeon. It is not a vaccine or a reverse-transcriptase inhibitor.

Sequence (36 aa): **YTSLIHSLIEESQNQQEKNEQELLELDKWASLWNWF**. It is a linear peptide without disulfide bonds. Molecular formula and mass must be recorded from the current product chemistry source, distinguishing free peptide from acetate salt and hydration state.

## Mechanism
HIV-1 gp120 binds CD4 and a co-receptor (CCR5 or CXCR4), triggering gp41 conformational rearrangement. HR1 and HR2 regions of gp41 interact to form a six-helix bundle that drives fusion of viral and host membranes. Enfuvirtide binds the HR1 region and prevents HR2-mediated six-helix-bundle formation, blocking membrane fusion and viral entry. It acts extracellularly before viral entry and has no direct effect on reverse transcriptase, integrase or protease.

## Pharmacodynamics and resistance
Enfuvirtide susceptibility is affected by substitutions in the gp41 HR1 region, particularly around amino acids 36–45. Resistance can emerge rapidly with ongoing viral replication under drug pressure. Baseline and on-treatment resistance interpretation should use validated HIV genotypic/phenotypic testing and current treatment guidelines. Enfuvirtide must be used as part of an active combination regimen, not as monotherapy.

## Pharmacokinetics
Administered subcutaneously, generally twice daily under the approved label. Absorption is relatively slow; plasma protein binding is high (approximately 92%). The elimination half-life is approximately 3.8 hours. It is catabolized to constituent amino acids and small peptides; CYP-mediated metabolism is not a major pathway. Renal and hepatic impairment data are limited; consult current label.

## Clinical evidence
The TORO 1 and TORO 2 phase 3 studies evaluated enfuvirtide added to optimized background therapy in heavily treatment-experienced adults. At 24 weeks, enfuvirtide plus optimized background therapy improved virologic and immunologic outcomes compared with optimized background therapy alone. The benefit depends strongly on the activity of the background regimen. The trials preceded many current antiretroviral options, so present-day positioning is guided by resistance profile, treatment history and guidelines.

## Safety
Injection-site reactions are very common and include pain, erythema, induration, nodules and cysts. Other concerns include hypersensitivity reactions and an increased incidence of bacterial pneumonia reported in clinical trials. Counsel on symptoms requiring urgent evaluation using the current label. Avoid interpreting injection-site events as proof of systemic allergy, but investigate serious hypersensitivity.

## Formulation and quality
Fuzeon is supplied as lyophilized powder for reconstitution for subcutaneous injection. Critical attributes include sequence identity, peptide purity, potency, related substances, oxidation/degradation, residual solvents, reconstitution characteristics, sterility, endotoxin and vial/container integrity. The peptide's hydrophobic sequence and aggregation propensity are relevant formulation challenges.

## Regulatory status
FDA approved March 2003 for use in combination with other antiretroviral medicinal products in HIV-1-infected patients who have received treatment and have evidence of HIV-1 replication despite ongoing antiretroviral therapy. Check current label for exact wording and product availability.
