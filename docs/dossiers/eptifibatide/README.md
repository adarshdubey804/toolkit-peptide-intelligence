## 2. Chemistry, sequence, and molecular identity
Eptifibatide is a cyclic heptapeptide composed of six amino-acid residues and one mercaptopropionyl (des-amino-cysteinyl) residue. The FDA label describes it chemically as N⁶-(aminoiminomethyl)-N²-(3-mercapto-1-oxopropyl)-L-lysylglycyl-L-α-aspartyl-L-tryptophyl-L-prolyl-L-cysteinamide, cyclic (1→6)-disulfide. The disulfide bridge links the cysteine amide and mercaptopropionyl moieties. This unusual non-proteinogenic residue and cyclic connectivity are essential identity features; do not represent it as an ordinary linear heptapeptide.

Do not assign a definitive molecular formula, exact mass, stereochemical structure, salt form, or sequence unless it has been checked against an authoritative product label, pharmacopoeial monograph, or primary structural publication. For mixtures and biologics, record heterogeneity and product-specific composition rather than forcing a single sequence.

## 3. Mechanism of action
A cyclic RGD-mimetic platelet glycoprotein IIb/IIIa (integrin αIIbβ3) antagonist. It binds the activated platelet receptor and competitively prevents fibrinogen, von Willebrand factor, and other adhesive ligands from bridging adjacent platelets. This blocks the final common pathway of platelet aggregation. Inhibition is concentration-dependent and reversible after the infusion is stopped as eptifibatide dissociates from the receptor. It does not directly dissolve an existing thrombus and is not a fibrinolytic.

Mechanistic confidence should be separated into (a) established target engagement, (b) downstream pharmacology, and (c) hypotheses that remain incompletely established in humans. Do not infer clinical benefit solely from in-vitro target activity.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics
After IV administration, the plasma elimination half-life is approximately 2.5 hours. Plasma protein binding is about 25%; clearance in coronary artery disease patients is approximately 55 mL/kg/hour. Renal clearance accounts for about half of total clearance in subjects with normal renal function. Most urinary drug-related material is unchanged eptifibatide, deamidated eptifibatide, and more polar metabolites; major plasma metabolites were not identified in the cited label. In patients with creatinine clearance below 50 mL/min, total clearance is reduced by approximately 50% and steady-state concentrations approximately double. Dialysis-dependent patients were not adequately studied. These values are label-derived and formulation/jurisdiction-specific; recheck against the latest local label.

## 15. Quantitative pharmacology and clinical evidence addendum

### Platelet pharmacodynamics
The approved label describes dose- and concentration-dependent inhibition of ex-vivo platelet aggregation after IV administration. Platelet inhibition is reversible after stopping the infusion. The short plasma half-life does not mean platelet function instantly normalizes; recovery is governed by drug dissociation and clearance. Quantitative inhibition estimates should be tied to the assay, sampling time, and clinical regimen in the cited study rather than treated as a universal constant.

### PURSUIT trial
The PURSUIT investigators studied eptifibatide in 10,948 patients with acute coronary syndromes without persistent ST-segment elevation. The trial evaluated eptifibatide as an adjunct to standard therapy, including aspirin and heparin, and demonstrated a reduction in the 30-day composite of death or nonfatal myocardial infarction in the overall study population. Interpretation should retain the trial's era-specific background antithrombotic therapy and distinguish the overall result from subgroup findings. Primary publication: *New England Journal of Medicine* (1998), DOI 10.1056/NEJM199808133390704; PMID 9705684.

### ESPRIT trial
ESPRIT evaluated adjunctive eptifibatide during nonurgent coronary stent implantation. In the 2,064-patient randomized study, the 48-hour composite endpoint of death, myocardial infarction, urgent target-vessel revascularization, or thrombotic bailout was reduced compared with placebo. The benefit was driven predominantly by fewer periprocedural myocardial infarctions. Follow-up publication reported a 6-month death-or-MI rate of 7.5% with eptifibatide versus 11.5% with placebo (HR 0.63; 95% CI 0.47–0.84). These results apply to the studied PCI context and should not be generalized to routine upstream use in all contemporary ACS patients. Primary trial publication: *JAMA* (2001), DOI 10.1001/jama.285.19.2468; PMID 11368699.

### Safety signal from pivotal trials
Bleeding is the principal pharmacodynamic hazard. A current DailyMed label's pooled trial tables report higher major and minor bleeding with eptifibatide than placebo in PURSUIT and ESPRIT; event definitions and populations differ between trials. Thrombocytopenia, including acute profound thrombocytopenia, is a recognized serious risk and warrants platelet-count monitoring according to the label. Avoid quoting a single bleeding rate without naming the trial, definition, and denominator.

## 16. Structure–activity and target context
- The cyclic disulfide-constrained scaffold presents a ligand-mimetic pharmacophore to αIIbβ3.
- The arginine-mimetic guanidino functionality and acidic residue contribute to integrin recognition; the exact three-dimensional conformation is relevant.
- The target is platelet integrin αIIbβ3, encoded by ITGA2B and ITGB3. It is a calcium-dependent adhesion receptor whose ligand binding enables platelet cross-linking.
- The mechanism is distinct from P2Y12 receptor antagonism (e.g., clopidogrel) and cyclooxygenase-1 inhibition (aspirin); combined antiplatelet effects increase bleeding risk.

## 17. Contraindication and monitoring themes
The current product label must be consulted for the complete list. Key label-based risk domains include active or recent clinically significant bleeding, prior haemorrhagic stroke or recent stroke, intracranial lesions, recent major surgery/trauma, thrombocytopenia, severe uncontrolled hypertension, severe renal impairment, and concomitant use of another parenteral GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor. Renal function affects exposure. Clinical monitoring focuses on bleeding, vascular access sites, haemoglobin/haematocrit, platelet count, and renal function as directed by the label and institutional protocol. This dossier intentionally does not provide a dosing or administration protocol.
