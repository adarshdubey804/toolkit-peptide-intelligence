## 2. Peptide identity and sequence

Exenatide is a synthetic 39-amino-acid peptide corresponding to exendin-4, originally identified in the saliva of the Gila monster (*Heloderma suspectum*). It shares approximately 53% sequence identity with human GLP-1 and is resistant to DPP-4 degradation.

**Sequence:**

`HGEGTFTSDLSKQMEEEAVRLFIEWLKNGGPSSGAPPPS-NH2`

- **Length:** 39 amino acids.
- **N-terminus:** histidine.
- **C-terminus:** amidated.
- **Disulfide bond:** Cys1–Cys7.
- **Lipid conjugation:** none.
- **Fc fusion:** none.
- **Formulation depot:** peptide is physically encapsulated in PLGA microspheres; it is not covalently attached to the polymer.

Sequence and terminal chemistry should be verified against the current FDA label/official substance record before canonical import.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Exenatide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. It binds and activates GLP-1R, a class B G-protein-coupled receptor involved in glucose regulation.

Label-described effects include:
- glucose-dependent enhancement of insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells;
- suppression of inappropriate glucagon secretion during hyperglycaemia;
- slowing of gastric emptying;
- reduced food intake and appetite;
- improvement in fasting and postprandial glucose.

Insulin secretion decreases as glucose approaches normal, reducing intrinsic hypoglycaemia risk when used alone. Hypoglycaemia risk increases when exenatide is used with insulin or sulfonylureas.

## 5. Extended-release formulation and delivery technology

### PLGA microsphere depot

Bydureon extended-release uses exenatide encapsulated in biodegradable poly(D,L-lactide-co-glycolide) microspheres. Following subcutaneous injection:
1. microspheres form a local depot in subcutaneous tissue;
2. water penetrates the polymer matrix;
3. exenatide is released through diffusion and polymer erosion/degradation;
4. PLGA degrades into lactic acid and glycolic acid, which enter normal metabolic pathways.

The release profile is multi-phase and should not be simplified to a single constant-rate release mechanism. Initial release, diffusion, polymer hydration and erosion contribute at different times.

### Important distinction from immediate-release Byetta

- **Byetta:** immediate-release exenatide solution administered twice daily.
- **Bydureon:** once-weekly exenatide in PLGA microspheres.
- **Bydureon BCise:** once-weekly microsphere formulation in a ready-to-use autoinjector presentation.

Do not transfer the immediate-release product's PK or administration instructions to the extended-release product.

## 6. Pharmacodynamics

Extended-release exenatide produces sustained GLP-1R agonism and improves fasting and postprandial glucose. The prolonged release profile supports once-weekly administration.

The exact magnitude of HbA1c reduction, fasting glucose change, weight change and hypoglycaemia must be captured from product-specific DURATION/EXSCEL or other trial records, with dose, population, comparator and follow-up. Do not merge Byetta and Bydureon outcomes without identifying formulation.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics

For extended-release exenatide, absorption is governed by microsphere release. The label describes a multi-phase concentration-time profile with gradual increases over the first weeks after initiation and steady state after repeated weekly dosing.

Current product-specific label values to extract and verify:
- concentration-time profile and time to steady state;
- Cmax and average concentration at steady state;
- apparent half-life/terminal decline after discontinuation;
- accumulation ratio;
- distribution and clearance;
- renal elimination of exenatide after release;
- effect of renal impairment.

**Do not quote the short half-life of immediate-release exenatide as the terminal half-life of the extended-release formulation.** The apparent prolonged exposure is controlled by release from microspheres (flip-flop kinetics), not simply by changing the intrinsic peptide half-life.

## 8. Manufacturing and formulation composition

The extended-release drug product contains exenatide and PLGA microspheres. Exact polymer composition, molecular weight, lactide:glycolide ratio, particle size distribution, encapsulation efficiency, residual solvents and release specifications require the approved CMC source.

Bydureon presentations and reconstitution systems differ:
- original Bydureon used a kit requiring mixing/reconstitution of microsphere powder and diluent;
- Bydureon BCise is a ready-to-use autoinjector with a suspension formulation.

The BCise formulation includes exenatide microspheres, medium-chain triglycerides, mannitol, polysorbate 20 and water for injection as described in the US label. Verify exact quantities and label revision. Do not transfer the BCise excipient list to the original kit.

Product-specific instructions include suspension handling, injection site rotation, device preparation, missed-dose handling and storage.

## 11. Safety profile

Product label warnings and precautions include:
- thyroid C-cell tumour warning in US labelling and contraindication for personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN2;
- pancreatitis;
- hypoglycaemia with concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea;
- acute kidney injury and renal impairment considerations;
- gastrointestinal adverse reactions;
- injection-site nodules and reactions, including rare abscess/cellulitis or necrosis reports;
- hypersensitivity reactions;
- immunogenicity and anti-exenatide antibodies.

The exact warning language differs between label revisions and jurisdictions. Do not transfer boxed warnings or contraindications from other GLP-1 medicines without checking the exenatide label.

## 12. Drug interactions

- **Warfarin:** postmarketing reports of increased INR with exenatide; monitor INR when initiating or changing therapy as stated in applicable label.
- **Oral medicines:** delayed gastric emptying may affect absorption and time to peak concentration.
- **Insulin/sulfonylureas:** increased hypoglycaemia risk; label may recommend reducing concomitant therapy.
- **Renal impairment:** assess product-specific use restrictions and risk associated with dehydration.

A full interaction matrix remains pending with drug, mechanism, clinical effect, management, formulation, label revision and evidence.

## 13. Clinical evidence

Key product-specific evidence includes:
- **DURATION programme:** glycaemic efficacy and safety of once-weekly exenatide extended-release across monotherapy and add-on settings.
- **DURATION-1:** once-weekly extended-release versus twice-daily exenatide.
- **DURATION-5:** once-weekly versus twice-daily exenatide.
- **EXSCEL:** cardiovascular outcomes trial of once-weekly exenatide; primary MACE non-inferiority was demonstrated, but superiority was not established in the primary analysis.

For each trial capture registry ID, DOI/PMID, population, formulation, dose, comparator, duration, HbA1c/weight endpoints, MACE definitions, effect estimates, confidence intervals and adverse events. Do not conflate immediate-release and extended-release arms.
