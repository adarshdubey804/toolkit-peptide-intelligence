# Exenatide (Byetta / Bydureon) — dossier 008

**Curation status:** `in_progress`

**Reference products:** Byetta (immediate-release exenatide injection); Bydureon / Bydureon BCise (extended-release exenatide formulations).  
**Scope warning:** immediate-release and extended-release products have different formulations, administration schedules, pharmacokinetics, device instructions, and label details. Never merge them into one undifferentiated product record.

## 1. Identity and nomenclature

- **INN:** exenatide.
- **Drug class:** glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist.
- **Molecular origin:** synthetic version of exendin-4, a peptide originally identified in the saliva of the Gila monster (*Heloderma suspectum*).
- **Peptide length:** 39 amino acids.
- **Primary target:** GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R).
- **US approval history:** Byetta approved in 2005; Bydureon extended-release formulation approved in 2012; Bydureon BCise approved in 2017.

## 2. Sequence and molecular design

Exenatide is a 39-residue peptide with sequence:

`HGEGTFTSDLSKQMEEEAVRLFIEWLKNGGPSSGAPPPS-NH2`

It shares substantial sequence similarity with mammalian GLP-1 but is not identical to human GLP-1. Its N-terminus is resistant to rapid degradation by dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4), contributing to a longer biological action than native GLP-1.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Exenatide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. GLP-1R activation produces glucose-dependent effects:
1. increases glucose-dependent insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells;
2. suppresses inappropriately elevated glucagon secretion;
3. slows gastric emptying; and
4. increases satiety, which can reduce food intake.

Because insulin secretion is glucose-dependent, the hypoglycaemia risk is generally lower when exenatide is used alone than when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea. The label warns that hypoglycaemia risk can increase with those concomitant therapies.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- Enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion.
- Suppresses inappropriate glucagon secretion.
- Slows gastric emptying.
- Reduces postprandial glucose excursions.
- May reduce appetite and body weight.
- Effects are mediated through GLP-1R; it is not an insulin analogue.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

### Immediate-release Byetta

The US label reports:
- subcutaneous peak plasma concentration generally reached in approximately 2 hours;
- apparent volume of distribution approximately 28.3 L;
- apparent clearance approximately 9.1 L/hour;
- elimination half-life approximately 2.4 hours;
- renal elimination is an important route.

### Extended-release Bydureon

The extended-release formulation uses exenatide encapsulated in biodegradable poly(D,L-lactide-co-glycolide) (PLG/PLGA) microspheres. Its release profile and PK are distinct from Byetta. The extended-release label describes an initial release followed by gradual release from microspheres and steady-state concentrations after repeated weekly dosing. Do not transfer immediate-release PK parameters to extended-release products.

## 7. Manufacturing and formulation

- **Active substance:** synthetic exenatide peptide.
- **Byetta:** immediate-release sterile solution for subcutaneous injection.
- **Bydureon:** extended-release exenatide in biodegradable PLG microspheres, supplied as a suspension/reconstitution product in the original presentation.
- **Bydureon BCise:** extended-release autoinjector presentation.
- Product-specific excipients, reconstitution, storage, device instructions, and in-use stability must be sourced from the exact current label.

## 8. Regulatory history and indications

- **Byetta:** FDA approval in 2005 as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes.
- **Bydureon:** FDA approval in 2012 for extended-release exenatide.
- **Bydureon BCise:** FDA approval in 2017.
- Indications and age ranges differ by product and label revision. Confirm the current prescribing information before displaying a unified indication.
- Approval history is not equivalent to current commercial availability. Verify marketed status separately by country and presentation.

## 9. Administration and handling

Byetta and extended-release products have different administration schedules and preparation steps. Byetta is administered before meals; extended-release products are administered weekly according to their own instructions. Do not interchange devices, preparation methods, or dose schedules. This dossier is scientific documentation, not a dosing recommendation.

## 10. Safety and precautions

Important product-label safety issues include:
- nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and other gastrointestinal effects;
- hypoglycaemia, especially with concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea;
- acute pancreatitis warning;
- acute kidney injury or worsening renal function, particularly in the setting of dehydration;
- hypersensitivity reactions;
- injection-site reactions and nodules, particularly with extended-release microsphere products;
- product-specific thyroid C-cell tumour warning/contraindication language for extended-release exenatide products, which must be quoted from the applicable current label rather than generalized across all formulations.

The complete contraindications, adverse-event frequencies, warnings, pregnancy/lactation data, paediatric evidence, and post-marketing reports remain to be extracted for each formulation separately.

## 11. Drug interactions

Delayed gastric emptying may affect the rate of absorption of concomitant oral medicines. Hypoglycaemia risk may increase with insulin or insulin secretagogues.
