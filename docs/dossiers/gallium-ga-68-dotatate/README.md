# Dossier 037 — Gallium Ga 68 dotatate (NETSPOT)

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Evidence cut-off:** 3 November 2026  
**Brand:** NETSPOT  
**Generic / nonproprietary name:** gallium Ga 68 dotatate; also called gallium (68Ga) oxodotreotide  
**Therapeutic category:** Diagnostic & imaging  
**Molecule class:** Radiolabelled cyclic somatostatin analogue peptide; PET radiopharmaceutical  
**Regulatory distinction:** Diagnostic imaging agent, not an antineoplastic treatment and not interchangeable with therapeutic lutetium-177 dotatate.

## 1. Executive identity

Gallium Ga 68 dotatate is a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical used with PET to localise somatostatin-receptor-positive neuroendocrine tumours (NETs) in adults and paediatric patients. NETSPOT is supplied as a kit containing the peptide-chelator precursor (dotatate) and reaction buffer. The peptide is labelled with gallium-68 obtained from an authorised germanium-68/gallium-68 generator before administration.

The biologically targeting component is dotatate, a cyclic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin. The radionuclide provides the PET signal; the peptide directs the radiotracer to somatostatin receptors.

## 3. Molecular identity and peptide sequence

The FDA prescribing information describes dotatate (DOTA-0-Tyr3-octreotate) as a cyclic eight-amino-acid peptide with a covalently attached DOTA chelator and one disulfide bond.

**Sequence:** H-D-Phe-Cys-Tyr-D-Trp-Lys-Thr-Cys-Thr-OH  
**Cyclisation:** disulfide bridge between Cys residues at positions 2 and 7 in the label's numbering.  
**Chelator:** DOTA, covalently attached to the peptide N-terminus.  
**Peptide molecular weight:** 1,435.6 Da (unlabelled dotatate precursor, as stated in US label).

The complete chemical name in the label identifies the DOTA-bearing D-phenylalanyl-L-cysteinyl-L-tyrosyl-D-tryptophanyl-L-lysyl-L-threoninyl-L-cysteinyl-L-threonine cyclic disulfide structure. The molecular weight above is for dotatate, not the radioactive gallium-68 complex.

## 4. Chemistry and radiochemistry

- DOTA is a macrocyclic polyaminocarboxylate chelator that coordinates gallium-68.
- The kit's lyophilised reaction vial contains 40 micrograms dotatate, 5 micrograms 1,10-phenanthroline, 6 micrograms gentisic acid and 20 mg mannitol.
- A separate buffer vial contains formic acid, sodium hydroxide and water for injection.
- After radiolabelling and pH adjustment, the preparation is a sterile, pyrogen-free, clear, colourless buffered solution; the US label specifies pH 3.2–3.8.
- Gallium-68 is obtained from a germanium-68/gallium-68 generator; the generator is not included in the NETSPOT kit.
- Radiochemical identity, purity, pH, activity concentration, residual metals, sterility and endotoxin are critical release/quality attributes.

Do not confuse the kit precursor with the final radiolabelled drug product.

## 5. Mechanism of action

1. Dotatate is an analogue of somatostatin with high affinity for somatostatin receptors, particularly SSTR2.
2. After intravenous administration, the radiotracer binds to somatostatin-receptor-expressing cells and is internalised to a degree dependent on receptor biology.
3. Gallium-68 emits positrons. Positron annihilation produces pairs of 511-keV photons detected by PET scanners.
4. PET reconstruction maps the distribution of radiotracer uptake, helping localise receptor-positive NETs and assess disease distribution.

This is a **target-binding diagnostic mechanism**, not a therapeutic cytotoxic mechanism. The agent does not deliver a therapeutic radiation dose intended to ablate tumour tissue. Therapeutic radioligands such as lutetium-177 dotatate use a different radionuclide and treatment intent.

## 6. Receptor pharmacology and selectivity

- The target family is somatostatin receptors SSTR1–SSTR5.
- Dotatate has particularly strong affinity for SSTR2, which is commonly expressed in well-differentiated gastroenteropancreatic NETs.
- Uptake is not absolutely tumour-specific: physiologic uptake and uptake in other SSTR-expressing tissues or non-NET pathologies can occur.
- The FDA label warns that uptake may occur in tumour types other than NETs, neural-crest-derived lesions, splenosis and other pathologic conditions; physiologic uptake may also occur, including in the pancreatic uncinate process.
- Imaging must be interpreted with anatomy, clinical history, other imaging and pathology where indicated.

Do not state that a positive scan alone proves histological NET diagnosis.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics and biodistribution

The radiotracer is administered intravenously and distributes to receptor-expressing tissues. The radiolabel's physical half-life is approximately 67.7 minutes. Elimination is substantially renal, with urinary excretion contributing to radiation exposure.

Clinical interpretation:
- Hydration and frequent voiding during the first hours after administration reduce radiation exposure.
- Renal function and urinary tract activity affect biodistribution and dosimetry.
- Physiologic uptake can occur in spleen, kidneys, adrenal glands, pituitary, liver, bowel and other tissues; refer to the current label and nuclear-medicine references for full biodistribution.
- Do not substitute the peptide precursor molecular weight for the mass of the administered radiopharmaceutical or confuse physical half-life with biological clearance.

## 8. Approved indication and clinical use

The FDA-approved indication is use, after radiolabelling with gallium-68, with PET for localisation of somatostatin-receptor-positive neuroendocrine tumours in adult and paediatric patients.

Typical clinical contexts include:
- localisation and staging of receptor-positive NETs;
- evaluation of suspected NET lesions where somatostatin-receptor imaging is appropriate;
- assessment of disease distribution and receptor expression;
- selection/evaluation in the broader pathway of somatostatin-receptor-targeted management, when interpreted within clinical guidelines.

The precise clinical question and scan protocol are determined by the nuclear-medicine team and current product information.

## 9. Administration and preparation

The US label recommends 2 MBq/kg (0.054 mCi/kg) up to a maximum of 200 MBq (5.4 mCi), administered as an intravenous bolus, in adults and paediatric patients. The injected activity is verified using a dose calibrator.

Important preparation principles from the label:
- prepare aseptically and follow the validated kit instructions for the specified generator;
- use appropriate shielding and radiation-safety controls;
- verify final activity and radiochemical quality before release;
- hydrate the patient and encourage frequent voiding during the first hours;
- restrict close contact with infants and pregnant people for 12 hours after administration, as directed by the label.

This is a dossier summary, not a radiopharmacy compounding procedure. Preparation must be performed by trained, authorised personnel under the current official instructions.

## 10. Safety, warnings and precautions

- **Radiation exposure:** gallium-68 contributes to cumulative radiation exposure. Apply ALARA principles and appropriate radiation protection.
- **Hypersensitivity:** reported reactions include rash and pruritus; most reported reactions were reversible spontaneously or with routine symptomatic management.
- **Image misinterpretation:** uptake can occur in non-NET tumours, neural crest-derived tumours, splenosis, other pathology and normal physiologic variants.
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** assess according to current radiopharmaceutical precautions and the official label; do not assume absence of foetal exposure.
- **Renal/urinary considerations:** hydration and voiding reduce bladder and whole-body radiation exposure.
- **Paediatric use:** FDA indication includes paediatric patients; activity is weight-based with a maximum cap.

No therapeutic adverse-event profile should be inferred from the diagnostic radiotracer.

## 11. Drug interactions and imaging confounders

The FDA label does not establish a conventional pharmacokinetic drug-interaction profile comparable to small-molecule therapeutics. However:
- somatostatin analogues may alter receptor availability or tracer uptake; timing relative to long-acting and short-acting analogues should follow current institutional protocols and applicable label/guideline advice;
- recent therapies and physiologic changes may affect uptake patterns;
- glucose, renal function and other patient factors may affect image quality or interpretation depending on protocol.

Specific withholding intervals should not be invented; verify current nuclear-medicine guidance and the patient's treatment context.

## 12. Clinical evidence and diagnostic performance

The FDA approval was supported by clinical data evaluating localisation of somatostatin-receptor-positive NETs. The product is a diagnostic imaging agent; conventional oncology response endpoints such as objective response rate or progression-free survival are not appropriate efficacy measures for the radiotracer itself.

Evidence appraisal should distinguish:
- lesion-level sensitivity/specificity from patient-level diagnostic accuracy;
- histopathology-confirmed subsets from composite reference standards;
- reader agreement and image quality from clinical outcome benefit;
- diagnostic performance in different primary sites and tumour grades.

The exact pivotal-study tables, denominators and confidence intervals should be transcribed from the full FDA multidisciplinary review before quantitative claims are added.

## 13. Formulation and product presentation

NETSPOT is supplied as a two-vial kit:
- **Reaction vial:** lyophilised dotatate formulation (40 micrograms) with 1,10-phenanthroline, gentisic acid and mannitol.
- **Buffer vial:** formic acid/sodium hydroxide buffer solution.
- **After radiolabelling:** gallium Ga 68 dotatate injection, a sterile buffered solution.

The 68Ge/68Ga generator and ancillary radiopharmacy supplies are not included in the kit. The final radioactive product has a short usable period governed by gallium-68 decay and the approved preparation instructions.

## 15. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- **Target:** somatostatin receptor 2 (SSTR2), with binding across the somatostatin receptor family.
- **Ligand family:** cyclic somatostatin analogues.
- **Imaging physics:** positron emission and annihilation-photon detection by PET.
- **Disease biology:** well-differentiated NETs often express somatostatin receptors; expression is heterogeneous across tumour types, lesions and grades.
- **Interpretive limitation:** tracer uptake is a functional receptor signal and is not equivalent to direct measurement of tumour viability or histological identity.
