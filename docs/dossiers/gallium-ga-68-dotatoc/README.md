# Dossier 038 — Gallium Ga 68 edotreotide (DOTATOC)

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Evidence cut-off:** 4 November 2026  
**US product name:** Gallium Ga 68 Edotreotide Injection (also called Ga-68 DOTATOC Injection)  
**US application:** NDA 210828  
**US initial approval:** 2019 (FDA approval letter / label; Orange Book records 21 August 2019)  
**Molecule class:** DOTA-chelated cyclic octapeptide radiopharmaceutical  
**Use:** PET diagnostic imaging of somatostatin-receptor-positive neuroendocrine tumours (NETs)

> This is a diagnostic radiopharmaceutical. It is not the same product as gallium Ga 68 dotatate (NETSPOT), although the two agents share a closely related peptide sequence and SSTR-targeting purpose. It is also distinct from lutetium-177 DOTATATE, which is a therapeutic radioligand.

## 1. Identity and classification

Gallium Ga 68 edotreotide is the gallium-68 radiolabelled form of edotreotide, commonly known as DOTATOC. It is a somatostatin analogue designed to bind somatostatin receptors on NET cells. Gallium-68 provides the positron-emitting signal for PET imaging.

- **Generic name:** gallium Ga 68 edotreotide.
- **Common name:** Ga-68 DOTATOC; gallium-68 DOTA-TOC.
- **Non-radioactive peptide name:** edotreotide / DOTA-0-Phe1-Tyr3-octreotide.
- **US brand/product label:** Gallium Ga 68 Edotreotide Injection; historical local naming includes Ga-68 DOTATOC Injection.
- **US NDA:** 210828.
- **Applicant/labeler:** University of Iowa Hospitals and Clinics PET Imaging Center.
- **Route:** intravenous.
- **Dosage form:** radioactive solution for injection.
- **FDA indication:** PET localisation of somatostatin-receptor-positive NETs in adults and paediatric patients.

## 2. Peptide sequence and structural identity

DOTATOC is a cyclic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin.

**Sequence:** H-D-Phe-Cys-Tyr-D-Trp-Lys-Thr-Cys-Thr-OH  
**Cyclisation:** disulfide bridge between the two cysteine residues (Cys2–Cys7).  
**Chelator:** DOTA (1,4,7,10-tetraazacyclododecane-N,N′,N″,N‴-tetraacetic acid) attached to the N-terminal D-phenylalanine.  
**Key substitution:** phenylalanine at position 1 and tyrosine at position 3 distinguish DOTATOC from some other somatostatin radioligands.

The FDA label identifies Ga-68 DOTATOC as gallium-68 (DOTA0-Phe1-Tyr3) octreotide, a cyclic eight-amino-acid peptide with a covalently bound DOTA chelator and one disulfide bond.

The labelled radioconjugate molecular weight is not interchangeable with the unlabelled peptide precursor molecular weight. Salt, chelator and radionuclide forms must remain separately represented in the database.

## 4. Mechanism of action

1. The DOTATOC peptide component structurally mimics somatostatin and binds somatostatin receptors (SSTRs), particularly SSTR2.
2. Receptor binding concentrates the radiotracer in cells and tissues expressing the target receptor.
3. The attached DOTA chelator holds gallium-68 in a stable coordination complex.
4. Gallium-68 decays by positron emission. Positrons annihilate with electrons and generate pairs of 511-keV photons.
5. PET scanners detect these coincident photons and reconstruct a three-dimensional distribution of radiotracer uptake.

**Pharmacological role:** receptor-targeted diagnostic localisation.  
**Not the mechanism:** tumour-cell killing or therapeutic radiation delivery. The administered diagnostic activity is intended to generate an image, not to provide peptide receptor radionuclide therapy.

## 5. Receptor pharmacology

- **Primary imaging target:** somatostatin receptor 2 (SSTR2).
- **Target family:** SSTR1–SSTR5.
- **Biological context:** many well-differentiated NETs express somatostatin receptors, often with substantial SSTR2 expression.
- **Specificity limitation:** receptor expression is not exclusive to NETs. Physiologic tissues and other lesions may show uptake.
- **Clinical interpretation:** uptake intensity is a functional imaging signal, not a direct histological diagnosis or a stand-alone measure of tumour viability.

DOTATOC, DOTATATE and DOTANOC have different receptor-affinity profiles and should not be treated as interchangeable ligands in detailed pharmacology or quantitative imaging analyses.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics and biodistribution

Following intravenous injection, the tracer distributes through the circulation and binds to somatostatin-receptor-expressing tissues. Renal clearance and urinary excretion are important for the radiopharmaceutical's biodistribution and radiation exposure.

- Gallium-68 physical decay is rapid (half-life approximately 68 minutes).
- Imaging is performed within the time window specified by the approved product label; the US label recommends starting PET acquisition 55–90 minutes after injection.
- Physiologic uptake and excretion can create expected activity in organs and urinary structures.
- Hydration and frequent voiding help reduce radiation exposure to the bladder and urinary tract.
- Physical radionuclide decay must not be confused with biological elimination of the peptide.

A detailed organ dosimetry table and quantitative PK parameters should be transcribed from the current official label before being used in calculations.

## 7. Approved indication and clinical applications

The US prescribing information indicates gallium Ga 68 edotreotide for use with PET to localise somatostatin-receptor-positive neuroendocrine tumours in adult and paediatric patients.

Potential clinical contexts include:
- localisation of suspected SSTR-positive NETs;
- staging and assessment of disease distribution;
- localisation of primary lesions and metastases;
- evaluation of receptor-positive lesions where SSTR PET is clinically appropriate.

The exact scan indication, protocol and interpretation must follow the current official label and specialist guidance.

## 8. Dose and imaging procedure

According to the US prescribing information:
- **Adults:** 148 MBq (4 mCi) as a bolus intravenous injection.
- **Paediatric patients:** 1.59 MBq/kg (0.043 mCi/kg), with a minimum activity of 11.1 MBq (0.3 mCi) and maximum of 111 MBq (3 mCi), as a bolus intravenous injection.
- **Imaging window:** initiate PET imaging 55–90 minutes after administration.

The product is a ready-to-use radioactive injection supplied by an authorised PET imaging centre. Preparation, activity measurement, administration and radiation controls must be performed by trained, authorised radiopharmacy/nuclear-medicine personnel. This dossier is not a compounding or administration protocol.

## 9. Formulation and product presentation

The DailyMed listing describes a multi-dose vial containing Ga-68 DOTATOC injection in sodium chloride solution, with alcohol listed as an inactive ingredient. Labelled activity concentration is specified at end-of-synthesis and the product has a short beyond-use period.

- **NDC:** 24417-681-30.
- **Presentation:** 14 mL in one multi-dose vial.
- **Route:** intravenous.
- **Labelled concentration range:** 18.5–148 MBq/mL (0.5–4 mCi/mL) at end of synthesis.
- **Storage:** 25°C, with permitted excursions 15–30°C; upright in a lead-shielded container.
- **Expiry:** 3 hours from end of synthesis, per the listed vial label.

These details refer to the listed University of Iowa product presentation. They should not be generalised to every DOTATOC kit or local radiopharmacy preparation.

## 10. Safety and radiation precautions

- **Radiation exposure:** use the lowest activity consistent with diagnostic image quality and follow ALARA principles.
- **Hypersensitivity:** monitor for reactions to the administered product and its excipients.
- **Pregnancy:** assess the justification and potential foetal radiation exposure before administration.
- **Lactation:** follow the current label and institutional radiation-safety guidance for interruption or precautions.
- **Hydration and voiding:** encourage fluid intake and frequent urination when clinically appropriate to reduce bladder dose.
- **Close contact:** follow product-specific radiation-safety instructions for contact with infants and pregnant people after administration.
- **Image interpretation:** uptake can occur in non-NET lesions and normal tissues; correlate with clinical and anatomical findings.

The agent is a diagnostic radiotracer; do not assign it the systemic cytotoxic adverse-effect profile of therapeutic alkylators or therapeutic radioligands.

## 11. Drug interactions and imaging considerations

The product label does not establish a conventional drug-interaction profile like that of a systemically active small-molecule medicine. However, somatostatin analogues used therapeutically can affect receptor availability and tracer uptake. Timing of short-acting or long-acting somatostatin analogue therapy should follow current official product information and specialist imaging protocols. Do not invent a withholding interval.

## 12. Clinical evidence and diagnostic performance

FDA approval supports use as a diagnostic imaging agent for SSTR-positive NET localisation. Appropriate evidence endpoints include:
- patient-level and lesion-level detection;
- sensitivity and specificity against the stated reference standard;
- positive and negative predictive values in the studied population;
- reader agreement and image interpretability;
- performance by primary tumour site, grade and disease setting.

The full pivotal diagnostic-performance dataset and FDA review tables have not yet been transcribed into this dossier. No numerical sensitivity or specificity is asserted here without denominator and reference-standard context.

## 13. Manufacturing and quality control

DOTATOC is a synthetic peptide with defined stereochemistry, one disulfide bond and a DOTA chelator. The final drug product is generated by coordination of gallium-68 to DOTA.

Quality attributes to control include:
- peptide identity and amino-acid stereochemistry;
- disulfide bond integrity;
- DOTA conjugation and precursor purity;
- radiochemical identity and radiochemical purity;
- radionuclidic identity/purity and generator-related breakthrough;
- activity concentration and calibration time;
- pH, appearance and residual reagents/solvents;
- sterility, bacterial endotoxins and particulate matter;
- validated shelf-life/beyond-use time and container shielding.

No proprietary process conditions or unverified release limits are claimed.
