> Educational dossier only. This is a prescription fertility medicine administered as part of a specialist-controlled ovarian stimulation protocol, not a self-treatment guide.

## 1. Executive overview
Ganirelix acetate is a synthetic decapeptide gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) receptor antagonist. It competitively blocks pituitary GnRH receptors, rapidly suppressing LH secretion and preventing a premature LH surge during controlled ovarian stimulation for assisted reproduction. Unlike GnRH agonists such as nafarelin, it does not require an initial stimulatory flare before suppression.

The US product is ganirelix acetate injection. International brand names include Orgalutran and Fyremadel; authorization and product availability vary by country. The workbook's 1999 first-US-approval field is retained as provenance and should be reconciled against the FDA application history before treating it as the definitive first approval date.

## 3. Sequence and chemical identity
The established condensed sequence is commonly represented as:

`Ac-D-2Nal-D-4Cpa-D-3Pal-Ser-Tyr-D-hArg(Et2)-Leu-hArg(Et2)-Pro-D-Ala-NH2`

Abbreviations: Ac = N-acetyl; D-2Nal = D-3-(2-naphthyl)alanine; D-4Cpa = D-4-chlorophenylalanine; D-3Pal = D-3-(3-pyridyl)alanine; hArg(Et2) = N9,N10-diethyl-homoarginine; D-Ala = D-alanine. This is a modified decapeptide with non-proteinogenic residues and N-/C-terminal modifications.

The acetate salt is the marketed drug-substance form. Molecular formula and mass vary with the exact salt/hydrate representation; do not record an unqualified formula or molecular weight until checked against the specific US label/official substance record and its stated basis.

## 4. Mechanism of action
Ganirelix competitively antagonizes pituitary GnRH receptors:
1. It occupies GnRH receptors on anterior pituitary gonadotrophs.
2. It prevents endogenous pulsatile GnRH from activating receptor signalling.
3. Gq/11–PLC signalling and downstream gonadotropin release are inhibited.
4. LH secretion is rapidly reduced; FSH is also suppressed to a lesser extent.
5. The risk of a premature LH surge and premature luteinization during ovarian stimulation is reduced.

This is direct receptor antagonism. There is no agonist flare phase analogous to nafarelin or leuprolide. Suppression is reversible after discontinuation as drug exposure declines.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics and endocrine response
- Rapid suppression of circulating LH is the principal clinical pharmacodynamic effect.
- FSH suppression also occurs, though the magnitude and clinical contribution differ.
- The drug is used with exogenous gonadotropins; it does not replace follicular stimulation.
- Monitoring of follicular development and hormone response remains part of the assisted-reproduction protocol.
- Do not infer pregnancy or live-birth benefit from LH suppression alone; clinical outcomes depend on the entire stimulation and embryo-transfer pathway.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics
Ganirelix is administered subcutaneously and is absorbed systemically. The product label reports a terminal elimination half-life of approximately 13 hours after repeated daily dosing in healthy women; exposure and half-life should be interpreted with the labelled regimen and population. The drug is highly protein-bound. Metabolism and elimination include peptide degradation and excretion; exact mass-balance fractions should be extracted from the current label rather than inferred from related GnRH antagonists.

Renal and hepatic impairment data are limited. Avoid assuming that the pharmacokinetics of cetrorelix, degarelix, or elagolix apply to ganirelix.

## 7. Clinical evidence
Ganirelix was evaluated in controlled ovarian stimulation studies comparing GnRH-antagonist protocols with GnRH-agonist down-regulation protocols. The main efficacy objective was prevention of premature LH surges while permitting controlled follicular development. Pregnancy and live-birth rates are influenced by age, ovarian reserve, stimulation regimen, embryo quality, and clinic practice; avoid quoting a single pregnancy rate without identifying the trial, denominator, and treatment protocol.

The US label includes clinical-study data supporting its use in controlled ovarian stimulation. Complete pivotal trial identifiers, comparator details, cancellation rates, ongoing pregnancy/live-birth endpoints, and confidence intervals remain to be extracted.

## 8. Safety
Important label-described adverse reactions include injection-site reactions, headache, nausea, and abdominal discomfort. Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylactoid reactions, have been reported; prior severe hypersensitivity to ganirelix or related products is a contraindication. Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) is a risk of the overall controlled ovarian stimulation process and is not attributable solely to ganirelix. Ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage, and multiple gestation risks are associated with assisted reproduction and should not be causally assigned to the antagonist without evidence.

## 9. Interactions and special populations
- Used with exogenous gonadotropins under a reproductive endocrinology protocol.
- No broad enzyme-mediated interaction profile should be inferred from the peptide structure.
- Pregnancy is not the therapeutic context; use is part of a cycle intended to achieve conception and product-specific timing is protocol-controlled.
- Hypersensitivity to GnRH analogues or product components requires label review.
- Paediatric use is not an established indication.

## 10. Formulation, manufacturing, and quality
The US product is a sterile aqueous subcutaneous injection of ganirelix acetate, supplied in a prefilled syringe. Critical quality attributes include peptide sequence and stereochemistry, acetate salt content, peptide assay and purity, related peptide impurities, degradation products, pH, particulate matter, sterility, endotoxin, syringe functionality, and container-closure integrity. ## 11. Bioinformatics and translational mapping
- Target: GnRH receptor, gene **GNRHR**.
- Pathway: Gq/11–PLCβ–IP3/DAG–Ca2+ signalling in pituitary gonadotrophs.
- Downstream endocrine readouts: LH, FSH, estradiol, progesterone, follicular response.
- Clinical endpoints: premature LH surge, premature luteinization, oocyte yield, clinical pregnancy, ongoing pregnancy, live birth, OHSS.
- Separate antagonist pharmacology from agonist desensitization; do not collapse both classes into one generic GnRH mechanism.
