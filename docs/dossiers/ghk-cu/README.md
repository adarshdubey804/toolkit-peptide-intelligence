# GHK-Cu (copper tripeptide)

> Workbook entry: **122/128** · Curation status: **in_progress**

## Overview

Copper(II) complex of the endogenous tripeptide glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine (GHK). Used as a cosmetic ingredient in topical skin-care products; not FDA-approved as a medicine for wound healing, hair growth, skin disease or systemic use.

## Identity and chemistry

Peptide sequence GHK (Gly-His-Lys) coordinated to copper(II). The metal complex has coordination chemistry and speciation that depend on pH, ligand-to-metal ratio and formulation; do not treat it as a single immutable covalent peptide structure.

## Mechanism of action

GHK can coordinate copper and has been studied for effects on extracellular-matrix remodelling, collagen-related processes, antioxidant enzymes and wound-repair signalling. Mechanistic evidence is largely preclinical; copper coordination and labile copper fraction are important determinants of biological activity and safety.

## Pharmacodynamics

Topical cosmetic studies are small and heterogeneous, with limited objective clinical evidence. Injectable GHK-Cu lacks established human pharmacodynamic or efficacy evidence.

## Pharmacokinetics

No validated systemic human PK profile for injectable GHK-Cu. Topical penetration and local exposure depend on formulation and skin-barrier condition; do not extrapolate topical cosmetic experience to injection.

## Clinical evidence

Some small topical studies and preclinical wound models exist, but evidence does not establish a medicinal wound-healing or hair-growth indication. A recent review notes sparse heterogeneous human evidence and a small controlled post-procedure study without objective benefit on several endpoints.

## Safety and tolerability

Injectable use has insufficient human safety data. FDA has raised concerns about potential immunogenicity from aggregation and peptide-related impurities and limited human safety information. Copper exposure and product quality must be considered. Cosmetic topical status does not establish safety of injection.

## Regulatory status

Not FDA-approved as a drug. Topical cosmetic products may be marketed subject to applicable cosmetic requirements and claims rules; that is distinct from drug approval. FDA compounding status for injectable and noninjectable bulk substances is a separate, changing regulatory question and must be checked against the current FDA list.

## Formulation and manufacturing

Topical cosmetic serums/creams are common. No FDA-approved injectable drug formulation exists. Formulation characterization should report total copper, GHK concentration, copper occupancy/speciation, free copper, aggregation and stability.

## Bioinformatics and translational research

Potential analyses: copper coordination chemistry, matrix metalloproteinases, collagen and extracellular-matrix networks, angiogenesis, oxidative stress and skin transcriptomics. Separate apo-GHK from the Cu complex and topical from parenteral evidence.
