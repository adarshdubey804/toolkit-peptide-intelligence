## 1. Executive overview

GHRP-2, internationally named pralmorelin, is a synthetic hexapeptide growth hormone secretagogue that activates the ghrelin / growth hormone secretagogue receptor (GHSR1a). It stimulates pituitary growth hormone (GH) release through a receptor pathway distinct from the growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor.

Unlike GHRP-6, pralmorelin has a recognized diagnostic role in Japan: it is used as a pharmacological stimulus in assessment of GH secretory reserve in the appropriate clinical context. A diagnostic test indication must not be confused with chronic GH replacement or treatment of body-composition concerns. The diagnostic product is not an FDA-approved US therapeutic product.

GHRP-2 is marketed online for muscle gain, fat loss, recovery, and anti-aging. Those uses are not established indications. FDA has listed GHRP-2 among Category 2 bulk drug substances under its interim 503B compounding policy and has described safety concerns involving potential immunogenicity, limited safety information, and possible effects on cortisol and glucose homeostasis.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

GHRP-2 is an agonist at GHSR1a, a G-protein-coupled receptor that responds to ghrelin and synthetic growth hormone secretagogues.

**Canonical GH secretagogue pathway:**

1. Pralmorelin binds GHSR1a on pituitary somatotrophs and at other receptor-expressing sites.
2. Receptor activation engages G-protein signalling, prominently Gq/11-linked signalling.
3. Phospholipase C activation generates IP₃ and DAG from membrane PIP₂.
4. IP₃-dependent calcium mobilization and associated signalling promote secretory activity.
5. Pituitary somatotrophs release GH.
6. GH acts directly and stimulates downstream IGF-1 production in liver and peripheral tissues.

GHRP-2 acts through the ghrelin/GHSR pathway, not by binding the GHRH receptor. GHRH and GHRP-2 can stimulate GH release through distinct pathways, which can interact synergistically in experimental and diagnostic settings.

The diagnostic response is a stimulated GH peak after pharmacological challenge. Interpretation depends on assay, protocol, patient characteristics, and the clinical diagnostic framework; a single value should not be interpreted without appropriate clinical context.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

### Growth hormone stimulation
- GHRP-2 produces acute GH release in human pharmacology studies.
- It has been used as a provocative stimulus in evaluation of GH secretory capacity.
- The response can be affected by age, sex, body composition, nutritional state, pituitary/hypothalamic function, and concomitant endocrine signals.
- GH stimulation is a pharmacodynamic response and does not itself establish a benefit from chronic exposure.

### Other endocrine effects
GHRP-2 is not absolutely selective for GH release. FDA's safety discussion identifies potential cortisol effects and possible glucose effects associated with changes in insulin sensitivity. Prolactin and other pituitary endpoints should be assessed in the context of specific studies rather than assumed absent.

### Appetite and metabolic effects
GHSR biology is relevant to appetite and energy balance. Do not treat the appetite or metabolic effects of one GHSR agonist as quantitatively interchangeable with another. Claims that GHRP-2 reliably reduces fat or increases lean mass in healthy people are not established clinical indications.

## 7. Clinical evidence and diagnostic use

### Japan: diagnostic GH stimulation

Pralmorelin hydrochloride has been used in Japan as a diagnostic GH secretagogue for assessment of GH secretion. The clinical context is diagnostic evaluation under specialist supervision, not ongoing treatment with GHRP-2.

Key interpretation points:
- A diagnostic stimulus tests the capacity to release GH under a defined protocol.
- The diagnostic threshold is assay- and protocol-dependent.
- Results are interpreted alongside clinical features, other endocrine testing, and the applicable local guidance.
- A diagnostic authorization in Japan does not establish approval in the United States or another jurisdiction.

The current Japanese package insert, approval details, and diagnostic cut-offs should be checked against the PMDA source before exact label language or thresholds are added to this dossier.

### Other therapeutic claims

There is no established evidence base supporting GHRP-2 as an approved treatment for:
- Growth hormone deficiency as a replacement therapy.
- Muscle hypertrophy or athletic performance.
- Obesity or fat loss.
- Anti-aging or longevity.
- Injury recovery or tissue repair.

These claims should remain labelled as unsubstantiated marketing unless controlled human clinical outcome data demonstrate otherwise.

## 8. Safety and tolerability

### FDA-identified concerns for compounded GHRP-2
FDA's 503B Category 2 entry identifies concerns including:
- Potential immunogenicity associated with peptide aggregation and peptide-related impurities.
- Limited safety-related information.
- Available data suggesting potential cortisol effects.
- Potential increases in blood glucose associated with decreases in insulin sensitivity.

These concerns do not constitute a quantified adverse-event rate, and causality should not be overstated. The safety profile of a regulated diagnostic product in one jurisdiction cannot automatically be extrapolated to unapproved compounded products with different routes, formulations, and quality controls.

### Diagnostic-product safety
For the Japanese diagnostic product, adverse reactions, precautions, and contraindications must be taken from the current official package insert. This revision does not reproduce an unverified adverse-event frequency table. Potential acute endocrine responses and patient-specific contraindications require specialist review.

### Long-term exposure
There is no adequate basis here to characterize the long-term safety of repeated GHRP-2 exposure for bodybuilding, wellness, or anti-aging. Diagnostic use does not establish chronic-use safety.
