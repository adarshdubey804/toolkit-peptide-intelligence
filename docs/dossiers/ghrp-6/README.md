**Workbook position:** Peptides sheet, row 55 (Excel row 56)

**Regulatory status:** No FDA-approved GHRP-6 medicine or therapeutic indication identified.

> **Safety scope:** GHRP-6 is an unapproved investigational/research peptide. This dossier is for scientific and regulatory information, not personal treatment. It does not provide preparation, injection, nasal-use, or dosing instructions.

## 1. Executive overview

GHRP-6 is a synthetic hexapeptide growth-hormone secretagogue. It activates the ghrelin / growth hormone secretagogue receptor (GHSR, particularly GHSR1a) and stimulates pituitary growth hormone release. It is an early member of the growth-hormone-releasing peptide family and is also associated with appetite-stimulating (orexigenic) activity.

GHRP-6 is not growth hormone, does not act through the canonical growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor, and has no established approved therapeutic use. Historical human studies support acute GH-releasing pharmacology, but this is not equivalent to evidence that GHRP-6 safely treats growth hormone deficiency, promotes muscle growth, heals injuries, or improves healthspan.

FDA lists GHRP-6 among Category 2 bulk drug substances under its interim 503B compounding policy. FDA's stated concerns include possible immunogenicity related to aggregation and peptide-related impurities, limited safety-related information, and available data suggesting potential cortisol effects and increased blood glucose associated with reduced insulin sensitivity.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

GHRP-6 is an agonist at the ghrelin / growth hormone secretagogue receptor, GHSR1a, a G-protein-coupled receptor. Its best-established pharmacological action is stimulation of GH release from pituitary somatotroph cells.

**Simplified signalling model:**

1. GHRP-6 binds and activates GHSR1a.
2. Receptor activation engages G-protein signalling, prominently involving Gq/11-family pathways.
3. G-protein signalling activates phospholipase C (PLC).
4. PLC hydrolyses PIP₂ to generate IP₃ and DAG.
5. IP₃-mediated calcium mobilization and downstream signalling promote secretory activity.
6. Pituitary somatotrophs release GH.
7. GH acts directly and can stimulate hepatic and peripheral IGF-1 production.

GHRP-6 and GHRH stimulate GH release through distinct receptors and signalling systems; their effects can be synergistic in experimental settings. GHRP-6 should therefore not be described as a GHRH-receptor agonist.

GHSR is expressed beyond the pituitary, including in pathways relevant to appetite and gastrointestinal physiology. This broader receptor distribution helps explain why GHRP-6 is not a GH-selective intervention in the clinical sense.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

### Growth hormone
- Historical human pharmacology studies report acute, dose-related GH release after GHRP-6 exposure.
- The magnitude of GH response is influenced by age, sex, nutritional state, baseline endocrine status, and concomitant hypothalamic/pituitary signals.
- Repeated exposure may show attenuation / desensitization; the magnitude and clinical relevance require careful study-specific interpretation.
- Acute GH stimulation is a surrogate pharmacodynamic response, not proof of improvement in patient-centred outcomes.

### Appetite and energy balance
GHRP-6 has orexigenic activity in experimental models and has been associated with increased food intake. It should not be represented as a weight-loss agent. Increased appetite may be counterproductive for people seeking weight reduction.

### Glucose and insulin physiology
FDA specifically notes available safety information suggesting increased blood glucose due to decreases in insulin sensitivity. This is a material concern for a compound marketed online for body composition or wellness.

### Cortisol and other endocrine effects
FDA notes potential effects on cortisol. GHRP-6 should not be described as exclusively GH-selective. Prolactin, ACTH/cortisol, glucose, and other endocrine endpoints require study-specific review; do not infer absence of effects from a limited early pharmacology report.

## 7. Clinical and preclinical evidence

### Human GH-secretagogue pharmacology
Historical human studies demonstrate that GHRP-6 can stimulate acute GH release. These studies establish pharmacological activity, not an approved therapeutic benefit. Study designs, populations, routes, and endpoints differ, and they should not be pooled into a claim of clinical efficacy without a formal systematic review.

### Therapeutic outcomes
No established therapeutic efficacy programme or FDA-approved indication was identified for GHRP-6. There is no basis to claim that it is an effective treatment for:
- Growth hormone deficiency.
- Sarcopenia or age-related muscle loss.
- Obesity or fat loss.
- Wound healing or tissue repair.
- Athletic performance or recovery.
- Anti-aging or longevity.

Preclinical reports involving cytoprotection, wound biology, or tissue repair must remain classified as preclinical until supported by controlled human outcome studies.

### Evidence quality
The available human evidence is best interpreted as limited pharmacodynamic evidence. Acute GH release is not a validated substitute for demonstrating improved function, morbidity, quality of life, or other clinical outcomes in a defined patient population.

## 8. Safety and risk profile

### FDA-identified concerns
FDA's Category 2 entry for GHRP-6 (503B) states:
- Compounded products may pose immunogenicity risk for certain routes because of potential aggregation and peptide-related impurities.
- FDA has identified limited safety-related information.
- Available data reveal potential effects on cortisol.
- Available data reveal increased blood glucose associated with decreases in insulin sensitivity.

These are FDA-identified concerns, not a complete adverse-event incidence table. The available public evidence does not establish the frequency of each event or a safe exposure threshold.

### Additional research considerations
- Endocrine perturbation from stimulating the GH axis.
- Glucose intolerance / insulin resistance.
- Appetite stimulation and potential unwanted weight gain.
- Immunogenicity and hypersensitivity associated with peptide aggregates or impurities.
- Unknown long-term consequences of repeated exposure.
- Route-specific risks where safety data are absent or inadequate.

### Contraindications and interactions
There is no approved prescribing information from which to derive a validated contraindication or interaction list. In particular, do not invent approved-label guidance. Effects on glucose homeostasis make concomitant glucose-lowering therapy an important research and clinical-evaluation consideration, but no self-adjustment of medication is appropriate.
