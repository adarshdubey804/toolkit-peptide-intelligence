# Dossier 080 — Glatiramer Acetate (Copaxone; Glatopa)

**Workbook entry:** 080 | **Category:** Neurology, Pain & CNS | **US approval:** 1996 | **Status:** FDA-approved  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and molecular nature
Glatiramer acetate is a synthetic heterogeneous polypeptide mixture composed of L-alanine, L-glutamic acid, L-lysine and L-tyrosine in approximate average molar ratios 0. alanine 0.43, glutamic acid 0.14, lysine 0.35 and tyrosine 0.08. The exact order and chain length vary from molecule to molecule. It is therefore incorrect to assign one canonical amino-acid sequence, exact molecular mass, or single molecular formula. The drug substance is a complex mixture of polypeptides with a specified molecular-weight distribution and composition controls.

## Mechanism
The exact mechanism in multiple sclerosis is not fully understood. Proposed immunomodulatory effects include competition with myelin antigens for antigen presentation, induction of glatiramer-reactive regulatory T-cell populations, and modulation of pro- and anti-inflammatory cytokine profiles. These are mechanistic models, not a fully resolved single target mechanism. Do not describe glatiramer as a conventional antigen-specific vaccine or as a direct remyelinating drug.

## Pharmacodynamics and PK
Glatiramer is administered subcutaneously. Its systemic pharmacokinetics are difficult to characterize because it is a heterogeneous mixture rapidly hydrolysed at the injection site and in tissues. No single plasma half-life or conventional bioavailability value adequately represents the product. The immunological response is more relevant than systemic peptide concentration.

## Clinical evidence
Copaxone is indicated for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis, including clinically isolated syndrome, relapsing-remitting disease, and active secondary progressive disease according to current US label wording. Pivotal trials showed reductions in relapse-related outcomes compared with placebo, but trial populations and diagnostic definitions predate some current MS criteria. Avoid comparing annualized relapse rates across trials without accounting for baseline disease activity and endpoint definitions.

## Safety
Important reactions include injection-site reactions, immediate post-injection reaction (flushing, chest pain, palpitations, anxiety, dyspnea, throat constriction and urticaria), and lipoatrophy/skin necrosis. Rare anaphylaxis, including fatal cases, has been reported. The current label carries a boxed warning for anaphylaxis. Patients should be counselled using the current label and clinician instructions; this dossier does not provide injection instructions.

## Formulation and quality
Glatiramer acetate is a complex mixture, not a single sequence-defined peptide. Critical quality attributes include amino-acid composition, molecular-weight distribution, sequence/structural heterogeneity, peptide content, residual reagents, impurities, aggregation, sterility, endotoxin and stability. Comparability of generic products requires rigorous analytical and immunological evidence; simple sequence identity is not an appropriate standard.

## Regulatory status
Copaxone first received US approval in 1996. Glatopa is an FDA-approved generic product. Exact indications and product-specific labeling should be checked separately. Approval does not imply that all glatiramer formulations or compounded mixtures are equivalent.
