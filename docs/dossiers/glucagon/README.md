# Glucagon

> Workbook entry: **015/128** · Curation status: **in_progress**

## Overview

Endogenous 29-amino-acid peptide hormone and active ingredient in several distinct rescue and diagnostic products. The workbook entry is a class-level row; formulations, routes, devices and indications must be separated by brand.

## Identity and chemistry

Human glucagon is a 29-residue linear peptide: HSQGTFTSDYSKYLDSRRAQDFVQWLMNT. Product-specific synthetic or recombinant sources and formulation details should be recorded independently.

## Mechanism of action

Activates the glucagon receptor (GCGR), a class B GPCR, predominantly in liver, increasing adenylyl cyclase/cAMP signalling and promoting glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis. It also affects gastrointestinal motility at pharmacologic exposure.

## Pharmacodynamics

Raises blood glucose in a glycogen-dependent manner. Response can be limited when hepatic glycogen stores are depleted; diagnostic formulations can transiently inhibit gastrointestinal motility.

## Pharmacokinetics

PK varies substantially by formulation and route. Use the relevant product label for absorption, distribution, metabolism and elimination; do not apply one rescue product's PK to another.

## Clinical evidence

FDA-approved products include glucagon for severe hypoglycaemia and diagnostic use, with product-specific age, route and device requirements. Evidence should be organized by product and indication.

## Safety and tolerability

Nausea and vomiting are known effects; hypersensitivity and cardiovascular effects can occur. Review exact product labeling for contraindications and precautions, including pheochromocytoma/insulinoma where applicable.

## Regulatory status

Initial US approval of glucagon injection: 1960 (product class history). This does not mean every current glucagon analogue or device was approved in 1960.

## Formulation and manufacturing

Products include reconstituted kits, ready-to-use autoinjectors and nasal powder. Excipients, concentration, stability and device differ by brand.

## Bioinformatics and translational research

Bioinformatics: GCGR/cAMP signalling, hepatic glucose metabolism, glycogen pathways and receptor-ligand structural analysis.
