# Glutathione (GSH)

> Workbook entry: **128/128** · Curation status: **in_progress**

## Overview

Endogenous tripeptide antioxidant used in supplements and some pharmaceutical contexts. FDA has not approved glutathione drugs for skin lightening or cosmetic whitening; injectable and compounded use must not be conflated with ordinary dietary supplement or topical products.

## Identity and chemistry

γ-L-glutamyl-L-cysteinylglycine (Glu-Cys-Gly), with an unusual γ-peptide bond between glutamate and cysteine. Reduced glutathione (GSH) is distinct from oxidized glutathione disulfide (GSSG).

## Mechanism of action

Central cellular redox buffer and substrate for glutathione peroxidases, transferases and other enzymes. Participates in peroxide detoxification, electrophile conjugation, protein thiol redox regulation and amino-acid transport cycles. This endogenous biochemical role does not establish efficacy of exogenous glutathione for skin lightening.

## Pharmacodynamics

Cellular GSH/GSSG ratio is a redox-state indicator, but systemic supplementation does not reliably translate into tissue-specific target engagement. Skin pigmentation claims involve proposed effects on melanogenesis and tyrosinase, with inconsistent clinical evidence.

## Pharmacokinetics

Oral, topical and parenteral routes have different exposure profiles. Oral bioavailability and tissue effects depend on formulation and metabolism; intravenous pharmacokinetics cannot be inferred from oral supplement studies. Quantitative parameters require route-specific primary data.

## Clinical evidence

Small heterogeneous studies have evaluated oral or topical glutathione for pigmentation, with limited evidence and uncertain durability. FDA has not approved injectable glutathione for skin whitening. Its established endogenous biochemical role should be separated from claims for cosmetic outcomes.

## Safety and tolerability

Adverse events and risks are route- and product-dependent. FDA has warned about adverse events associated with compounded injectable glutathione products made with unsuitable ingredients/contaminated components. Do not treat supplement safety as evidence of injectable safety.

## Regulatory status

Glutathione is an endogenous molecule and is sold in supplements/cosmetics; this does not mean FDA-approved drug status for skin lightening. No FDA-approved glutathione drug product for cosmetic skin whitening.

## Formulation and manufacturing

Available as oral supplements, topical cosmetics and compounded/injectable preparations in some settings. Product quality, oxidation state, excipients, sterility and route-specific regulatory status must be separately documented.

## Bioinformatics and translational research

Potential research: glutathione redox cycling, GSH/GSSG metabolomics, NRF2, glutathione peroxidases, glutathione-S-transferases, melanogenesis and oxidative-stress signatures.
