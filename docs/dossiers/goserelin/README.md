# Dossier 026 — Goserelin (goserelin acetate)

**Curation status:** in progress — FDA identity and current US product labels reviewed; indication-level trial evidence, global regulatory status, full PK and product CMC remain to be completed.  
**Asset class:** synthetic decapeptide analogue of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH/LHRH).  
**Pharmacology:** GnRH receptor agonist; sustained exposure suppresses pituitary gonadotropins and gonadal sex steroids after an initial stimulatory phase.  
**Reference product:** ZOLADEX biodegradable subcutaneous implant (3.6 mg and 10.8 mg goserelin equivalents).

## 1. Identity and classification

Goserelin is a synthetic decapeptide GnRH analogue. The active peptide is commonly formulated as goserelin acetate. The reference ZOLADEX product is a biodegradable polymer implant that releases goserelin over either approximately 28 days (3.6 mg) or 12 weeks (10.8 mg). These are product-specific presentations, not interchangeable dose forms. [1–3]

**Terminology:** D-Ser(But) means D-serine bearing an O-tert-butyl substituent. Azgly denotes the C-terminal azaglycinamide-derived residue in the official chemical description. The 10-residue count matters: goserelin is a decapeptide, unlike leuprolide, which is a nonapeptide. FDA GSRS separately identifies goserelin and goserelin acetate; do not collapse their substance identifiers. [1,4]

## 2. Mechanism of action

Goserelin is an agonist of pituitary GnRH receptors. As with other continuous-exposure GnRH agonists, the endocrine response is biphasic:

1. **Initial stimulation (flare):** GnRH receptor activation transiently increases LH and FSH secretion. In males this can increase serum testosterone; in females it can transiently increase ovarian steroid production.
2. **Sustained exposure:** continuous agonist exposure leads to pituitary GnRH receptor desensitisation/downregulation and reduced gonadotropin release.
3. **Downstream suppression:** lower LH/FSH reduces testicular testosterone or ovarian oestradiol production. This can suppress growth of hormone-responsive tumours or induce a reversible hypo-oestrogenic state in selected gynaecological indications.

The current FDA label describes the initial increase in LH/FSH and testosterone followed by sustained suppression; in males, testosterone typically reaches the castrate range within approximately 2–4 weeks after treatment initiation. The exact time course depends on sex, baseline endocrine state, dose form and individual response. [2,3]

## 3. Molecular structure and structure–activity features

Goserelin is a modified decapeptide based on the native GnRH sequence. Key substitutions include:
- position 6: D-serine with an O-tert-butyl substituent [D-Ser(But)] rather than glycine;
- position 10: azaglycinamide-related terminal modification;
- N-terminal pyroglutamate (pGlu).

The official chemical description in the FDA label is pyro-Glu-His-Trp-Ser-Tyr-D-Ser(But)-Leu-Arg-Pro-Azgly-NH₂ acetate. These substitutions produce a potent GnRH analogue with altered stability and sustained pharmacological action when delivered continuously from the depot. Avoid assigning a quantitative contribution to each substitution without primary SAR data. [2,5]

## 4. Receptor pharmacology and pharmacodynamics

The principal target is the pituitary GnRH receptor, a class A GPCR. GnRH receptor activation engages Gq/11-associated phospholipase-C signalling, intracellular calcium mobilisation and downstream pathways that regulate gonadotropin synthesis and release.

With continuous goserelin exposure, pituitary responsiveness falls and LH/FSH secretion is suppressed. In men, sustained testosterone suppression can be maintained during treatment; in women, ovarian oestradiol production is reduced. Hormonal recovery after discontinuation is variable and should be assessed in the context of indication, treatment duration and patient factors. [2,3]

A complete quantitative receptor profile (binding affinity, EC50, intrinsic efficacy, species comparison and signalling bias) has not been established in this curation pass and remains open.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics and depot release

Goserelin PK must be interpreted in the context of its biodegradable implant. The FDA label describes a 3.6 mg implant designed for continuous release over 28 days and a 10.8 mg implant designed for continuous release over 12 weeks. The active peptide's plasma disposition and the implant's release duration are distinct concepts. [2,3]

The 3.6 mg label reports that radiolabelled-drug absorption is rapid after administration, with peak blood radioactivity between 0.5 and 1 hour. For the 3.6 mg depot, the label reports sex-specific PK estimates:
- males (n=7): mean Cmax 2.84 ± 1.81 ng/mL; Tmax 12–15 days; AUC₀–₂₈ days 27.8 ± 15.3 ng·day/mL; systemic clearance 110.5 ± 47.5 mL/min;
- females (n=9): mean Cmax 1.46 ± 0.82 ng/mL; Tmax 8–22 days; AUC₀–₂₈ days 18.5 ± 10.3 ng·day/mL; systemic clearance 163.9 ± 71.0 mL/min.

The label notes an initial slower release over approximately the first eight days, followed by more rapid and continuous release through the remainder of the 28-day interval. These data are from a small PK dataset and must not be transferred to the 10.8 mg implant without evidence. [3]

## 6. Approved indications and product-specific use

The US ZOLADEX labels differ by strength and product presentation.

### ZOLADEX 3.6 mg

The current US label includes:
- management of prostate carcinoma (including use with flutamide in the specified locally confined setting and palliative treatment of advanced disease);
- management of endometriosis;
- endometrial thinning before endometrial ablation for dysfunctional uterine bleeding;
- palliative treatment of advanced breast cancer in pre- and perimenopausal women.

The full label specifies the clinical context, treatment duration and restrictions for each indication. [2]

### ZOLADEX 10.8 mg

The current US label is narrower and lists:
- use in combination with flutamide for management of locally confined prostate carcinoma;
- palliative treatment of advanced prostate carcinoma.

It is administered as a subcutaneous implant every 12 weeks according to the product label. Do not transfer the 3.6 mg indication list to the 10.8 mg presentation. [1]

Other countries may have additional or different indications, including fertility-related or breast-cancer uses, depending on local product authorisation. These require country-specific label verification.

## 7. Regulatory status

**United States:** FDA-approved ZOLADEX 3.6 mg and 10.8 mg implants. The current DailyMed labels list initial US approval in 1989 for the 3.6 mg product and 1996 for the 10.8 mg product. Those dates are presentation-specific. [1,2]

**Other jurisdictions:** goserelin products are authorised in multiple countries, but this dossier does not assert universal approval or identical indications. Each country/product/strength combination requires an independent regulator source and current label.

**Identity vs approval:** FDA GSRS UNII records identify the substance; they do not independently establish approval. Approval is supported here by the FDA-regulated ZOLADEX product labels. [1,4]

## 8. Safety and tolerability

The adverse-effect profile is substantially related to sex-steroid suppression and the initial hormonal flare. Important label safety domains include:
- transient tumour flare, including potential worsening of prostate-cancer symptoms and complications in patients at risk of obstruction or spinal cord compression;
- hot flashes, sweating, reduced libido, erectile or other sexual-function effects;
- bone mineral density loss with prolonged sex-steroid suppression;
- hyperglycaemia and diabetes risk reported with GnRH analogues in men;
- cardiovascular events and QT/QTc considerations;
- mood changes/depression and rare neurological events reported in GnRH-analogue labeling;
- hypersensitivity, including systemic reactions;
- injection/implant-site reactions, bleeding or device-related complications;
- pregnancy contraindication and embryo-fetal risk.

The current 10.8 mg label includes warnings on tumour flare, hypersensitivity, hyperglycaemia/diabetes and cardiovascular disease. The full current 3.6 mg label should be consulted for the broader indication-specific warnings. This dossier is not a patient-specific treatment guide. [1–3]

## 9. Formulation, administration and product quality

ZOLADEX is a sterile, biodegradable implant in which goserelin acetate is dispersed in a D,L-lactic/glycolic acid copolymer matrix. The 3.6 mg product contains goserelin acetate equivalent to 3.6 mg goserelin and is designed for continuous release over 28 days. The 10.8 mg product is designed for continuous release over 12 weeks. The implants are supplied preloaded in dedicated single-use syringes; needle gauge and implant dimensions differ by presentation. [2,3]

CMC attributes to capture:
- peptide sequence and stereochemical control, especially D-Ser(But) at position 6;
- terminal azaglycinamide and pyroglutamate identity;
- acetate content and peptide-related substances;
- polymer identity, composition, molecular-weight distribution and degradation;
- implant dimensions, content uniformity and release profile;
- residual solvents/reagents, sterility, endotoxin and particulate matter;
- syringe/device functionality and implant delivery;
- stability and moisture/light protection.

Do not substitute research-grade goserelin powder or compounded formulations for the authorised implant's product-specific quality specifications.
