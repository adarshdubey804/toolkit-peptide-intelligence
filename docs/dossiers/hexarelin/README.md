## 1. Executive overview

Hexarelin (also called examorelin) is a synthetic hexapeptide growth hormone secretagogue. It activates the ghrelin / growth hormone secretagogue receptor, GHSR1a, and can stimulate pituitary growth hormone (GH) release. It is pharmacologically distinct from growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analogues, which act at the GHRH receptor.

Human research has explored acute GH release and possible cardiovascular effects, including studies of cardiac function and cardiac protection-related biomarkers. These investigations do not establish hexarelin as an approved treatment for heart disease, GH deficiency, muscle loss, obesity, or aging. Repeated exposure can produce endocrine adaptation, and hexarelin should not be described as a selective GH-only agent.

No FDA-approved hexarelin product or indication has been identified. Research-vendor availability and online claims are not evidence of pharmaceutical quality, lawful therapeutic use, or clinical benefit.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

Hexarelin is a GHSR1a agonist. GHSR1a is a G-protein-coupled receptor that responds to endogenous ghrelin and synthetic secretagogues.

**Canonical pathway:**

1. Hexarelin binds and activates GHSR1a.
2. Receptor activation engages G-protein signalling, prominently Gq/11-linked pathways.
3. Phospholipase C activation generates IP₃ and DAG from PIP₂.
4. IP₃-mediated calcium mobilization and downstream signalling contribute to secretory activity.
5. Pituitary somatotrophs release GH.
6. GH can act directly and stimulate downstream IGF-1 production.

The GH response occurs through the ghrelin/secretagogue receptor pathway, not through the GHRH receptor. GHRH and GHSR agonists may interact synergistically in pituitary GH release, but their receptor targets are distinct.

GHSR1a is present in tissues beyond the pituitary, including cardiovascular and metabolic systems. Consequently, hexarelin's biological effects cannot be assumed to be limited to GH release.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

### Growth hormone secretion
- Human pharmacology studies demonstrate acute GH-releasing activity.
- GH response depends on the population, baseline endocrine status, age, sex, nutritional state, sampling schedule, and study protocol.
- Repeated stimulation may lead to attenuation of GH response; study-specific evidence is required to quantify this.
- A transient GH peak is not evidence of a long-term clinical benefit.

### Other endocrine activity
Hexarelin should not be characterized as completely selective for GH. Other pituitary and metabolic endpoints have been examined in the GHRP class. For hexarelin, cortisol, prolactin, glucose, and insulin-related observations should be extracted from primary studies before any quantitative claim is made.

### Cardiovascular research
Small human studies have explored possible effects on cardiac function and cardiovascular physiology. These are hypothesis-generating research findings, not evidence that hexarelin prevents myocardial injury, treats heart failure, or improves cardiovascular outcomes. Clinical use for cardiovascular disease is not established.

## 7. Clinical research and evidence

### Human endocrine studies
Hexarelin has been studied as a pharmacological GH secretagogue in humans. These studies support acute pharmacodynamic activity but do not establish a licensed therapeutic indication or the long-term benefit-risk profile of chronic exposure.

### Cardiovascular studies
Published human research has investigated cardiac and cardiovascular endpoints. Such studies are generally small and do not provide the evidence base required to claim efficacy for heart failure, ischemic disease, cardioprotection, or prevention of cardiovascular events.

### Unapproved promotional claims
The following are not established therapeutic indications:
- Muscle growth or bodybuilding.
- Fat loss or obesity treatment.
- Anti-aging or longevity.
- Injury recovery or tissue regeneration.
- Treatment of GH deficiency.
- Cardiovascular disease treatment.

Preclinical findings and acute biomarker changes must be clearly separated from controlled human clinical outcomes.

## 8. Safety and tolerability

There is no FDA-approved hexarelin prescribing information from which to extract a validated adverse-reaction frequency table, contraindication list, or monitoring protocol.

Relevant research and clinical safety questions include:
- Endocrine effects from GH-axis stimulation.
- Changes in IGF-1 and possible downstream GH-related effects.
- Glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity.
- Appetite and metabolic changes.
- Pituitary hormone effects beyond GH.
- Cardiovascular responses and their clinical relevance.
- Immunogenicity, aggregation, and peptide-related impurities in nonstandard preparations.
- Unknown long-term safety with repeated exposure.

These are areas requiring evaluation, not a claim that each event has a quantified incidence with hexarelin. Do not assume that data from other GHSR agonists provide a complete hexarelin safety profile.
