## 1. Executive overview

“HGH Fragment 176–191” is a name used for a peptide corresponding to the C-terminal region of human growth hormone. It is marketed online for fat loss, but it is not an FDA-approved medicine and there is no established clinical indication for obesity or weight management.

The term is chemically ambiguous in the commercial marketplace. Sources may use “176–191,” “177–191,” or refer to AOD-9604 as though they were interchangeable. They must not be treated as synonyms without an explicit chemical structure. AOD-9604 is a separately developed modified hGH-derived analogue; the generic fragment name does not by itself define AOD-9604's exact structure or terminal modifications.

Numbering can differ depending on whether a source counts the mature secreted hormone or precursor sequence.

**Sequence status:** deliberately not asserted as a definitive product identity in this revision. Before assigning a canonical sequence, formula, molecular weight, CAS number, or SMILES, the dossier must specify:

A peptide fragment derived from hGH is not equivalent to full-length recombinant somatropin. It is also not automatically equivalent to AOD-9604, which is a separately described modified analogue.

## 4. Biological rationale and proposed mechanism

The C-terminal region of GH has been investigated in experimental research for possible metabolic effects. The general hypothesis behind fragment development is that selected lipolytic actions might be retained without reproducing all biological effects of full-length GH.

For the generic 176–191 fragment name, a specific clinically validated receptor-level mechanism has not been established in this dossier. Do not state that the fragment selectively activates lipolysis in humans or that it avoids all GH-related endocrine effects.

Evidence must be separated into:
1. Biochemical or cell-based observations.
2. Animal adipose-tissue and metabolic studies.
3. Human pharmacodynamic studies, if any.
4. Controlled clinical weight-loss outcomes.

An effect in an adipocyte assay or animal model is not proof of human weight reduction.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics and efficacy evidence
