# Dossier 099 — Icatibant (Firazyr; Sajazir)

## Identity and chemistry
Icatibant is a synthetic decapeptide and selective bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist. It contains several non-proteinogenic residues, including D-arginine and hydroxyproline, which contribute to receptor affinity and proteolytic stability. It is supplied as a solution for subcutaneous injection. ## Mechanism
During hereditary angioedema attacks, excess bradykinin increases vascular permeability through B2 receptors on endothelial cells. Icatibant competitively antagonizes the bradykinin B2 receptor, blocking bradykinin signalling and reducing vascular leakage and swelling. It acts downstream of bradykinin generation and does not inhibit plasma kallikrein or replace C1 inhibitor.

## Pharmacology and PK
Icatibant is administered subcutaneously and has high B2-receptor affinity. It is degraded primarily by proteolytic enzymes rather than CYP metabolism. The terminal half-life is approximately 1–2 hours; clinical response can outlast measurable plasma exposure. Renal and hepatic impairment data should be taken from current label. Distinguish the pharmacokinetic profile from ecallantide, which inhibits plasma kallikrein.

## Clinical evidence and indication
Firazyr is indicated for treatment of acute attacks of hereditary angioedema in adults. The FAST-1, FAST-2 and FAST-3 studies evaluated symptom relief after icatibant compared with placebo or tranexamic acid, with endpoints including time to onset of symptom relief. The current US label and product-specific age indication should be checked; do not assume paediatric approval from international labels.

## Safety
Common adverse reactions include injection-site erythema, swelling, pain, burning, itching and bruising. Hypersensitivity is possible. Because laryngeal attacks can be life-threatening, emergency assessment and airway management remain essential; receptor antagonism is not a substitute for emergency care. Review the current label for repeat treatment and attack-management instructions rather than deriving them from this dossier.

## Formulation and quality
Single-dose prefilled syringe solution for subcutaneous injection. Critical quality attributes include sequence identity, stereochemistry, peptide purity, related substances, concentration, pH, particulate matter, sterility, endotoxin and syringe/container integrity.

## Regulatory status
FDA first approved in 2011 for acute HAE attacks in adults. Sajazir is a later generic product; maintain distinct application/product records while mapping to the same active ingredient.
