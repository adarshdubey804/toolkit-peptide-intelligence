# Dossier 040 — Indium In 111 pentetreotide (OctreoScan)

**Curation status:** `in_progress`

**Brand:** OctreoScan  
**Generic name:** indium In 111 pentetreotide  
**Common name:** In-111 pentetreotide; In-111 octreotide

**Historical first US approval:** 1994  
**Use:** Scintigraphic localisation of somatostatin-receptor-positive neuroendocrine tumours and selected other receptor-positive lesions.

> OctreoScan is a diagnostic imaging agent, not a therapeutic peptide or peptide-receptor radionuclide therapy. It is distinct from Ga-68 DOTATATE PET agents and Lu-177 DOTATATE therapy.

## 1. Identity and classification

Indium In 111 pentetreotide is a radiolabelled somatostatin analogue used for planar scintigraphy and SPECT imaging. Pentetreotide (also known as DTPA-octreotide) provides receptor targeting; indium-111 is chelated by DTPA and provides gamma emissions detected by a gamma camera.

- **Brand:** OctreoScan.
- **Active radiopharmaceutical:** indium In 111 pentetreotide.
- **Unlabelled peptide-chelator:** pentetreotide / DTPA-D-Phe-cyclo(Cys-Tyr-D-Trp-Lys-Thr-Cys)-Thr-ol.
- **US application:** NDA 020414 (verify current FDA database record and supplement history).
- **Initial US approval:** 1994.
- **Route:** intravenous.
- **Dosage form:** kit for preparation of indium In 111 pentetreotide injection.
- **Use:** diagnostic localisation; no antitumour therapeutic indication.

## 2. Peptide sequence and molecular architecture

Pentetreotide is a synthetic cyclic octapeptide somatostatin analogue derived from octreotide.

**Peptide sequence (linear shorthand):** D-Phe-Cys-Tyr-D-Trp-Lys-Thr-Cys-Thr-ol  
**Cyclisation:** intramolecular disulfide bridge between the two cysteine residues.  
**Chelator:** diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid (DTPA), conjugated to the N-terminal D-phenylalanine.  
**Radiometal:** indium-111 coordinated by DTPA.

The peptide is a modified octreotide analogue, with D-Phe at the N-terminus and a threoninol C-terminal residue. This is not an unmodified endogenous somatostatin sequence.

## 3. Chemical and radiochemical identity

- **Unlabelled precursor name:** pentetreotide (DTPA-octreotide).
- **Radiolabelled product:** indium In 111 pentetreotide.
- **Radionuclide:** indium-111.
- **Physical half-life:** approximately 67.3 hours.
- **Photon emissions:** principal gamma emissions at approximately 171 keV and 245 keV, used for gamma-camera imaging.
- **Chelation:** DTPA coordinates indium-111.
- **CAS / UNII:** exact precursor-versus-radiolabelled-complex mappings require GSRS reconciliation; do not conflate the two substances.

Molecular formula and molecular weight depend on whether the record represents free peptide, DTPA-conjugated pentetreotide, counterion form or the radiolabelled indium complex. A single formula should not be assigned until the exact registered substance form is confirmed.

## 4. Mechanism of action

1. Pentetreotide mimics somatostatin and binds somatostatin receptors on receptor-expressing cells.
2. Binding is strongest at SSTR2, with relevant affinity at additional receptor subtypes.
3. DTPA holds indium-111 in a chelated complex attached to the peptide.
4. Indium-111 emits gamma photons.
5. A gamma camera detects emitted photons to produce planar scintigraphic images; SPECT acquisition can provide tomographic localisation.

This is a receptor-targeted imaging mechanism. The administered activity is for diagnosis and is not intended to deliver a tumour-ablative radiation dose.

## 5. Receptor pharmacology and specificity

- **Target family:** somatostatin receptors SSTR1–SSTR5.
- **Important target:** SSTR2, commonly expressed by well-differentiated NETs.
- **Binding profile:** pentetreotide binds multiple SSTR subtypes; receptor affinity differs from modern DOTA-conjugated analogues such as DOTATATE and DOTATOC.
- **Limitations:** uptake is not exclusive to NETs; physiologic receptor expression and other receptor-positive pathologies may produce uptake.
- **Interpretation:** positive uptake supports receptor expression but does not replace histopathology or clinical correlation.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics and biodistribution

After intravenous administration, indium In 111 pentetreotide distributes in blood and tissues, binds somatostatin receptors and is cleared predominantly through the kidneys.

- Urinary excretion is an important elimination route.
- Renal activity and bladder contents contribute to radiation exposure.
- Physiologic uptake may be seen in spleen, liver, kidneys, pituitary, thyroid, bowel and other tissues.
- Imaging may include early and delayed acquisitions according to the product label and institutional protocol.
- Hydration and frequent voiding are used to reduce urinary tract radiation exposure.

Exact percentage excretion, organ residence times and dosimetry values should be transcribed from the current official label rather than inferred from general radiopharmaceutical behaviour.

## 7. Approved indication and clinical use

The historical US OctreoScan indication is scintigraphic localisation of primary and metastatic neuroendocrine tumours bearing somatostatin receptors, including selected gastroenteropancreatic and carcinoid tumours, using planar imaging and SPECT.

Potential contexts include:
- localisation of receptor-positive NETs;
- evaluation of suspected primary or metastatic disease;
- assessment of disease distribution;
- selected assessment of somatostatin-receptor expression where the scan is clinically appropriate.

The exact current label wording and any limitations should be confirmed against the latest FDA-approved prescribing information.

## 8. Preparation, administration and imaging

OctreoScan is supplied as a kit; indium-111 is added to the pentetreotide precursor according to the approved kit instructions by qualified radiopharmacy personnel.

- **Route:** intravenous.
- **Adults:** the historic US label describes a typical administered activity of approximately 111 MBq (3 mCi); confirm current product-label dose and local protocol before clinical use.
- **Imaging:** planar whole-body and targeted views, with SPECT where indicated; timing may include early and delayed imaging.
- **Paediatric dosing:** must follow the current official label and specialist nuclear-medicine protocol; do not extrapolate adult activity.

This dossier is not a preparation procedure. Radiolabelling, quality control, dose measurement and administration must follow the current manufacturer instructions and applicable radiation regulations.

## 9. Formulation and product presentation

The OctreoScan kit contains a vial of pentetreotide precursor and formulation components intended for radiolabelling with indium In 111 chloride. The final product is a sterile radioactive solution for intravenous administration.

The precise vial composition, excipients, activity limits, pH, reconstitution steps, post-labelling stability and expiry must be transcribed from the current package insert. Do not use historical kit details as current manufacturing specifications without confirmation.

## 10. Safety and precautions

- **Radiation exposure:** use appropriate shielding, handling controls and ALARA principles.
- **Renal clearance:** consider renal function and urinary tract exposure; encourage hydration and voiding as appropriate.
- **Hypersensitivity:** monitor for reactions to the radiopharmaceutical and formulation components.
- **Pregnancy:** justify the scan and assess foetal radiation exposure.
- **Lactation:** follow current product-specific radiation precautions.
- **Image interpretation:** normal physiologic uptake and non-NET pathology may produce findings that mimic tumour localisation.
- **Paediatric use:** apply age- and weight-appropriate protocols from current official information.

Do not infer a therapeutic toxicity profile from this diagnostic radiopharmaceutical.

## 11. Drug interactions and imaging confounders

Somatostatin analogues, including therapeutic octreotide, may alter receptor availability and tracer uptake. The timing of short-acting and long-acting analogues should follow the current product information and specialist nuclear-medicine guidance. Do not invent a universal withholding interval.

Other confounders include receptor heterogeneity, lesion size, physiologic uptake, renal function, bowel activity and acquisition technique.

## 12. Clinical evidence and diagnostic performance

OctreoScan was an established somatostatin-receptor scintigraphy agent before the adoption of modern SSTR PET agents. Diagnostic performance depends on tumour type, receptor density, lesion size, acquisition timing and the reference standard used.

Evidence should distinguish:
- patient-level from lesion-level detection;
- planar scintigraphy from SPECT performance;
- histopathology-confirmed results from composite reference standards;
- diagnostic detection from clinical outcome benefit.
