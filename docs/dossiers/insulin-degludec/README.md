**Reference product:** Tresiba (insulin degludec), U-100 and U-200

## 1. Identity and nomenclature

- **INN / active substance:** insulin degludec.
- **Reference brand:** Tresiba.
- **Molecule type:** long-acting basal human insulin analogue, chemically modified at LysB29.
- **Production:** recombinant DNA expression in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, followed by chemical modification.
- **Primary biological target:** human insulin receptor.
- **US initial approval:** 2015, according to the US prescribing information.

## 2. Sequence and structural modifications

The prescribing information identifies two modifications relative to human insulin:
1. threonine at position B30 is omitted; and
2. a side chain comprising a glutamic-acid spacer and a C16 fatty-acid moiety is attached to LysB29.

The label's chemical name is LysB29(Nε-hexadecanoyl-γ-Glu) des(B30) human insulin. The full chain sequence and exact atom-level stereochemistry should be imported from a curated sequence/chemical structure record and independently validated before being treated as canonical machine-readable sequence data.

## 4. Mechanism of action

### 4.1 Insulin receptor pharmacology

Insulin degludec binds specifically to the human insulin receptor and produces the same general pharmacological effects as human insulin. Receptor activation:
- increases glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue;
- suppresses hepatic glucose output;
- inhibits lipolysis and proteolysis; and
- promotes protein synthesis.

### 4.2 Molecular basis of prolonged action

After subcutaneous injection, insulin degludec forms soluble multi-hexamers, creating a depot in subcutaneous tissue. Monomers are released slowly from this depot into systemic circulation. The C16 fatty-acid side chain also supports reversible binding to circulating albumin, contributing to prolonged exposure. The US label describes delayed absorption from subcutaneous tissue as the predominant contributor, with albumin binding contributing to a lesser extent.

**Important distinction:** the prolonged action is primarily a delivery/absorption phenomenon; the pharmacological target remains the insulin receptor.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- Long-acting basal insulin with a relatively flat and stable glucose-lowering profile.
- The US label reports glucose-lowering activity lasting at least 42 hours at steady state in the studied dose range.
- The EU product information describes a stable profile with the glucose-lowering effect evenly distributed across the first and second 12-hour periods of a 24-hour dosing interval in the cited clamp assessment.
- Do not interpret these population study findings as a guarantee of an individual duration or as a dosing instruction.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

The product information describes:
- slow release from the subcutaneous multi-hexamer depot;
- reversible albumin binding;
- a prolonged half-life of approximately 25 hours at steady state in the US label;
- steady state after approximately 3–4 days of once-daily administration.

Complete dose-proportionality, absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination, renal/hepatic impairment, and paediatric PK details should be extracted from the current full label and recorded with the relevant study conditions.

## 7. Manufacturing and formulation

- **Expression system:** recombinant *S. cerevisiae*.
- **Post-expression modification:** chemical attachment of the LysB29 γ-Glu/C16 side chain and omission of B30 threonine.
- **Dosage forms:** sterile aqueous solution for subcutaneous injection, U-100 and U-200.
- **Reference US label pH:** approximately 7.6.
- **Labelled excipients:** glycerol, phenol, metacresol, zinc, and water for injection; zinc concentration differs between U-100 and U-200 presentations.

Do not transfer the excipient concentrations or pen/device details to other national labels without verification.

## 8. Regulatory history and indications

- **United States:** initial approval in 2015. The cited US prescribing information indicates Tresiba to improve glycaemic control in adults and paediatric patients with diabetes mellitus; consult the current label for age-specific wording and limitations.
- **European Union:** authorised centrally; the EMA product information describes insulin degludec as a long-acting insulin analogue and provides the EU indication and product particulars.
- **Jurisdiction-specific status:** this dossier does not yet contain a complete country-by-country approval and marketing-status table.

## 9. Administration and handling

The reference US product is administered subcutaneously. U-100 and U-200 presentations are not interchangeable by volume, and device-specific instructions must be followed. The full prescribing information must be consulted for missed-dose guidance, injection-site rotation, mixing restrictions, storage, in-use expiry, and paediatric administration details.

This dossier is scientific documentation, not a dosing recommendation.

## 10. Safety and precautions

Key insulin-class and product-label risks include:
- hypoglycaemia, including severe episodes;
- hypokalaemia;
- hypersensitivity reactions;
- fluid retention and heart failure risk when used with thiazolidinediones;
- medication errors, including confusion between insulin products and concentrations;
- injection-site reactions, lipodystrophy, and localized cutaneous amyloidosis.

The complete contraindications, adverse-event frequencies, warnings, pregnancy/lactation evidence, paediatric safety, overdose management, and post-marketing reports remain to be systematically extracted from current jurisdiction-specific labels.
