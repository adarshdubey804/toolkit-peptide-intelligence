## 1. Identity and classification

Insulin glargine is a long-acting recombinant human insulin analogue. The current LANTUS label identifies it as a recombinant product produced using a non-pathogenic laboratory strain of *Escherichia coli* K12. The workbook lists Lantus, Toujeo, Basaglar and Semglee as associated product names; this dossier does not imply identical formulations or devices across those products.

## 2. Sequence and molecular modifications

Relative to human insulin, insulin glargine has:
- Asparagine at A-chain position A21 replaced by glycine (A21G).
- Two arginine residues added to the C-terminus of the B-chain (B31Arg and B32Arg).

The official label describes the analogue as 21A-Gly-30B-L-Arg-30B-L-Arg-human insulin.

**Sequence note:** the complete one-letter A- and B-chain sequences and disulfide-bond mapping should be transcribed from the official structural diagram and independently cross-checked against a curated sequence record before this field is marked complete. Do not substitute the human-insulin sequence without applying and documenting these modifications.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Insulin glargine acts through insulin's established metabolic activity. The label states that insulin lowers blood glucose by:
- Stimulating peripheral glucose uptake, especially in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.
- Inhibiting hepatic glucose production.
- Inhibiting lipolysis and proteolysis.
- Enhancing protein synthesis.

This is the labelled pharmacological mechanism. Detailed receptor-binding kinetics, receptor phosphorylation, downstream IRS–PI3K–AKT signalling and tissue-specific effects require additional primary references and are not asserted here as glargine-specific findings.

## 5. Formulation-dependent prolonged action

LANTUS is formulated as an acidic solution (approximately pH 4). The label explains that insulin glargine is soluble at this acidic pH. After subcutaneous injection, neutralisation in tissue leads to formation of microprecipitates from which small amounts of insulin glargine are slowly released. This produces a relatively constant concentration/time profile without a pronounced peak and supports once-daily basal administration.

This mechanism is formulation-dependent; do not apply it indiscriminately to other insulin glargine products or concentrations without checking their labels.

## 6. Pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics

The LANTUS label reports a median end of pharmacological effect of 24 hours in the cited type 1 diabetes study; 24 hours was also the end of the observation period, and the reported range was 10.8 to greater than 24 hours. The label cautions that insulin action can vary between and within patients. Duration is not a guarantee of an exact 24-hour effect in an individual.

Detailed absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination and comparative product PK are not yet curated in this record.

## 7. Regulatory and product history

The LANTUS label states initial U.S. approval in 2000. This is the approval history for LANTUS, not a universal approval date for every insulin glargine product or presentation. Verify each product's current FDA application and label independently.

## 8. LANTUS formulation example

The current LANTUS label describes a 100 units/mL subcutaneous solution. Listed inactive ingredients include glycerol 85%, m-cresol, zinc and water for injection; the vial presentation additionally lists polysorbate 20. LANTUS has a pH of approximately 4.

These excipients are product-specific. Do not copy them to Toujeo, Basaglar or Semglee without checking the relevant current label.
