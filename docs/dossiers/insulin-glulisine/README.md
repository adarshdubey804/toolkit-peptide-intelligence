## 1. Identity and classification

Insulin glulisine is a rapid-acting recombinant human insulin analogue used to improve glycaemic control in adults and paediatric patients with diabetes mellitus. The Apidra label identifies the molecule as a recombinant human insulin analogue produced using *Escherichia coli* (K12) bacteria.

## 2. Sequence and molecular modifications

Compared with human insulin:
- Asparagine at position B3 is replaced by lysine (AsnB3Lys).
- Lysine at position B29 is replaced by glutamic acid (LysB29Glu).

These substitutions alter the molecule's physicochemical behaviour and support rapid-acting use. The exact complete A-chain and B-chain sequence, disulfide bond map and stereochemical record must be transcribed and cross-checked against an authoritative sequence/structural record before this dossier is marked sequence-complete.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Insulin glulisine has the same general glucose-lowering mechanism as human insulin:
- Increases peripheral glucose uptake, particularly in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.
- Inhibits hepatic glucose production.
- Inhibits lipolysis and proteolysis.
- Enhances protein synthesis.

The Apidra label notes that insulin activity is mediated through insulin receptors on target cells. Detailed glulisine-specific receptor affinity, receptor internalisation, IRS–PI3K–AKT signalling and tissue-specific mechanistic claims require additional primary citations and are not asserted here beyond the label.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

The label describes Apidra as a rapid-acting insulin. In a euglycaemic clamp study in healthy volunteers, the onset of glucose-lowering activity was approximately 15 minutes after subcutaneous administration; the duration was approximately 2–4 hours. These are study observations, not a guarantee of an individual's response. Timing varies with dose, injection site, local blood flow, temperature, physical activity and other patient factors.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

The label reports that insulin glulisine is absorbed more rapidly than regular human insulin after subcutaneous administration. Product-specific values for peak concentration, time to peak, bioavailability, distribution, metabolism and elimination half-life must be captured with the study population, route and study design. They are not fully reproduced in this first-pass dossier.
