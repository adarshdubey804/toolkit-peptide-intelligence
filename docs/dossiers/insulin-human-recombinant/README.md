# Insulin (human, recombinant)

> Workbook entry: **001/128** · Curation status: **in_progress**

## Overview

Human insulin is a two-chain peptide hormone produced by recombinant DNA technology and used in multiple distinct products and formulations. The workbook entry is a class-level row; this dossier does not replace individual product dossiers such as insulin aspart, glargine or lispro.

## Identity and chemistry

Mature human insulin comprises A chain (GIVEQCCTSICSLYQLENYCN; 21 residues) and B chain (FVNQHLCGSHLVEALYLVCGERGFFYTPKT; 30 residues), joined by two interchain disulfide bonds; the A chain contains one intrachain disulfide bond. Recombinant human insulin is sequence-identical to endogenous mature human insulin. Product-specific salts, concentrations and excipients must be recorded separately.

## Mechanism of action

Binds and activates the insulin receptor, a receptor tyrosine kinase. Receptor autophosphorylation and downstream IRS/PI3K-AKT and related signalling promote peripheral glucose uptake and suppress hepatic glucose production; insulin also regulates lipid and protein metabolism.

## Pharmacodynamics

Pharmacodynamic response depends on formulation, route, concentration, injection site and patient factors. Regular human insulin and rapid/long-acting analogues are not pharmacodynamically interchangeable.

## Pharmacokinetics

For Humulin R, the label identifies recombinant human insulin produced in E. coli, formula C257H383N65O77S6 and molecular weight 5,808 Da. PK must be recorded by exact product and formulation; do not apply regular-insulin values to analogues or suspensions.

## Clinical evidence

Human insulin products have extensive clinical use in diabetes. The workbook's generic row includes many brands/formulations; split product-specific indications, age groups, route and trial evidence where needed.

## Safety and tolerability

Major risks include hypoglycaemia and hypokalaemia; additional warnings and interactions are formulation/product specific. Use each current product label rather than a class-wide adverse-event table.

## Regulatory status

US initial approval for Humulin R: 1982. This is not the approval date for all insulin products; each recombinant insulin analogue and formulation has its own application and approval history.

## Formulation and manufacturing

Available as solutions, suspensions, concentrated products, premixes, cartridges and pens depending on product. Humulin R's current label lists sterile solution and recombinant E. coli production; do not generalize its excipients to other brands.

## Bioinformatics and translational research

Bioinformatics: insulin receptor signalling, insulin sequence/structure, disulfide connectivity, insulin analog comparison, metabolic pathway networks and product-specific pharmacovigilance.
