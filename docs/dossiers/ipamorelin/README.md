# Dossier 053 — Ipamorelin

**Canonical name:** Ipamorelin  
**Other identifiers:** NNC 26-0161; ipamorelin acetate (salt form used in some compounding nominations)  
**Workbook position:** Peptides sheet, row 53 (Excel row 54)  
**Curation status:** `in_progress` — core identity and mechanism are supported; clinical-development history, full safety characterization, and product-quality specifications require further primary-source verification.  
**Regulatory classification:** Investigational peptide; no FDA-approved ipamorelin drug product identified.

> **Scope and safety:** This is a scientific and regulatory dossier, not a treatment recommendation. Ipamorelin is not an FDA-approved medicine. This dossier intentionally does not provide self-administration instructions, preparation instructions, or a non-approved dosing regimen.

## 1. Executive summary

Ipamorelin is a synthetic pentapeptide growth-hormone secretagogue and ghrelin-receptor agonist. It was investigated as a potential treatment for postoperative ileus, based on ghrelin-receptor-mediated effects on gastrointestinal motility. A small phase 2 proof-of-concept randomized trial enrolled 117 patients; the primary efficacy comparison for time to tolerance of a standardized solid meal was not statistically significant. Ipamorelin has not become an approved medicine.

The peptide is also marketed online for muscle gain, fat loss, recovery, and “anti-aging.” Those promotional uses are not established indications and should not be inferred from its ability to stimulate growth hormone release in experimental settings.

FDA has identified ipamorelin acetate as a 503B Category 2 bulk drug substance that may present significant safety risks. FDA's stated concerns include potential immunogenicity from aggregation or peptide-related impurities, the analytical complexity introduced by unnatural amino acids, and serious adverse events—including deaths—reported in an intravenous gastric-motility study. FDA notes that the causal relationship is uncertain and that safety information for some other proposed injectable routes is insufficient.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

Ipamorelin is described as an agonist of GHSR1a, the ghrelin / growth hormone secretagogue receptor. Its best-characterized pharmacological action is stimulation of pituitary growth hormone release.

**Proposed signalling chain:**

1. Ipamorelin binds and activates GHSR1a.
2. GHSR1a signalling engages G-protein-dependent intracellular pathways, commonly described as Gq/11–phospholipase C signalling.
3. PLC activation generates IP₃ and DAG; IP₃-dependent calcium mobilization and related excitability pathways contribute to secretory signalling.
4. Somatotroph cells release growth hormone (GH).
5. GH can act directly and through induction of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), principally in the liver and peripheral tissues.

GHSR1a is also expressed in gastrointestinal and other tissues. Ghrelin-receptor activation can influence gastrointestinal motor function, which provided the rationale for investigating ipamorelin in postoperative ileus.

**Evidence qualification:** the receptor pharmacology is well established at the class level, but tissue-specific effects, clinical magnitude, and long-term consequences of ipamorelin exposure cannot be inferred solely from receptor activation or short-term GH measurements.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- **GH release:** Ipamorelin stimulates GH secretion in experimental and early human pharmacology studies.
- **Selectivity claim:** Early development literature described a comparatively selective GH-releasing profile versus some older growth-hormone-releasing peptides. This should not be interpreted as proof of absence of effects on cortisol, glucose regulation, appetite, or other endocrine systems.
- **Gastrointestinal effects:** Ghrelin-receptor agonism provided a rationale for pro-motility investigation in postoperative ileus.
- **IGF-1:** GH stimulation may alter downstream IGF-1; a clinically meaningful long-term effect has not been established for unapproved ipamorelin products.
- **Body composition claims:** Claims of reliable fat loss, muscle gain, anti-aging, injury recovery, or improved athletic performance are not established therapeutic benefits.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

Publicly available information is insufficient to establish a complete, regulator-reviewed human pharmacokinetic profile for a marketed product. The postoperative ileus trial used intravenous study drug, but its regimen must not be repurposed as a general-use dose.

The following require additional primary-source confirmation before being treated as definitive:
- Absolute bioavailability by route.
- Human distribution volume and tissue distribution.
- Plasma protein binding.
- Enzymatic degradation and metabolite identity.
- Renal and hepatic clearance fractions.
- Terminal half-life across clinically relevant populations.
- Exposure–response relationships and accumulation with repeated administration.

Do not extrapolate pharmacokinetics from other GHSR agonists or from unverified vendor product sheets.

## 7. Clinical development and evidence

### Postoperative ileus

A prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 2 proof-of-concept study evaluated intravenous ipamorelin in adults undergoing small- or large-bowel resection.

- ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: **NCT00672074**.
- Published study: Beck et al., *International Journal of Colorectal Disease* (2014).
- Enrolled: 117 patients; 114 were included in the safety and modified intention-to-treat populations.
- Primary efficacy endpoint: time from first study-drug dose to tolerance of a standardized solid meal.
- Reported median time: 25.3 hours in the ipamorelin group versus 32.6 hours in placebo.
- Reported p-value: 0.15; the between-group difference was not statistically significant.
- The investigators characterized the trial as a small proof-of-concept study with broad underlying patient characteristics.

**Interpretation:** this trial does not establish efficacy for postoperative ileus. The numerical difference in medians must not be represented as a proven treatment benefit. It also does not establish efficacy for unrelated indications such as weight loss, bodybuilding, or age-related GH decline.

### Evidence limitations

- No FDA-approved clinical indication.
- No established commercial prescribing information.
- Limited controlled human evidence relative to approved endocrine medicines.
- A single small proof-of-concept study cannot establish effectiveness or long-term safety.
- Online testimonials, vendor claims, and non-peer-reviewed protocols are not substitutes for controlled clinical evidence.

## 8. Safety and tolerability

### FDA-identified concerns for compounded ipamorelin acetate

FDA's Category 2 assessment for 503B compounding identifies:
- Potential immunogenicity associated with aggregation and peptide-related impurities.
- Additional complexity in peptide characterization because ipamorelin contains unnatural amino acids.
- A published study reporting serious adverse events, including deaths, in subjects who received intravenous ipamorelin for gastric-motility improvement.
- Insufficient safety-related information for certain other proposed injectable routes, leaving FDA unable to determine whether administration by those routes would cause harm.

FDA's discussion does not establish that ipamorelin caused every reported event. Causality and route-specific risk must be described accurately; absence of adequate data is not evidence of safety.

### Potential pharmacological concerns

Because ipamorelin stimulates the GH axis, relevant areas for clinical evaluation include:
- Glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity.
- Fluid retention and other GH/IGF-1-related effects.
- Endocrine feedback and changes in IGF-1.
- Gastrointestinal effects.
- Hypersensitivity and immune responses to peptide impurities or aggregates.

These are monitoring and research considerations, not a complete adverse-event frequency table. Reliable incidence estimates for unapproved formulations are unavailable.

### Contraindications and interactions

There is no approved ipamorelin label from which to extract validated contraindications, warnings, or drug-interaction instructions. Do not invent a label-equivalent list. Potential interactions with glucose-lowering therapy or other agents affecting the GH/IGF-1 axis require clinical evaluation rather than self-management.

## 9. Regulatory status and compounding

- **FDA approval:** no FDA-approved ipamorelin drug product or therapeutic indication identified.
- **Investigational history:** studied for postoperative ileus; clinical investigation did not establish efficacy in the published phase 2 proof-of-concept trial.
- **503B:** FDA lists ipamorelin acetate in Category 2 of its interim policy for bulk drug substances nominated for 503B compounding, citing potential significant safety risks.
- **503A review:** FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee materials from October 2024 describe the agency's concerns and its proposal not to add ipamorelin acetate or ipamorelin free base to the 503A bulks list. The applicable current FDA list and any subsequent policy changes must be checked before making a present-day legal determination.
- **Compounding is not approval:** a compounded preparation is not FDA-approved and is not equivalent to an FDA-reviewed drug product for quality, safety, efficacy, or labeling.
- **Research chemical sales:** availability from a vendor does not establish pharmaceutical quality, lawful human-use status, or clinical effectiveness.

This dossier does not provide legal advice. Compounding eligibility depends on the applicable statutory pathway, current FDA lists and policies, and the facts of the specific preparation.

## 10. Manufacturing, formulation, and quality

No approved commercial formulation or validated public manufacturing specification is asserted.

A scientifically adequate quality package for a synthetic peptide would ordinarily need to address:
- Confirmed amino-acid sequence and residue stereochemistry.
- Identity and peptide content.
- Related peptide impurities, deletion sequences, and truncations.
- Oxidation, deamidation, and other degradation products where relevant.
- Aggregate/particulate burden and immunogenicity risk.
- Counter-ion identity and content for salt forms.
- Residual solvents and process-related impurities.
- Water content, microbial quality, and endotoxin for parenteral products.
- Stability-indicating analytical methods and justified storage conditions.

For ipamorelin specifically, Aib and D-amino-acid residues make stereochemical and sequence-related impurity control especially important. A certificate of analysis from a seller is not, by itself, evidence of FDA approval or clinical suitability.
