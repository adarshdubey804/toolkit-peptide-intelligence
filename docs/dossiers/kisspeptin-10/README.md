# Dossier 073 — Kisspeptin-10 (KP-10)

**Workbook entry:** 073  
**Category:** Reproductive Health & Fertility  
**Workbook indication:** Reproductive hormone regulation (research)  
**Workbook regulatory status:** Not FDA-approved; research/investigational  
**Curation status:** `in_progress`

> Research dossier only. Kisspeptin-10 is not an FDA-approved fertility medicine, and experimental administration should not be interpreted as a treatment recommendation.

## 1. Identity and classification
Kisspeptin-10 (KP-10) is the C-terminal decapeptide of the human kisspeptin family, derived from the larger KISS1 precursor. Endogenous kisspeptins include KP-54, KP-14, KP-13 and KP-10; their shared C-terminal region is required for activation of KISS1R (also called GPR54). KP-10 is studied as a neuroendocrine regulator upstream of hypothalamic GnRH neurons.

## 2. Sequence and chemistry
**Human KP-10 sequence:** `YNWNSFGLRF-NH2` (Tyr-Asn-Trp-Asn-Ser-Phe-Gly-Leu-Arg-Phe-amide).  
Length: 10 residues; C-terminal amidation is a defining feature. It is a linear peptide, not a disulfide-cyclized peptide. Do not conflate the native decapeptide with longer kisspeptin-54 or with synthetic analogues carrying non-native substitutions. Molecular formula and mass should be generated from a stereochemically validated structure record before being stored as definitive.

## 3. Mechanism of action
1. KP-10 binds and activates KISS1R/GPR54, a class A GPCR.
2. KISS1R signalling predominantly engages Gq/11 and phospholipase C.
3. PLC signalling generates IP3 and DAG, mobilizing intracellular calcium and activating downstream kinase pathways.
4. Kisspeptin signalling stimulates hypothalamic GnRH neuronal activity and GnRH release.
5. GnRH acts on pituitary gonadotrophs to regulate LH and FSH secretion, thereby influencing gonadal steroidogenesis and reproductive function.

Kisspeptin is upstream of GnRH; it is not itself a gonadotropin and does not directly replace LH or FSH. Receptor activation and the exact downstream neuronal circuitry vary with species, sex, hormonal milieu, pulse pattern, and experimental paradigm.

## 4. Pharmacodynamics
Human studies demonstrate that kisspeptin administration can alter LH secretion, with effects dependent on peptide form, route, dose, sex, menstrual-cycle phase, and baseline reproductive endocrine state. KP-54 generally has a longer circulating half-life than KP-10 and may be more suitable for bolus exposure in some human research paradigms. Do not transfer KP-54 PK values to KP-10.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics
KP-10 is a short-lived peptide subject to proteolytic degradation. Published human estimates vary by assay, route and study design. A single universal terminal half-life should not be asserted without specifying study conditions. Bioavailability by oral administration is not established as a practical therapeutic route. Distribution, metabolism, clearance, and exposure-response parameters remain incompletely standardized across studies.

## 6. Clinical and translational evidence
Research areas include:
- Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and reproductive-axis activation.
- Functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea and disorders of GnRH pulsatility.
- Assisted reproduction and controlled ovarian stimulation research.
- Polycystic ovary syndrome and other reproductive endocrine phenotypes.
- Human neuroendocrine studies of LH/FSH response.

Evidence is heterogeneous and often consists of small mechanistic studies rather than adequately powered efficacy trials. A rise in LH is a pharmacodynamic signal, not proof of pregnancy, live-birth, or durable restoration of fertility. No FDA-approved KP-10 drug product or established clinical indication is recorded.

## 7. Safety
There is no approved product label defining contraindications, common adverse reactions, reproductive safety, or long-term risks for KP-10. Small experimental studies cannot establish safety for unsupervised use. Potential endocrine effects depend on baseline physiology and exposure; the risk profile of research-grade material, compounded preparations, and investigational formulations cannot be assumed equivalent.

## 8. Bioinformatics and pathway mapping
- Precursor gene: **KISS1**.
- Receptor: **KISS1R / GPR54**.
- Upstream signalling: Gq/11–PLCβ–IP3/DAG–Ca2+.
- Neuroendocrine axis: kisspeptin → GnRH neurons → pituitary LH/FSH → gonadal steroidogenesis.
- Capture species, sex, menstrual-cycle phase, hormonal background, route, sampling schedule, assay, and peptide form for every study.

## 9. Regulatory status
KP-10 is a research peptide and is not FDA-approved as a medicine. A research paper, supplier catalogue, or compounded-product listing is not evidence of marketing authorization. No approved human therapeutic product label or FDA application for KP-10 has been identified in the curated sources.
