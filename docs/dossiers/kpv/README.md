# Dossier 105 — KPV (α-MSH C-terminal Tripeptide)

**Workbook entry:** 105 | **Category:** Immunology, inflammation & transplant | **FDA approval:** No | **Status:** Research peptide; not an FDA-approved therapeutic  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and sequence
KPV is the tripeptide Lys-Pro-Val (one-letter sequence `KPV`), corresponding to the C-terminal tripeptide motif of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH; residues 11–13). It is a short linear peptide and is not the full α-MSH hormone. Do not confuse KPV with the N-terminal melanocortin receptor-active motif of α-MSH or with other melanocortin peptides.

## Proposed mechanism and biology
KPV has been investigated for anti-inflammatory effects in cellular and animal models, including models of intestinal inflammation and skin inflammation. Proposed activities include modulation of inflammatory cytokines and epithelial/immune signalling. The precise molecular target and receptor dependence remain unresolved; it should not automatically be annotated as a proven MC1R agonist or as having the same pharmacology as full-length α-MSH. The evidence is predominantly preclinical.

## Pharmacology and translational limitations
As a short peptide, KPV is susceptible to enzymatic degradation and has uncertain systemic oral bioavailability. Experimental findings depend on formulation, route, model and local tissue exposure. Topical, oral and parenteral research formulations cannot be assumed bioequivalent. There is no validated human PK, dose-response or clinical exposure target for an approved medicinal product.

## Clinical evidence
KPV is researched in inflammatory bowel disease models, mucosal inflammation and dermatological contexts. Preclinical results do not establish clinical efficacy in Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, psoriasis or other inflammatory disorders. No FDA-approved KPV medicinal product or established therapeutic indication exists.

## Safety
A standardized clinical safety database is not available. Potential concerns include immunological effects, hypersensitivity, impurities, contamination and unknown systemic exposure. Research-grade or compounded products may have variable identity, purity and sterility. Do not imply established human safety.

## Formulation and quality
Research material should be characterized for sequence identity, purity, counterion, peptide content, degradation products, aggregation, residual solvents, endotoxin, microbial contamination and stability. Route-specific GMP development and toxicology would be required before clinical claims.

## Regulatory status
Not FDA-approved. The workbook's research/compounding status must not be upgraded based on vendor availability, patents, social-media reports or animal studies. Compounding does not establish FDA approval or efficacy.

## Evidence gaps
1. Curate primary preclinical studies and distinguish in-vitro from in-vivo findings.
2. Establish target engagement and receptor dependence with orthogonal assays.
3. Determine proteolytic stability, local/systemic PK and formulation effects.
4. Identify any registered human studies and verify outcomes; do not infer a trial from a keyword match.
5. Keep `in_progress` as research-only, pending reproducible translational evidence.
