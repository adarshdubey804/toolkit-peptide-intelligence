## 2. Molecular identity and sequence

Lanreotide is a synthetic cyclic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin.

**Sequence:** H-D-β-Nal-Cys-Tyr-D-Trp-Lys-Val-Cys-Thr-NH₂

- Eight amino-acid-derived residues.
- Contains D-β-naphthylalanine (D-β-Nal) and D-tryptophan.
- One intramolecular disulfide bond between the two cysteine residues.
- C-terminal amide.
- Molecular formula (free peptide): C₅₄H₆₉N₁₁O₁₀S₂.
- Molecular weight: approximately 1096.3 g/mol (average molecular mass; verify basis against official chemistry record).
- The commercial depot is lanreotide acetate in a prolonged-release aqueous formulation.

Preserve D stereochemistry, β-naphthylalanine identity and disulfide connectivity in sequence and chemical structure records. Do not represent the drug as an unmodified natural somatostatin sequence.

## 3. Structure and formulation biology

Lanreotide is a conformationally constrained cyclic peptide. Its receptor-binding pharmacophore includes non-proteinogenic D-β-Nal and D-Trp residues. The disulfide bond stabilises the cyclic structure and the terminal amide contributes to peptide stability.

Somatuline Depot is a ready-to-use, supersaturated aqueous formulation that forms a depot after deep subcutaneous injection. It is not the same dosage form as octreotide LAR microspheres; release and handling instructions are product-specific.

## 4. Disease biology

### Acromegaly
Most acromegaly is caused by a GH-secreting pituitary adenoma. Excess GH drives hepatic and peripheral IGF-1 production, causing tissue overgrowth and cardiometabolic, respiratory and skeletal complications. Somatostatin receptor activation on responsive pituitary tumour cells suppresses GH secretion and reduces IGF-1.

### GEP-NETs
Gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumours arise from neuroendocrine cells of the GI tract or pancreas. Some express somatostatin receptors and secrete hormones or biogenic amines. Tumour growth and hormone-related symptoms may be controlled in selected patients with somatostatin analogues.

### Carcinoid syndrome
Serotonin and other vasoactive mediators released by functional NETs can cause flushing, diarrhoea, bronchospasm and cardiac complications. Somatostatin receptor agonism reduces secretion of these mediators in responsive disease.

## 5. Mechanism of action

Lanreotide binds somatostatin receptors, with high affinity for SSTR2 and lower affinity for other subtypes, including SSTR5. Relative affinity depends on assay conditions and receptor system.

1. Receptor engagement activates Gi/o-coupled signalling.
2. Adenylyl cyclase activity and intracellular cAMP are reduced.
3. Calcium-dependent secretory processes and hormone release are inhibited.
4. GH and selected gastrointestinal/pancreatic hormones are suppressed in receptor-responsive tissues.

For GEP-NETs, the antiproliferative clinical effect is not fully reducible to acute hormone suppression; do not claim a single fully established tumour-growth mechanism.

## 6. Approved indications and boundaries

**US SOMATULINE DEPOT label:**
- Long-term treatment of acromegaly in patients with inadequate response to or who cannot be treated with surgery and/or radiotherapy.
- Treatment of adult patients with unresectable, well- or moderately differentiated, locally advanced or metastatic GEP-NETs to improve progression-free survival.
- Treatment of adults with carcinoid syndrome when therapy with a somatostatin analogue is required to reduce the frequency of short-acting somatostatin analogue use.

The precise indication wording and any limitations should be checked against the current US label. Do not automatically transfer US indications to Somatuline Autogel labels in other countries.

## 7. Administration and dosing

For US Somatuline Depot, the labelled regimen is indication- and response-dependent:
- Acromegaly: commonly initiated at 90 mg by deep subcutaneous injection every four weeks, then adjusted using GH/IGF-1 and clinical response; lower or higher labelled doses may be used.
- GEP-NETs: 120 mg by deep subcutaneous injection every four weeks.
- Carcinoid syndrome: 120 mg by deep subcutaneous injection every four weeks.

The above is a concise label-oriented summary, not patient-specific dosing advice. Follow the current label for injection site, administration technique, dose adjustment, rescue short-acting therapy and monitoring.

## 8. Pharmacodynamics

- Suppresses GH secretion and can reduce circulating IGF-1 in acromegaly.
- Reduces secretion of selected GI and pancreatic hormones.
- In GEP-NETs, the pivotal antiproliferative evidence is based on progression-free survival rather than symptom reduction alone.
- In carcinoid syndrome, reduces flushing and diarrhoea in some patients and can reduce the need for short-acting rescue analogue use.

Monitor indication-specific outcomes: GH and age-adjusted IGF-1 in acromegaly; radiographic disease and clinical course in GEP-NETs; frequency/severity of flushing and diarrhoea in carcinoid syndrome.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics

After deep-SC administration of the depot formulation, lanreotide is released over an extended interval, supporting monthly dosing. Exposure is prolonged compared with immediate-release peptides. The US label describes nonlinear pharmacokinetics in some settings; parameters depend on dose, repeated administration and disease population.

Do not transfer half-life or exposure estimates from immediate-release lanreotide or other somatostatin analogues to Somatuline Depot. Extract numerical PK values from the current label with the relevant study/population and sampling interval.

## 10. Safety and monitoring

Important label risks and precautions include:
- **Cholelithiasis and gallbladder complications:** gallstones, biliary sludge, cholecystitis, cholangitis and pancreatitis have been reported.
- **Glucose abnormalities:** hyperglycaemia or hypoglycaemia may occur through changes in insulin and glucagon secretion; monitor glucose and adjust antidiabetic treatment as needed.
- **Cardiac effects:** bradycardia and other heart-rate changes may occur, particularly with underlying cardiac disease or rate-limiting medicines.
- **Thyroid dysfunction:** hypothyroidism and altered thyroid function have been reported; assess thyroid function as clinically appropriate.
- **Steatorrhoea and pancreatic exocrine insufficiency:** consider evaluation when persistent diarrhoea, weight loss or fatty stools develop.
- **Injection-site reactions:** pain, nodules and local reactions.
- **Hypersensitivity:** serious allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, are possible.

Refer to current label for contraindications, frequencies, monitoring recommendations and post-marketing events.

## 11. Interactions

Potential interactions include:
- additive bradycardia with beta-blockers and other heart-rate-lowering drugs;
- altered insulin or oral antidiabetic requirements;
- altered absorption or exposure of concomitant medicines due to effects on gastrointestinal motility;
- potential effects on cyclosporine exposure.

Check current product information for specific interaction data and manage based on the patient's medication profile.

## 12. Formulation, manufacturing and quality

Somatuline Depot is a sterile, ready-to-use, prolonged-release lanreotide acetate formulation supplied in prefilled syringes for deep subcutaneous administration. It forms a depot at the injection site. It is not an octreotide-style polymer microsphere product.

Critical quality attributes include:
- peptide identity, purity and stereochemical integrity;
- D-β-Nal and D-Trp configuration;
- correct disulfide connectivity and terminal amidation;
- peptide-related impurities and aggregation;
- acetate content and formulation composition;
- syringeability, viscosity and injection force;
- depot formation and in-vitro release profile;
- sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter and container-closure integrity.
