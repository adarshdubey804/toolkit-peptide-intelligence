# Dossier 077 — Larazotide (AT-1001)

**Workbook entry:** 077  
**Category:** Gastrointestinal  
**Workbook indication:** Celiac disease  
**Workbook regulatory status:** Investigational; not FDA-approved  
**Curation status:** `in_progress`

> Larazotide is investigational. It is not an approved treatment for celiac disease and must not be presented as a substitute for a gluten-free diet or specialist care.

## 1. Identity and classification
Larazotide acetate (AT-1001) is an orally administered synthetic peptide investigated as an adjunctive therapy intended to reduce intestinal permeability associated with gluten exposure in celiac disease. It is not a gluten-digesting enzyme and does not remove gluten from food. No FDA-approved product exists.

## 2. Sequence and chemistry
Larazotide is described in development literature as an eight-amino-acid peptide (an analogue of the Vibrio cholerae zonula occludens toxin-derived peptide) and administered as an acetate salt. Exact residue sequence, stereochemistry, terminal modifications, salt stoichiometry, formula and molecular mass must be verified from primary patent/CMC literature before database release. Do not infer sequence from the parent toxin or from supplier catalogues.

## 3. Proposed mechanism of action
Larazotide was developed to modulate intestinal tight-junction permeability. Proposed actions include interference with zonulin-associated signalling and reduction of gliadin-peptide-induced tight-junction opening, thereby limiting paracellular passage of immunogenic gluten peptides. The exact molecular target and mechanism remain incompletely established; avoid describing it as a definitively validated zonulin receptor antagonist.

The proposed therapeutic concept is barrier modulation, not immune tolerance induction and not direct neutralization of gluten-specific T-cell responses.

## 4. Pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics
The intended action is local within the intestinal lumen/epithelium, with low systemic exposure expected after oral administration. Human PK and exposure-response data are limited and formulation-dependent. The exact systemic bioavailability, metabolism, and elimination profile should be taken from full clinical pharmacology publications or sponsor reports, not inferred from peptide size.

## 5. Clinical evidence
Early phase studies evaluated safety, tolerability and effects on intestinal permeability or symptoms following gluten challenge. A phase 2b study evaluated multiple larazotide doses in adults with celiac disease on a gluten-free diet and reported a signal in symptom outcomes at a selected low dose, but did not establish histological mucosal healing or a definitive disease-modifying benefit.

A subsequent phase 3 programme did not establish the efficacy required for approval; development was discontinued/not advanced to a regulatory filing. Exact trial names, registry IDs, endpoint definitions, effect estimates and discontinuation history must be reconciled against the clinical trial registry and primary publications before stronger claims are made.

## 6. Safety
Larazotide has no approved label, so there is no regulator-defined contraindication or postmarketing safety profile. Published trials generally describe tolerability in studied adult cohorts, but this cannot establish long-term safety or safety in children, pregnancy, or other populations. Research-grade or compounded material should not be assumed equivalent to trial material.

## 7. Regulatory status
Larazotide acetate remains investigational and is not FDA-approved for celiac disease. It should be distinguished from approved management of celiac disease, for which a strict gluten-free diet remains foundational. A development programme or trial registration does not confer marketing authorization.
