# Dossier 025 — Leuprolide (leuprorelin; leuprolide acetate)

**Curation status:** in progress — molecular identity and core GnRH pharmacology established; product-by-product indications, global regulatory status, formulation-specific PK, safety and CMC still require full extraction.  
**Asset class:** synthetic nonapeptide GnRH analogue.  
**Pharmacology:** gonadotropin-releasing hormone receptor (GnRHR) agonist; continuous exposure produces pituitary receptor downregulation and gonadal steroid suppression.  
**US brand examples:** Lupron Depot, Lupron Depot-PED, Eligard, Fensolvi, Camcevi (products differ in formulation, delivery system and approved indications).  
**US initial approval:** 1985 for leuprolide products; Lupron Depot label states 1989 for that product. Do not collapse product-specific approval dates.

## 1. Identity and classification

Leuprolide is a synthetic nonapeptide analogue of endogenous gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH, also called LHRH). It is used in multiple endocrine and oncology settings. The active peptide is commonly supplied as leuprolide acetate; depot products use distinct release technologies and are not interchangeable solely by nominal milligram strength.

PubChem independently lists the peptide formula and molecular weight. [1–3]

## 2. Mechanism of action

Leuprolide is an agonist at pituitary GnRH receptors. Its biological effect depends critically on the exposure pattern:

1. **Initial agonism / flare:** GnRHR activation initially stimulates pituitary gonadotrophs, increasing luteinising hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). Gonadal testosterone or oestradiol may transiently rise.
2. **Continuous exposure:** sustained agonist exposure causes GnRHR desensitisation/downregulation and reduces pituitary LH/FSH release.
3. **Downstream endocrine suppression:** reduced LH/FSH lowers testicular testosterone and ovarian oestradiol production. Suppression is generally reversible after treatment is stopped, although recovery timing depends on patient and product context.

This is why a GnRH agonist can ultimately suppress gonadal steroid production despite being a receptor agonist. FDA-approved labeling explicitly describes the initial stimulation followed by suppression during continuous administration. [1,2]

## 3. Molecular structure and structure–activity features

Leuprolide differs from native mammalian GnRH at key positions:
- Position 6: glycine is replaced by **D-leucine**.
- Position 10: the native glycinamide terminus is removed and the peptide is terminated as **proline ethylamide**.
- N-terminus: pyroglutamyl (pGlu) residue.
- The resulting molecule is a nonapeptide with sequence pGlu-His-Trp-Ser-Tyr-D-Leu-Leu-Arg-Pro-NHEt.

The D-amino-acid substitution and terminal modification are associated with increased potency and altered enzymatic stability relative to native GnRH. Do not describe the peptide as a decapeptide: the active leuprolide sequence contains nine residues. [2,3,4]

## 4. Receptor pharmacology and pharmacodynamics

The primary pharmacological target is the pituitary GnRH receptor, a class A GPCR. Receptor activation engages Gq/11-associated phospholipase-C signalling, intracellular calcium and protein kinase pathways involved in gonadotropin synthesis and secretion. Continuous therapeutic exposure leads to receptor desensitisation/downregulation and endocrine suppression.

A complete quantitative receptor profile (binding affinity, functional EC50, intrinsic efficacy, species comparisons and signalling bias) is not asserted here because a single authoritative primary dataset was not established in this curation pass.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics and delivery technologies

Leuprolide pharmacokinetics are formulation-dependent. Products include:
- immediate-release aqueous subcutaneous injection;
- intramuscular depot suspensions with monthly, 3-month, 4-month or 6-month release schedules;
- paediatric depot products with age- and indication-specific presentations;
- subcutaneous depot implants or long-acting delivery systems under separate brand names.

Do not transfer PK values between these products. Depot absorption is governed by the formulation and release system, not merely the peptide's intrinsic clearance. The current Lupron Depot label specifies different strengths and intervals for the 1-, 3-, 4- and 6-month products. [1]

A product-specific PK extraction remains required for Cmax, Tmax, terminal half-life, exposure profile, bioavailability, depot release, accumulation and endocrine recovery. The plasma terminal half-life of the released peptide should not be confused with the duration of a depot product's clinical effect.

## 6. Clinical uses and product-specific indications

Leuprolide products are approved for several indications, but each label is specific to the brand, formulation, age group and jurisdiction. US product families include:
- **Advanced prostate cancer:** Lupron Depot and other leuprolide products.
- **Central precocious puberty:** Lupron Depot-PED and Fensolvi presentations.
- **Endometriosis:** selected Lupron Depot presentations, with add-back therapy and duration limits specified in the label.
- **Uterine fibroids:** selected leuprolide depot presentations, often as preoperative treatment and with product-specific restrictions.
- **Assisted reproduction / controlled ovarian stimulation:** leuprolide injection is used in certain protocols; this is not the same indication as every depot product.

This list is a product-family overview, not a substitute for checking the current label for a named product. The current Lupron Depot label opened for this dossier specifically identifies advanced prostate cancer as its indication; paediatric and other product labels must be evaluated separately. [1,2]

## 7. Regulatory status

**United States:** FDA-approved leuprolide products exist. FDA label records show product-specific initial approval dates; Lupron Depot states initial US approval in 1989, while other leuprolide presentations have earlier dates. [1,2,5]

**International:** leuprorelin/leuprolide products are authorised in multiple jurisdictions, but indication, depot technology, brand, strength and age limits differ. This dossier does not claim universal approval or a single global label. Each national approval should be stored as a regulator/product-specific record.

**Important distinction:** an active ingredient's long history of approval does not mean every newer formulation, route, strength or indication is automatically approved.

## 8. Safety and tolerability

Safety is strongly linked to the degree and duration of sex-steroid suppression and to the initial hormone flare. Label-specific warnings and precautions include:
- transient worsening of disease-related symptoms during the initial hormonal flare (particularly relevant to prostate cancer);
- hot flashes, sweating, fatigue and other hypogonadal symptoms;
- decreased bone mineral density with prolonged sex-steroid suppression;
- metabolic changes and potential cardiovascular risk considerations;
- QT/QTc prolongation considerations in susceptible patients;
- mood changes/depression and rare convulsions reported in product labeling;
- injection-site reactions and formulation/device-specific complications;
- paediatric-specific effects and monitoring for central precocious puberty products.

For prostate cancer, clinicians may consider flare protection depending on the clinical situation and label. This dossier does not provide patient-specific treatment or dosing advice. Consult the exact current product label for contraindications, warnings, monitoring and duration restrictions. [1,2]

## 9. Formulation, administration and product quality

Leuprolide is marketed in different dosage forms, including aqueous injection and polymer-based or other depot systems. Product composition, reconstitution, route, needle/device, storage, shelf life and release characteristics are formulation-specific. For example, the current Lupron Depot label describes several intramuscular depot strengths with distinct release intervals; aqueous leuprolide acetate injection is a separate sterile solution product. [1,6]
