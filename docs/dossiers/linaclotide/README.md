# Dossier 074 — Linaclotide (Linzess)

**Workbook entry:** 074  
**Category:** Gastrointestinal  
**Workbook indication:** IBS with constipation; chronic idiopathic constipation  
**FDA approval:** 2012  
**Curation status:** `in_progress`

## 1. Identity and classification
Linaclotide is an orally administered, locally acting 14-amino-acid peptide and guanylate cyclase-C (GC-C) agonist. It is structurally related to endogenous guanylin and uroguanylin. US brand: Linzess. It is minimally absorbed systemically under recommended use; therapeutic action occurs primarily at the luminal intestinal epithelium.

## 2. Sequence and chemistry
Label-defined sequence: `CCELCCNPAC TGCY` (spacing omitted: `CCELCCNPACTGCY`).  
Chemical name: L-cysteinyl-L-cysteinyl-L-glutamyl-L-tyrosyl-L-cysteinyl-L-cysteinyl-L-asparaginyl-L-prolyl-L-alanyl-L-cysteinyl-L-threonyl-glycyl-L-cysteinyl-L-tyrosine, cyclic (1–6), (2–10), (5–13)-tris(disulfide).  
Formula: C59H79N15O21S6. Molecular weight: approximately 1526.8 Da. Three disulfide bonds stabilize the peptide. Linaclotide is supplied as coated beads in hard gelatin capsules; strengths include 72, 145 and 290 mcg.

## 3. Mechanism of action
1. Linaclotide and its active metabolite bind GC-C on the luminal surface of intestinal epithelial cells.
2. GC-C activation increases intracellular and extracellular cGMP.
3. Intracellular cGMP activates CFTR, promoting chloride and bicarbonate secretion into the lumen.
4. Sodium and water follow osmotically, increasing intestinal fluid and accelerating transit.
5. Extracellular cGMP may reduce activity of pain-sensing afferents in animal models; the clinical contribution of this pathway is less firmly established than the secretory mechanism.

Target gene: **GUCY2C**. Downstream ion channel: **CFTR**.

## 4. Pharmacodynamics
The principal effect is increased intestinal secretion and transit. In IBS-C, improvement in abdominal symptoms is also observed clinically, but the precise mechanism for visceral pain relief is not fully established. The active metabolite is produced by proteolytic cleavage in the intestinal lumen and retains GC-C agonist activity.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics
Systemic exposure is negligible at therapeutic doses; plasma concentrations are generally below quantification limits. Linaclotide is degraded within the gastrointestinal tract to smaller peptides and amino acids. The active metabolite is also locally active. Conventional systemic half-life and volume-of-distribution values are not meaningful for the usual clinical exposure profile. Renal and hepatic impairment are not expected to materially alter exposure because systemic absorption is minimal, but use must follow the current label.

## 6. Clinical evidence
FDA-approved for adults with IBS-C and chronic idiopathic constipation (CIC); paediatric indications have subsequently been added for selected age groups and strengths. The adult registration programme included randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials using composite bowel-movement and abdominal-pain endpoints for IBS-C and spontaneous bowel movement endpoints for CIC. Treatment effects should be quoted by indication and trial rather than merged into one response rate. Current label tables provide the definitive trial-specific responder definitions and rates.

## 7. Safety
Diarrhoea is the principal adverse reaction and may be severe. The current US label carries a boxed warning/contraindication against use in paediatric patients younger than 2 years because of risk of serious dehydration; use is contraindicated in patients with known or suspected mechanical gastrointestinal obstruction. In younger children, age-specific restrictions apply. Other reported reactions include abdominal pain, flatulence and abdominal distension. Avoid extrapolating adult safety rates to paediatric populations.

## 8. Formulation and quality
Oral capsule contains linaclotide-coated beads. Strength-specific inactive ingredients differ. Critical quality attributes include peptide identity, disulfide connectivity, purity, potency, bead coating/content uniformity, dissolution/release characteristics, moisture, microbial quality and capsule integrity. The low microgram dose makes content uniformity especially important.

## 9. Regulatory status
Initial US approval: August 30, 2012. Current label includes IBS-C and CIC and age-specific paediatric use. Verify current indication age bands and strengths in the live label before downstream clinical display. Approval is product- and jurisdiction-specific.
