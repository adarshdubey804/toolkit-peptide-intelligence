# Liraglutide (Victoza / Saxenda) — dossier 009

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Entity class:** 31-amino-acid, fatty-acylated GLP-1 receptor agonist peptide analogue.  
**Reference products:** Victoza (type 2 diabetes indication); Saxenda (chronic weight-management indication); other regional liraglutide presentations must be separately checked.  
**Scope warning:** Same active molecule, but product-specific indication, strength, device, age range, dosing instructions, and label warnings differ. Do not merge product labels into one undifferentiated record.

## 1. Identity and nomenclature

- **INN:** liraglutide.
- **Drug class:** glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist.
- **Peptide length:** 31 amino acids.
- **Molecular design:** human GLP-1(7–37) analogue with Lys34 replaced by Arg and a C16 fatty-acid side chain attached to Lys26 through a glutamic-acid spacer.
- **Primary target:** GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R).
- **Reference brands:** Victoza and Saxenda.
- **US approval history:** Victoza approved in 2010; Saxenda approved in 2014.

## 2. Sequence and structural modifications

Liraglutide is a modified analogue of human GLP-1. The design includes:
1. Arg substitution for Lys at position 34 (relative to the GLP-1(7–37) reference numbering); and
2. attachment of a palmitic-acid (C16) side chain to Lys26 through a γ-glutamyl spacer.

The sequence is commonly represented as:

`HAEGTFTSDVSSYLEGQAAKEFIAWLVRGRG`

The chain contains 31 amino acids. The complete chemical representation must include the Lys26 side-chain acylation, the γ-Glu linker, and the precise numbering convention. A plain one-letter sequence does not fully represent liraglutide's molecular identity.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Liraglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist with approximately 97% amino-acid sequence homology to native human GLP-1. GLP-1R activation:
1. increases glucose-dependent insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells;
2. suppresses inappropriately elevated glucagon secretion;
3. slows gastric emptying; and
4. increases satiety and reduces appetite and energy intake.

Liraglutide's fatty-acid side chain promotes self-association and reversible albumin binding, slowing absorption and reducing renal clearance. These properties contribute to once-daily pharmacological activity. The pharmacological target remains GLP-1R.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- Enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion.
- Suppresses inappropriate glucagon secretion.
- Delays gastric emptying.
- Reduces postprandial and fasting glucose.
- In weight-management studies, reduces appetite and energy intake and supports body-weight reduction.
- Effects and approved outcomes must be reported separately for the Victoza and Saxenda product indications.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

The US product information describes:
- subcutaneous bioavailability of approximately 55%;
- time to maximum concentration of approximately 8–12 hours;
- apparent volume of distribution after subcutaneous administration approximately 0.12 L/kg;
- plasma protein binding greater than 98%, mainly to albumin;
- elimination half-life approximately 13 hours;
- metabolism through general protein catabolism pathways, with no single organ identified as the major elimination route.

These are label-reported values; preserve study conditions and formulation/label version when structuring them.

## 7. Manufacturing and formulation

- **Production:** recombinant DNA technology using *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*.
- **Reference dosage form:** sterile solution for subcutaneous injection.
- **Victoza reference strength:** 6 mg/mL multidose pen.
- **Saxenda reference strength:** 6 mg/mL multidose pen.
- **Formulation:** aqueous solution with product-specific excipients and pH.
- Product-specific device instructions, storage, in-use expiry, and excipient concentrations must be extracted from each current label separately.

## 8. Regulatory history and indications

- **Victoza (US):** FDA-approved in 2010 to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes, with later label changes and cardiovascular-risk-reduction language for the indicated population. Paediatric indication details must be checked against the current label.
- **Saxenda (US):** FDA-approved in 2014 for chronic weight management in eligible adults and, following later label changes, selected paediatric patients. Exact BMI criteria, age range, and stopping rules must be copied from the current label rather than inferred.
- **Regulatory distinction:** Victoza and Saxenda are not interchangeable in their approved indications or label directions, despite containing liraglutide.
- Non-US status and marketed presentations remain to be checked country by country.

## 9. Administration and handling

Both reference products are subcutaneous injections, but their dose escalation, target doses, indications, and device instructions differ. Do not combine the product-specific dosing tables. This dossier is scientific documentation, not a dosing recommendation.

## 10. Safety and precautions

Important label safety issues include:
- nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, and other gastrointestinal effects;
- acute pancreatitis warning;
- gallbladder disease, including cholelithiasis and cholecystitis;
- hypoglycaemia, particularly with concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea;
- increased heart rate;
- dehydration-related acute kidney injury;
- hypersensitivity reactions;
- delayed gastric emptying and aspiration-related considerations for procedures involving anaesthesia/deep sedation, where described in current label updates;
- thyroid C-cell tumour warning and contraindication language for people with a personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2, as specified in US labels.

Victoza and Saxenda labels can differ in warnings and population-specific details. Extract contraindications, adverse-event frequencies, pregnancy/lactation information, paediatric safety, and post-marketing reports separately for each product.

## 11. Drug interactions

Delayed gastric emptying may affect absorption of concomitant oral medicines. Hypoglycaemia risk may increase with insulin or insulin secretagogues. A full interaction matrix is pending; record the affected medicine/class, mechanism, clinical consequence, management advice, label version, and source.
