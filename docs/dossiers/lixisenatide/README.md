# Lixisenatide (Adlyxin / Lyxumia) — dossier 013

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Entity class:** synthetic, C-terminally amidated 44-amino-acid exendin-4 analogue with a C-terminal hexalysine extension.  
**Pharmacology:** short-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist with a pronounced effect on postprandial glucose, particularly after the first meal following administration.  
**Product separation:** Adlyxin (US), Lyxumia (EU) and lixisenatide-containing fixed-ratio combination products such as Suliqua/Soliqua must be distinct product records. The combination product contains insulin glargine as a second active substance.

## 2. Sequence and structural identity

Lixisenatide is an exendin-4-derived peptide. It is described as a 44-amino-acid peptide formed by deletion of the C-terminal proline of exendin-4 and addition of six lysine residues at the C-terminus. The C-terminal residue is amidated.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Lixisenatide is a selective GLP-1 receptor agonist. GLP-1R is a class B G-protein-coupled receptor expressed in pancreatic beta cells and other tissues.

Its label-described pharmacological effects include:
- glucose-dependent enhancement of insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells;
- reduction of glucagon secretion when glucose concentrations are elevated;
- slowing of gastric emptying;
- reduction of postprandial glucose excursions, with a particularly marked effect after the first meal following injection.

As glucose falls, the insulinotropic effect diminishes. Hypoglycaemia risk is nevertheless increased when lixisenatide is combined with insulin or insulin secretagogues.

## 5. Pharmacological profile and short-acting behaviour

Lixisenatide is generally characterised as a short-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist. Its most prominent glycaemic effect is reduction of postprandial glucose, in part through delayed gastric emptying. The gastric-emptying effect is strongest after the first dose/meal and attenuates with continued administration; it should not be described as a uniform, constant effect across all meals.

The six-lysine C-terminal extension distinguishes lixisenatide from native exendin-4 and contributes to its physicochemical properties. Unlike long-acting, fatty-acid-acylated GLP-1 analogues, lixisenatide does not rely on a covalent albumin-binding fatty-acid side chain for once-weekly exposure.

## 6. Pharmacodynamics

The US label reports, in adults with type 2 diabetes after a standardised test meal:
- placebo-adjusted reduction in fasting plasma glucose of approximately 33.8 mg/dL;
- reduction in postprandial glucose AUC over 0–300 minutes of approximately 387 mg·h/dL;
- reduction in postprandial glucagon AUC over 0–300 minutes of approximately 15.6 h·pmol/L after 20 mcg once daily.

The postprandial glucose effect was most notable after the first meal and attenuated with later meals in the day. These are study-specific pharmacodynamic findings and must retain the study context when entered into structured data.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics

EMA product information reports:
- median time to maximum plasma concentration after subcutaneous administration: approximately 1–3.5 hours;
- plasma protein binding: approximately 55%;
- apparent volume of distribution after subcutaneous administration: approximately 100 L;
- mean terminal half-life after multiple dosing: approximately 3 hours;
- mean apparent clearance: approximately 35 L/hour;
- elimination through glomerular filtration, tubular reabsorption and subsequent metabolic degradation into smaller peptides and amino acids.

Renal impairment increases exposure. EMA product information reports AUC increases of approximately 46%, 51% and 87% in mild, moderate and severe renal impairment groups, respectively, in the cited studies. The US label does not recommend use in end-stage renal disease and advises monitoring in severe renal impairment. Exact eGFR/creatinine-clearance definitions and label wording must be retained with these values.

Lixisenatide is not primarily cleared by hepatic metabolism. The EMA product information notes that no dedicated pharmacokinetic study was performed in hepatic impairment.

## 9. Formulation and product presentations

### Adlyxin (United States)
The FDA label describes a sterile, clear, colourless solution for subcutaneous use in 3 mL single-patient-use prefilled pens:
- 50 mcg/mL pen (green), delivering 10 mcg per dose;
- 100 mcg/mL pen (burgundy), delivering 20 mcg per dose.

Label-listed inactive ingredients include glycerol 85%, metacresol, methionine, sodium acetate trihydrate and water for injection. Hydrochloric acid and/or sodium hydroxide may be used for pH adjustment. Label pH is approximately 4.5.

### Lyxumia (European Union)
EU product information describes a solution for injection in pre-filled pens, with product-specific strengths and administration instructions. The EU authorisation was withdrawn in December 2025 for commercial reasons; this is a regulatory authorisation status, not evidence that the molecule was withdrawn for a newly identified safety issue.

### Suliqua / Soliqua fixed-ratio combination
Some markets have authorised fixed-ratio insulin glargine/lixisenatide products. These are combination products, not lixisenatide-only presentations. Record both active substances, their strengths/ratio, device and product-specific label. Do not treat Suliqua/Soliqua as a lixisenatide brand synonym.

## 10. Regulatory history and market status

### United States
- FDA BLA 208471; original approval date 27 July 2016.
- Adlyxin was indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- FDA Purple Book product details list the two pen presentations as **discontinued**. Discontinued marketing status should not be interpreted as withdrawal of FDA approval for safety reasons without a separate regulatory record.
- Current FDA label source reviewed: May 2025 revision, including updated severe gastrointestinal adverse reactions and pulmonary aspiration precautions.

### European Union
- Lyxumia received EU marketing authorisation in 2013.
- The European Commission withdrew the marketing authorisation on 18 December 2025 at the request of Sanofi Winthrop Industrie, which cited permanent discontinuation for commercial reasons.
- EMA's EPAR and product information remain useful regulatory-history and scientific sources, but the withdrawn authorisation must not be represented as currently active.

### Other markets / India
Check CDSCO and each national regulator independently. EU and US historical approval do not establish current local marketing or availability. A combination product's status must be assessed separately from lixisenatide monotherapy.

## 11. Administration and handling

The US Adlyxin label specifies once-daily subcutaneous administration within one hour before the first meal of the day. It describes a 10 mcg once-daily initiation period for 14 days followed by 20 mcg once daily from day 15, subject to the applicable label. Injection sites include abdomen, thigh or upper arm.

These details are product- and jurisdiction-specific. Do not apply Adlyxin dosing instructions to a fixed-ratio insulin glargine/lixisenatide product. The current local label must govern storage, missed-dose and device handling instructions.

## 12. Safety profile

Important current US label warnings and precautions include:
- anaphylaxis and serious hypersensitivity reactions;
- acute pancreatitis;
- hypoglycaemia when combined with insulin or insulin secretagogues;
- acute kidney injury associated with volume depletion;
- severe gastrointestinal adverse reactions;
- immunogenicity and anti-drug antibodies;
- acute gallbladder disease;
- pulmonary aspiration during general anaesthesia or deep sedation;
- severe gastroparesis: the US label says ADLYXIN is not recommended in patients with severe gastroparesis;
- end-stage renal disease: use is not recommended in the US label.

The US label lists nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, headache, dizziness and hypoglycaemia among adverse reactions, with incidence dependent on trial and concomitant treatment. Extract exact frequencies from each label table, retaining dose, population, comparator and duration.

## 13. Drug interactions and precautions

- **Delayed gastric emptying:** may affect the rate of absorption of concomitant oral medicines. Evaluate medicines where a delayed onset or altered peak concentration could be clinically important.
- **Warfarin and other narrow-therapeutic-index medicines:** follow product-label monitoring advice where applicable; do not generalise a single interaction study to all oral medicines.
- **Insulin or sulfonylureas:** hypoglycaemia risk may increase; label recommends considering dose reduction of the concomitant insulin secretagogue/insulin.
- **Combination product:** insulin glargine/lixisenatide has additional insulin-related risks and dosing rules. Keep it separate from lixisenatide monotherapy interaction data.

A complete interaction matrix remains pending with drug, mechanism, consequence, evidence, management, product, route and jurisdiction.

## 14. Clinical evidence

### GetGoal programme
The lixisenatide clinical programme includes monotherapy and add-on studies in adults with type 2 diabetes, including background metformin, sulfonylurea, pioglitazone and basal insulin regimens.

### ELIXA cardiovascular outcomes trial
ELIXA studied lixisenatide in people with type 2 diabetes and a recent acute coronary syndrome. It demonstrated cardiovascular safety/non-inferiority for the primary composite endpoint and did not establish cardiovascular superiority. The precise endpoint definition, hazard ratio, confidence interval, follow-up and event counts must be imported from the primary publication and trial registry before use in a quantitative evidence table.

For each trial, capture registry ID, DOI/PMID, protocol, population, sample size, comparator, dose, duration, primary/secondary endpoints, effect estimates, confidence intervals, adverse events and limitations. Do not infer a cardiovascular risk-reduction indication from a neutral cardiovascular outcomes trial.
