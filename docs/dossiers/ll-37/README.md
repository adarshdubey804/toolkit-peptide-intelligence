# Dossier 096 — LL-37 (Human Cathelicidin Antimicrobial Peptide)

**Workbook entry:** 096 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **FDA approval:** No | **Status:** Research peptide; not an FDA-approved therapeutic  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and sequence
LL-37 is the 37-amino-acid C-terminal antimicrobial peptide generated by proteolytic processing of the human cathelicidin precursor hCAP18, encoded by **CAMP**. The mature peptide sequence is:

`LLGDFFRKSKEKIGKEFKRIVQRIKDFLRNLVPRTES`

It is an amphipathic, cationic, predominantly α-helical peptide in membrane-mimetic environments. It has no disulfide bonds. LL-37 must be distinguished from the full-length hCAP18 precursor and from shorter LL-37-derived fragments. The workbook correctly lists it as research-only, not FDA-approved.

## Mechanism and biology
LL-37 interacts with microbial membranes through electrostatic attraction and hydrophobic insertion, producing membrane disruption in susceptible organisms. It can also bind bacterial components such as LPS and modulate host inflammatory responses. In mammalian systems it has been associated with chemotaxis, angiogenesis, wound repair, epithelial responses and innate immune signalling through several receptors and pathways. These effects are context-dependent and sometimes pro-inflammatory; they should not be presented as one validated drug mechanism.

## Pharmacology and translational limits
Activity depends on ionic strength, serum proteins, peptide concentration, proteolysis, membrane composition and bacterial species. Physiological salts and host proteins can reduce antimicrobial activity. LL-37 is susceptible to proteolytic degradation and can have cytotoxic or haemolytic effects at higher concentrations. In-vitro MIC results should not be extrapolated directly to clinical efficacy.

## Clinical and research evidence
LL-37 is studied in innate immunity, infection, wound healing and inflammatory disease. Human studies of topical LL-37 in wound healing have been exploratory and do not establish an FDA-approved indication. There is no FDA-approved LL-37 medicinal product. Compounded or research-grade material is not equivalent to an approved drug and should not be represented as established therapy.

## Safety
Potential concerns include host-cell cytotoxicity, haemolysis, inflammatory activation and immunogenicity. LL-37 can have both antimicrobial and immunomodulatory effects; in some contexts it may contribute to inflammatory pathology. No standardized clinical safety profile or approved dosing regimen exists.

## Formulation and quality
Research preparations require defined peptide identity, purity, counterion, oxidation/degradation profile, aggregation, solubility, endotoxin, sterility where relevant and stability. Formulation is challenging because cationic amphipathic peptides can adsorb to surfaces and interact with excipients. Any clinical translation requires route-specific toxicology and GMP manufacture.

## Regulatory status
Not FDA-approved. Research use only; no approved label, dose, route or therapeutic claim. Do not infer approval from trials, patents, vendor availability or compounded formulations.

## Evidence gaps
1. Curate human clinical studies separately from cell and animal studies.
2. Assess reproducibility of antimicrobial activity under physiological conditions.
3. Define structure-activity relationships and proteolytic stability.
4. Evaluate haemolysis, cytotoxicity, immunogenicity and inflammatory liabilities.
5. Keep `in_progress`; research status is not a gap to be "upgraded" without regulatory evidence.
