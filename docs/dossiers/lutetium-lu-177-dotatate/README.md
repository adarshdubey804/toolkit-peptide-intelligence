# Dossier 031 — Lutetium Lu 177 dotatate

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Evidence cut-off:** 3 November 2026  
**Reference product:** LUTATHERA; other 2026 US-labelled presentations include BEXLUTRY  
**Therapeutic class:** Somatostatin-receptor-targeted radioligand therapy (radiopharmaceutical)  
**Molecule class:** Radiometal-chelator-peptide complex; not an unlabelled peptide drug

> Classification correction: lutetium Lu 177 dotatate contains a peptide targeting vector (Tyr³-octreotate), a DOTA chelator, and radioactive lutetium-177. It must be catalogued as a radiopharmaceutical peptide conjugate, not as a conventional peptide-only therapeutic.

## 1. Identity and classification

- **Generic name:** lutetium Lu 177 dotatate; also written lutetium-177-DOTATATE or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-TATE.
- **Brand:** LUTATHERA (Novartis/Advanced Accelerator Applications); BEXLUTRY is a separately labelled US product presentation introduced in 2026.
- **Substance name in FDA GSRS:** lutetium oxodotreotide Lu-177.
- **FDA GSRS UNII:** AE221IM3BB.
- **CAS mapping:** 437608-50-9 (FDA GSRS synonym mapping).
- **ATC:** V10XX04 (lutetium (177Lu) oxodotreotide).
- **Route:** intravenous infusion.
- **Initial US approval:** 2018, NDA 208700.
- **Radiation type:** lutetium-177 is a beta-minus-emitting radionuclide with accompanying gamma emissions useful for imaging/dosimetry.
- **Molecule class:** cyclic somatostatin analogue peptide covalently attached to DOTA and complexed with Lu-177.

## 2. Molecular composition and peptide targeting vector

The active radiopharmaceutical has three functionally distinct components:

1. **Targeting peptide:** Tyr³-octreotate (TATE), a cyclic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin.
2. **Chelator:** DOTA (1,4,7,10-tetraazacyclododecane-1,4,7,10-tetraacetic acid), which binds the radiometal.
3. **Radionuclide:** lutetium-177, coordinated within the DOTA chelator.

The FDA label describes the peptide-chelator structure as lutetium (Lu-177)-N-[(4,7,10-tris(carboxymethyl)-1,4,7,10-tetraazacyclododec-1-yl)acetyl]-D-phenylalanyl-L-cysteinyl-L-tyrosyl-D-tryptophanyl-L-lysyl-L-threoninyl-L-cysteinyl-L-threonine, cyclic (2-7) disulfide.

The cyclic peptide disulfide bridge is essential to the somatostatin-analogue scaffold. The DOTA group is the radiometal chelator and is not itself the receptor-binding peptide. Avoid treating the full radioconjugate as though it had the same structure or mass as the peptide TATE alone.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Lutetium Lu 177 dotatate is a targeted radionuclide therapy:

1. The TATE peptide binds with high affinity to somatostatin receptors, especially **SSTR2**, expressed on many well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumour cells.
2. The receptor-ligand complex is internalised into the tumour cell.
3. Lu-177 emits beta particles that deposit ionising radiation over a short tissue range, producing DNA damage and tumour-cell death.
4. Gamma emissions can contribute to post-therapy imaging and biodistribution assessment.

The antitumour effect is therefore not simply pharmacological receptor agonism or antagonism. The peptide provides tumour targeting and internalisation; the principal cytotoxic mechanism is radiation delivered by the chelated radionuclide.

## 5. Pharmacology and biodistribution

- The targeting vector has affinity for somatostatin receptors, particularly SSTR2.
- Tumour uptake depends on receptor expression, lesion accessibility, perfusion, and patient-specific biodistribution.
- Physiological uptake occurs in tissues with somatostatin-receptor expression and in organs involved in clearance.
- The kidneys are an important exposure/critical-organ consideration; amino-acid co-infusion is used to reduce renal tubular reabsorption and renal radiation dose.
- Bone marrow is a dose-limiting tissue due to circulating activity and radiation exposure.
- A positive SSTR imaging study is used to establish receptor expression/eligibility according to the applicable label and protocol.

Do not infer uniform tumour uptake from the presence of a neuroendocrine tumour diagnosis alone.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics and dosimetry

Radiopharmaceutical disposition must be described using both peptide-conjugate kinetics and radionuclide dosimetry.

- After intravenous administration, activity distributes rapidly to receptor-expressing tissues and is cleared predominantly through the kidneys/urinary tract.
- Renal tubular reabsorption can increase kidney radiation exposure; co-administered positively charged amino acids reduce renal uptake.
- The radiolabel's physical decay follows Lu-177's approximately 6.65-day physical half-life; biological clearance and physical decay together determine activity retained in tissues.
- Tumour and organ absorbed doses vary substantially with patient and lesion characteristics.
- The FDA label includes pharmacokinetic and dosimetry information; values must be quoted with the specific study population, sampling schedule, and dosimetry method.

No universal patient-specific absorbed dose is estimated in this dossier. Renal function, marrow reserve, prior therapies, and individual biodistribution are clinically important.

## 7. Clinical evidence and labelled indication

**Current US LUTATHERA indication:** treatment of adult and paediatric patients aged 12 years and older with somatostatin receptor-positive gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (GEP-NETs), including foregut, midgut, and hindgut neuroendocrine tumours.

The indication was expanded in April 2024 to include patients aged 12 years and older. Initial US approval in 2018 covered adults with SSTR-positive GEP-NETs.

The pivotal NETTER-1 trial studied patients with progressive, well-differentiated, locally advanced or metastatic, SSTR-positive midgut NETs. Its results should not be generalised without qualification to every GEP-NET subtype or to all treatment lines. Later evidence and regulatory expansions should be distinguished from the original pivotal population.

## 8. Administration and formulation

- Route: intravenous infusion only.
- LUTATHERA concentration: 370 MBq/mL (10 mCi/mL) at calibration time.
- Standard labelled course: 7.4 GBq (200 mCi) every 8 weeks (±1 week), for a total of four doses, subject to dose modification/withholding rules in the current label.
- Renal-protective amino-acid solution is administered before, during, and after radiopharmaceutical infusion according to the product instructions.
- Long-acting octreotide is administered after each treatment in the labelled regimen; short-acting octreotide may be used for symptom control as specified in the label.
- Administer in a facility authorised and equipped for therapeutic radiopharmaceutical handling, radiation safety, contamination control, and patient release instructions.
- The product is a radioactive single-dose vial; activity is time-dependent and must be verified at administration.

This dossier is not a treatment protocol. Preparation, infusion, amino-acid timing, dose modification, radiation precautions, and patient instructions must follow the current local product information and institutional radiation-safety procedures.

## 9. Safety and major warnings

Important risks in the current US label include:

- **Myelosuppression:** anaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and lymphopenia; monitor blood counts.
- **Secondary myelodysplastic syndrome and acute leukaemia:** delayed serious risks have been reported after peptide receptor radionuclide therapy.
- **Renal toxicity:** radiation exposure to kidneys; amino-acid co-infusion and renal-function monitoring are important.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** monitor liver tests; severe hepatic impairment is not well characterised.
- **Neuroendocrine hormonal crisis:** tumour hormone release can cause acute clinical deterioration in susceptible patients.
- **Hypersensitivity and infusion-related reactions.**
- **Embryo-fetal toxicity:** radiation can cause fetal harm; pregnancy status and contraception requirements are specified in product labeling.
- **Infertility:** potential impairment of fertility in both sexes.
- **Radiation exposure:** follow radiation protection, contamination prevention, waste handling, and patient-contact instructions.

The label includes specific contraception periods for females and males with partners of reproductive potential, and breastfeeding restrictions. These periods must be taken from the current product-specific label rather than copied across different radiopharmaceuticals.

## 10. Drug interactions and concomitant treatment

The label's interaction information should be consulted for each product version. Clinically relevant considerations include:
- Somatostatin analogues (e.g., octreotide) are part of the labelled treatment context; timing around radiopharmaceutical administration matters.
- Amino-acid infusion is a required renal-protective co-medication, not an optional unrelated supplement.
- Prior or concomitant myelosuppressive treatments may increase marrow toxicity.
- Nephrotoxic medicines and pre-existing renal impairment may increase renal risk.
- Do not assume conventional CYP-mediated interaction rules fully describe a radiopharmaceutical peptide conjugate.

## 13. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- Principal target: **SSTR2** (somatostatin receptor 2), a class A GPCR.
- Other somatostatin receptor subtypes may contribute to binding to varying degrees; SSTR2 is the principal clinical targeting focus.
- Tumour uptake is linked to receptor abundance and internalisation.
- Downstream cell death is driven primarily by beta radiation-induced DNA damage rather than a conventional peptide signalling pathway.
- Relevant tumour biology includes neuroendocrine differentiation and SSTR expression.
- SSTR PET imaging (commonly using gallium-68-labelled somatostatin analogues) is used to assess receptor-positive disease; diagnostic radiotracer and therapeutic Lu-177 product are distinct substances.
