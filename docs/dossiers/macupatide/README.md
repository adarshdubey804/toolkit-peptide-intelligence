# Dossier 023 — Macupatide (LY3532226)

**Curation status:** in progress — identity and core pharmacology established; full clinical, safety, PK, CMC and jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction regulatory review remains open.  
**Asset class:** synthetic, long-acting, lipidated peptide; GIP receptor agonist.  
**Sponsor / developer:** Eli Lilly and Company.  
**Nonproprietary name:** macupatide (USAN adopted; WHO proposed INN publication).  
**Development code:** LY3532226.  
**Dossier rule:** distinguish macupatide monotherapy from studies combining it with eloralintide or dulaglutide.

## 1. Executive identity

Macupatide is a chemically modified, long-acting analogue designed to activate the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR). Lilly describes it as a long-acting GIPR agonist and is studying it in cardiometabolic indications, including obesity and type 2 diabetes. It is investigational; the clinical-trial listings and Lilly pipeline reviewed for this dossier do not establish marketing approval. [1–3]

## 2. Mechanism of action

**Primary target:** GIPR, the class B G-protein-coupled receptor for glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide.

Macupatide is described by Lilly as a long-acting GIPR agonist. GIPR activation is expected to engage Gs/cAMP signalling in receptor-expressing cells. In pancreatic beta cells, GIP contributes to glucose-dependent insulin secretion; GIP biology also has effects in adipose and other metabolic tissues. The exact tissue-specific contribution of macupatide to weight change, insulin sensitivity, appetite, glucagon regulation and energy expenditure must be treated as a research question rather than assumed from receptor classification alone. [2,5]

**Evidence in humans:** a 12-week Phase 1b randomised trial in people with type 2 diabetes compared macupatide, dulaglutide and their combination. The publication reports that macupatide alone increased the clamp-derived insulin-sensitivity M value and, to a lesser extent, insulin secretion rate; the combination produced numerically greater changes in several measured indices. Injection-site reactions and diarrhoea were among the most frequent adverse events in the abstract. These findings are trial-specific and do not establish long-term clinical benefit or approval. [5]

## 3. Molecular structure and sequence

The USAN Council statement and WHO proposed-INN publication provide the chemical identity, molecular formula, CAS number and a residue-level description. The peptide backbone contains 39 residues and several noncanonical modifications:

- 2-methyl-L-alanine at positions 2 and 20;
- 2-methyl-L-leucine at position 13;
- a lysine side-chain conjugated through a linker to a γ-glutamyl and C20 dicarboxylic fatty-acid moiety;
- 3-(pyridin-4-yl)-L-alanine at position 25;
- C-terminal L-serinamide.

The lipidated lysine side-chain and linker are integral structural features, not a separate co-administered drug. They are consistent with an engineered long-acting peptide design, but the precise contribution of each modification to absorption, albumin association, proteolytic stability and receptor pharmacology should be sourced to primary experimental work before assigning quantitative effects. [1,3]

**Sequence representation (WHO proposed-INN notation):**

`YAEGTFISDYSILLDKKHQADFVE X LLEAGPSSGAPPPS`

This compact representation must be read with the WHO modified-residue legend; it is not a complete conventional one-letter sequence. WHO lists modified residues A at positions 2 and 20 (2-Me-L-Ala), L at position 13 (2-Me-L-Leu), the lipid-linker-bearing lysine at position 17, X at position 25 (pyridin-4-yl-L-Ala), and C-terminal L-Ser-NH2. The official chemical name/structural description takes precedence over simplified vendor sequence strings. [3]

**Identifiers:** CAS 2923558-68-1; UNII YWF427CA5D; formula C232H357N49O72; molecular weight 4,984.68. The UNII is reported in the USAN statement. [1]

## 4. Pharmacodynamics

The best-supported pharmacodynamic classification is GIPR agonism. Lilly's current pipeline explicitly calls LY3532226 a long-acting GIPR agonist. A detailed receptor pharmacology package (binding affinity, functional potency, efficacy, species cross-reactivity, signalling bias, receptor internalisation and comparative activity against native GIP) is not reproduced here because a sufficiently complete primary dataset was not verified in the sources used for this dossier. [2]

Human Phase 1b clamp data support biological activity on insulin sensitivity and insulin secretion over 12 weeks in the studied T2D population. Do not extrapolate those results to weight-loss efficacy or to non-diabetic populations. [5]

## 5. Pharmacokinetics and duration

Macupatide is described as long-acting and has been studied using once-weekly subcutaneous regimens in clinical research materials. However, a verified primary PK table with Cmax, Tmax, half-life, exposure proportionality, accumulation, bioavailability, clearance and renal/hepatic contribution was not established in this curation pass. Those fields remain **unresolved**, rather than being populated from vendor summaries or inferred from the lipidated structure.

## 6. Clinical development

### NCT05887999 — Phase 1, type 1 diabetes

A Lilly Phase 1, randomised, placebo-controlled crossover study evaluated LY3532226 in adults with type 1 diabetes, focusing on glucagon response and recovery after insulin-induced hypoglycaemia. Lilly's trial page lists the study as completed, with 30 participants and study dates from June 2023 to February 2024. Completion of a study is not proof of efficacy or approval. [6]

### NCT06557356 — Phase 1, obesity

Lilly's 2025 investor trial table identifies a Phase 1 study of LY3532226 in participants with obesity, planned enrolment 129, with primary completion and study completion projected for November 2025 in that historical table. Current registry status and results require direct registry verification; the historical table is not evidence that results were posted. [7]

### Phase 1b — macupatide, dulaglutide, and combination in T2D

A 12-week randomised, double-blind Phase 1b study assessed macupatide alone, dulaglutide alone, and their combination in people with T2D on metformin. Hyperinsulinaemic-euglycaemic and hyperglycaemic clamp methods were used. The publication reports improvements in insulin sensitivity and insulin secretion measures, with combination-arm numerical increases in several indices. The sponsor funded the study and participated in its design, data collection, review, analysis and manuscript preparation; this should be disclosed when interpreting the results. [5]

### NCT07215559 — Phase 2, obesity/overweight with T2D

Lilly's study page describes a double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial of macupatide and eloralintide, alone or in combination, in adults with obesity or overweight and T2D. The listed enrolment goal is 200; the trial began 16 October 2025 and is planned to run to May 2027. The primary weight endpoint is planned at week 32. The registry-derived status reported in October 2026 is active, not recruiting. No efficacy result should be inferred from the existence or status of the trial. [8,9]

### NCT07589608 — Phase 2, obesity/overweight without T2D

Lilly lists a Phase 2 study of macupatide and eloralintide, alone or in combination, in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes. The listed enrolment goal is 400 and the study period is May 2026 to February 2028. This is a combination-development programme; interpret each treatment arm separately and do not attribute combination outcomes to macupatide monotherapy. [10]

## 7. Regulatory status

**United States:** investigational in the sources reviewed. ClinicalTrials.gov trial participation and an FDA-regulated-drug designation do not constitute FDA approval. No FDA-approved label or marketing authorisation for macupatide was identified in the evidence curated here.

**Other jurisdictions:** not established in this dossier. A global approval claim requires regulator-by-regulator confirmation (for example, EMA, MHRA, PMDA, CDSCO and relevant national agencies). Do not treat a proposed INN or USAN adoption as regulatory approval.

**Naming:** USAN adoption and WHO proposed-INN publication standardise the name and chemical description; neither is a marketing authorisation. [1,3]

## 8. Safety and tolerability

The Phase 1b abstract identifies injection-site reactions and diarrhoea among the most frequent adverse events and reports uncommon discontinuations due to treatment-emergent adverse events in that study. These limited-duration findings do not define the complete safety profile. [5]

Outstanding safety domains requiring extraction from trial protocols, results and future publications include:
- gastrointestinal tolerability and dehydration-related events;
- hypoglycaemia risk, particularly in combination with insulin or insulin secretagogues;
- heart rate and haemodynamic effects;
- pancreatitis and gallbladder events;
- immunogenicity and injection-site reactions;
- reproductive and developmental safety;
- long-term cardiovascular, renal and hepatic safety.

This list is a research checklist, not a statement that each event has been observed with macupatide. No patient-specific dosing or self-administration guidance is provided for this investigational agent.

## 9. Formulation, manufacturing and quality
