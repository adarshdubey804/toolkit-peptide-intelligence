# Dossier 024 — Mazdutide (IBI362; LY3305677)

**Curation status:** in progress — authoritative identity and China approval records located; complete primary pharmacology, PK, long-term safety, CMC and international regulatory review remain open.  
**Asset class:** synthetic, lipidated peptide analogue of oxyntomodulin.  
**Pharmacology:** dual glucagon receptor (GCGR) and GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) agonist.  
**Developer / sponsor:** Innovent Biologics, with Eli Lilly involvement in development history.  
**INN:** mazdutide.  
**Other codes:** IBI362, LY3305677, USAN JK-37.  
**Key distinction:** approved in China; not thereby FDA-approved or globally approved.

## 1. Executive identity

Mazdutide is a long-acting, lipidated peptide analogue derived from the oxyntomodulin pharmacology concept. It activates both GCGR and GLP-1R. Unlike selective GLP-1R agonists, dual receptor activity is intended to combine incretin-mediated glucose control and appetite/weight effects with glucagon-receptor biology. The net clinical contribution of each receptor is dose-, tissue- and context-dependent and should not be reduced to a simplistic claim that glucagon activation necessarily increases or decreases weight.

The FDA Global Substance Registration System (GSRS) has a validated public peptide record for mazdutide, UNII **MB76Z4IBZ5**, and explicitly lists both glucagon receptor and GLP-1 receptor as agonist targets. [1]

## 2. Mechanism of action

Mazdutide is a **dual agonist** at:

1. **GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R):** activation of this class B GPCR stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppresses inappropriate glucagon secretion in a glucose-dependent setting, slows gastric emptying (particularly early in treatment) and engages central appetite/satiety pathways.
2. **Glucagon receptor (GCGR):** activation can increase hepatic glucose production and energy expenditure and influence lipid metabolism. In a dual agonist, GLP-1R activity is intended to counterbalance the glycaemic liability of GCGR activation while retaining potentially useful metabolic effects.

Mazdutide's observed clinical effect is the integrated outcome of both receptor pathways. The precise relative potency, efficacy, receptor occupancy, tissue distribution, signalling bias and contribution of each receptor at clinical exposures should be taken from primary receptor-pharmacology studies; this dossier does not invent numerical potency values. The FDA GSRS target relationship confirms agonism at both receptors. [1,2]

## 3. Molecular identity and chemistry

The FDA GSRS public record identifies mazdutide as a peptide with a complete sequence and records the following modifications:

- 2-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib) at residue 2;
- a lysine side-chain bearing an N6-linked hydrogen-icosanedioyl (C20 diacid) group through a γ-glutamyl / bis(iminobis(ethylenoxyacetyl)) linker;
- C-terminal glycinamide.

The GSRS sequence is displayed as:

`HGQGTFTSDYSKYLDEKKAKEFVEWLLEGGPSSG`

The sequence must be interpreted together with the GSRS modification table: the displayed residue string alone does not represent the lipidated side chain or the C-terminal glycinamide. [1]

**Important structure-data discrepancy:** public records are not fully concordant on the terminal peptide composition and calculated molecular formula/mass. FDA GSRS provides a complete 34-residue sequence with C-terminal glycinamide and lists an estimated formula/mass property that does not reconcile cleanly with all third-party calculated values. PubChem CID 167312357 lists formula C207H317N45O65, while a separate structure reconstruction and secondary curated records report a 34-residue glycinamide form with different atom counts. These are not safely reconciled as salt/protonation differences. Therefore:
- CAS 2259884-03-0 and UNII MB76Z4IBZ5 are retained as identifiers;
- the sequence and modification description are taken from FDA GSRS;
- a single definitive molecular formula and molecular weight are **not asserted** in this dossier until the GSRS substance structure is reconciled against the WHO INN structure and sponsor reference standard.

Do not copy vendor formula, mass or sequence fields into clinical or regulatory records without structure-level reconciliation. [1,3,4]

## 4. Pharmacodynamics and translational biology

Mazdutide's dual receptor design is based on oxyntomodulin, an endogenous proglucagon-derived peptide with activity at GLP-1R and GCGR. The drug is engineered for prolonged exposure through a fatty-diacid side-chain modification. The design rationale is to achieve sustained dual-receptor activation with weekly subcutaneous administration.

Clinical outcomes have been studied across obesity, overweight, type 2 diabetes and metabolic comorbidity populations. Evidence from one population should not be directly transferred to another. For example, weight-loss endpoints in obesity trials and HbA1c endpoints in T2D trials answer different questions.

## 5. Pharmacokinetics

Mazdutide has been developed for once-weekly subcutaneous administration. The lipidated side-chain is a structural feature intended to prolong exposure. This dossier does not yet include a fully verified primary PK table for absolute bioavailability, Tmax, terminal half-life, clearance, accumulation, dose proportionality, food effect or organ impairment.

A 2026 conference abstract is cited in some secondary sources for half-life information, but conference-level data should be obtained and reviewed directly before any value is entered into the curated PK table. No numerical half-life is asserted here.

## 6. Clinical development and efficacy evidence

### Early clinical development

A randomised, placebo-controlled Phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose study evaluated 9 mg and 10 mg regimens in Chinese adults with overweight or obesity. The publication abstract reports weight reduction and describes once-weekly subcutaneous dosing over 12 or 16 weeks. These early dose-escalation findings are not interchangeable with pivotal Phase 3 outcomes. [5]

### Obesity / chronic weight management — GLORY programme

Innovent reported that China’s NMPA approved mazdutide for chronic weight management in Chinese adults with overweight or obesity on 27 June 2025. The approval announcement identifies mazdutide as a first-in-class dual GCG/GLP-1 receptor agonist. [2]

Innovent's development programme includes Phase 3 studies such as GLORY-1, GLORY-2, GLORY-3 and GLORY-OSA. The sponsor's interim 2025 presentation lists trial comparisons and programme milestones; sponsor presentations are useful pipeline records but should not replace peer-reviewed primary trial reports or regulator-approved product information. [6]

### Type 2 diabetes — DREAMS programme

Innovent announced on 19 September 2025 that China's NMPA had approved mazdutide for glycaemic control in adults with T2D. [7]

A Phase 3 randomised trial in Chinese adults with T2D inadequately controlled with diet and exercise randomised 320 participants to weekly subcutaneous mazdutide 4 mg, 6 mg or placebo for 24 weeks, followed by a 24-week extension. The published abstract identifies HbA1c, body weight and safety as relevant outcomes. Extract full numerical efficacy and safety tables from the full publication before populating a quantitative comparison table. [8]

## 8. Safety and tolerability

The complete safety profile requires systematic extraction from Phase 2/3 publications and the approved Chinese prescribing information. Important monitoring categories for a dual incretin/glucagon agonist dossier include:
- gastrointestinal adverse events (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation);
- dehydration and renal consequences secondary to gastrointestinal intolerance;
- gallbladder and biliary events;
- pancreatitis warnings and adjudicated events;
- heart rate and blood-pressure effects;
- hypoglycaemia, especially with concomitant insulin or insulin secretagogues;
- injection-site reactions and immunogenicity;
- hepatic laboratory changes and glucagon-pathway pharmacodynamic effects;
- pregnancy and reproductive safety.

This list is a curation checklist, not a claim that all events have been demonstrated to be caused by mazdutide. Use trial incidence tables and the regulator-approved label for frequency, severity and causality.
