## 2. Structure and primary chemical identity

Melphalan flufenamide is a dipeptide-like conjugate comprising:
- **Melphalan-derived moiety:** L-phenylalanine bearing a para-bis(2-chloroethyl)amino nitrogen-mustard substituent.
- **Linkage/amino-acid component:** L-phenylalanine bearing a para-fluoro substituent.
- **C-terminal ethyl ester:** increases lipophilicity and contributes to the prodrug design.

IUPAC chemical name (free base):

**Ethyl (2S)-2-[[(2S)-2-amino-3-[4-[bis(2-chloroethyl)amino]phenyl]propanoyl]amino]-3-(4-fluorophenyl)propanoate.**

Condensed peptide-like representation reported by IUPHAR/BPS: **H-Phe(Unk)-Phe(4-F)-OEt**. The noncanonical melphalan-bearing residue is not adequately represented by an ordinary one-letter amino-acid code. Do not confuse this with a full biologically encoded peptide sequence.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Melflufen is a **lipophilic peptide-conjugated prodrug** designed for rapid cellular entry and intracellular enzymatic activation.

1. Its lipophilicity supports rapid passage across cell membranes.
2. Intracellular aminopeptidases and other peptidases hydrolyse the peptide/ester-related linkage.
3. This releases melphalan or melphalan-related active alkylating species and can produce intracellular accumulation of the cytotoxic payload.
4. Melphalan forms reactive aziridinium intermediates that alkylate DNA, particularly guanine N7 positions.
5. DNA intra- and inter-strand cross-links impair replication and transcription, leading to DNA damage and cell death.

This is not a receptor agonist/antagonist mechanism. The peptide-like component is a delivery/prodrug design element; cytotoxicity is primarily due to the released nitrogen-mustard alkylator.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics and enzyme biology

- Melflufen is a substrate for aminopeptidases/peptidases that cleave the prodrug and release melphalan.
- Aminopeptidase activity is elevated in some malignant cells, including multiple-myeloma models, but this does not establish a validated patient-selection biomarker.
- The released melphalan alkylates DNA and induces cross-linking and DNA damage.
- The FDA/EMA clinical evidence does not establish a companion diagnostic based on aminopeptidase expression.
- Proposed tumour-selective accumulation is a mechanistic rationale; it should not be described as guaranteed selectivity or freedom from systemic alkylator toxicity.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

Melflufen is administered intravenously and rapidly distributes into tissues/cells. The EMA assessment report and FDA label describe rapid conversion to melphalan and other metabolites.

Key interpretation points:
- Parent melflufen is rapidly hydrolysed; exposure to parent and released melphalan are distinct analytes.
- Plasma concentrations of parent drug and melphalan should not be conflated.
- Renal impairment may affect exposure to melphalan and/or metabolites; use the exact jurisdictional product information.
- The US FDA label and EU SmPC differ in indication and product context; numeric PK values should be quoted with their assay, dose, and sampling schedule.
- A full PK table with parent, melphalan, and metabolite parameters remains to be curated from the final EU SmPC and FDA review documents.

## 7. Clinical evidence and efficacy

### US HORIZON (NCT02963493)

The 2021 US accelerated approval was based on a single-arm HORIZON study in heavily pretreated relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma. In the FDA efficacy subpopulation of 97 patients who had received at least four prior lines and were refractory to a proteasome inhibitor, an immunomodulatory agent, and a CD38-directed antibody:
- Objective response rate: 23.7% (95% CI 15.7–33.4).
- Median duration of response: 4.2 months (95% CI 3.2–7.6).

These response data supported accelerated approval but did not establish a survival benefit.

### Confirmatory phase 3 OCEAN / OP-103 (NCT03151811)

The confirmatory randomised trial compared melflufen plus dexamethasone with pomalidomide plus dexamethasone. It did not confirm the required clinical benefit; FDA's final withdrawal decision cited failure to meet the prespecified primary PFS endpoint and a detrimental overall-survival signal. FDA also rejected a sponsor reanalysis that departed from the prespecified analysis plan.

Do not report the sponsor's reanalysis as the confirmatory primary result. The FDA final decision is the authoritative regulatory interpretation for the US withdrawal.

## 8. Administration and formulation

**Historical US PEPAXTO regimen (not a current US-approved regimen):**
- 40 mg melphalan flufenamide intravenously over 30 minutes on day 1 of each 28-day cycle, in combination with dexamethasone.
- Supplied as a sterile lyophilised powder for concentrate for solution for infusion, 20 mg base-equivalent per vial (hydrochloride salt).
- The FDA indication and dosing are historical because the NDA approval was withdrawn.

**European Pepaxti:** EU product information provides a different indication and product instructions; follow the current EU SmPC rather than importing the former US regimen.

This dossier is not a treatment protocol or prescribing advice.

## 9. Safety and warnings

Important safety findings in the clinical programme and regulatory reviews include:
- **Myelosuppression:** thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, and anaemia.
- **Infections:** respiratory infections and other serious infections in heavily pretreated patients.
- **Gastrointestinal effects:** nausea and diarrhoea.
- **Fatigue and pyrexia.**
- **Renal laboratory abnormalities:** increased creatinine was among common laboratory abnormalities in the HORIZON safety population.
- **Overall survival concern:** the confirmatory OCEAN trial showed a detrimental OS signal in the melflufen arm compared with the control arm; this was central to the FDA benefit-risk determination.

The FDA withdrawal was explicitly based on both failure to confirm clinical benefit and evidence that the drug was not shown to be safe or effective under the US conditions of use. This is more serious than a simple commercial discontinuation.

## 10. Drug interactions and concomitant therapy

- Melflufen is an alkylating cytotoxic agent; concomitant myelosuppressive therapies may compound marrow toxicity.
- Dexamethasone was part of the approved US combination regimen and remains part of the EU authorised combination context.
- Renal function, marrow reserve, prior alkylator exposure, and infection risk are important clinical considerations.
- Formal interaction statements must be taken from the current EU SmPC; do not assume no interactions because the molecule contains peptide-like bonds.

## 11. Regulatory status and product history

- **United States:** FDA accelerated approval granted 26 February 2021 under NDA 214383 for melflufen plus dexamethasone in a narrowly defined triple-class-refractory multiple-myeloma population.
- **US withdrawal:** FDA final decision dated 23 February 2024; approval withdrawn effective that date. FDA determined the confirmatory study did not confirm clinical benefit and available evidence did not show the drug to be safe or effective under the conditions of use. The NDA was removed from the Orange Book; FDA stated it would not accept or approve ANDAs referencing PEPAXTO.
- **European Union:** Pepaxti received central marketing authorisation on 17 August 2022. The European Commission Union Register listed the authorisation as ACTIVE in its 28 May 2026 update. EU indication is narrower/different from the former US indication and must be quoted from current EU product information.
- **India:** CDSCO approval and commercial availability have not been independently verified.
- **Other jurisdictions:** national status and commercial availability require individual verification.

## 13. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- Enzymatic activation: aminopeptidases/peptidases; specific enzyme contribution and clinically validated expression thresholds remain unresolved.
- Cytotoxic payload: melphalan, a nitrogen-mustard DNA-alkylating agent.
- DNA damage: guanine alkylation, intra- and inter-strand cross-linking, replication/transcription blockade.
- Cell fate: DNA-damage response and apoptosis.
- Disease context: relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma.
- No validated pharmacogenomic companion test is required by the US or EU product information.
