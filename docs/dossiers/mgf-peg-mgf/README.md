# Dossier 060 — Mechano Growth Factor (MGF / PEG-MGF)

**Workbook name:** MGF / PEG-MGF (Mechano Growth Factor)  
**Related biological term:** IGF-1Ec (human), often called mechano-growth factor in the literature  
**Workbook position:** Peptides sheet, row 60 (Excel row 61)  
**Curation status:** `in_progress` — biological nomenclature and evidence limitations curated; exact synthetic product identity, human clinical evidence, and product-specific regulatory status remain unresolved.  
**Regulatory status:** No FDA-approved MGF or PEG-MGF medicine or therapeutic indication identified.

> **Safety scope:** This dossier is for scientific and regulatory information. MGF and PEG-MGF products sold online are not FDA-approved medicines. No dosing, preparation, injection, or self-experimentation guidance is provided.

## 1. Executive overview

“Mechano Growth Factor” (MGF) is a term used in the growth-factor literature for an IGF-1 splice-variant-associated transcript/protein expressed in response to mechanical loading and tissue injury. In humans, the relevant splice variant is commonly designated IGF-1Ec. The corresponding terminology differs across species; for example, the rodent splice variant often called MGF is commonly designated IGF-1Eb. These terms should not be treated as universally interchangeable.

The workbook groups “MGF / PEG-MGF” together, but this label is chemically under-specified. Endogenous IGF-1Ec is a splice-variant product, whereas commercial “MGF peptide” products generally claim to be synthetic peptides derived from a unique C-terminal region or E-peptide sequence. PEG-MGF additionally implies polyethylene glycol conjugation, which changes the molecular entity and requires a defined conjugation site, linker, PEG size/distribution, and analytical specification.

There is no established FDA-approved MGF or PEG-MGF drug product. Claims about muscle hypertrophy, injury repair, tendon healing, or anti-aging are not established clinical indications.

## 3. Molecular biology and sequence identity

MGF is associated with alternative splicing of the IGF1 gene. The IGF1 gene produces transcripts encoding IGF-1 precursor proteins with different E-peptide regions. Mechanical stimulation, exercise, and tissue injury have been investigated as contexts in which splice-variant expression may change.

Important distinctions:
- The mature IGF-1 hormone is not the same molecule as the pro-IGF-1 precursor containing an E-peptide.
- The human IGF-1Ec transcript/protein context should not be assigned a rodent IGF-1Eb sequence without species qualification.
- A synthetic peptide sold as “MGF” is usually described as a fragment of the E-peptide region, not as the complete endogenous splice-variant protein.
- PEG-MGF is not a single fully specified structure unless the peptide sequence, PEG molecular-weight distribution, attachment site, linker, and conjugation chemistry are defined.

**Sequence status:** no definitive synthetic MGF sequence is asserted in this dossier. Commonly circulated commercial sequences are not sufficient evidence of a single standardized active ingredient. The exact sequence and terminal modifications must be verified against a primary structural source or a validated product specification before formula, molecular weight, CAS number, or canonical SMILES are added.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

### Endogenous IGF-1 splice-variant biology

The IGF1 gene encodes precursor forms that include the mature IGF-1 domain and a C-terminal E-peptide. Alternative splicing produces different E-peptide-containing precursor isoforms. The term MGF has been used for an IGF-1 splice-variant-associated response in mechanically stimulated tissue.

The precise biological role of the E-peptide and its proposed independent signalling activity remain areas of research. It is not scientifically justified to assume that every synthetic peptide marketed as MGF reproduces the effects of the intact endogenous splice-variant precursor.

### IGF-1 receptor pathway

Mature IGF-1 binds the IGF-1 receptor (IGF1R), a receptor tyrosine kinase, and can activate:
- IRS–PI3K–AKT signalling, associated with cell survival and anabolic responses.
- RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK signalling, associated with growth and proliferation.

These are established pathways for mature IGF-1. They must not automatically be attributed to a synthetic MGF fragment as a proven direct IGF1R agonist. Fragment-specific receptor binding and target engagement require direct experimental evidence.

### Mechanical loading and tissue response

MGF expression has been studied in relation to mechanical loading, exercise, and injury-associated tissue responses. These observations concern endogenous gene expression and do not demonstrate that exogenous administration of a marketed MGF peptide improves muscle or tendon healing in humans.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics and biological evidence

Research areas include:
- IGF1 splice-variant expression after mechanical loading.
- E-peptide biology and possible local/autocrine or paracrine effects.
- Skeletal-muscle satellite-cell activity and regeneration models.
- Muscle cell proliferation and differentiation in experimental systems.
- Tissue injury and repair models.

The evidence must be separated into transcript-expression studies, protein-level studies, isolated peptide experiments, animal models, and controlled human trials. An increase in IGF1 transcript expression after exercise does not establish that injected synthetic MGF has the same biological effect.

Claims that MGF reliably produces muscle hypertrophy, accelerates tendon repair, prevents injury, or reverses aging are not established clinical benefits.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

There is no regulator-reviewed human PK profile for a defined MGF or PEG-MGF medicinal product.

For an unmodified synthetic peptide, relevant questions include enzymatic degradation, tissue distribution, clearance, and exposure at the proposed site of action. PEGylation may alter apparent molecular size, distribution, proteolytic susceptibility, and clearance, but these effects depend on the actual conjugate.

The following cannot be stated without a defined material and primary data:
- Bioavailability by route.
- Plasma half-life.
- Tissue penetration and local retention.
- Metabolite identity.
- Renal/hepatic clearance.
- PEG conjugate distribution and clearance.
- Exposure–response relationship.

Do not transfer PK parameters from full-length IGF-1, mecasermin, other PEGylated proteins, or unrelated PEG-peptide conjugates.

## 7. Clinical evidence

No established, regulator-approved clinical efficacy programme for synthetic MGF or PEG-MGF was identified in this dossier.

Evidence limitations:
- Human exercise studies of IGF1 splice-variant expression are not trials of administered MGF.
- Preclinical cell and animal studies do not establish human therapeutic efficacy.
- Online product testimonials and bodybuilding protocols are not clinical evidence.
- No validated therapeutic indication, patient selection framework, or approved clinical endpoint is established.

The dossier should not imply that endogenous MGF expression after exercise validates the efficacy of commercial peptide injections.

## 8. Safety and tolerability

No FDA-approved MGF/PEG-MGF prescribing information exists. Therefore, an approved-label adverse-event frequency table, contraindication list, or monitoring schedule cannot be provided.

Key uncertainties include:
- Identity, purity, and consistency of commercial materials.
- Immunogenicity and aggregation.
- Effects of uncharacterized PEG conjugates and PEG-related impurities.
- Potential effects on IGF/insulin signalling.
- Uncontrolled cellular proliferation concerns if growth signalling is altered.
- Local and systemic effects after repeated exposure.
- Route-specific microbial, endotoxin, and particulate risks.

These are areas requiring formal evaluation, not a claim that a particular adverse event has been quantified for MGF. The safety profile of mecasermin or full-length IGF-1 cannot be directly substituted for a synthetic MGF fragment.

## 9. Regulatory status and compounding

- **United States:** no FDA-approved MGF or PEG-MGF drug product or indication identified.
- **Compounding:** do not infer eligibility under FDA 503A or 503B from vendor availability or from the regulatory status of other peptides. Verify the exact substance and current FDA policy.
- **Research supply:** “research use only” labelling does not establish human-use authorization or pharmaceutical quality.
- **International status:** no country-specific authorization is claimed without an official regulator record.

PEG-MGF should be treated as a distinct chemical entity from non-PEGylated MGF. Regulatory review of one cannot be assumed to cover the other.

## 10. Manufacturing, formulation, and quality

A defined MGF material requires a complete identity package:
- Exact peptide sequence, stereochemistry, and terminal chemistry.
- Confirmation of whether the material is an E-peptide fragment or another construct.
- Peptide content and related-substance profile.
- Sequence-related impurities and degradation products.
- Residual solvents and synthesis-related impurities.
- Aggregate and particulate burden.
- Counter-ion and water content.
- Microbial quality and endotoxin for parenteral products.

For PEG-MGF, additional controls are required:
- PEG molecular-weight distribution and polydispersity.
- Conjugation site and occupancy.
- Linker identity and stability.
- Free peptide and unconjugated PEG content.
- Mono- versus multi-PEGylated species.
- Aggregates and high-molecular-weight species.
- Conjugate-specific potency and stability assays.

A generic certificate stating “99% purity” is inadequate if it does not identify the exact conjugate and analytical method.

## 11. Bioinformatics and computational research

- Map transcript and protein records to the species-specific IGF1 reference sequence.
- Store transcript accession, exon usage, protein isoform, and residue-numbering convention.
- Keep endogenous IGF-1Ec/IGF-1Eb biology distinct from synthetic peptide product entities.
- Store peptide sequence and terminal chemistry explicitly when a defined synthetic product is established.
- Represent PEG conjugation as a separate molecular entity with linker, attachment site, and PEG distribution metadata.
- Curate transcript expression, protein abundance, isolated peptide activity, and clinical outcome evidence separately.
- Do not infer direct receptor agonism from transcript-expression associations.
