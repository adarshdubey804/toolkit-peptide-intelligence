# Dossier 093 — Micafungin (Mycamine)

## Identity and chemistry
Micafungin is a semisynthetic echinocandin antifungal derived from a fermentation product of *Coleophoma empetri*. It is a cyclic hexapeptide-like lipopeptide with a characteristic lipid side chain and multiple non-proteinogenic residues. The active pharmaceutical ingredient is micafungin sodium. It is not a conventional linear peptide; represent the complete stereochemical macrocycle and side chain from authoritative structure records.

## Mechanism
Micafungin inhibits the fungal cell-wall enzyme complex β-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase. Reduced β-(1,3)-D-glucan weakens the fungal cell wall and impairs growth and survival. It is generally fungicidal against many *Candida* species and fungistatic against *Aspergillus* species. FKS gene hotspot substitutions can reduce echinocandin susceptibility.

## Pharmacology and PK
Micafungin is administered intravenously. It is highly protein bound, undergoes hepatic metabolism and has a terminal half-life of approximately 11–17 hours in adults, depending on population and study. Renal elimination of unchanged drug is limited; routine renal dose adjustment is generally not required, but hepatic impairment and drug interactions should be assessed from the current label. It has poor urinary concentrations and is not a treatment for urinary candidiasis.

## Clinical evidence and indications
Mycamine is indicated for adults and paediatric patients for treatment of candidemia, acute disseminated candidiasis, Candida peritonitis and abscesses; treatment of esophageal candidiasis; and prophylaxis of Candida infections in haematopoietic stem-cell transplant recipients, with age-specific label details. Randomized studies support efficacy in invasive candidiasis and esophageal candidiasis. Do not assume echinocandin class interchangeability across every indication or paediatric age group.

## Safety
Common adverse reactions include diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, fever, headache, infusion-related reactions and abnormal liver tests. Serious hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, haemolysis and hepatic effects are important label concerns. Review interactions with sirolimus, nifedipine and itraconazole as described in current prescribing information.

## Formulation and quality
Micafungin sodium is supplied as sterile lyophilized powder for IV infusion. Critical quality attributes include macrocycle/lipid identity, stereochemical integrity, potency, related substances, residual solvents, water content, reconstitution, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter and container closure integrity.

## Regulatory status
FDA first approved in 2005. Mycamine's approved indications include invasive candidiasis, esophageal candidiasis and prophylaxis in HSCT recipients; verify current age-specific wording.
