# Dossier 032 — Motixafortide

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Evidence cut-off:** 3 November 2026  
**Reference product:** APHEXDA (motixafortide) for injection  
**Therapeutic class:** CXCR4 antagonist; hematopoietic stem-cell mobilizer  
**Molecule class:** Synthetic, modified cyclic peptide

> Motixafortide is not an antineoplastic peptide that directly kills myeloma cells. Its approved use is to mobilize hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells into peripheral blood, in combination with filgrastim, for collection before autologous transplantation.

## 2. Sequence and primary structure

The current US APHEXDA label gives the following sequence:

**Fba-Arg-Arg-Nal-Cys-Tyr-Cit-Lys-D-Lys-Pro-Tyr-Arg-Cit-Cys-Arg-NH₂**

Where:
- **Fba:** 4-fluorobenzoic acid N-terminal cap.
- **Nal:** 3-naphthylalanine.
- **Cit:** citrulline.
- **D-Lys:** D-lysine.
- The peptide is cyclised by a disulfide bond between cysteine residues 4 and 13 in the label's residue numbering.

The peptide is a modified cyclic peptide rather than a conventional linear sequence. Do not omit the N-terminal Fba group, the D-lysine stereochemistry, or the disulfide bridge when constructing a structure record.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Motixafortide is a potent antagonist of **C-X-C chemokine receptor 4 (CXCR4)**. It blocks binding of the endogenous ligand stromal cell-derived factor-1 alpha, also called **CXCL12**.

1. CXCL12/CXCR4 signalling contributes to retention, homing, and anchoring of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) in the bone-marrow niche.
2. Motixafortide blocks CXCL12 binding to CXCR4.
3. Disruption of this retention signal promotes movement of CD34-positive HSPCs from marrow into peripheral blood.
4. Circulating cells can then be collected by apheresis and used for autologous transplantation.

The mechanism is a receptor-antagonist effect on stem-cell trafficking, not direct stimulation of stem-cell proliferation and not direct cytotoxicity against multiple-myeloma cells.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- In vitro CXCR4 inhibition IC₅₀ values reported in the label range from 0.42 to 4.5 nM.
- Reported binding affinity (Kᴅ) is 7.9 pM.
- Receptor dissociation rate is reported as 3.38 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, with receptor occupancy maintained for more than 72 hours in the cited pharmacology studies.
- In clinical studies, motixafortide increased circulating CD34-positive cells and supported collection of transplantable HSPCs.
- Filgrastim (G-CSF) is co-administered to enhance mobilization; the approved clinical regimen is a combination strategy.

In vitro receptor occupancy duration should not be interpreted as the plasma half-life or as a guarantee of identical duration in every patient.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

According to current APHEXDA product information:
- Following subcutaneous administration, time to maximum plasma concentration is approximately 0.25–1.17 hours over studied dose levels.
- Plasma protein binding is greater than 99%.
- Estimated central-compartment volume of distribution in a typical subject is approximately 27 L.
- Motixafortide is degraded non-specifically to smaller peptides, amino acids, and derivatives through catabolic pathways, including in blood and liver microsomes.
- Effective plasma half-life is approximately 2 hours.
- Apparent total clearance in a typical subject is approximately 46.5 L/hour.
- No human mass-balance study was conducted. In rat and dog studies, most radiolabel-related material was recovered in urine, without detectable parent drug in urine.

Do not confuse the approximately 2-hour plasma half-life with the longer receptor occupancy reported in in-vitro pharmacology experiments.

## 7. Clinical evidence and labelled indication

**US indication:** APHEXDA is indicated in combination with filgrastim to mobilize hematopoietic stem cells to peripheral blood for collection and subsequent autologous transplantation in patients with multiple myeloma.

**Pivotal evidence — GENESIS (NCT032 sain? no):** The FDA approval was based on the GENESIS randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in 122 patients with multiple myeloma. Participants received motixafortide plus filgrastim or placebo plus filgrastim. The primary collection goal was at least 6 × 10⁶ CD34-positive cells/kg within up to two aphereses.

The FDA Drug Trials Snapshot reports 80 patients in the motixafortide arm and 42 in the placebo arm. Trial outcomes should be reported with the precise endpoint and analysis population; successful cell collection is not the same as a demonstrated improvement in long-term survival.

## 8. Administration and formulation

- Route: subcutaneous injection.
- Product: sterile, preservative-free, white-to-off-white lyophilised powder in a single-dose vial.
- Each vial contains 73 mg motixafortide acetate, equivalent to 62 mg motixafortide in 1.7 mL after reconstitution.
- Reconstitution uses 2 mL of 0.45% sodium chloride solution, or the alternative diluent preparation specified in the label.
- Formulation includes mannitol and hydrochloric acid for pH adjustment.
- Labelled regimen: initiate after four days of daily filgrastim; 1.25 mg/kg actual body weight subcutaneously 10–14 hours before apheresis. A second dose may be given before a third apheresis under the label's criteria.
- Current label recommends premedication to reduce hypersensitivity and injection-site reactions.

This is a research dossier, not a patient-specific administration guide. Preparation, premedication, timing, apheresis, and dose decisions must follow the current product label and transplant-centre protocol.

## 9. Safety and warnings

Important warnings and precautions in the current US label include:
- Anaphylactic shock and other hypersensitivity reactions.
- Injection-site reactions, pain, erythema, pruritus, and swelling.
- Tumour-cell mobilisation in patients with leukaemia; APHEXDA is not indicated for stem-cell mobilisation in patients with leukaemia.
- Leukocytosis.
- Potential mobilisation of tumour cells in patients with multiple myeloma; the clinical significance and implications must be considered in the transplant setting.
- Embryo-fetal toxicity; effective contraception is required for the label-specified period.
- Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- Common adverse reactions include injection-site reactions, injection-site pain, erythema, pruritus, generalised pruritus, flushing, and back pain.

Safety frequencies must be linked to the study population and current label version. Pediatric safety and effectiveness have not been established.

## 10. Drug interactions and co-medications

- Filgrastim is a required component of the approved mobilization regimen, not an optional interaction.
- The current label includes premedication with an H1 antihistamine, H2 blocker, and leukotriene inhibitor to reduce hypersensitivity/injection-site reaction risk.
- Formal interaction data are limited; do not infer absence of interaction from peptide catabolism alone.
- The mobilisation context, concomitant anti-myeloma therapy, blood counts, and apheresis timing require specialist review.

## 13. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- Primary target: **CXCR4**, a class A chemokine GPCR.
- Endogenous ligand: **CXCL12/SDF-1α**.
- Biological process: hematopoietic stem/progenitor cell retention, homing, and trafficking between marrow niche and peripheral circulation.
- Functional biomarker: circulating CD34-positive cell count, measured during mobilization and apheresis.
- Relevant pathways include CXCL12-CXCR4 signalling, marrow stromal-cell interactions, adhesion, and hematopoietic cell migration.
- No validated germline pharmacogenomic test is required by the label to select patients for motixafortide.
