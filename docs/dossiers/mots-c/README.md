# MOTS-c

> Workbook entry: **125/128** · Curation status: **in_progress**

## Overview

Mitochondria-derived 16-amino-acid peptide studied in metabolic and stress-response biology. It is not FDA-approved; claims for weight loss, longevity or metabolic disease treatment are investigational.

## Identity and chemistry

Human MOTS-c is a 16-residue peptide encoded within the mitochondrial 12S rRNA region: MRWQEMGYIFYPRKLR. Verify mitochondrial genetic-code translation and residue annotation against the primary sequence publication before registering the structure.

## Mechanism of action

Experimental studies indicate cellular stress-responsive nuclear translocation and regulation of metabolic pathways, including folate/methionine-cycle-related signalling and AMPK-associated effects. Precise receptor/target relationships and translation to human therapeutic action remain under investigation.

## Pharmacodynamics

Preclinical studies report changes in glucose metabolism, insulin sensitivity, exercise adaptation and stress responses in cells and animal models. Human pharmacodynamic and exposure-response evidence remains limited.

## Pharmacokinetics

No approved human formulation or robust clinical PK label. Endogenous circulating MOTS-c measurements do not establish PK of exogenous synthetic peptide.

## Clinical evidence

Human observational studies and early translational work do not establish efficacy of administered MOTS-c for diabetes, obesity, exercise performance or aging. Animal findings should not be extrapolated to treatment claims.

## Safety and tolerability

Human safety of exogenous MOTS-c is not established. Immunogenicity, off-target effects, dose-related toxicity and long-term safety require formal clinical study; research-grade material quality is variable.

## Regulatory status

Not FDA-approved; no verified major-regulator marketing authorization identified. Commercial research products are not approved medicines.

## Formulation and manufacturing

No standardized authorized medicinal formulation or validated commercial GMP specification. Endogenous peptide biology and synthetic administered product must be distinguished.

## Bioinformatics and translational research

Potential research: mitochondrial-derived peptide biology, AMPK, one-carbon metabolism, insulin signalling, exercise adaptation, mitochondrial-nuclear communication and multi-omics.
