## 2. Sequence and primary structure

The FDA label identifies the molecule as:

**5-oxo-L-prolyl-L-histidyl-L-tryptophyl-L-seryl-L-tyrosyl-3-(2-naphthyl)-D-alanyl-L-leucyl-L-arginyl-L-prolyl-glycinamide.**

Condensed sequence: **pGlu-His-Trp-Ser-Tyr-D-2Nal-Leu-Arg-Pro-Gly-NH₂**.

Nafarelin is a decapeptide analogue of native GnRH. The key substitution is D-3-(2-naphthyl)alanine at residue 6, replacing the native glycine at that position; the C-terminus is glycinamide and the N-terminus is pyroglutamyl. The D-amino-acid/aromatic substitution contributes to agonist potency and peptide stability. Do not confuse nafarelin with related analogues that have different residue-6 substitutions or terminal modifications.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Nafarelin is a potent **GnRH receptor agonist** acting at pituitary gonadotrophs.

1. Initial exposure activates pituitary GnRH receptors and transiently increases luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH).
2. This initial flare can temporarily increase gonadal steroidogenesis.
3. With continuous/repeated exposure, pituitary responsiveness is desensitised/downregulated, reducing LH and FSH secretion.
4. Gonadal sex-steroid production falls, suppressing oestrogen- or testosterone-dependent tissues and functions.

This is a biphasic agonist mechanism, not immediate receptor antagonism. The product label reports that twice-daily administration reduces gonadal steroid secretion over approximately four weeks. In CPP, suppression of LH response to GnRH stimulation to a prepubertal response was observed within one month in clinical studies.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics and biological effects

- **Endometriosis:** suppression of ovarian steroidogenesis produces a hypo-oestrogenic state and reduces stimulation of endometriotic tissue.
- **Central precocious puberty (CPP):** suppression of LH/FSH and gonadal sex steroids arrests or regresses gonadotropin-dependent pubertal progression and slows growth and skeletal maturation.
- **Initial flare:** transient vaginal bleeding, breast enlargement, or other early pubertal/endocrine signs can occur during the first weeks in children; the label describes these as consequences of initial stimulation.
- **Reversibility:** return of menses, pubertal gonadotropin/sex-steroid levels, and secondary sexual development has been documented in followed children after stopping treatment. Long-term fertility outcomes are not established by pregnancy data in the label.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

Intranasal pharmacokinetics are formulation- and population-dependent.

**Adults (label data):**
- Rapid absorption; peak serum concentrations at approximately 10–40 minutes.
- Following 200 µg base, mean peak concentration 0.6 ng/mL (range 0.2–1.4); following 400 µg, mean peak 1.8 ng/mL (range 0.5–5.3).
- Mean bioavailability after 400 µg: 2.8% (range 1.2–5.6%).
- Mean serum half-life after intranasal administration: approximately 3 hours.
- Approximately 80% plasma protein binding at 4°C (label condition).
- No significant accumulation in a small study of 18 healthy women receiving twice-daily administration for 22 days.

**Children (label data):**
- Peak concentrations at approximately 10–45 minutes.
- After 400 µg base, peak 2.2 ng/mL; after 600 µg, peak 6.6 ng/mL.
- Mean half-life after 400 µg: approximately 2.5 hours.

Following radiolabelled subcutaneous administration in men, 44–55% of dose-related radioactivity was recovered in urine and 18.5–44.2% in faeces; approximately 3% appeared as unchanged nafarelin in urine. These data are not equivalent to intranasal human mass-balance data. Six metabolites were identified; the major reported metabolite was Tyr-D(2)-Nal-Leu-Arg-Pro-Gly-NH₂ (residues 5–10). Pharmacokinetics in hepatic or renal impairment and nasal mucosal metabolism have not been determined in the label.

The current US SYNAREL label presents separate physician-labeling sections for:

## 8. Administration and formulation

- Route: intranasal spray to nasal mucosa.
- Labelled concentration: 2 mg/mL, expressed as nafarelin base.
- Metered actuation: approximately 100 µL containing approximately 200 µg nafarelin base.
- Bottle: 8 mL; intended to deliver at least 60 sprays after priming.
- Excipients listed: benzalkonium chloride, glacial acetic acid, sodium hydroxide or hydrochloric acid for pH adjustment, sorbitol, and purified water.
- Storage: upright at 25°C; permitted excursions 15–30°C; protect from light.

This dossier does not provide patient-specific dosing instructions. Refer to the current approved product labeling and the treating clinician for indication-specific administration.

## 9. Safety and contraindications

The current label contraindicates use in patients with hypersensitivity to GnRH/GnRH agonists or excipients, undiagnosed abnormal vaginal bleeding, pregnancy or potential pregnancy during treatment, and breastfeeding.

Important label warnings/precautions include:
- Initial gonadotropin/sex-steroid flare.
- Mood and psychiatric events, including emotional lability, irritability, anger, and aggression.
- Postmarketing convulsions, including reports in patients with and without recognised risk factors; concomitant medicines associated with seizures are relevant to review.
- GnRH-agonist class reports of severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP).
- Pseudotumor cerebri/idiopathic intracranial hypertension reports in paediatric patients.
- Ovarian cysts/enlargement in some women, particularly early in therapy.
- Hypo-oestrogenic effects and other endocrine consequences described in the endometriosis labeling.
- Pregnancy-related fetal risk and lack of established safety in nursing mothers.

The label reports common paediatric trial events including acne, transient breast enlargement, vaginal bleeding, emotional lability, transient pubic-hair increase, body odour, and seborrhoea. Rates from paediatric CPP trials must not be represented as adult endometriosis adverse-event rates.

## 10. Drug interactions

No pharmacokinetic-based drug–drug interaction studies were conducted according to the label. The label states interactions are not expected on the basis that nafarelin is a peptide primarily degraded by peptidases rather than CYP450 enzymes and is approximately 80% protein-bound under the stated assay condition. This is a mechanistic expectation, not proof that clinically relevant interactions are impossible. Review concomitant medicines, especially where seizure risk is relevant.

## 13. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- Primary target: **GNRHR** (pituitary GnRH receptor; GPCR).
- Downstream endocrine axis: hypothalamic GnRH → pituitary LH/FSH → gonadal steroidogenesis.
- Relevant physiological outputs: ovarian oestradiol/progesterone and testicular testosterone; sex-steroid-dependent reproductive tissues and pubertal maturation.
- A systems-biology dossier should distinguish receptor-level agonism from downstream suppression caused by sustained receptor stimulation.
- No nafarelin-specific omics signature or validated pharmacogenomic biomarker is established in this dossier.
