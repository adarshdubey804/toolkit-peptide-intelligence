## 2. Molecular identity and sequence

Octreotide is a synthetic cyclic octapeptide derived from the somatostatin pharmacophore.

**Sequence:** D-Phe-Cys-Phe-D-Trp-Lys-Thr-Cys-Thr-ol

- Eight amino-acid-derived residues.
- One intramolecular disulfide bond between the two cysteine residues.
- C-terminal threoninol (Thr-ol), rather than the native somatostatin C-terminal amino-acid carboxylate.
- Contains D-phenylalanine and D-tryptophan residues.
- Molecular formula (free peptide): C₄₉H₆₆N₁₀O₁₀S₂.
- Molecular weight: approximately 1019.2 g/mol (average molecular mass; verify salt/hydrate basis for a specific product).

The marketed injection is supplied as octreotide acetate. The active peptide sequence and acetate counterion must be represented separately in chemical records.

## 3. Structural and chemical properties

Octreotide retains the key somatostatin receptor-binding pharmacophore while being substantially more resistant to enzymatic degradation than native somatostatin. Its constrained cyclic structure, D-residues and C-terminal threoninol contribute to longer biological activity.

The disulfide bridge is essential to the cyclic conformation. A linear representation without the S–S bond is not an adequate molecular identity record.

## 4. Disease biology

### Acromegaly
Acromegaly is usually caused by a growth-hormone-secreting pituitary somatotroph adenoma. Excess GH stimulates hepatic and peripheral IGF-1 production, leading to somatic overgrowth, metabolic complications and increased morbidity. Somatostatin receptors on pituitary tumour cells provide a pharmacological route to suppress GH secretion.

### Functional neuroendocrine tumours
Some neuroendocrine tumours secrete serotonin and other vasoactive mediators, producing carcinoid syndrome (including flushing and diarrhoea). VIP-secreting tumours can cause profound secretory diarrhoea, hypokalaemia and achlorhydria. Somatostatin analogues suppress secretion from receptor-expressing tumour cells and can reduce hormone-mediated symptoms.

## 5. Mechanism of action

Octreotide binds somatostatin receptors (SSTRs), with high affinity for SSTR2 and appreciable activity at SSTR5 and other receptor subtypes depending on assay and system.

1. Receptor binding activates Gi/o-coupled signalling.
2. Adenylyl cyclase activity and intracellular cAMP are reduced.
3. Voltage-gated calcium entry and secretory-granule exocytosis are inhibited.
4. GH release from pituitary somatotrophs is suppressed; secretion of serotonin, VIP and other peptides from susceptible neuroendocrine tumours is reduced.

The receptor-subtype profile is not identical to native somatostatin. Avoid describing octreotide as a selective SSTR2-only ligand.

## 6. Indications: formulation-specific distinctions

**Immediate-release Sandostatin injection (US label):**
- Acromegaly in patients with inadequate response to or unsuitable for surgery and/or radiotherapy, with label-specific biochemical and symptom objectives.
- Severe diarrhoea and flushing episodes associated with metastatic carcinoid tumours.
- Profuse watery diarrhoea associated with VIP-secreting tumours (VIPomas).

**Sandostatin LAR Depot:** long-acting intramuscular formulation for label-defined acromegaly and symptom control in carcinoid syndrome and VIPoma; the exact indication wording and prior stabilization requirements must be taken from the current LAR label.

**Mycapssa:** oral delayed-release octreotide capsules for long-term maintenance treatment in acromegaly patients who have responded to and tolerated octreotide or lanreotide injection. It is not interchangeable milligram-for-milligram with injectable octreotide.

**Bynfezia Pen:** subcutaneous octreotide injection product with formulation-specific labelled uses including acromegaly and symptom control in selected functional neuroendocrine tumours. Verify exact current label text and age/population limits.

Do not infer that every indication is approved for every formulation or route.

## 7. Administration and dosage overview

Dosing varies materially by indication, formulation, prior response and jurisdiction.

- Immediate-release injection is administered subcutaneously; IV use is described for selected clinical circumstances and label instructions.
- Sandostatin LAR Depot is administered by deep intramuscular injection at extended intervals, typically every four weeks after appropriate stabilization with short-acting octreotide.
- Mycapssa is an oral delayed-release capsule taken on an empty stomach according to its own titration and monitoring instructions.
- Bynfezia Pen is a subcutaneous product with its own device-specific instructions.

**No universal octreotide dose is supplied here.** Consult the current label for the exact brand, indication, starting dose, titration, conversion, missed-dose and administration instructions. Never convert between immediate-release, LAR and oral formulations by simple arithmetic.

## 8. Pharmacodynamics

- Suppresses GH secretion in responsive acromegaly.
- Reduces IGF-1 downstream when GH control is achieved.
- In functional NETs, reduces secretion of serotonin and other vasoactive peptides, improving diarrhoea and flushing in some patients.
- In VIPoma, suppresses VIP secretion and can reduce secretory diarrhoea.
- Can inhibit pancreatic and gastrointestinal hormone secretion and alter gallbladder motility.

Clinical response should be assessed using indication-specific endpoints, such as age-adjusted IGF-1 and GH for acromegaly, and symptom/hormone measures for functional NETs.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics and formulation effects

Immediate-release octreotide is absorbed rapidly after subcutaneous administration and has a short plasma elimination half-life (approximately 1.5 hours in label-described studies). It is metabolized and eliminated through hepatic and renal pathways.

Sandostatin LAR uses biodegradable polymer microspheres to release octreotide over weeks. Mycapssa uses an oral delayed-release formulation with absorption-enhancing technology; its exposure and dosing are not equivalent to injection. Product-specific pharmacokinetic parameters must not be transferred between formulations.

## 10. Safety and monitoring

Important risks and precautions include:
- **Gallbladder abnormalities:** gallstones, sludge, cholecystitis, cholangitis and pancreatitis; assess when clinically indicated.
- **Glucose disturbances:** hyperglycaemia or hypoglycaemia due to changes in insulin, glucagon and counter-regulatory hormone secretion; monitor glucose, particularly at initiation and dose changes.
- **Thyroid effects:** possible hypothyroidism; monitor thyroid function as indicated.
- **Cardiac effects:** bradycardia, conduction abnormalities and ECG changes have been reported; review cardiac history and interacting medicines.
- **Steatorrhoea / malabsorption:** suppression of pancreatic enzymes and bile-related effects can cause loose stools, fat malabsorption and nutritional consequences.
- **Vitamin B12:** levels may decrease during chronic treatment; monitor where clinically appropriate.
- **Injection-site effects:** pain and local reactions, especially with depot formulation.
- **Hypersensitivity:** serious reactions are possible.

For Mycapssa and other specific products, consult their own labels for product-specific warnings, contraindications and monitoring.

## 11. Drug interactions

Potential interactions include:
- altered absorption of oral medicines due to changes in gastrointestinal motility;
- possible effects on cyclosporine exposure;
- additive bradycardia or conduction effects with beta-blockers and other rate-limiting drugs;
- altered insulin and oral hypoglycaemic requirements;
- possible interactions with drugs whose absorption depends on gastrointestinal transit.

The clinically relevant interaction profile differs by formulation. Verify the current label and assess the patient's medication list.

## 12. Formulation and manufacturing

Immediate-release octreotide injection is a sterile aqueous solution of octreotide acetate. LAR is a long-acting microsphere suspension for deep IM administration. Mycapssa is an oral delayed-release capsule. Bynfezia Pen is a prefilled multidose subcutaneous injection device.

Quality attributes include peptide identity and purity, correct disulfide formation, stereochemical integrity, acetate content, potency, related substances, residual solvents/reagents, sterility and endotoxin. For LAR, additional critical attributes include microsphere size distribution, polymer properties, peptide loading, release kinetics and suspension performance. For oral formulations, dissolution, stability and bioavailability performance are product-specific.
