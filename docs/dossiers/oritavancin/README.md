# Dossier 087 — Oritavancin (Orbactiv; Kimyrsa)

**Workbook entry:** 087 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **US approval:** Orbactiv 2014; Kimyrsa 2021 | **Status:** FDA-approved products  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and product distinction
Oritavancin is a semisynthetic lipoglycopeptide antibacterial derived from a glycopeptide natural-product scaffold. It contains a lipophilic side chain and structural modifications that support binding to cell-wall precursors and membrane effects. It is not a simple linear peptide and should be represented using a stereochemically complete chemical structure rather than a one-letter sequence.

Orbactiv and Kimyrsa contain the same active antibacterial moiety but are distinct approved products/formulations with different preparation/administration characteristics and product labels. Preserve product-level records and do not assume all excipients, infusion instructions or product-specific labeling are interchangeable.

## Mechanism
Oritavancin has three described antibacterial actions: (1) inhibition of transglycosylation by binding peptidoglycan stem-peptide precursors; (2) inhibition of transpeptidation by binding peptide-bridging segments; and (3) disruption of bacterial membrane integrity, including depolarization and permeabilization. Its activity is concentration-dependent against susceptible Gram-positive organisms. Resistance can arise in serial passage and should be considered in susceptibility interpretation.

## Pharmacokinetics
Oritavancin is administered intravenously as a long-acting agent. It is approximately 85% bound to plasma proteins, has extensive tissue distribution, and a terminal half-life of approximately 245 hours in population PK analyses. It is slowly excreted unchanged in urine and feces; human metabolism is limited. The long half-life is clinically important when managing adverse reactions and antimicrobial stewardship.

## Clinical evidence and indication
Orbactiv is indicated for adult patients with acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections (ABSSSI) caused by designated susceptible Gram-positive bacteria. Kimyrsa is another FDA-approved oritavancin product for ABSSSI, with product-specific infusion and administration instructions. SOLO I and SOLO II were pivotal randomized trials comparing single-dose oritavancin with vancomycin for ABSSSI and supported noninferiority on early clinical response. Do not extrapolate this evidence to bacteremia, endocarditis or other deep infections without separate evidence.

## Safety and laboratory interference
Serious hypersensitivity, infusion reactions and *C. difficile*-associated diarrhea are important risks. A distinctive laboratory issue is artificial prolongation of phospholipid-dependent coagulation assays: aPTT may be prolonged for up to 120 hours, PT/INR up to 12 hours and ACT up to 24 hours after a dose. This is an in-vitro assay interference, not a true anticoagulant effect. Intravenous unfractionated heparin is contraindicated for 120 hours after administration because aPTT monitoring may be unreliable. Drug-interaction potential involving CYP enzymes and warfarin should be reviewed using current label.

## Formulation and quality
Orbactiv and Kimyrsa are supplied as lyophilized IV products. Critical attributes include active moiety identity, salt form, potency, related substances, glycopeptide impurity profile, reconstitution, particulate matter, sterility, endotoxin and container closure. Maintain distinct formulation and product identifiers.

## Regulatory status
Orbactiv received initial US approval in 2014; Kimyrsa was approved in 2021. Both are for ABSSSI with product-specific prescribing information. Verify current label and availability independently.
