## 3. Mechanism of action
Oxytocin is a cyclic nonapeptide with a Cys1-Cys6 disulfide bond and a C-terminal glycinamide. It activates the oxytocin receptor, a Gq/11-coupled GPCR, increasing intracellular calcium in uterine myometrium and promoting contraction. It also supports milk ejection via mammary myoepithelial contraction. Uterine tachysystole, foetal compromise, and water intoxication are important risks.

Mechanistic confidence should be separated into (a) established target engagement, (b) downstream pharmacology, and (c) hypotheses that remain incompletely established in humans. Do not infer clinical benefit solely from in-vitro target activity.

## 15. Verified chemistry and molecular structure

The current US Pitocin label identifies synthetic oxytocin as a nonapeptide with empirical formula **C43H66N12O12S2** and molecular weight **1007.19 Da**. Mature oxytocin sequence is **CYIQNCPLG-NH2** (Cys-Tyr-Ile-Gln-Asn-Cys-Pro-Leu-Gly-amide), with an intramolecular disulfide bond between Cys1 and Cys6. The C-terminal glycinamide and disulfide-constrained six-residue ring are essential features of the active hormone.

Oxytocin differs from arginine vasopressin at two of the eight residues in the cyclic portion. This close structural relationship helps explain some shared receptor-family pharmacology, including oxytocin's antidiuretic and pressor effects at sufficiently high exposure.

## 16. Receptor signalling and pharmacodynamics

The oxytocin receptor (OXTR) is a class A GPCR that predominantly couples to Gq/11 in uterine myometrium:
1. Oxytocin binds OXTR.
2. Gq/11 activates phospholipase Cβ.
3. PIP2 is hydrolysed to IP3 and DAG.
4. IP3 promotes sarcoplasmic-reticulum Ca2+ release; membrane depolarization and calcium influx further increase cytosolic Ca2+.
5. Calcium-calmodulin activates myosin light-chain kinase, increasing actin–myosin interaction and uterine contraction.

OXTR expression increases markedly during pregnancy and is high near term. Uterine sensitivity is therefore not fixed; the same exposure can produce different contractile responses depending on gestational stage, receptor abundance, prior uterine activity, and individual physiology.

In mammary tissue, oxytocin contracts myoepithelial cells and causes milk ejection. It is distinct from prolactin, which primarily supports milk synthesis.

## 17. Pharmacokinetics

The current US label reports:
- Plasma half-life: approximately **1–6 minutes**, shorter in late pregnancy and during lactation.
- Distribution: extracellular fluid; small amounts may reach fetal circulation.
- IV uterine response: almost immediate, generally subsiding within about one hour after stopping exposure.
- IM uterine response: begins within approximately 3–5 minutes and may persist for 2–3 hours.
- Elimination: rapid removal mainly by kidney and liver; only small amounts are excreted unchanged in urine.

These response durations describe uterine pharmacodynamics and must not be mistaken for plasma half-life. Oxytocin also has antidiuretic properties; prolonged administration with large volumes of electrolyte-free fluid can contribute to water intoxication and hyponatraemia.

## 18. Clinical use and evidence context

### Labour induction and augmentation
Pitocin is labelled for initiation or improvement of uterine contractions when medically indicated and considered suitable to achieve vaginal delivery. The current label explicitly states that it is **not indicated for elective induction of labour** when there is no medical indication, because available data are inadequate to assess the benefit–risk balance. Uterine activity and fetal heart rate require continuous clinical monitoring during infusion; this dossier intentionally does not reproduce an administration protocol.

ACOG's 2024 guidance considers either low-dose or high-dose oxytocin strategies reasonable for active management of labour. Its evidence synthesis reports that early augmentation in protracted labour modestly increased spontaneous vaginal delivery (RR 1.09; 95% CI 1.03–1.17), while increasing tachysystole (RR 2.90; 95% CI 1.21–6.94). These findings relate to selected labour-management contexts, not all induction scenarios.

### Postpartum haemorrhage prevention
Oxytocin is a uterotonic used to prevent postpartum haemorrhage by promoting uterine contraction after birth. A Cochrane review of prophylactic oxytocin versus no uterotonic/placebo found lower risks of blood loss ≥500 mL (RR 0.51; 95% CI 0.37–0.72; low-certainty evidence) and ≥1000 mL (RR 0.59; 95% CI 0.42–0.83; low-certainty evidence), and probably reduced need for additional uterotonics (RR 0.54; 95% CI 0.36–0.80; moderate-certainty evidence).

WHO's consolidated PPH guideline recommends a uterotonic for prevention at all births and lists oxytocin among the recommended options. Distinguish prevention from treatment of established PPH, and distinguish guideline recommendations from the precise approved product label.

### Route comparison
A Cochrane review of seven trials (7,817 women) comparing IV versus IM oxytocin after vaginal birth found high-certainty evidence that IV administration reduced PPH ≥500 mL (RR 0.78; 95% CI 0.66–0.92) and blood transfusion (RR 0.44; 95% CI 0.26–0.77) in the studied hospital settings. These data do not establish that one route is preferable in every clinical context.

## 19. Safety and major risk domains

Important hazards include:
- **Uterine tachysystole / hyperstimulation:** may impair uteroplacental perfusion and cause fetal heart-rate abnormalities.
- **Uterine rupture:** risk is increased in susceptible settings, including certain uterine scars or excessive uterine stimulation.
- **Fetal compromise:** excessive contraction frequency or duration can reduce fetal oxygenation.
- **Water intoxication and hyponatraemia:** oxytocin has antidiuretic activity; prolonged exposure with excessive electrolyte-free fluid can cause seizures, coma, or death.
- **Hypotension and cardiovascular effects:** especially with rapid IV administration; product and route context matter.
- **Anaphylactic or hypersensitivity reactions:** rare but clinically important.
- **Neonatal effects:** fetal exposure and perinatal complications depend on maternal, fetal, and intrapartum context.

The product label requires continuous assessment of uterine activity and fetal heart rate during labour infusion. This dossier does not provide patient-specific dosing or obstetric management instructions.

## 20. Formulation and quality

Pitocin is supplied as a sterile, clear, colourless aqueous solution of synthetic oxytocin for IV infusion or IM injection. Current US product presentations differ; the May 2026 DailyMed label for a pharmacy bulk package describes 10 units/mL and a formulation containing chlorobutanol preservative, acetic acid and ammonium acetate buffers. Do not assume all oxytocin products share the same preservative or concentration.

Critical quality attributes include:
- Correct nonapeptide sequence, stereochemistry and Cys1–Cys6 disulfide.
- Peptide content and biological potency in oxytocic units.
- Related peptide impurities, including vasopressin-related contaminants.
- Oxidation, deamidation and other degradation products.
- pH, preservative and buffer content for the specific presentation.
- Sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter, container-closure integrity and stability.

## 21. Regulatory and label distinctions

- **United States:** Pitocin is an FDA-approved synthetic oxytocin injection. The current label includes medically indicated labour induction/augmentation, selected incomplete or inevitable abortion contexts, and control of postpartum uterine bleeding.
- **Elective induction:** the current label states Pitocin is not indicated for elective induction without medical indication.
- **International guidance:** WHO recommendations for PPH prevention are clinical-practice guidance and should not be conflated with a specific national product label.
- **Approval-year field:** workbook's 1980 year is a provenance entry and should not be interpreted as the date of first human use or first availability of oxytocin.
