> **Scope and classification.** Pegulicianine is an intravenously administered, protease-activatable optical imaging agent used with a compatible fluorescence imaging system during breast-conserving surgery. It is not an anticancer medicine. Structurally it is a complex peptide-containing, dye/quencher/PEG conjugate rather than a conventional standalone peptide.

## 2. Clinical purpose and labelled indication

LUMISIGHT is indicated for fluorescence imaging in adults with breast cancer as an adjunct to intraoperative detection of cancerous tissue within the resection cavity after removal of the primary specimen during lumpectomy. Fluorescent positive areas can alert the surgeon to tissue that may warrant additional excision.

It is an adjunctive visualization aid. A negative signal does not exclude residual cancer, and positive signal can occur in non-cancerous tissue. Imaging findings must be interpreted with the operative context and standard pathology.

## 3. Molecular architecture and chemistry

Pegulicianine acetate is a large, heterogeneous conjugate comprising:
- a protease-cleavable amino-acid/peptide backbone;
- a Cy5-derived fluorescent dye (fluorophore);
- a QSY21-derived dark quencher;
- spacers, including an aminohexanoic-acid (Ahx) spacer and PEG2 spacer; and
- a methoxy-polyethylene glycol chain of approximately 20 kDa (approximately 450 ethylene oxide repeat units).

The intact construct is optically quenched. The labelled chemical description gives a molecular formula with a variable PEG repeat number, n approximately 450, and an approximate molecular-weight range of 20–25 kDa. Because the PEG chain is polydisperse, this is not a single small-molecule molecular weight. The full chemical name and structural diagram are provided in the FDA label.

The labelled construct contains amino-acid residues and peptide bonds, but its pharmacological function depends on the integrated dye–quencher–peptide–PEG architecture. Do not represent it as a simple linear therapeutic peptide or infer a conventional one-letter sequence from the long conjugate name.

## 4. Mechanism of action

**Protease-activated fluorescence (activatable optical imaging):**

1. In the intact administered molecule, the QSY21 dark quencher is close to the Cy5 fluorophore and suppresses fluorescence.
2. In and around tumour-associated tissue, proteolytic activity—including cathepsins and matrix metalloproteases (MMPs)—cleaves the peptide backbone.
3. Cleavage separates the quencher-containing fragment from fluorophore-containing products, generating optically active fragments.
4. These fluorescent products can be excited by the compatible imaging system and detected in the operative cavity.

The FDA label reports peak absorption near **650 nm** and peak fluorescence emission near **675 nm** for the active fragments. Enzyme activity is higher in and around tumour and tumour-associated cells than in normal tissue on average, but this biochemical contrast is not perfectly tumour-specific. Protease activity in non-malignant tissue can contribute to false-positive signal; absence of signal cannot rule out malignancy.

## 5. Administration and imaging workflow

- **Dose:** 1 mg/kg based on actual body weight.
- **Route and injection time:** IV injection over 3 minutes.
- **Timing:** administer 2–6 hours before imaging of the lumpectomy cavity.
- **Imaging platform:** Lumicell DVS or another fluorescence imaging device FDA-approved for specific use with pegulicianine in the indicated population.
- **Preparation:** supplied as a dark-blue lyophilized powder. Reconstitute each vial with 4 mL of 0.45% sodium chloride injection; the label describes withdrawal of 3.9 mL at 10 mg/mL. Each vial contains 40 mg pegulicianine (equivalent to 40.1 mg pegulicianine acetate).
- **Handling:** protect from light. The current label specifies use within 4 hours at 20–25°C or within 24 hours refrigerated at 2–8°C after reconstitution.

This is prescribing-information content for trained clinical teams, not instructions for self-administration. Follow the current product and device instructions for preparation, training, and imaging.

## 6. Pharmacology and disposition

- **Activation:** enzymatic cleavage by cathepsins and MMPs produces optically active fragments.
- **Optical properties:** active fragments absorb around 650 nm and emit around 675 nm.
- **Metabolism:** minimal hepatic metabolism was observed in vitro; the principal described transformation is proteolytic cleavage.
- **Excretion:** the human excretion pathway is not fully established. Blue/green urine discoloration (chromaturia) suggests renal excretion of pegulicianine and/or metabolites may occur.
- **Renal impairment:** mild renal impairment did not appear to have a clinically meaningful effect on the reported pharmacokinetics, safety, or efficacy for pegulicianine and fragment 3; fragment 2 data were unavailable. Moderate and severe renal impairment have not been adequately evaluated.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** concentration–response relationships for the active metabolites have not been fully characterized.

## 7. Clinical evidence

The FDA Drug Trials Snapshot identifies the pivotal study as **CL0007 (NCT03686215)**, enrolling 406 patients with confirmed invasive breast cancer, ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS), or both at 14 US sites. Patients who had received neoadjuvant chemotherapy or radiation therapy before surgery were not enrolled in that study.

The device and drug are used as an adjunctive cavity-imaging system. For exact endpoint definitions, analysis populations, sensitivity/specificity estimates, confidence intervals, and subgroup limitations, consult the FDA multidisciplinary review, Summary of Safety and Effectiveness Data, and current label rather than quoting unverified headline values. These metrics should not be generalized to all breast cancer surgery populations.

## 8. Safety

**Boxed warning:** anaphylaxis and other serious hypersensitivity reactions can occur during or following administration. The original FDA label reports anaphylaxis in 4 of 726 patients (0.6%) in clinical studies.

Key label precautions:
- assess history of hypersensitivity to contrast media and products containing polyethylene glycol (PEG) before administration;
- contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity reaction to pegulicianine;
- have emergency resuscitation medicines, equipment, and trained personnel immediately available;
- monitor patients for hypersensitivity and discontinue administration and treat promptly if a reaction is suspected;
- fluorescence signal must not be used to rule out cancer, and positive signal may arise from non-cancerous tissue;
- avoid sentinel lymph-node mapping dyes before imaging the lumpectomy cavity because dyes can interfere with imaging.

Consult the complete current prescribing information for adverse reactions, drug interactions, and all warnings. Do not use this dossier as a substitute for the label.

## 10. Formulation, storage and quality notes

The product is a sterile, dark-blue, lyophilized powder for IV solution. The vial includes pegulicianine acetate and phosphate-buffer/mannitol excipients. Protect the product from light. Reconstituted solution has specified room-temperature and refrigerated in-use windows; see Section 5 and the current label for exact conditions. The complex PEG-conjugated dye architecture and polydispersity make conventional peptide purity/sequence characterization alone insufficient to describe the complete drug substance.
