# Dossier 090 — Polymyxin B

**Workbook entry:** 090 | **Category:** Infectious disease | **First US approval:** historical date not supplied | **Status:** FDA-approved legacy products  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and chemistry
Polymyxin B is a mixture of closely related cyclic cationic lipopeptide antibiotics, principally polymyxin B1 and B2, with minor components depending on the product. The molecules contain a cyclic heptapeptide ring, a linear tripeptide segment and an N-terminal fatty-acyl chain. They are natural-product mixtures rather than a single sequence-defined peptide. Distinguish polymyxin B from polymyxin E (colistin) and from colistimethate sodium.

## Mechanism
Polymyxin B binds lipid A in Gram-negative bacterial LPS, displacing calcium and magnesium that stabilize the outer membrane. This increases membrane permeability and disrupts the cytoplasmic membrane, producing bactericidal activity. LPS modification, including addition of phosphoethanolamine or related substitutions, can reduce binding and cause resistance. Activity is primarily against Gram-negative organisms and is not reliable against Gram-positive bacteria.

## Pharmacology
Parenteral polymyxin B is used for selected serious infections caused by susceptible Gram-negative bacteria. Unlike colistimethate, polymyxin B is administered as the active drug rather than as a sulfomethylated prodrug. It is largely cleared through nonrenal pathways, with complex PK in critical illness; renal function still matters clinically because nephrotoxicity is a major adverse effect. Do not transfer colistimethate conversion assumptions or CMS unit conversions to polymyxin B.

## Clinical evidence and use
Polymyxin B has historical and salvage use for multidrug-resistant Gram-negative infections. Contemporary guidance recommends reserving polymyxins for situations where safer effective agents are unavailable, with choice between polymyxin B and colistin depending on infection site, organism and PK considerations. Evidence is limited by observational designs, heterogeneous dosing and confounding by severity. Avoid claiming equivalence to newer beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor agents.

## Safety
Nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity are major class toxicities. Neurological effects include paresthesias, dizziness, weakness and potentially neuromuscular blockade. Hypersensitivity and local toxicity may occur. Topical ophthalmic and otic products have different exposure and risk profiles from systemic injection; keep formulations separate.

## Formulation and quality
Commercial products may contain polymyxin B sulfate for injection or topical use. Critical quality attributes include component ratio (B1/B2 and minor congeners), identity, potency, peptide profile, related substances, residual solvents, sterility/endotoxin for parenteral products, particulate matter and stability. Do not represent the drug as a single exact sequence unless product composition supports it.

## Regulatory status
The workbook identifies FDA-approved status but does not provide a first approval date. Multiple dosage forms and combination topical products exist; verify each application and indication separately. Do not infer a universal approval history.
