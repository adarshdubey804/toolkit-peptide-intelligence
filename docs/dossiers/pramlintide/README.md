## 1. Identity and nomenclature

- **Generic name:** pramlintide.
- **Reference salt:** pramlintide acetate.
- **Drug class:** amylin analogue; adjunctive antihyperglycaemic therapy.
- **Therapeutic context:** used with mealtime insulin in selected people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes.
- **Molecular type:** 37-amino-acid synthetic peptide, structurally related to human amylin.
- **Approval history:** US approval in 2005 (NDA 021332).

## 2. Sequence and molecular design

Pramlintide is a synthetic analogue of human amylin with three substitutions that reduce the peptide's tendency to aggregate:
- Ala at position 25 is replaced by Pro;
- Ser at position 28 is replaced by Pro;
- Ser at position 29 is replaced by Pro.

The sequence is reported as:

`KCNTATCATQRLANFLVHSSNNFGPILPPTNVGSNTY-NH2`

The peptide has an intramolecular disulfide bond between Cys2 and Cys7 and a C-terminal amide. Sequence numbering and terminal chemistry must be cross-checked against a curated primary chemical record before downstream structure generation.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Pramlintide is an amylin analogue and acts through amylin receptor systems. Its principal glucose-regulatory effects are:
1. slowing gastric emptying, thereby reducing the rate at which meal-derived glucose enters circulation;
2. suppressing inappropriate postprandial glucagon secretion; and
3. promoting satiety and reducing food intake.

These actions complement insulin's effects. Pramlintide is not a substitute for insulin in people who require insulin therapy.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- Reduces postprandial glucose excursions when used as an adjunct to mealtime insulin.
- Slows gastric emptying.
- Suppresses postprandial glucagon secretion.
- Can reduce caloric intake and body weight.
- The glucose-lowering effect is meal-related and depends on concomitant insulin management.

Quantitative effect sizes should be extracted from pivotal trials and the current label with population, endpoint, dose, and follow-up clearly stated.

## 8. Regulatory history and indications

- **United States:** FDA-approved in 2005 as an adjunct treatment for people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes who use mealtime insulin and have not achieved desired glucose control despite optimal insulin therapy.
- It is not indicated for treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis.

## 9. Administration and use constraints

Pramlintide is administered subcutaneously before major meals, in addition to mealtime insulin. The prescribing information requires a reduction in mealtime insulin when initiating therapy to reduce severe hypoglycaemia risk; exact adjustment and titration instructions belong to the clinician and must be taken from the current label. Pramlintide and insulin must not be mixed in the same syringe.

This dossier is scientific documentation, not a dosing recommendation.

## 10. Safety and precautions

Key safety issues include:
- severe hypoglycaemia, particularly in people with type 1 diabetes when used with mealtime insulin;
- nausea, vomiting, anorexia, and other gastrointestinal adverse effects;
- delayed gastric emptying;
- risk of altered absorption of concomitant oral medicines;
- contraindication in confirmed gastroparesis and hypoglycaemia unawareness, as specified in the US label.

The full contraindications, adverse-event frequencies, warnings, patient selection, pregnancy/lactation data, paediatric evidence, and post-marketing reports remain to be extracted and checked against the current label.

## 11. Drug interactions

Because pramlintide delays gastric emptying, it may alter the rate of absorption of concomitant oral medicines. The label provides timing and monitoring cautions for selected medicines. A full interaction matrix is pending; each interaction must include the affected medicine/class, mechanism, clinical consequence, recommended label action, and source.
