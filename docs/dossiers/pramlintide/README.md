# Pramlintide (Symlin) — dossier 007

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Workbook position:** metabolic peptide medicines, following the insulin analogues.  
**Entity class:** synthetic, non-glycosylated amylin analogue (37-residue peptide).  
**Reference product:** Symlin / SymlinPen (pramlintide acetate).

## 1. Identity and nomenclature

- **Generic name:** pramlintide.
- **Reference salt:** pramlintide acetate.
- **Drug class:** amylin analogue; adjunctive antihyperglycaemic therapy.
- **Therapeutic context:** adjunct to mealtime insulin in selected people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes.
- **Molecular type:** synthetic 37-amino-acid polypeptide related to human amylin.
- **US initial approval:** 2005, NDA 021332.

## 2. Sequence and molecular design

Pramlintide differs from human amylin at three positions:
- Ala25 → Pro;
- Ser28 → Pro;
- Ser29 → Pro.

**Label-reported sequence:**

`KCNTATCATQRLANFLVHSSNNFGPILPPTNVGSNTY-NH2`

The peptide has a disulfide bridge between Cys2 and Cys7 and a C-terminal amide. The FDA label provides the structural formula and confirms the substitutions and disulfide bond.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Pramlintide is an analogue of human amylin, a hormone co-secreted with insulin by pancreatic beta cells in response to food intake. It acts as an amylin-receptor agonist. Its principal glucose-regulatory actions are:
1. slowing gastric emptying, reducing the rate of meal-derived glucose appearance;
2. suppressing inappropriate postprandial glucagon secretion; and
3. increasing satiety and reducing food intake.

These actions complement insulin. Pramlintide is not a substitute for insulin in people who require insulin therapy. Receptor subtype-specific statements require separate receptor pharmacology evidence and are not inferred from the broad class mechanism.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- Reduces postprandial glucose excursions when used as an adjunct to mealtime insulin.
- Slows gastric emptying.
- Suppresses postprandial glucagon secretion.
- Promotes satiety and can reduce food intake and body weight.
- Its clinical effect is meal-related and depends on concomitant insulin management.

Quantitative effect sizes should be extracted from pivotal trials with population, endpoint, comparator, dose, and follow-up specified.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

The FDA label describes rapid subcutaneous absorption and a short elimination half-life of approximately **48 minutes in healthy individuals**. Complete Cmax, Tmax, bioavailability, distribution, metabolism, and clearance values should be extracted from the full label and clinical pharmacology review before structured entry. The 48-minute value is population-specific and should not be generalized without qualification.

## 7. Manufacturing and formulation

- **Production type:** chemically synthesized peptide.
- **Reference dosage form:** clear, isotonic, sterile solution for subcutaneous injection.
- **Reference SymlinPen concentration:** 1000 mcg/mL in the cited label.
- **Label excipients:** metacresol, D-mannitol, acetic acid, sodium acetate, and water for injection.
- **Reference label pH:** approximately 4.0.
- **Salt:** acetate.

Vial and pen concentrations/presentations differ across label revisions. Excipients, concentration, storage, and in-use stability must be tied to the exact presentation and jurisdiction.

## 8. Regulatory history and indications

- **United States:** FDA-approved in 2005 as an adjunct treatment for people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes who use mealtime insulin and have not achieved desired glucose control despite optimal insulin therapy.
- It is not indicated for treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis.
- Approval and current marketing status must be reported separately. Current commercial availability and presentation-specific status require a fresh regulatory/market check.
- Non-US regulatory status remains to be verified country by country.

## 9. Administration and use constraints

Pramlintide is administered subcutaneously before major meals, in addition to mealtime insulin. The prescribing information requires a reduction in mealtime insulin when initiating therapy to reduce severe hypoglycaemia risk; exact adjustment and titration instructions must be taken from the current label and managed by a clinician. Pramlintide and insulin must not be mixed in the same syringe.

This dossier is scientific documentation, not a dosing recommendation.

## 10. Safety and precautions

Key safety issues include:
- severe insulin-induced hypoglycaemia, particularly in people with type 1 diabetes;
- nausea, vomiting, anorexia, and other gastrointestinal adverse effects;
- delayed gastric emptying;
- altered absorption of concomitant oral medicines;
- contraindication in confirmed gastroparesis and hypoglycaemia unawareness, as specified in the US label.

The complete contraindications, adverse-event frequencies, warnings, patient selection, pregnancy/lactation data, paediatric evidence, and post-marketing reports remain to be extracted and checked against the current label.

## 11. Drug interactions

Because pramlintide delays gastric emptying, it may alter the rate of absorption of concomitant oral medicines. The label provides timing and monitoring cautions for selected medicines. A full interaction matrix is pending; each entry must include the affected medicine/class, mechanism, clinical consequence, label action, and source.

## 12. Clinical evidence

The FDA label and FDA review documents summarize controlled clinical studies in type 1 and type 2 diabetes. The structured clinical evidence section remains incomplete. Required extraction:
- pivotal type 1 diabetes trials;
- pivotal type 2 diabetes trials;
- HbA1c and postprandial glucose endpoints;
- body-weight outcomes;
- severe hypoglycaemia and nausea rates;
- study design, sample size, treatment duration, comparator, registry ID, and publication DOI/PMID.

Avoid generalized weight-loss or cardiovascular-benefit claims without direct evidence.
