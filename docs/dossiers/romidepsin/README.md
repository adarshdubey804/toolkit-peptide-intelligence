# Dossier 033 — Romidepsin

**Curation status:** `in_progress`

**Reference product:** ISTODAX; generic romidepsin injection products also listed in US DailyMed  
**Therapeutic class:** Histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor; antineoplastic

> Classification correction: romidepsin is a sulfur-containing bicyclic depsipeptide natural-product-derived molecule. Its macrocycle includes ester and amide linkages; it should not be treated as an ordinary linear peptide or peptide hormone.

## 2. Structure and chemical identity

The current US label describes romidepsin as a **bicyclic depsipeptide** with the chemical name:

(1S,4S,7Z,10S,16E,21R)-7-ethylidene-4,21-bis(1-methylethyl)-2-oxa-12,13-dithia-5,8,20,23-tetraazabicyclo[8.7.6]tricos-16-ene-3,6,9,19,22-pentone.

The compound contains:
- A constrained bicyclic macrocyclic framework.
- Two sulfur atoms forming a disulfide-containing structural motif in the natural product.
- Multiple amide and ester/depsipeptide linkages.
- Non-proteinogenic structural features; it is not represented adequately by a standard one-letter amino-acid sequence.

A simple linear peptide sequence is therefore not assigned in this dossier. A curated 2D structure and stereochemistry record should be imported from a verified chemical registry and compared with the FDA label depiction before release.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Romidepsin is a **histone deacetylase inhibitor**.

1. HDAC enzymes remove acetyl groups from lysine residues on histones and selected non-histone proteins.
2. Romidepsin enters cells and is converted intracellularly to an active reduced form containing free thiol groups.
3. The active species inhibits zinc-dependent HDAC enzymes, increasing acetylation of histones and other substrates.
4. Altered chromatin structure and transcriptional regulation can induce cell-cycle arrest, differentiation-associated changes, and apoptosis in susceptible malignant cells.

The exact contribution of individual HDAC isoforms and downstream pathways to clinical antitumour activity is not fully characterised in the product label. Avoid presenting one downstream gene or apoptotic pathway as the sole established clinical mechanism.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- Romidepsin produces accumulation of acetylated histones in vitro.
- In susceptible cancer cell lines, it can induce cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis at nanomolar concentrations.
- HDAC inhibition affects both histone and non-histone protein acetylation.
- The product label notes that the mechanism underlying the antineoplastic effect observed in nonclinical and clinical studies has not been fully characterised.
- Cardiac electrophysiology and electrolyte status are relevant to safe administration; the label describes ECG/QTc considerations.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

The US product label describes intravenous pharmacokinetics after infusion. Romidepsin has a multiphasic concentration-time profile and is extensively metabolised.

- Administration is by IV infusion; oral bioavailability is not relevant to the marketed product.
- Romidepsin is metabolised through CYP3A4 and other pathways; CYP3A4 inhibitors can increase exposure and toxicity.
- Strong CYP3A4 inducers such as rifampin should be avoided according to product labeling.
- Hepatic impairment affects exposure and requires label-specific dose adjustment.
- The label reports extensive plasma protein binding and a multiphasic elimination profile.

Exact numerical PK parameters should be transcribed from the current applicable label with dose, infusion duration, sampling, and hepatic-function context. Do not mix values from early phase studies, different infusion durations, or the withdrawn PTCL indication without qualification.

## 7. Clinical evidence and labelled indication

**Current US labelled indication:** treatment of adult patients with cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL) who have received at least one prior systemic therapy.

**Important indication history:**
- ISTODAX received initial US approval in 2009 for CTCL.
- A peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL) indication was granted under accelerated approval in 2011.
- The PTCL indication was removed from the US label in July 2021.
- Current US labels should therefore not list PTCL as an active approved indication.

Romidepsin has been studied in other haematologic malignancies and in HIV latency-reversal research. These investigations do not constitute approved indications.

## 8. Administration and formulation

- Route: intravenous infusion only.
- Dosage form: sterile lyophilised powder in a single-dose vial.
- Current generic label presentation: 10 mg romidepsin per vial; excipients include povidone and hydrochloric acid for pH adjustment.
- The originator ISTODAX presentation has historically been supplied as a two-vial kit with a separate diluent containing propylene glycol and dehydrated alcohol; product-specific presentations differ.
- The 2021 US label regimen for CTCL is 14 mg/m² infused over 4 hours on days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle, with interruption/reduction as required for toxicity.
- Hepatic impairment and toxicity require label-specific modification.

This dossier is not a patient-specific treatment instruction. Use the current product-specific label, oncology protocol, and institutional hazardous-drug procedures.

## 9. Safety and warnings

Important label warnings and precautions include:
- **Myelosuppression:** thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, and anaemia.
- **Infections:** serious and fatal infections have been reported; viral reactivation, including hepatitis B and Epstein–Barr virus, is a concern.
- **ECG changes/QT considerations:** evaluate cardiovascular history, concomitant QT-prolonging medicines, and potassium/magnesium abnormalities.
- **Tumour lysis syndrome:** monitor patients at risk.
- **Embryo-fetal toxicity:** effective contraception and pregnancy precautions are required as described in the label.
- **Hepatic impairment:** increased exposure can increase toxicity; adjust starting dose according to current product labeling.
- **Gastrointestinal toxicity and fatigue:** nausea, vomiting, anorexia, and asthenia are common in clinical experience.
- **Electrolyte abnormalities:** monitor and correct potassium and magnesium as specified in the label.

Safety profiles and rates must be linked to the particular trial population and label version. Do not reuse historical PTCL trial rates as though they describe the current CTCL indication population.

## 10. Drug interactions

- **Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors:** may increase romidepsin exposure and toxicity; dose modification/monitoring is required according to label.
- **Strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampin):** avoid concomitant use according to the label.
- **Warfarin/coumarin derivatives:** monitor prothrombin time/INR more frequently when co-administered, as specified in product labeling.
- **QT-prolonging agents:** assess additive cardiac risk and correct electrolyte abnormalities.
- Concomitant antiemetics and supportive medicines should be reviewed for QT effects and metabolic interactions.

## 13. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- Primary pharmacological target class: zinc-dependent histone deacetylases (HDACs).
- Biological processes: histone acetylation, chromatin accessibility, transcriptional regulation, cell-cycle control, and apoptosis.
- Relevant disease context: malignant mature T-cell populations in CTCL.
- Romidepsin is a pharmacological epigenetic modulator, not a ligand for a cell-surface peptide receptor.
- HIV latency-reversal research examines HDAC inhibition as a way to induce transcription from latent proviral reservoirs; this remains research use and is not the approved cancer indication.
