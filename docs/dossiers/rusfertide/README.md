# Dossier 034 — Rusfertide

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Evidence cut-off:** 3 November 2026  
**Reference product:** MIMRYLO (rusfertide) for injection  
**Therapeutic class:** Hepcidin mimetic; iron-homeostasis modulator  
**Molecule class:** Synthetic, modified cyclic disulfide peptide

> Regulatory update: MIMRYLO received US FDA approval on 28 August 2026. This dossier uses the approved label, not the earlier investigational status. The approved indication is treatment of erythrocytosis in adults with polycythemia vera (PV).

## 1. Identity and classification

- **Generic name:** rusfertide.
- **Brand:** MIMRYLO.
- **Development programme:** PTG-300 / rusfertide.
- **Originator discovery/development:** Protagonist Therapeutics; global development and commercialisation rights transferred to Takeda.
- **US FDA approval:** 28 August 2026.
- **NDA:** 220605.
- **Route:** subcutaneous injection.
- **Active substance form:** rusfertide acetate.
- **Classification:** synthetic cyclic peptide that mimics the endogenous iron-regulatory hormone hepcidin.
- **Therapeutic role:** reduces iron availability for erythropoiesis; it is not a cytoreductive chemotherapy and does not directly target the JAK2 mutation.

## 2. Sequence and primary structure

The FDA label identifies rusfertide as:

**S⁶,S¹⁶-cyclo[N-isovaleryl-Asp-Thr-His-Phe-Pro-Cys-Ile-Nε-(N-palmitoyl-γ-Glu)-Lys-Phe-Glu-Pro-Arg-Ser-Lys-Gly-Cys-Lys-NH₂] acetate**

Key structural elements:
- Cyclic peptide with a disulfide bond between Cys⁶ and Cys¹⁶ (numbering in the label's peptide scaffold).
- N-terminal isovaleryl cap.
- Lys side-chain modification with an N-palmitoyl-γ-glutamyl substituent.
- C-terminal amide.
- Acetate salt; the salt has variable acetate content.

The long-chain lipid modification contributes to pharmacokinetic persistence. The structure is not a simple linear sequence; retain all noncanonical caps, side-chain conjugation, cyclisation, and terminal amide in any chemical structure record.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Rusfertide is a **hepcidin mimetic** that inhibits the iron exporter **ferroportin (SLC40A1)**.

1. Endogenous hepcidin binds ferroportin on enterocytes, macrophages, and other iron-handling cells.
2. Hepcidin binding reduces ferroportin-mediated iron export, limiting release of iron into plasma.
3. Rusfertide mimics this regulatory action and blocks ferroportin function.
4. Reduced circulating iron availability limits iron delivery to erythroid precursors.
5. Reduced iron supply decreases haemoglobin synthesis and red blood cell production, helping lower haematocrit in PV.

This is iron-restriction biology rather than direct inhibition of JAK2 signalling or direct destruction of erythroid progenitors. It acts downstream of the clonal driver by controlling substrate availability for erythropoiesis.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- In healthy participants receiving single subcutaneous doses of 9.5–54 mg, maximum serum-iron reduction occurred approximately 24–48 hours after dosing.
- At higher single doses (41 and 54 mg), serum-iron reduction was more sustained, up to approximately 96 hours.
- In the Phase 3 VERIFY study, mean ferritin in patients assigned to rusfertide increased from 21 µg/L at baseline to 124 µg/L at Week 32 and 174 µg/L at Week 52.
- Inhibition of ferroportin decreases iron availability for red-cell production and lowers haematocrit.
- Exposure-response relationships have not been fully characterised in the approved label.

Ferritin changes are a pharmacodynamic observation and should be interpreted alongside iron indices, blood counts, clinical status, and the label's monitoring recommendations.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

The FDA label characterises rusfertide after subcutaneous administration:
- Cmax and AUC increased less than dose-proportionally over the studied single-dose range of 9.5–54 mg.
- Population PK analyses were used to predict steady-state exposure in patients with PV.
- At the 19 mg weekly starting dose, predicted mean steady-state Cmax was 220 ng/mL and AUC was 17,800 h·ng/mL.
- At 82 mg weekly, predicted mean steady-state Cmax was 751 ng/mL and AUC was 66,200 h·ng/mL.
- At the maximum recommended 108 mg weekly dose, predicted mean steady-state Cmax was 867 ng/mL and AUC was 105,000 h·ng/mL.
- No clinically meaningful PK differences were observed by age, race, sex, body weight, or mild-to-moderate renal impairment in the studied ranges.
- No clinically meaningful exposure difference was observed in the studied severe renal impairment cohort after a 19 mg dose.
- Moderate hepatic impairment did not meaningfully alter exposure in the studied cohort; severe hepatic impairment has not been studied.

These values are label-derived population predictions, not individual patient exposure targets.

## 7. Clinical evidence and approved indication

**Current US labelled indication:** treatment of erythrocytosis in adults with polycythemia vera.

**VERIFY — Phase 3, NCT05210790**
- Multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study.
- 293 adults with PV and inadequate haematocrit control requiring frequent phlebotomies despite ongoing standard of care.
- Participants received rusfertide or placebo in addition to standard of care.
- The primary response endpoint assessed absence of phlebotomy eligibility during Weeks 20–32.
- FDA reports 76.9% of patients receiving rusfertide versus 32.9% receiving placebo required no phlebotomies during the 32-week assessment period.
- The study is ongoing in longer-term extension phases; the randomised controlled portion and longer follow-up should be reported separately.

The primary endpoint is a phlebotomy/haematocrit-control outcome, not proof of reduced thrombosis, transformation, or mortality. Do not infer those clinical outcomes from the phlebotomy endpoint alone.

## 8. Administration and formulation

- Route: subcutaneous injection only.
- Presentation: sterile, preservative-free, white-to-off-white lyophilised powder in single-dose vials, supplied with diluent.
- Vial strengths: 9.5 mg, 19 mg, 28 mg, 41 mg, and 54 mg rusfertide (as acetate).
- Labelled starting dose: 19 mg subcutaneously once weekly.
- Weekly dose is adjusted according to efficacy and safety, within a labelled range of 9.5–108 mg.
- Doses above 54 mg require two injections; doses above 82 mg are administered on separate days according to the label.
- The label provides specific reconstitution, injection-site rotation, missed-dose, and dose-modification instructions.

This dossier is not a patient-specific dosing guide. Preparation, titration, monitoring, and administration must follow the current approved MIMRYLO label and the treating clinician's direction.

## 9. Safety and warnings

Important label warnings and precautions:
- **New or worsening thrombocytosis:** platelet counts may rise; monitor CBC every 2–4 weeks after initiation and during dose modification, or as clinically indicated.
- **Injection-site reactions:** pain, swelling, erythema, pruritus, and related local reactions.
- **Anaemia:** the most common adverse reactions in the label include injection-site reactions and anaemia.
- **Embryo-fetal toxicity:** based on animal data, potential fetal harm; pregnancy testing and effective contraception are advised for females of reproductive potential during treatment and for at least 30 days after the final dose.
- Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 30 days after the final dose.
- No formal contraindications are listed in the initial US label.

The label reports platelet increases within four weeks; some patients exceeded 600 × 10⁹/L and a smaller proportion exceeded 1,000 × 10⁹/L. Platelet elevation may require changes to cytoreductive therapy or rusfertide dosing.

## 10. Drug interactions

The label reports in-vitro assessment of CYP enzymes and transporter systems:
- Rusfertide did not inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, or CYP3A at clinically relevant concentrations.
- It did not induce CYP1A2, CYP2B6, or CYP3A.
- It was not identified as a substrate of or inhibitor of the listed transporters, including BCRP, BSEP, MATE1/2K, OATP1B1/1B3, OAT1/3, OCT1/2, and P-gp.

These in-vitro findings do not establish that every clinical interaction is impossible. Consider the patient's full PV regimen and monitor blood counts and iron indices as directed.

## 13. Bioinformatics and pathway context

- Primary target: **SLC40A1 (ferroportin)**, the principal cellular iron exporter.
- Endogenous ligand/pathway regulator: **HAMP (hepcidin)**.
- Biological process: systemic iron homeostasis, intestinal iron absorption, macrophage iron recycling, plasma iron availability, and erythropoiesis.
- Disease context: PV is commonly driven by acquired JAK2 pathway mutations; rusfertide does not directly correct the clonal mutation but limits iron availability for excess red-cell production.
- Relevant biomarkers: haematocrit, haemoglobin, RBC count, ferritin, serum iron, transferrin saturation, and platelet count.
- No label-required pharmacogenomic test is established for selecting rusfertide treatment.
