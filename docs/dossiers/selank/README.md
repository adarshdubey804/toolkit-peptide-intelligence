# Selank

> Workbook entry: **116/128** · Curation status: **in_progress**

## Overview

Synthetic tuftsin-analogue heptapeptide developed in Russia and reported as a locally registered anxiolytic product. It is not FDA-approved; human evidence is limited and largely regional.

## Identity and chemistry

Common sequence: Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro (TKPRPGP), a tuftsin-related tetrapeptide extended by Pro-Gly-Pro. Verify exact salt and formulation for a particular product.

## Mechanism of action

A definitive molecular target has not been established. Literature proposes effects on enkephalin metabolism and GABAergic/serotonergic signalling, but these remain incompletely validated. It is structurally and mechanistically distinct from Semax despite the shared Pro-Gly-Pro motif.

## Pharmacodynamics

Small Russian clinical reports describe anxiolytic outcomes; mechanistic and biomarker findings are limited. Do not infer a proven benzodiazepine-like mechanism or established nootropic effect.

## Pharmacokinetics

Intranasal presentations are reported in Russian use. Reliable human PK parameters and exposure-response relationships are not sufficiently established for generalization to research-grade products.

## Clinical evidence

Small regional comparative studies have reported anxiolytic effects, but reporting of randomization/blinding and independent replication is limited. Evidence for cognitive enhancement or neuroprotection is very low certainty.

## Safety and tolerability

Safety data are limited. FDA identifies potential immunogenicity risks for compounded selank acetate due to aggregation and peptide-related impurities, and notes important gaps in human safety knowledge. Unregulated supply adds identity and sterility uncertainty.

## Regulatory status

Not FDA-approved or centrally EMA-authorized. Russian registration is reported in secondary literature, but current product-specific state-register status and indication should be verified from the official register.

## Formulation and manufacturing

Intranasal drops/spray are reported in Russian product use. Confirm exact formulation, salt, concentration and excipients from the authorized product documentation; research supply is not equivalent.

## Bioinformatics and translational research

Potential analyses: tuftsin-related immunomodulation, enkephalin metabolism, GABAergic and serotonergic pathway hypotheses, and anxiety-related neurobiology. Avoid assigning a direct target without experimental support.
