# Semaglutide — dossier 011

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Entity class:** chemically modified, fatty-diacid-conjugated GLP-1 analogue (synthetic modification of a recombinant/fermentation-produced peptide backbone).  
**Related products:** Ozempic, Rybelsus, Wegovy; product indications, dosage forms, strengths, excipients and regulatory histories must remain separate records.  
**Core regulatory references:** current FDA prescribing information and EMA product information.

## 1. Identity and nomenclature

- **INN / active substance:** semaglutide.
- **Drug class:** long-acting glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA).
- **Target:** GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R).
- **ATC code:** A10BJ06.
- **Initial US approval of semaglutide products:** 2017 (product-specific approval dates differ; Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy must not be treated as one approval).
- **Molecular entity:** 31-amino-acid GLP-1 analogue with covalent side-chain modification and a single defined lipid-conjugation site.
- **Sequence homology:** 94% to native human GLP-1, as stated in FDA and EMA product information.

## 2. Sequence and molecular modifications

Semaglutide is based on the GLP-1(7–37) peptide scaffold. Three key modifications distinguish it from native GLP-1:
1. **Position 8:** alanine is replaced with 2-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib), increasing resistance to DPP-4-mediated cleavage.
2. **Position 26:** lysine carries a hydrophilic spacer and a C18 fatty di-acid side chain, enabling strong reversible albumin binding.
3. **Position 34:** lysine is replaced by arginine, ensuring the lipid side chain is attached at only one defined lysine position.

The peptide backbone is produced by yeast fermentation. The fatty-diacid conjugate and linker are part of the final active molecule and must be represented in chemical identity records, not treated as a formulation excipient.

**Sequence representation policy:** do not store a plain 31-letter amino-acid string as the complete chemical identity. Aib is non-proteinogenic and the Lys26 side-chain conjugate is covalent. The canonical record should include (a) a residue-level sequence with Aib represented explicitly, (b) position numbering convention, (c) linker and lipid-side-chain structure, (d) terminal chemistry, and (e) an authoritative structure file or official structural formula. The current FDA labels provide the molecular formula and structural formula but not a text sequence string suitable for blind import. Full residue-by-residue transcription and checksum are pending official-source verification.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Semaglutide selectively binds to and activates GLP-1R, a class B G-protein-coupled receptor. GLP-1R activation in pancreatic islet cells and relevant central nervous system pathways produces several effects:

- **Pancreatic beta cells:** increases insulin secretion in a glucose-dependent manner.
- **Pancreatic alpha cells:** reduces glucagon secretion when blood glucose is elevated.
- **Gastrointestinal tract:** delays gastric emptying, with the effect particularly relevant in the early postprandial period.
- **Central appetite pathways:** reduces appetite and energy intake, supporting body-weight reduction.

During hypoglycaemia, semaglutide's insulin-stimulating effect diminishes and it does not impair the normal glucagon response, according to EMA product information. The exact mechanisms for particular cardiovascular or renal outcome benefits are not fully established; do not present a proposed pathway as a proven causal mechanism.

## 5. Molecular basis of prolonged action

Semaglutide's approximately one-week half-life is principally explained by:
- reversible binding to circulating albumin through its C18 fatty-diacid side chain;
- reduced renal clearance while albumin-bound;
- protection from metabolic degradation through steric/structural effects of albumin binding; and
- resistance to DPP-4 degradation from the Aib substitution at position 8.

The lipid side chain is covalently attached to the peptide via a hydrophilic spacer. It is not a non-covalently mixed excipient.

## 6. Pharmacodynamics

Label-described pharmacodynamic effects include:
- glucose-dependent enhancement of insulin secretion;
- glucose-dependent suppression of glucagon when glucose is high;
- reduction in fasting and postprandial glucose;
- minor delay in early postprandial gastric emptying;
- reduced appetite and energy intake;
- reduction in body weight and body fat mass.

In weight-management studies, weight reduction was generally greater for fat mass than lean mass. Magnitude depends on dose, indication, duration, background lifestyle intervention and trial population. Do not combine efficacy values across Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy without recording the specific product, formulation and regimen.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics

The current EMA Wegovy product information reports:
- maximum concentration typically reached about 1–3 days after administration;
- steady state after approximately 4–5 weeks of once-weekly administration;
- elimination half-life approximately 1 week;
- semaglutide may remain in circulation for approximately 7 weeks after the last 2.4 mg dose;
- clearance in people with overweight/obesity approximately 0.05 L/hour in the cited population;
- extensive metabolism through proteolytic cleavage of the peptide backbone and sequential beta-oxidation of the fatty-acid side chain;
- approximately 3% of absorbed dose excreted in urine as intact semaglutide.

Product-specific FDA labels should be used for the detailed PK parameter table and special-population analysis. Oral semaglutide bioavailability is low and formulation-dependent; do not transfer subcutaneous bioavailability or exposure estimates to oral tablets.

## 8. Manufacturing and drug substance

- **Peptide backbone production:** yeast fermentation, as stated in FDA product descriptions.
- **Post-production chemical modification:** installation of the position-26 hydrophilic spacer and C18 fatty di-acid side chain; detailed process chemistry and process controls require manufacturer/CMC-source review.
- **Product owner / manufacturer:** Novo Nordisk; legal manufacturer and batch-release site vary by market and product presentation.
- **Quality attributes requiring characterization:** identity, purity, related peptides, oxidation/deamidation, aggregation, residual host-cell proteins/DNA, residual solvents/reagents, lipid-side-chain occupancy, stereochemical integrity, and potency.

Do not infer the exact fermentation strain, purification train, conjugation reagents, yields or process parameters from the fact that yeast fermentation is used. Those details require authoritative CMC or patent evidence.

## 9. Formulations and product differentiation

### Ozempic
- Subcutaneous injection.
- Product-specific pen concentrations and dose strengths vary by jurisdiction and label revision.
- US label includes type 2 diabetes indications and additional risk-reduction indications in specified populations; transcribe the current local label rather than relying on brand shorthand.
- FDA initial approval: 2017.

### Rybelsus
- Oral tablet formulation of semaglutide.
- Contains the absorption enhancer salcaprozate sodium (SNAC), which facilitates gastric absorption.
- Oral administration instructions are product-specific and materially affect absorption.
- FDA initial approval: 2019.

### Wegovy
- Semaglutide product for chronic weight management and additional indications in specified populations, depending on current jurisdictional label.
- Available as subcutaneous injection and, in some jurisdictions, oral formulation; keep each presentation and label version separate.
- FDA initial approval: 2021 for injection.

**Critical distinction:** the active substance is semaglutide, but products are not interchangeable for indication, dose, titration, presentation, route, device, excipients or evidence. The dossier database should create product-specific child records linked to this active-substance dossier.

## 11. Safety profile

Important safety topics in current product labels include:
- gastrointestinal adverse reactions (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation and abdominal symptoms);
- acute pancreatitis warning;
- gallbladder disease (including cholelithiasis and cholecystitis);
- hypoglycaemia, particularly with concomitant insulin or insulin secretagogues;
- volume-depletion-related acute kidney injury, especially with significant gastrointestinal fluid loss;
- diabetic retinopathy complications in certain patients with diabetes, requiring product-label-specific interpretation;
- hypersensitivity reactions;
- increased heart rate and other product-specific warnings;
- aspiration risk considerations around anaesthesia/deep sedation in relevant updated labels;
- thyroid C-cell tumour warning and contraindications in US labels for products carrying that boxed warning.

**Do not generalize contraindications or boxed warnings across every jurisdiction and formulation.** For example, US labelling includes a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rodents and contraindicates use in people with personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2. Capture the exact label wording and jurisdiction.

## 12. Drug interactions and precautions

- Concomitant insulin or sulfonylureas can increase hypoglycaemia risk; product labels may recommend dose reduction/monitoring of those medicines.
- Delayed gastric emptying may affect absorption of concomitant oral medicines; assess narrow-therapeutic-index drugs and label-specific recommendations.
- Oral semaglutide has specific administration requirements and potential interactions with medicines taken at the same time.
- The oral formulation's SNAC-mediated absorption is a formulation feature and should not be attributed to injectable semaglutide.
