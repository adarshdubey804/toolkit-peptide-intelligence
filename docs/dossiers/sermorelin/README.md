## 2. Molecular identity and sequence

Sermorelin is the biologically active N-terminal 1–29 fragment of human growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), with a C-terminal amide.

**Sequence (29 residues):**  
YADAIFTNSYRKVLGQLSARKLLQDIMSR-NH₂

- Length: 29 amino-acid residues.
- C-terminal modification: amidation.
- Salt used in drug substance: acetate.
- No disulfide bond is present in the 1–29 sequence.
- It retains the N-terminal receptor-activating domain of endogenous human GHRH but lacks the C-terminal residues 30–44.
- Formula and molecular mass: confirm the free-peptide versus acetate-salt basis from authoritative chemistry documentation before registering a canonical value.

Do not confuse sermorelin with full-length 44-aa tesamorelin or endogenous 44-aa GHRH. It is not a long-acting DAC-modified GHRH analogue.

## 3. Structure–function context

The first 29 residues of GHRH retain substantial activity at the pituitary GHRH receptor. The C-terminal amide is part of the medicinal-chemistry identity and should be preserved in structure records.

Sermorelin is a linear peptide, not a cyclic peptide. Do not add a disulfide bridge or other covalent modification absent from the verified drug substance.

## 4. Disease biology

Growth hormone (GH) is secreted by anterior pituitary somatotroph cells in a pulsatile manner. Hypothalamic GHRH stimulates GH release, while somatostatin inhibits it. GH acts directly on peripheral tissues and stimulates hepatic and peripheral production of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1).

In children with inadequate endogenous GH secretion, impaired GH/IGF-1 signalling can result in reduced linear growth. A GHRH challenge can also be used in selected diagnostic settings to assess pituitary somatotroph reserve. Test interpretation depends on assay, protocol, age, pubertal status, body composition and clinical context.

## 5. Mechanism of action

Sermorelin is an agonist of the growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor (GHRHR) on anterior pituitary somatotroph cells.

1. Sermorelin binds GHRHR.
2. GHRHR activates Gs protein signalling.
3. Gs stimulates adenylyl cyclase, increasing intracellular cAMP.
4. cAMP-dependent signalling promotes GH synthesis and release.
5. Circulating GH stimulates peripheral actions and IGF-1 production.

Sermorelin stimulates endogenous pituitary GH release; it is not recombinant GH and does not directly replace IGF-1. Its effect requires functional pituitary somatotroph tissue.

## 6. Historical indications and clinical context

Historical GEREF products were used in two distinct contexts:

- **Diagnostic:** stimulation testing to assess pituitary GH secretory capacity in suspected GH deficiency.
- **Therapeutic:** treatment of selected children with growth failure due to inadequate endogenous GH secretion, under the historical product's labelled criteria.

The diagnostic and therapeutic products/labels should be curated separately. Do not assume that an historical indication, protocol or dose is currently approved or available.

## 7. Administration and dose

Historical GEREF regimens were product- and indication-specific. Diagnostic stimulation testing used a supervised protocol with timed GH sampling. Historical therapeutic use involved repeated subcutaneous administration.

This dossier intentionally does not provide a current treatment regimen: the historical product has been discontinued, and compounded sermorelin preparations are not interchangeable with the FDA-approved historical product. Any use of a compounded preparation requires clinician assessment and a separate review of its legal status, quality and risks.

## 8. Pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics

- Stimulates pituitary GH release in subjects with functional somatotroph reserve.
- GH response is pulsatile and depends on age, nutritional status, pubertal state, pituitary function and test conditions.
- IGF-1 is a downstream biomarker, not an immediate substitute for a timed GH stimulation response.
- As a short linear peptide, sermorelin is subject to proteolytic degradation.
- Numerical half-life, bioavailability and exposure values should be extracted from historical FDA product information or primary studies with route and sampling context; do not infer values from tesamorelin or other GHRH analogues.

## 9. Clinical evidence

Historical diagnostic studies evaluated GH responses after sermorelin administration. Therapeutic studies assessed growth velocity and changes in height-related measures in children selected for inadequate GH secretion.

## 10. Safety and monitoring

Potential adverse effects reported with sermorelin include injection-site reactions, flushing, headache, dizziness, nausea and transient symptoms associated with GH release. Historical labels and clinical studies should be consulted for frequency and severity.

For GH-axis stimulation, clinically relevant monitoring may include:
- growth velocity and height trajectory in paediatric treatment contexts;
- IGF-1 and relevant pituitary/endocrine assessment;
- glucose metabolism where clinically indicated;
- symptoms suggesting intracranial hypertension, slipped capital femoral epiphysis or progression of pre-existing conditions in a child receiving GH-axis treatment.

These are clinical considerations for the GH axis, not a claim that every event is a sermorelin-specific labelled warning. Obtain the historical label before attributing a warning to GEREF.
