## 4. Mechanism of action

Survodutide is a dual agonist of:
- **GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R):** incretin receptor signalling contributes to glucose-dependent insulin secretion, appetite reduction, satiety and delayed gastric emptying.
- **Glucagon receptor (GCGR):** hepatic glucagon signalling affects glucose and lipid metabolism and may contribute to energy expenditure and reduction of hepatic fat.

The design seeks to combine GLP-1-mediated effects on energy intake and glycaemic control with glucagon-mediated effects on energy expenditure and liver metabolism. The net effect depends on the relative receptor potency/efficacy, tissue exposure, dose and patient population.

Zealand Pharma describes survodutide as deliberately biased towards GLP-1 receptor activity while retaining glucagon receptor activity, with the goal of combining weight reduction and glycaemic control with direct hepatic effects. Quantitative receptor potency and bias claims must be tied to the original assay system and species; do not compare EC50 values from different assay formats as directly interchangeable.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics and biological effects

Clinical development evaluates:
- body weight and waist circumference;
- glycaemic control, including HbA1c and fasting glucose;
- appetite, hunger and energy intake;
- liver fat and MASH histology;
- fibrosis and markers of liver injury;
- cardiometabolic risk markers;
- body composition and visceral adiposity.

Maintain separate evidence strata for obesity without type 2 diabetes, obesity with type 2 diabetes, MASH with fibrosis, and cirrhotic MASH. Do not pool these populations into a single efficacy claim.

## 10. Clinical development

### Obesity / overweight — SYNCHRONIZE

- **SYNCHRONIZE-1:** Phase 3, adults with overweight/obesity without type 2 diabetes. EU CTIS protocol 1404-0038, trial identifier 2022-502529-17-00. The public CTIS record reported the study ended 20 February 2026; results reporting should be tracked separately.
- **SYNCHRONIZE-2:** Phase 3, adults with overweight/obesity and type 2 diabetes. EU CTIS protocol 1404-0041, identifier 2022-502531-18-00. Boehringer Ingelheim reported positive topline/full results in October 2026; detailed results were presented at EASD and published in the New England Journal of Medicine.
- **SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT:** Phase 3 cardiovascular safety study; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06077864. EU CTIS record 2022-502442-27-00 describes an event-driven cardiovascular safety trial in people with overweight/obesity and elevated cardiovascular/renal risk. CTIS record lists end date 30 June 2026; outcome reporting remains pending in the public record.
- Other SYNCHRONIZE studies/substudies should be mapped individually from sponsor and registry sources, including osteoarthritis and obstructive sleep apnoea where applicable.

### MASH — LIVERAGE

- **LIVERAGE:** Phase 3 programme in adults with MASH and moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3); capture exact registry identifiers, histological endpoints and final protocol.
- **LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis:** Phase 3 programme in compensated MASH cirrhosis; maintain as a separate disease cohort and endpoint set.
- Do not transfer obesity trial results to MASH histology or fibrosis outcomes.

### Phase 2 evidence

The Phase 2 obesity study is a key source for dose-ranging efficacy and tolerability. The 2026 review reports that the 4.8 mg group achieved mean body-weight reduction up to 18.7% at 46 weeks versus 2.3% with placebo; treat this as a secondary-source summary until reconciled with the original peer-reviewed trial publication and its estimand. Capture the exact primary publication, trial registry, dose-escalation scheme and missing-data methods before using this number as a canonical result.

## 11. Phase 3 SYNCHRONIZE-2 reported results

Boehringer Ingelheim and Zealand Pharma announced on 1 October 2026 that the Phase 3 SYNCHRONIZE-2 study met its co-primary endpoints in adults with overweight/obesity and type 2 diabetes.

Sponsor-reported efficacy-estimand results at 76 weeks included:
- mean body-weight change up to −13.1% with survodutide versus −3.1% with placebo;
- up to 79.3% achieving at least 5% body-weight reduction versus 32.7% with placebo;
- HbA1c reduction up to 1.21 percentage points from a baseline of 7.4%.

These are sponsor-reported results as presented at EASD and reported as simultaneously published in the *New England Journal of Medicine*. Preserve the exact dose arms, estimand, confidence intervals, treatment-regimen estimand, multiplicity and adverse-event denominators from the peer-reviewed paper. Do not describe the highest dose-arm result as the average effect across all participants.
