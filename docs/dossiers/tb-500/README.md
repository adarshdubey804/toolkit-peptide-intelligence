# TB-500 (thymosin beta-4 fragment)

> Workbook entry: **114/128** · Curation status: **in_progress**

## Overview

Unapproved synthetic peptide marketed for wound healing and recovery. TB-500 is not equivalent to full-length thymosin beta-4 and is not FDA-approved.

## Identity and chemistry

FDA briefing material describes TB-500 free base as a seven-amino-acid synthetic fragment corresponding to thymosin beta-4 residues 17–23, with N-terminal acetylation: Ac-Leu-Lys-Lys-Thr-Glu-Thr-Gln-OH. The common name has been applied inconsistently to salts and derivatives; identity must be specified chemically.

## Mechanism of action

The fragment is marketed as related to thymosin beta-4 biology. Full-length thymosin beta-4 participates in actin sequestration and cell migration, but those functions must not automatically be attributed to this short fragment. A clinically validated mechanism for TB-500 has not been established.

## Pharmacodynamics

No robust human pharmacodynamic profile or validated exposure-response relationship. Wound-healing and recovery claims are not established clinical outcomes for the marketed fragment.

## Pharmacokinetics

No approved label or reliable human PK profile for TB-500. Do not substitute full-length thymosin beta-4 PK for the fragment.

## Clinical evidence

No adequate controlled clinical evidence establishes benefit for wound healing, sports recovery or musculoskeletal repair. Evidence involving full-length thymosin beta-4 is not interchangeable with TB-500 fragment evidence.

## Safety and tolerability

Safety is inadequately characterized. FDA materials identify substantial concerns about inconsistent naming, multiple salts/derivatives and uncertainty about the active moiety and reference standards. Unregulated injectable products add impurity, sterility and identity risks.

## Regulatory status

Not FDA-approved. FDA compounding review documents distinguish TB-500-related bulk substances and note that the common name may describe chemically different materials. Review status is not authorization.

## Formulation and manufacturing

No standardized FDA-approved drug formulation or established commercial GMP specification. Any analytical reference must define salt form, termini, sequence and purity.

## Bioinformatics and translational research

Research opportunities include actin dynamics, cell migration and wound-repair pathways, but analyses must distinguish the seven-residue fragment from full-length thymosin beta-4.
