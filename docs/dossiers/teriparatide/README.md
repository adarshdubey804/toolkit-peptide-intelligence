# 046 — Teriparatide (PTH 1–34)

**Curation status:** in_progress  
**Workbook sequence:** 046  
**Entity class:** recombinant/synthetic peptide hormone fragment; parathyroid hormone receptor agonist  
**Evidence cut-off:** 4 November 2026

> **Scope.** Teriparatide is the biologically active N-terminal 1–34 fragment of human parathyroid hormone (PTH). Intermittent subcutaneous exposure stimulates bone formation and is used for selected patients at high risk of fracture. It is not equivalent to continuous exposure to endogenous PTH, which can promote bone resorption.

Teriparatide is produced using recombinant DNA technology in an *Escherichia coli* expression system. The amino-acid sequence is identical to the 1–34 N-terminal sequence of endogenous human PTH.

The N-terminal region is essential for PTH1R activation. Teriparatide is not full-length PTH(1–84), and should not be annotated as a parathyroid gland hormone replacement product.

## 3. Physiological role of PTH

Endogenous PTH is secreted by the parathyroid glands in response to reduced extracellular calcium. It regulates calcium and phosphate homeostasis through actions in bone and kidney and indirectly influences intestinal calcium absorption through activation of vitamin D metabolism.

PTH acts through PTH1R in osteoblast-lineage cells and renal tissues. Its skeletal effect depends on the pattern and duration of receptor stimulation.

## 4. Mechanism of action

**PTH1R agonism with exposure-pattern-dependent bone remodelling:**

1. Teriparatide binds PTH1R, a class B GPCR expressed on osteoblast-lineage cells and renal tubular cells.
2. PTH1R activates Gs/adenylyl cyclase/cAMP/PKA signalling and can also engage Gq/11–PLC signalling.
3. In bone, intermittent exposure increases osteoblast number and activity, promotes osteoblast differentiation and survival, and increases bone formation.
4. Teriparatide also affects osteoblast-lineage regulation of osteoclastogenesis, including the RANKL/OPG axis; net skeletal response reflects coupled remodelling.
5. Repeated once-daily dosing produces a net anabolic response, whereas sustained PTH exposure can increase bone resorption and produce a different skeletal outcome.

The mechanism is not simply “blocks osteoclasts.” Teriparatide is an anabolic PTH1R agonist whose net effect depends on intermittent dosing and coordinated bone-cell signalling.

## 5. Approved clinical use

In the United States, FORTEO is indicated for treatment of:
- postmenopausal women with osteoporosis at high risk for fracture;
- men with primary or hypogonadal osteoporosis at high risk for fracture; and
- men and women with osteoporosis associated with sustained systemic glucocorticoid therapy who are at high risk for fracture.

Other jurisdictions and product labels may have different wording, restrictions and duration recommendations. The current local label should be used for prescribing decisions.

Teriparatide is not indicated as routine treatment for every person with low bone density. It is generally reserved for appropriately selected patients with severe osteoporosis or high fracture risk.

## 6. Administration and dosing

The US reference product label describes:
- **Dose:** 20 micrograms once daily.
- **Route:** subcutaneous injection into the thigh or abdominal wall.
- **Delivery:** prefilled multidose pen; product presentations and instructions vary by brand.
- **Duration:** treatment duration and any subsequent antiresorptive therapy should follow current jurisdiction-specific labelling and specialist guidance.

The historical US label carried a limitation of use advising avoidance of use beyond two years over a patient's lifetime. FDA subsequently removed the boxed warning and lifetime-use limitation in 2020. The current US label should be consulted for present duration wording; do not retain obsolete boxed-warning language in current safety fields.

Patients should be instructed on product-specific injection technique, pen priming, needle disposal and storage. This dossier is not a self-injection guide.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics

- **Absorption:** rapidly absorbed after subcutaneous administration.
- **Peak concentration:** reached within approximately 30 minutes after injection in label-described studies.
- **Elimination:** plasma concentrations decline rapidly; the terminal half-life after subcutaneous administration is approximately 1 hour.
- **Metabolism:** peptide is cleared through nonspecific proteolytic degradation, including hepatic and extrahepatic pathways.
- **Excretion:** metabolites are eliminated through renal and other catabolic pathways; renal impairment may affect exposure.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** transient increases in serum calcium may occur after dosing; intermittent administration is associated with increased bone formation markers and, over time, increased bone mineral density.

## 8. Clinical evidence

The pivotal teriparatide development programme included randomized trials in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis and in men with osteoporosis. The landmark Fracture Prevention Trial demonstrated reduced vertebral and nonvertebral fracture risk and increased bone mineral density with daily teriparatide compared with placebo in women with prior vertebral fractures.

Clinical interpretation must distinguish:
- fracture outcomes from BMD surrogate outcomes;
- vertebral from nonvertebral and hip fracture endpoints;
- trial populations from broader real-world populations;
- teriparatide monotherapy from sequential treatment after an anabolic course.

Do not copy a relative-risk reduction or number-needed-to-treat without specifying population, endpoint, follow-up and confidence interval.

## 9. Safety and precautions

Important safety considerations from product labelling and clinical practice include:
- transient hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria;
- orthostatic hypotension, particularly with initial doses;
- nausea, pain, arthralgia and injection-site reactions;
- caution in patients with active or recent urolithiasis or pre-existing hypercalcemia;
- avoid use in patients at increased baseline risk of osteosarcoma, including those with Paget disease of bone, unexplained elevations of alkaline phosphatase, open epiphyses, prior external-beam or implant radiation involving the skeleton, or hereditary predisposition to osteosarcoma, as specified in the current label;
- evaluate calcium, renal function, vitamin D status and relevant secondary causes of osteoporosis as clinically appropriate.

**Regulatory warning history:** FDA removed the boxed warning regarding osteosarcoma and the two-year lifetime-use limitation in November 2020 after review of postmarketing data. Osteosarcoma risk factors and label precautions remain relevant; removal of the boxed warning does not mean risk is impossible.

Consult the current local label for contraindications, interactions, monitoring and full adverse-event information.

## 11. Formulation and quality

The reference product is a sterile solution for subcutaneous injection in a multidose delivery pen. Recombinant production in *E. coli* requires control of sequence identity, peptide-related impurities, aggregates, host-cell proteins and DNA, endotoxin, potency, sterility, particulates and stability. Formulation and device-specific quality attributes include pH, excipient content, container closure integrity, delivered-dose accuracy and in-use stability.
