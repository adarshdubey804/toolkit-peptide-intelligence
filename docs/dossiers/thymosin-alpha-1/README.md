# Dossier 104 — Thymosin Alpha-1 (Thymalfasin; Zadaxin)

**Workbook entry:** 104 | **Category:** Immunology, inflammation & transplant | **US approval:** No | **Status:** Approved in selected countries; not FDA-approved  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

## Identity and sequence
Thymosin alpha-1 (Tα1; thymalfasin) is a 28-amino-acid N-terminal peptide fragment of prothymosin alpha. It is N-terminally acetylated. Mature peptide sequence:

`Ac-SDAAVDTSSEITTKDLKEKKEVVEEAEN-OH`

Molecular formula and mass reported for the acetylated peptide are approximately C129H215N33O55 and 3,108.3 Da; verify salt/counterion and terminal chemistry against the specific medicinal product. It is distinct from full-length prothymosin alpha and from the broader historical thymosin fraction preparations.

## Mechanism and biology
Tα1 is an immunomodulatory peptide studied for effects on innate and adaptive immune responses. Reported activities include modulation of dendritic-cell maturation, Toll-like receptor signalling, cytokine production, T-cell activation and restoration of immune function in selected immunosuppressed settings. The mechanism is pleiotropic and not reducible to a single validated receptor or target. Avoid claims that it is a universal immune booster or that all proposed pathways are clinically established.

## Pharmacology and PK
Clinical products are generally administered parenterally in jurisdictions where authorized. The peptide is subject to proteolytic degradation; robust human PK and exposure-response characterization is less complete than for FDA-approved peptide drugs. Route, formulation, population and regional product label should be recorded separately. Do not invent a plasma half-life or bioavailability value.

## Clinical evidence
Thymalfasin has been studied in chronic hepatitis B, hepatitis C, cancer adjunct settings, sepsis and other immune-related conditions. Evidence is heterogeneous, and results vary by disease, background therapy, trial design and geographic region. Some countries authorize thymalfasin products for specific indications; this does not establish FDA approval or global guideline endorsement. The workbook lists immune modulation and viral hepatitis adjunct use as context, not a universal approved indication.

## Safety
Published studies often describe tolerability, but the evidence base is heterogeneous and may be underpowered for rare adverse events. Potential concerns include injection-site reactions, hypersensitivity and immunological effects. Use only product-specific national labels and published clinical evidence; do not provide a general safety guarantee.

## Formulation and quality
Zadaxin-type products are parenteral peptide formulations. Critical quality attributes include exact sequence, N-terminal acetylation, peptide purity, related substances, counterion, residual solvents, sterility, endotoxin, particulate matter, stability and container closure. Compounded/research-grade material should not be assumed equivalent to a nationally authorized product.

## Regulatory status
Not FDA-approved in the United States. Thymalfasin is authorized in selected non-US jurisdictions, but country-specific product registration, indication, label and current marketing status must be independently verified. Do not describe it as FDA-approved for hepatitis B/C.

## Evidence gaps
1. Verify country-by-country registration and current label/marketing status.
2. Capture exact product sequence, acetylation, counterion and analytical specifications from primary regulatory sources.
3. Systematically review randomized trials and meta-analyses by indication and risk of bias.
4. Separate immunological biomarkers from clinical outcomes such as mortality, virological response or recurrence.
