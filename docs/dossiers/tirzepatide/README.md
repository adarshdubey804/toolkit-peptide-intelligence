# Tirzepatide (Mounjaro / Zepbound) — dossier 012

**Curation status:** `in_progress`  
**Entity class:** chemically modified, lipidated 39-residue GIP-based peptide agonist with a covalently attached linker–C20 fatty diacid.  
**Pharmacology:** dual glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR) and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R) agonist.  
**Product separation:** Mounjaro and Zepbound are distinct product/label records. Indications, dosage instructions, device, presentations and regulatory status must not be merged.

## 1. Identity and nomenclature

- **INN:** tirzepatide.
- **Brand names:** Mounjaro; Zepbound (United States). Other brand/presentation naming varies by jurisdiction.
- **Drug class:** dual GIPR/GLP-1R agonist; incretin mimetic.
- **Primary targets:** GIP receptor (GIPR) and GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R).
- **ATC code:** A10BX16.
- **Initial US approval:** 2022.
- **Peptide origin:** based on the GIP amino-acid sequence, with defined non-coded amino-acid substitutions and a lipidated lysine side chain.
- **Entity classification:** modified peptide, not an unmodified native hormone and not a fusion protein.

## 2. Sequence and chemical modifications

Tirzepatide is based on a 39-amino-acid GIP sequence and includes:
1. **Aib (2-aminoisobutyric acid) at positions 2 and 13** (non-proteinogenic/non-coded amino-acid substitutions);
2. a **C-terminal amide**;
3. a **Lys residue at position 20** covalently attached to a C20 fatty diacid through a hydrophilic linker;
4. the linker is described in the EMA assessment report as one γ-glutamic acid and two 8-amino-3,6-dioxaoctanoic-acid units.

The fatty diacid is 1,20-eicosanedioic acid. It enables reversible albumin binding and prolongs systemic exposure.

**Sequence representation caution:** the FDA label structural diagram depicts the peptide sequence and lipid linker. Because Aib is non-coded and the Lys20 side chain is covalently modified, a plain canonical 20-amino-acid alphabet string is not a complete chemical representation. The canonical database entry must encode residue-level Aib substitutions, C-terminal amidation, linker stereochemistry/connectivity and lipid attachment as explicit structural features. Do not use an unannotated sequence string as the complete identity.

**FDA label sequence depiction:** `H-Y-E-G-T-F-T-S-D-Y-S-I-L-D-K-I-A-Q-A-F-V-Q-W-L-I-A-G-G-P-S-S-G-A-P-P-P-S-NH2`  
This is the sequence string shown in the FDA structural-formula diagram; it is not sufficient alone to represent the two Aib substitutions and Lys20 side-chain conjugation. Confirm residue numbering and non-coded residue annotations against the current official structural formula before importing into sequence databases.

## 4. Mechanism of action

Tirzepatide selectively binds to and activates both GIPR and GLP-1R. Both receptors are incretin hormone receptors involved in glucose homeostasis.

Label-described effects include:
- enhanced first- and second-phase insulin secretion in a glucose-dependent manner;
- reduced glucagon secretion in a glucose-dependent manner;
- increased insulin sensitivity in clinical pharmacology studies;
- reduced fasting and postprandial glucose;
- decreased calorie intake and appetite;
- delayed gastric emptying, with the effect greatest after the first dose and attenuating over time;
- reduction in body weight, with greater fat-mass than lean-mass loss in label-described studies.

GIPR and GLP-1R activation contribute to the pharmacology. The precise relative contribution of each receptor to clinical weight and glycaemic outcomes is not fully resolved; avoid unsupported claims that a specific receptor accounts for a quantified proportion of efficacy.

## 5. Molecular basis of prolonged action

The C20 fatty diacid side chain, attached through a hydrophilic linker to Lys20, enables reversible binding to serum albumin. Albumin binding reduces renal clearance and protects the molecule from rapid elimination, supporting once-weekly dosing. The two Aib substitutions are part of the peptide's engineered sequence and contribute to its stability profile.

## 6. Pharmacodynamics

FDA label evidence describes:
- glucose-dependent insulinotropic activity;
- reduction in fasting and postprandial glucagon;
- improved insulin sensitivity in a hyperinsulinaemic-euglycaemic clamp study after 28 weeks in people with type 2 diabetes;
- reduced food/calorie intake;
- reduction in body weight, with greater fat-mass than lean-mass loss;
- delayed gastric emptying, most prominent after the first dose.

The Mounjaro label reports reductions in fasting glucagon and mixed-meal glucagon exposure in a 28-week study. Preserve study population, dose and comparator when importing numeric results rather than treating these as universal pharmacodynamic constants.

## 7. Pharmacokinetics

Current US label information reports:
- time to maximum concentration: approximately 8–72 hours;
- steady state: approximately 4 weeks of once-weekly dosing;
- elimination half-life: approximately 5 days;
- apparent volume of distribution: approximately 9.7 L;
- apparent clearance: approximately 0.061 L/hour;
- metabolism: proteolytic cleavage of the peptide backbone and beta-oxidation of the fatty-diacid side chain;
- elimination: metabolites excreted in urine and faeces; intact tirzepatide is not the principal excreted form.

PK parameters can vary by label revision and analysis population. Confirm numeric values and special-population statements against the exact current local label before structured import.

## 9. Formulation and product presentations

### Mounjaro (US)
The current label includes single-dose presentations and multi-dose presentations/KwikPen in specified markets. Single-dose pen/vial strengths include 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5 and 15 mg per 0.5 mL. Multi-dose product concentration/volume and excipients differ by presentation.

The single-dose formulation includes sodium chloride, sodium phosphate dibasic heptahydrate and water for injection. Some multi-dose presentations contain additional excipients, including benzyl alcohol, glycerin and phenol. Do not merge excipient lists across device/presentation types.

### Zepbound (US)
A separate US product label for chronic weight management and other specifically approved indications. The active substance is tirzepatide, but product-specific label, device, presentation, indication and dosing records must remain distinct.

## 10. Regulatory history and indications

### United States
- **Initial US approval:** 2022.
- **Mounjaro:** indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults and paediatric patients aged 10 years and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus, as specified in the current label.
- **Zepbound:** separate product with weight-management and other label-specific indications. Check current prescribing information for the precise population and any additional indication, including obstructive sleep apnoea, before structured regulatory import.
- Both US product labels contain a boxed warning concerning thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rats and contraindicate use in people with personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN2.

### European Union
The EMA Mounjaro EPAR and current product information are the primary sources for EU indications, dosing, warnings and presentations. The EPAR product information was updated in September 2026; the indication and population wording should be transcribed from the current annex rather than copied from an older US label.

## 11. Administration and handling

Tirzepatide is administered subcutaneously once weekly using the authorised product/device. Product-specific dose escalation, missed-dose instructions, injection sites, storage, in-use period and device operation must be transcribed from the current Mounjaro or Zepbound label for the relevant jurisdiction. ## 12. Safety and precautions

Current US label warnings and precautions include:
- boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours in rats; human relevance is unknown;
- contraindication in patients with personal/family history of MTC or MEN2;
- acute pancreatitis;
- hypoglycaemia, especially when used with insulin or insulin secretagogues;
- acute kidney injury associated with volume depletion;
- severe gastrointestinal adverse reactions;
- hypersensitivity reactions;
- gallbladder disease;
- diabetic retinopathy complications in relevant populations/label contexts;
- pulmonary aspiration risk considerations around general anaesthesia or deep sedation, as described in updated product labelling.

Common adverse reactions in Mounjaro label data include nausea, diarrhoea, decreased appetite, vomiting, constipation, dyspepsia and abdominal pain. Incidence depends on dose, indication, trial and comparator. Preserve exact table, denominator and treatment duration when extracting rates.

## 13. Drug interactions and special precautions

- **Insulin and insulin secretagogues:** concomitant use can increase hypoglycaemia risk; follow label recommendations for monitoring and dose adjustment of the concomitant medicine.
- **Oral medicines:** delayed gastric emptying can affect absorption; assess medicines where a change in absorption may be clinically important.
- **Oral hormonal contraceptives:** current US labelling advises additional/non-oral contraception for a specified period after initiation and dose escalation; copy exact current label wording and duration.
- **Anaesthesia/deep sedation:** delayed gastric emptying and residual gastric contents may be relevant; follow current peri-procedural label and professional guidance.

A complete interaction matrix remains pending and must be product- and jurisdiction-specific.
