## 1. Executive overview

Vasopressin, also called arginine vasopressin (AVP) or antidiuretic hormone (ADH), is an endogenous cyclic nonapeptide synthesized in hypothalamic neurons and released from the posterior pituitary. It regulates water balance through renal V2 receptors and vascular tone through V1a receptors. It also acts at V1b (V3) receptors in the anterior pituitary and other tissues.

Pharmaceutical vasopressin injection is used in selected clinical settings, including vasodilatory shock, where it increases vascular tone through V1a-receptor activation. Its clinical effects are receptor- and dose-dependent: vasoconstriction, antidiuresis, and endocrine effects are distinct pharmacological actions.

The workbook lists Vasostrict and Pitressin. Brand availability and approved indications vary by product and jurisdiction. The workbook's first US approval date of 2014 is specifically noted as applying to the current Vasostrict formulation; it should not be interpreted as the first-ever approval or clinical use of vasopressin.

## 4. Mechanism of action (MoA)

Vasopressin activates three principal receptor subtypes. These receptors are GPCRs with different tissue distributions and intracellular signalling pathways.

### V1a receptor (AVPR1A): vascular smooth muscle
1. Vasopressin binds V1a receptors on vascular smooth-muscle cells.
2. V1a couples predominantly to Gq/11.
3. Gq activates phospholipase C-β (PLCβ).
4. PLCβ generates IP₃ and DAG from PIP₂.
5. IP₃ increases intracellular calcium; calcium–calmodulin signalling promotes smooth-muscle contraction.
6. Systemic vascular resistance and arterial pressure can increase.

This pathway underlies vasopressin's vasopressor effect in vasodilatory shock. Regional vasoconstriction can also occur, so haemodynamic response and tissue perfusion require monitoring.

### V2 receptor (AVPR2): renal collecting duct
1. Vasopressin binds V2 receptors on collecting-duct principal cells.
2. V2 couples predominantly to Gs.
3. Adenylyl cyclase increases intracellular cAMP.
4. PKA signalling promotes aquaporin-2 (AQP2) trafficking and insertion into the apical membrane.
5. Collecting-duct water permeability increases, enhancing water reabsorption and concentrating urine.

This is the antidiuretic action of endogenous AVP.

### V1b / V3 receptor (AVPR1B): pituitary
V1b receptors couple mainly to Gq/11 and contribute to corticotroph signalling and ACTH release, particularly in coordination with hypothalamic CRH.

## 5. Pharmacodynamics

- **Vasopressor action:** V1a-mediated vascular smooth-muscle contraction increases vascular tone.
- **Antidiuretic action:** V2-mediated AQP2 trafficking increases renal water reabsorption.
- **Endocrine action:** V1b contributes to pituitary ACTH regulation.
- **Receptor selectivity:** Native vasopressin activates more than one receptor subtype; it is not V1a-selective.
- **Physiological regulation:** Endogenous release is influenced by plasma osmolality, effective circulating volume, baroreceptor input, and neuroendocrine signals.

Pharmacodynamic effects depend on the clinical context, receptor expression, volume status, concurrent medicines, and route/formulation. Do not assume that the effects of vasopressin are interchangeable with those of desmopressin or terlipressin.

## 6. Pharmacokinetics

Vasopressin is a peptide hormone with rapid enzymatic metabolism and a short circulating half-life. Exact PK values and labelled administration details should be taken from the current product-specific prescribing information.

Important PK considerations:
- It is susceptible to enzymatic degradation in plasma and tissues.
- Exposure and duration differ by route and formulation.
- Renal and hepatic handling may influence clinical response, but vasopressin clearance is not described adequately by a simple renal-only model.
- Continuous intravenous infusion in critical care produces a different exposure profile from endogenous pulsatile secretion or non-IV historical formulations.

## 7. Clinical uses and evidence

### Vasodilatory shock
Vasopressin injection is used as a vasopressor in adults with vasodilatory shock, including septic shock, to increase blood pressure when vascular tone is inadequate. It is generally used as part of a monitored critical-care strategy, alongside treatment of the underlying cause and appropriate fluid/vasopressor management.

The evidence base includes randomized and comparative critical-care studies evaluating haemodynamic effects and clinical outcomes. Vasopressin's role should be interpreted in the context of current sepsis and critical-care guidelines; it is not a substitute for source control, antimicrobial therapy, or appropriate resuscitation.

### Other physiological and historical uses
Endogenous vasopressin regulates water balance and vascular tone. Other pharmaceutical uses have varied over time and by formulation and jurisdiction. A historical brand name or a use in older literature does not establish that a particular product remains marketed or currently approved for that use.

### Distinguish from desmopressin
Desmopressin is a synthetic analogue with greater V2 receptor selectivity. It is used in selected conditions such as central diabetes insipidus and certain bleeding disorders. It should not be substituted for vasopressin in shock or assumed to have the same vasopressor activity.

## 8. Safety and monitoring considerations

Vasopressin is a potent medicine with clinically important haemodynamic and water-balance effects. Current product labeling should be consulted for complete contraindications, warnings, adverse reactions, and administration requirements.

Important risks and monitoring domains include:
- **Ischaemia:** excessive or regional vasoconstriction can compromise tissue perfusion, including coronary, mesenteric, cutaneous, or peripheral circulation.
- **Hyponatraemia and water retention:** V2-mediated antidiuresis can reduce free-water excretion.
- **Cardiovascular effects:** changes in afterload, cardiac output, and coronary perfusion may be clinically important.
- **Arrhythmia and haemodynamic instability:** monitor in the context of critical illness and concomitant vasoactive drugs.
- **Gastrointestinal effects:** splanchnic vasoconstriction may be relevant to gut perfusion.
- **Hypersensitivity:** evaluate according to product labeling and clinical context.

These are recognized pharmacological risk domains; their incidence depends on indication, exposure, population, and study definition. Do not present them as a uniform frequency estimate without label- or trial-specific data.

## 9. Drug interactions and clinical context

Vasopressin is commonly used in critically ill patients receiving multiple vasoactive and supportive medicines. The clinical effect can be influenced by:
- Other vasopressors and inotropes.
- Drugs affecting vascular tone or cardiac function.
- Fluid administration and electrolyte management.
- Medicines and conditions affecting water balance.
- Baseline cardiac, renal, hepatic, and peripheral vascular status.

A complete interaction list must be taken from the current product label and evaluated in the context of the patient's critical-care regimen. This dossier does not provide patient-specific prescribing advice.

## 11. Manufacturing, formulation, and quality

Vasopressin is a small cyclic peptide. Critical quality attributes include:
- Correct amino-acid sequence and stereochemistry.
- Correct intramolecular disulfide bond formation.
- Control of reduced, mispaired, or otherwise related peptide species.
- Peptide assay and potency.
- Oxidation, deamidation, and other degradation products.
- Residual solvents and process-related impurities.
- Sterility, endotoxin, and particulate control for injectable products.
- Formulation pH, excipient compatibility, container-closure integrity, and stability.

Product-specific excipients, concentration, container, storage, and in-use stability must be taken from the approved label and manufacturer documentation. Do not generalize a formulation's stability data to all vasopressin products.
