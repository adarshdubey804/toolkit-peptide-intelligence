# Dossier 078 — Ziconotide (Prialt)

**Workbook entry:** 078 | **Category:** Neurology, Pain & CNS | **US approval:** 2004 | **Status:** FDA-approved  
**Curation:** `in_progress`

> Intrathecal prescription medicine; highly specialized administration. This dossier is not a dosing or device-management guide.

## Identity and chemistry
Ziconotide is a synthetic 25-amino-acid peptide based on ω-conotoxin MVIIA from the marine cone snail *Conus magus*. It is a disulfide-rich conopeptide containing six cysteines forming three disulfide bonds. The sequence is commonly represented as **CKGKGAKCSRLMYDCCTGSCRSGKC-NH2**; residue stereochemistry and disulfide connectivity must be taken from the approved product structural record before a machine-readable structure is released. It is not a conventional endogenous human peptide. Prialt is a sterile intrathecal solution.

## Mechanism
Ziconotide selectively blocks neuronal N-type voltage-sensitive calcium channels (CaV2.2), particularly at presynaptic terminals in the dorsal horn. Reduced calcium influx suppresses release of pronociceptive neurotransmitters, including glutamate, substance P and calcitonin gene-related peptide. It acts in the spinal cord and does not rely on opioid receptors. The mechanism explains analgesia as well as neurological adverse effects from interruption of normal neurotransmission.

## Pharmacology and PK
Administration is intrathecal because systemic delivery does not reproduce the intended spinal exposure and peptide penetration into the CNS is limited. CSF distribution and clearance are influenced by CSF turnover and catheter/pump delivery. Plasma PK is not a suitable proxy for spinal target exposure. Exact CSF half-life and distribution parameters vary by study and should be transcribed with method and infusion conditions. Ziconotide is a peptide subject to proteolytic degradation; it is not metabolized by CYP pathways in the conventional small-molecule sense.

## Clinical evidence
Prialt is indicated for management of severe chronic pain in patients for whom intrathecal therapy is warranted and who are intolerant of or refractory to other treatments, including systemic analgesics and adjunctive therapies. Randomized controlled trials in severe chronic pain demonstrated analgesic activity, but tolerability and titration limitations are substantial. Trial populations and infusion protocols differ; report effect sizes alongside discontinuations and adverse events rather than implying uniform benefit.

## Safety
The US label carries a boxed warning for severe psychiatric symptoms and neurological impairment. Contraindication includes a history of psychosis. Important adverse effects include dizziness, nausea, confusion, nystagmus, memory impairment, gait abnormalities, somnolence, speech disorder and urinary retention. Serious events can include altered consciousness, hallucinations, delirium, psychosis, meningitis related to intrathecal delivery, and neurological complications. Safe use depends on an appropriate intrathecal delivery system and specialist monitoring.

## Product quality
Sterile, preservative-free intrathecal solution. Critical quality attributes include sequence identity, disulfide pairing, peptide purity/potency, aggregation and degradation, particulate matter, sterility, endotoxin, container closure, and compatibility with the intrathecal pump and catheter materials. Intrathecal products require stringent control of endotoxin and particulate contamination.

## Regulatory status
FDA approval in 2004 for severe chronic pain requiring intrathecal therapy. The approval does not authorize epidural, intravenous, oral, or self-administered use. Check the current Prialt label for exact indication wording and contraindications.
