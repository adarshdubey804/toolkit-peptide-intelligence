## Executive summary

Ipamorelin (NNC 26-0161) is a synthetic, modified pentapeptide growth-hormone secretagogue. The original pharmacology report describes GH release through a GHRP-like receptor in rat pituitary cells and in rat and swine models. The evidence in that paper is preclinical; it does not establish clinical benefit or long-term safety in humans. A phase 2 study evaluated intravenous ipamorelin for postoperative ileus, but FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee briefing states that the available study did not demonstrate significant differences from placebo in primary or secondary efficacy analyses and that serious adverse events, including a death, were reported in the clinical study context. FDA lists ipamorelin acetate under 503B Category 2 because of potential immunogenicity/aggregation/impurity concerns and limited safety information for certain routes. These are distinct evidence and regulatory statements and must not be collapsed into a claim of approval or proven efficacy.

## E. Mechanism of action — evidence-qualified

**Working mechanism:** ipamorelin acts as an agonist at the growth-hormone secretagogue receptor (GHSR; ghrelin receptor), stimulating pituitary growth-hormone release.

**Evidence level:** preclinical / source-reported from the original pharmacology study; not a regulator-label mechanism.

Raun et al. (1998) used pharmacological profiling with GHRP and GHRH antagonists and reported that ipamorelin stimulated GH release through a GHRP-like receptor. The experiments included primary rat pituitary cells, anaesthetised rats and conscious swine. The article predates current receptor nomenclature in some passages; map “GHRP-like receptor” to GHSR only with a transparent nomenclature note and additional receptor-specific evidence. Do not infer a complete intracellular signalling cascade, receptor binding affinity, human target engagement, or clinical outcome from this paper alone.

## F. Pharmacodynamics

The 1998 preclinical paper reports GH release potency/efficacy in rat pituitary cells and GH responses in rat and swine models. It reports no significant effect on FSH, LH, PRL or TSH in the tested swine experiments. These are model- and protocol-specific findings, not a comprehensive human pharmacodynamic profile.

## H. Human clinical evidence

A phase 2 randomized study evaluated ipamorelin for postoperative ileus following bowel resection (NCT00672074; publication PMID 25331030). FDA's 2024 Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee briefing states that the only published study evaluating efficacy for postoperative ileus used IV infusion and did not show significant differences between ipamorelin and placebo in primary or secondary efficacy analyses; development was reportedly discontinued after disappointing results. ## J. Jurisdictional regulatory status

### United States

FDA's current compounding-risk page lists **ipamorelin acetate** under 503B Category 2 (added September 29, 2023). FDA describes potential immunogenicity for certain routes because of aggregation or peptide-related impurities, complexity of characterizing unnatural amino acids, and serious adverse events including death in a study where ipamorelin was administered intravenously for gastric motility. FDA also states it lacks sufficient safety information for certain other injectable routes. Category 2 is a compounding bulk-substance risk classification under interim policies; it is not a marketing authorization, an FDA approval, or by itself a complete statement of every legal pathway.

The 2024 FDA Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee briefing concludes available information is insufficient to conclude ipamorelin (free base) or ipamorelin acetate do not present the identified risks. It also states FDA did not identify evidence supporting efficacy for postoperative ileus.
