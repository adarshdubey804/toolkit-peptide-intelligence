## B. Biological class and research context

GHRP-2 is a synthetic growth hormone-releasing peptide and ghrelin-receptor agonist. Pralmorelin has been used in Japan as a pharmacological diagnostic stimulus in evaluation of growth hormone deficiency. It has also been studied for acute GH responses and appetite-related effects. Diagnostic use must not be conflated with chronic GH treatment, muscle-building, anti-ageing or performance claims.

## C. Sequence and residue annotation

## E. Mechanism of action — evidence-qualified

**Working mechanism:** GHRP-2 (pralmorelin) is an agonist of the growth hormone secretagogue receptor type 1a (GHSR1a, also known as the ghrelin receptor), stimulating endogenous pituitary GH secretion.

**Evidence level:** human pharmacodynamic and preclinical evidence are reported in the literature; exact receptor-binding and downstream-signalling details require primary-source curation.

The 2020 review by Ishida et al. summarizes pralmorelin as an orally active, short-acting synthetic peptide and describes GH responses in animal models and healthy subjects. The FDA GSRS identity record confirms the substance name and chemical identity but is not a mechanism source. Vendor descriptions are not used as authoritative mechanistic evidence.

## F. Pharmacodynamics

The literature review reports that pralmorelin stimulates GH release and that GH responses have been studied in healthy subjects and people with severe GH deficiency. It also summarizes animal comparisons with GHRP-6. These findings are assay-, population- and route-specific. A diagnostic GH response is not itself evidence that repeated administration improves growth, body composition, recovery or other clinical outcomes.

## G. Pharmacokinetics

Ishida et al. (2020) summarize human intravenous elimination half-life estimates of approximately 0.42–0.69 hours and predominant biliary excretion. These are secondary-source reported values; extract the cited primary PK study, study population, route, assay and variability before promoting them to verified PK fields. Oral bioavailability and route-specific exposure need separate primary-source review. Do not infer a dosing schedule.

## H. Human clinical and diagnostic evidence

Pralmorelin has an established diagnostic role in Japan for stimulation testing in evaluation of GH deficiency, as summarized in the 2020 peer-reviewed review. The review reports that after a single administration plasma GH levels exceeded 15 μg/L in healthy subjects while lower values were observed in patients with severe GHD; the threshold and protocol are assay- and jurisdiction-specific and must be verified against the Japanese product information and primary clinical validation studies before clinical interpretation.

This dossier does not provide a diagnostic protocol, dose, cut-off recommendation or patient-specific interpretation. It does not establish efficacy for chronic GH replacement or non-diagnostic uses.

## J. Jurisdictional regulatory status

### Japan
The peer-reviewed review by Ishida et al. reports pralmorelin (intravenous) as approved in Japan in October 2004 for diagnosis of GH deficiency in adults and children older than four years. The exact current product name, label, formulation, age wording and authorization holder should be confirmed in the current Japanese PMDA product information before the dossier marks the product-level record curator-verified.

### United States
FDA lists “Growth hormone releasing peptide-2 (GHRP-2) (for injectable and nasal routes of administration)” in **503B Category 2**, added September 29, 2023. FDA identifies potential immunogenicity risks related to aggregation and peptide-related impurities, and notes that the unnatural amino acid complicates peptide characterization. FDA also reports serious adverse-event reports in patients receiving GHRP-2, including increased insulin requirement to maintain blood glucose, deaths of critically ill study subjects, infection and pancreatitis; FDA explicitly states causality has not been established.

This is an FDA compounding bulk-substance risk classification under interim policy. It is not an FDA marketing approval, and it does not establish a blanket regulatory status for every route, product or jurisdiction.

### Australia
Australian Therapeutic Goods (Poisons Standard—February 2024) material lists pralmorelin (GHRP-2) in Schedule 4 and Appendix D, clause 5. This is a scheduling/control classification, not evidence of TGA marketing authorization. Confirm the current Poisons Standard and TGA product register independently before making a current product-authorization statement.

### Other jurisdictions
EMA, MHRA, Health Canada and additional national product registers have not been systematically checked in this dossier. Status: **not yet curated**, not “not approved worldwide”.
